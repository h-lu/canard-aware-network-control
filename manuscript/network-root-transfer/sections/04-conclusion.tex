\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Projection-blind delayed redistributions contain a sharp amount of recoverable
information.  Their zeroth delay moment vanishes exactly, but two separated
delay atoms allow the first moment to generate every transverse forcing
direction.  The source map has a sharp right inverse, and the stable Markov
inverse transfers this controllability to a dimension-uniform observation map
on transverse return covectors.  When the merged delay support contains only
one location, the same source is identically zero.  This
necessity--sufficiency dichotomy is the operator core of the paper.

The RFDE analysis turns that core into a nonlinear readout.  A
Dobrushin-controlled complete-history graph, selected one-sided traces, and a
finite-section estimate show that normalized fold-matching root increments
recover the return pairings with error \(C(\delta+|\zeta|)\), uniformly over
network size and, under the stated compatible base-layer and preparation
hypotheses, the unit probe ball.  Generator-supported directions preserve
the chosen base-layer support, and special positive base layers admit a common
signed perturbation interval.  Heterogeneous fast curvature and heterogeneous slow
recovery sensing yield distinct return covectors; the second model class
realizes every nonzero transverse covector ray at fixed network size.  Sparse
reset--cycle families show that these effects persist on \(O(N)\)-edge
networks rather than only in dense mean-field examples.

The identification claim is deliberately narrower than full network
reconstruction.  The network skeleton, stationary weights, delay locations,
and scalar coefficients are known; the root responses identify the compressed
return covector.  Its observation map is uniformly conditioned as an
isomorphism onto its range when that range carries the inherited dual norm.
Coordinate recovery from a finite noisy probe frame needs a separate lower
frame bound, and absolute root-measurement noise
is amplified by \(\delta^{-3}|\zeta|^{-1}\).  Thus the present theorem gives
uniform mathematical identifiability of weighted pairings, not a noise-robust
experimental inversion theorem.

The root itself has an equally sharp scope boundary.  In the shared-resource
family, localized admissible changes of the artificial outer completion move
the exact finite-\(\delta\) root while leaving the physical RFDE and retained
fold data fixed.  The current
finite-section axioms therefore do not define a preparation-independent
maximal canard.  Once each preparation's baseline is subtracted, however, the
leading response germ and its limiting covector are independent of the fixed
preparation within the stated comparison class.  The relative-canonical
object proved here is the hidden-return response functional with the physical
model, projection, matching data, and parameter normalization fixed; it is
not the finite-section representative.  Geometrically, after separate
baseline centering and \(\delta^{-3}\) blow-up, the shared-resource selected
connection locus on the full structural ball has
\(\dd\xi\)-calibrated limiting conormal
\((1,-\Lambda_N)\); along a ray \(\zeta R_N\) this is
\(\operatorname{span}\{\dd\xi-\Lambda_N(R_N)\dd\zeta\}\).  Its
cokernel-valued form is independent of a scalar gap row, but it remains a
jet of the selected connection relation.  Identifying it with a physical
maximal-canard locus requires a preparation-free outer-history construction
and a weighted \(C^1\) comparison.  The abstract criterion in
\cref{prop:physical-weighted-c1-transfer} states these comparison obligations
and proves formal naturality under an exact parameter-coherent section
holonomy, without constructing that physical holonomy or asserting that the
hypotheses have been verified for this model.

The physical RFDE nevertheless fixes an exact constant-history critical curve
and a dimension-uniform leading frozen-voltage splitting
(\cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton}).  The latter persists, with a capped
rate, as a positive-\(\delta\) complete-history splitting for the
frozen-resource voltage RFDE
(\cref{prop:frozen-voltage-history-splitting}); its reduced critical-layer
equation also has positive truncated actions on both branches
(\cref{cor:outer-reduced-actions}).  This does not yet construct a
nonautonomous invariant slow history.  In fact, the raw voltage--resource
frozen system can have two repelling roots, so the next theorem must first
construct a true tracker and separate its longitudinal direction
(\cref{rem:frozen-resource-phase-quotient}).  Conditional on such a tracker,
\cref{prop:exact-outer-history-equation,prop:physical-history-phase-quotient,%
prop:exact-resource-gauge-quotient} now provide the exact uncut invariance
equation, transported time-tangent quotient, and a resource-gauge
triangularization conjugate to an ordinary voltage-history RFDE by an
\(O(S_\delta^{-1})\) Volterra operator.  They do not prove tracker existence,
the required relative parameter jets, or the quotient dichotomy.
\Cref{prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form} nevertheless gives an exactly
equivalent fixed-delay fold-time system, while
\cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer} proves its fixed-parameter
\(C^0\) complete-history Green--collar inverse after the current collective
phase has been fixed.  The repelling compatible-collar feedback changes no
finite boundary row and uses only the one-dimensional future scalar Green
line.  The scalar phase--event core is explicitly invertible after scaling
the event-time variation by \(\delta\); it also proves that any net phase
shift produced by a compact speed pulse persists.  After deleting every
zero-delay difference intact and absorbing all current positive-delay pieces,
 \cref{lem:current-absorbed-delay-splitting,%
    prop:attracting-finite-generation-flushing,%
    lem:repelling-component-generations,%
    prop:repelling-component-flushing,%
    prop:compatible-repelling-collar-closure} prove genuine high-order
finite-generation flushing theorems for both fixed-parameter outer branches.
The repelling proof does not take powers of a noncausal mixed Green operator:
it resums the current loop, counts only strict delayed evaluations, and uses
a terminal first-exit action to separate the outer-small and action-small
word classes.  The normalized component traces tend to zero uniformly in
network size and lose only one reconstructed derivative in the speed
component.  The repelling \(p(0)\)-to-collar feedback is closed in that
strong class without adding a boundary row.  In addition,
\cref{prop:normal-to-phase-action-coupling} proves that the full
boundary-layer normal column has zeroth-order phase action
\(O(r_{\rm out}^2+\delta^{2-2\vartheta})\).
\Cref{lem:reduced-flow-phase-defect,prop:delayed-phase-event-core} then
derive the reverse formal phase-delay column exactly, prove that its
constant mode cancels, and invert the resulting delayed scalar phase block.
They also expose the two phase-induced normal sources; the persistent
constant part is precisely the derivative of the affine normal residual.
\Cref{prop:event-aligned-normal-traces} triangularizes the moving terminal
trace on a fixed \(r\)-section, eliminating the scaled event-time unknown
from normal rows at the reduced base and identifying the sharp
\(\delta^{-2}\) loss of the raw moving-trace formulation.
\Cref{lem:terminal-relative-trace-scale-chart} gives the corresponding
nonlinear \(C^2\) strong-to-weak moving-window coordinates at a nonzero
terminal base.  The event time is retained, the fixed-section equation is a
derived border functional, the nonzero terminal columns and mixed second
derivatives remain explicit, and raw translations incur the sharp
\(\delta^{-2}\)/\(\delta^{-4}\) losses.  This is trace calculus on a supplied
strong buffer, not a terminal solution family or moving-event inverse.
\Cref{prop:phase-delay-action-shift,prop:structured-phase-action-green}
replace the nonuniform old-state reverse column by an exact shifted response
whose true-speed action is
\(O(r_{\rm out}^2+\delta/S_\delta)\).
\Cref{lem:raw-phase-boundary-column-assembly} identifies the differential of
the raw-compatible collar, including the repelling closed feedback, and
\cref{thm:raw-compatible-phase-normal-inverse} closes the resulting
zeroth-order phase--normal border in a dimension-uniform graph/action norm.
The combined action is
\(O(r_{\rm out}^2+\delta/S_\delta+\delta^{2-2\vartheta})\).
\Cref{lem:raw-compatible-base-collar-refinement} is an independent
preparatory refinement: it proves that the base collar correction vanishes
like \(O(\varepsilon|e|)\), without replacing the sharp
\(O(\varepsilon)\) moving-endpoint column used in the Schur inverse.
Meanwhile,
\cref{lem:inner-anchored-nonlinear-phase-chart} constructs an exact
same-sign nonlinear phase chart fixed at the fold-side section.
\Cref{prop:inner-anchored-relative-phase-schur} then closes its one-row
relative-phase linearization with pointwise raw action
\[
 O\!\left(r_{\rm out}
   \{r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-2}+\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\}\right).
\]
\Cref{prop:relative-structured-phase-response} then uses the relative anchor
to prove an \(O(\delta)\) delay profile and
\(O(r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-1})\) bounds for both the returned phase column in
the old normalized state norm and its pointwise true action.  The associated
relative graph--action norm is uniformly equivalent to the anchored graph
norm.  The unrestricted old-state obstruction is not contradicted: its bad
constant-phase pulses do not remain in bounded relative balls.  These results
are complemented by
\cref{lem:nonlinear-reduced-flow-phase-delay}: the exact formal
\(q_0\)-history delay functional linearizes to \(\mathscr P_{\sigma,N}\)
and has a dimension-uniform two-point remainder controlled by
\(\delta|r|\) in both value and piecewise derivative.
\Cref{lem:second-jet-raw-history-assembler} additionally closes the
raw-compatible old-history boundary nonlinearity on a fixed-reference ball:
the endpoint/collar second jet and the normalized quadratic graph remainder
are dimension-uniform, while overlap with the original collar chart follows
from the finite compatibility recursion rather than a global equivalence of
fading norms.  This assembler is not a past orbit.  The fixed-section
assembled normal graph residual is now closed by
\cref{thm:fixed-section-nonlinear-graph-residual}: on the inner-anchored
fixed-section graph space its derivative is the existing uniform Schur
isomorphism, its base defect is
\(O(r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-2})\), and its remainder is uniformly quadratic
in two-point form.  Although that theorem itself stops before solving the
residual, \cref{cor:fixed-section-canonical-zero} closes the pointwise
fixed-parameter contraction.  It gives one unique small canonical zero on
each outer branch, a first Newton jet, strict speed sign, and an exact finite
forward RFDE segment.  On every retained subsegment whose delay collar lies
beyond the first maximal delay,
\cref{cor:generated-interior-canonical-tracker} reparameterizes that solution
by the physical collective coordinate, proves the exact backtrack and uncut
history equations, and realizes both quotient constructions with an
\(O(S_\delta^{-1})\) Volterra correction.  This removes the earlier purely
conditional status of the quotient on the generated interior only.
Parameter jets, the moving terminal event, both physical branch endpoints, a
past-complete history, the cross-branch handoff, and a physical connection
root remain open.
Moreover,
\cref{prop:backward-asymptotic-nonselection} shows that convergence in an
unnormalized history norm, even with one phase row, does not by itself select
a unique tame history family.  Physical identification therefore still
requires a nonempty physical tracker/representative class, its attracting
point and repelling codimension-one history sheet, and the weighted
comparison stated above.

Closing Dobrushin gaps, moving or proliferating delay atoms, infinite networks,
and order-one layer changes would require new uniform range estimates.
Likewise, an intrinsic maximal canard would require physical outer invariant
histories absent from the local selection.
The present result isolates the reusable part of the mechanism: exact
projection blindness, delay-moment controllability, transverse return, and
nonlinear complete-history readout.
