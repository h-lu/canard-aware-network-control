\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proves that exact neutrality under same-history stationary
projection of the vector field does not imply dynamical irrelevance.  In the shared-resource
retarded Markov networks considered here, stationary-row-neutral
redistributions leave the projected RFDE vector field unchanged at every full
history.  Two separated delay locations nevertheless make their first moment
span all transverse forcing directions.  The associated source has an
explicit sharp right inverse; a common Dobrushin gap carries this range through
the stable history lift and produces a return covector with bounds independent
of network size.  With one merged delay location the same leading source
vanishes.  This source dichotomy is the algebraic core of the
projection-blind return mechanism.

The global result turns that mechanism into an intrinsic nonlinear
observable.  For every fixed member of a declared anchored recovery class, we
construct the incoming past-complete branch of one hyperbolic equilibrium and
the future-asymptotic stable history sheet of another.  Their exact membership
relation has a unique local parameter root and yields a complete heteroclinic
orbit.  Quantitative tracking on both slow branches and through the fold shows
that this connection is a canard, rather than an arbitrary heteroclinic.  Its
structural derivative satisfies
\[
 D_\eta\mu_{c,N}^{\rm anc}
   =\delta^3\Lambda_N+O(\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|)
\]
in the full dual norm, uniformly in \(N\).  Thus the same hidden covector that
is reconstructed by the two-delay source controls the displacement of a
genuine complete-history canard.

The scope boundaries are part of the result.  The original unanchored
recovery law has no equilibria on the required outer components and does not
acquire a maximal canard merely from a finite matching construction.  The
anchor multiplier is instead part of a different autonomous RFDE, fixed
before its root is defined and identical to the original law throughout the
retained fold-history tube.  Exact roots for different anchors may differ.
After each fixed model is centered at its own baseline, their
\(\delta^{-3}\)-weighted conormals converge uniformly to the same
\((1,-\Lambda_N)\).  The invariant object shared across the anchor class is
therefore the leading connection jet, not an anchor-independent scalar root.

Nor is the dual statement a full reconstruction theorem.  The network
skeleton, stationary weights, delay locations, and scalar coefficients are
known; the measurements determine only the compressed transverse return
covector.  Its observation map is uniformly conditioned onto its inherited
range, but inversion from a particular noisy finite probe frame additionally
requires a frame lower bound and incurs the expected
\(\delta^{-3}\)-scale noise amplification.

The reusable mathematical principle is the composition
\[
 \text{blind structural range}
 \longrightarrow \text{transverse history inverse}
 \longrightarrow \text{connection conormal}.
\]
The abstract results isolate these three interfaces, while the anchored
shared-resource model verifies them together.  Extending the principle to
other RFDE architectures now requires model-specific complete-history objects
and a weighted \(C^1\) passage comparison, rather than another formal
implicit-function argument.  A distinct future problem is to identify a
natural global completion supplied by an application and then test whether
its experimentally accessible event data provide a well-conditioned probe
frame for the covector established here.
