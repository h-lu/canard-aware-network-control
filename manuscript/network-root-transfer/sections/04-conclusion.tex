\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The main conclusion is a noncommutation principle.  Stationary projection
annihilates the structural perturbation as an exact functional identity on
the full RFDE history space, but invariant-history reduction retains a
transverse displacement which heterogeneous curvature returns to the scalar
matching gap.  Thus identical projected vector-field functionals need not
identify a selected complete-history root.  The dimension-uniform expansion,
the codimension-one classification of zero leading-response directions, and the
curvature-variance witness make this obstruction quantitative rather than
merely heuristic.

The explicit family shows that the phenomenon survives the two most obvious
degeneracy objections.  Its response stays bounded away from zero on a
fixed-radius neighborhood in a network-uniform direction norm, and its
matched root trajectory has a transverse amplitude of order \(\delta^2\)
uniformly in network size.  Pairwise distinct curvatures eliminate every
nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal.  The root displacement is therefore not a
synchronous scalar result copied to larger networks.

The baseline root remains tied to the declared fixed preparation.  What
is universal here is the leading response increment: any two fixed admissible
preparations produce the same coefficient and differ only at the proved
remainder order.  Establishing a preparation-independent maximal canard,
allowing moving delay support or a closing Dobrushin gap, or connecting this
local history root to a physical pulse threshold requires separate arguments.
