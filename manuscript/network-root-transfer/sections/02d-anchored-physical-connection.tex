\section{A globally anchored complete-history connection}
\label{sec:anchored-physical-connection}

The original shared-resource equation has no equilibrium on either outer
branch, by \cref{prop:main-original-recovery-anchor-no-go}.  We now specify a
global model class for which the missing physical objects do exist.  This is
an enlargement of the physical recovery law, not a preferred preparation of
the old equation.  The enlargement agrees literally with the old equation on
a fixed neighborhood of every retained fold and delay-history tube.  It
therefore leaves the blind redistribution mechanism and its local response
covector unchanged.

\subsection{Anchored recovery data and exact equilibria}

Fix numbers
\begin{equation}
 0<r_{\rm loc}<r_A<r_0,                              \label{eq:anchor-radii}
\end{equation}
where \(r_0\) is supplied by
\cref{prop:anchor-toolkit-critical-splitting}.  An \emph{anchor multiplier} is a
function \(g_{\rm anc}\in C^{12}(\mathbb R)\) satisfying
\begin{align}
 g_{\rm anc}(r)&=1 &&(|r|\le r_{\rm loc}),\notag\\
 g_{\rm anc}(r)&>0 &&(-r_A<r<r_A),\notag\\
 g_{\rm anc}(\pm r_A)&=0,&
 g_{\rm anc}'(-r_A)&>0>g_{\rm anc}'(r_A).           \label{eq:anchor-multiplier}
\end{align}
The simple zeros are crossed; in particular, the multiplier is not continued
as zero outside the anchor interval.  Such functions exist, for example by
smoothly joining the constant function on the central interval to
\(1-r^2/r_A^2\) near the two anchors.

For a completely explicit nonempty class, choose
\(r_{\rm loc}<r_{\rm mid}<r_A\), set
\begin{equation}
 h(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\le0,\\ e^{-1/x},&x>0,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 S(x)=\frac{h(x)}{h(x)+h(1-x)},                    \label{eq:anchor-smooth-step}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 \chi_{\rm anc}(r)
 =S\left(\frac{r^2-r_{\rm loc}^2}
                 {r_{\rm mid}^2-r_{\rm loc}^2}\right),
 \qquad
 g_{\rm anc}(r)=1-\chi_{\rm anc}(r)\frac{r^2}{r_A^2}.       \label{eq:anchor-explicit-multiplier}
\end{equation}
Then \(g_{\rm anc}=1\) on the central interval,
\(g_{\rm anc}=1-r^2/r_A^2\) near and beyond the anchors, and all conditions
in \cref{eq:anchor-multiplier} hold.  This formula is fixed as part of the
physical equation; it is not varied during matching.

For the uniform results, fix \(g_*,G_*>0\) and let
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm anc}(r_A;g_*,G_*)\) be any closed collection of such
multipliers for which
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \|g_{\rm anc}\|_{C^{12}([-r_0,r_0])}&\le G_*,
 &g_{\rm anc}'(-r_A)&\ge g_*,
 &-g_{\rm anc}'(r_A)&\ge g_* ,\\
 g_{\rm anc}(r)&\ge
 g_*\min\{1,r_A-|r|\},&& |r|<r_A .
\end{aligned}                                      \label{eq:anchor-admissible-class}
\end{equation}
The last line is the quantitative lower envelope used in every annular
clock and Green estimate; it replaces an implicit ``common lower bound on
compact subannuli''.  These are quantitative conditions, and
\cref{eq:anchor-explicit-multiplier} belongs to such a class after its radii
and a sufficiently small \(g_*\) are fixed.  The theorem therefore concerns a
nonempty class of global completions, not one interpolation formula chosen
after a root.  Asymmetric anchors are treated by the same proof but are not
included in the notation.

Put
\begin{equation}
 r(\phi)=\pi_N^T\{\phi(0)-\mathbf1\},\qquad
 \sigma_{\rm anc}(r;\delta,\nu)
  =(r-\delta^2\nu)g_{\rm anc}(r),                   \label{eq:anchor-source}
\end{equation}
and replace only \cref{eq:shared-rfde-w} by
\begin{equation}
 \dot w(t)=\delta^2\sigma_{\rm anc}(r(v_t);\delta,\nu).
                                                               \label{eq:anchored-recovery-law}
\end{equation}
The voltage equation \cref{eq:shared-rfde-v}, including every structural
redistribution, is unchanged.  We call the resulting equation the
\emph{globally anchored shared-resource RFDE} associated with
\(g_{\rm anc}\).

\begin{proposition}[Central identity and physical anchor equilibria]
\label[proposition]{prop:anchor-central-identity-equilibria}
After decreasing the common diagonal wedge, the anchored and original
shared-resource RFDEs agree as maps on the full depth-two retained history
tube.  In particular,
\cref{prop:projection-blindness,thm:shared-resource,%
thm:joint-redistribution-conormal} and the two-delay range and no-go results
apply there without a coefficient change.

For every admitted \(N,\delta,\nu,\eta\), the constant histories
\begin{equation}
 E_{N}^{\pm}
 =\left(\theta\longmapsto V_{0,N}(\pm r_A),\,
                   w_{0,N}(\pm r_A)\right)          \label{eq:anchor-equilibria}
\end{equation}
are exact equilibria of the anchored RFDE.  They are independent of the
redistribution \(\eta\).  If \(|\delta^2\nu|<r_A/2\), then
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_\pm'
 :=\partial_r\sigma_{\rm anc}(\pm r_A;\delta,\nu)<0. \label{eq:anchor-source-derivative-sign}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The adjective ``physical'' below always refers to the fixed anchored RFDE.
Changing \(g_{\rm anc}\) changes the global physical equation and need not
preserve the exact finite-\(\delta\) root.  What will be independent of the
anchor choice is the centered front-face conormal.

\subsection{Hyperbolic anchors and their history objects}

Let \(\mathcal H_{N,\delta}\) be the original-time history space in
\cref{eq:shared-parameters}.  The solution manifold of compatible
\(C^1\) histories is used whenever a differentiable event holonomy is
required.

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-uniform anchor indices and physical history objects]
\label[theorem]{thm:anchor-indices-manifolds}
Fix one bounded admissible class
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm anc}(r_A;g_*,G_*)\) and one compact structural parameter
ball.  After
decreasing \(r_A\), then the parameter ball and \(\delta_0\), independently
of \(N\) and of the member of \(\mathfrak G_{\rm anc}\), the equilibria
\(E_N^\pm\) are hyperbolic.  Their Morse indices are
\begin{equation}
 \dim W^u(E_N^+)=1,
 \qquad \operatorname{codim}W^s(E_N^-)=1.           \label{eq:anchor-Morse-indices}
\end{equation}
More precisely, each equilibrium has one simple slow root
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{s,N}^{\pm}
 =\delta^2\frac{\sigma_\pm'}{w_{0,N}'(\pm r_A)}
       +O_{\rm unif}(\delta^4),                     \label{eq:anchor-slow-roots}
\end{equation}
so \(\lambda_{s,N}^+>0\) and \(\lambda_{s,N}^-<0\).
At \(E_N^+\) every remaining characteristic root is stable.  At
\(E_N^-\) there is exactly one further open-right-half-plane root, the real
simple strong voltage root, and every remaining root is stable.  The stable
part is measured in the capped fading norm of
\cref{prop:anchor-toolkit-critical-splitting}; no \(\delta\)-independent
spectral gap is asserted for the long-delay tail.

There are common \(c_A>0\) and \(M_A<\infty\) for which one may take
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{A,\delta}=c_A\delta^4,
 \qquad d_{A,\delta}=\tfrac12\rho_{A,\delta}.       \label{eq:anchor-local-manifold-scale}
\end{equation}
The inward branch of \(W^u(E_N^+)\) consists of histories with a unique
past-complete extension converging to \(E_N^+\).  It meets every sufficiently
nearby section \(r=r_A-d_A\), in particular the section with
\(d_A=d_{A,\delta}\), in one point and transversely.
The local stable manifold of \(E_N^-\) is a codimension-one
\(C^2\) history sheet.  In the slow coordinate divided by
\(\rho_{A,\delta}\), the local unstable and stable graph charts have a
common radius and contraction margin.  The incoming point, the stable
sheet, and their graph charts are \(C^1\) in \((\nu,\eta)\) in the
fixed strong-to-weak charts used below; their unscaled first-jet bounds are
at most \(C\delta^{-M_A}\), uniformly in \(N\).  No unscaled
\(\delta\)-independent local-manifold radius is claimed.

The point denoted \(C^-_{A,N,\delta,\nu,\eta}\) is selected later: it is
the first local-chart hit of the exact half-line orbit constructed in
\cref{thm:anchor-annulus-flat-forgetting}.  Thus no local coordinate is
silently continued backward to the inner section.  This later selection is
one chart center only; it does not define an invariant one-dimensional
weak-stable family, a preferred strong-stable foliation, or a monotone
\(r\)-clock on the full stable sheet.

Only the incoming branch is asserted to be past complete away from a
connection.  The later selected chart-center orbit and every history in the
local stable sheet are future asymptotic to \(E_N^-\).  At a root the incoming
branch belongs to that sheet and gives one orbit complete in both time
directions; no ambient backward RFDE semiflow is used to define the stable
sheet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Anchored annuli, nonlinear stable sheet, and flat forgetting]
\label[theorem]{thm:anchor-annulus-flat-forgetting}
Retain \cref{thm:anchor-indices-manifolds}.  There are fixed inner sections
in \(|r|<r_{\rm loc}\), one common complete-history central chart on the
phase section
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_0=\{X=0\},                                  \label{eq:anchor-central-section}
\end{equation}
constants \(M_{\rm anc}\ge0\), and
\(C_{\rm anc},c_{\rm anc}>0\), all independent of \(N\), with the following
properties.

The inward unstable branch of \(E_N^+\) has an exact forward hit
\(A^0_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}\) of \(\Sigma_0\), constructed before any
connection condition is imposed.  The central-section histories whose
forward orbits remain in the declared channel and converge to \(E_N^-\)
form, in the common chart \((\psi,u)\), the nonlinear graph
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S^-_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}
 =\{(\psi,u):u=F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(\psi)\}, \label{eq:anchor-stable-sheet-graph}
\end{equation}
where the superscript \(0\) distinguishes the central-section graph from
the entry graph used in its construction, and \(F^{-,0}\) is \(C^2\) in
its state variable and \(C^1\) in
\((\nu,\eta)\), in the fixed strong-to-weak parameter chart used below.
The intrinsic entry graph is constructed directly on the fixed fold-time
half-line by a stable-forward/one-dimensional-unstable-backward
Lyapunov--Perron problem.  The central graph is its exact preimage under a
forward event holonomy.  Its terminal condition is convergence as
\(s\to+\infty\), not a row at a finite outer section and not a backward
RFDE semiflow.

The following finite-core comparison is made inside one fixed anchored
RFDE.  Choose a common buffer time \(L_\delta\) on the negative channel so
that the exact and truncated problems use the same compatible entry chart,
phase row, coefficients, and generated delay histories on
\([0,L_\delta]\), and so that the capped unstable action satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_{\rm cap}(L_\delta,0)\ge
 \frac{c_{\rm anc}\log(1/\delta)}{\delta}.       \label{eq:anchor-comparison-buffer-action}
\end{equation}
An admissible finite-core tail selector is a scalar terminal graph row at
\(L_\delta\), \(C^2\) in state and \(C^1\) in \((\nu,\eta)\), whose graph
stays in the declared tube, whose row and first parameter derivative have
at most polynomial \(\delta^{-1}\) size, and whose value on the transported
unstable column has a polynomially bounded inverse.  Let
\(F^{-,L}_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}\) be the resulting finite-core entry graph and
let \(\lambda^{L}_{\rm ent}\) be its first conormal, normalized on the
same entry column as the intrinsic half-line row
\(\lambda_{\rm ent}\).  Then their entry/inner restrictions differ by at
most
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak f_\delta
 :=C_{\rm anc}\delta^{-M_{\rm anc}}
       \exp\left\{-\frac{c_{\rm anc}\log(1/\delta)}{\delta}\right\}.
                                                               \label{eq:anchor-flat-envelope}
\end{equation}
This is the full-history capped-action envelope.  It is weaker than the
\(e^{-A/\delta^2}\) decay of the generated current core when a nonzero
delay is present, but it is \(O(\delta^P)\) for every fixed \(P\) and is
the rate justified by the long-delay fading norm.
For histories and nonlinear graph maps, the same estimate holds for the
operators
\begin{equation}
 1,\quad \partial_\nu,\quad D_\eta,\quad D_\psi,\quad
 \partial_{(\nu,\eta)}D_\psi.                       \label{eq:anchor-flat-jet-list}
\end{equation}
in the normalized entry and inner-history norms.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \|\lambda_{\rm ent}-\lambda^{L}_{\rm ent}\|
 +\|D_{(\nu,\eta)}
       (\lambda_{\rm ent}-\lambda^{L}_{\rm ent})\|
 \le \mathfrak f_\delta .                           \label{eq:anchor-flat-conormal-comparison}
\end{equation}
The comparison permits only coefficient or asymptotic differences supported
after \(L_\delta\).  Changing the entry stable coordinate, phase row, or
chart is not a forgettable tail change.  Such a post-buffer difference is
an auxiliary realization of the finite tail selector; the intrinsic graph
on the other side of the comparison always belongs to the one fixed
anchored RFDE.  This is not a comparison of exact roots of two different
global physical models.

The finite-core row used in the outer interface is not identified with
\(\lambda_{\rm ent}\) here.  It is a linear graph selected on a different
pulled finite core and does not by itself construct the nonlinear stable
sheet of the fixed anchored RFDE.  The physical gap and its response below
use the intrinsic half-line sheet and therefore do not require that
additional identification.
\end{theorem}

The proof is carried out in fold time \(s=\delta t\).  Every delay is the
literal fixed translation \(s\mapsto s-\theta_k\).  No division by an outer
speed and no state-dependent delay foot occurs.  The old
original-endpoint family is invoked only after the exact anchored histories
have entered \(|r|<r_{\rm loc}\).

\subsection{The anchored membership gap and heteroclinic root}

We now fix the scalar coordinate without identifying a domain column with
a codomain cokernel.  Let \(\mathcal M_{\Sigma_0}^2\) be the compatible
\(C^2\) solution-manifold section.  In its raw central chart, write
\(z(\phi)=(0,Y(\phi))\) for the planar coordinate and let
\(\mathfrak n_p(\phi)\) collect the complete normal-history coordinate
\(h-H_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(z)\), together with all remaining compatible
history coordinates; here \(p=(\nu,\eta)\).  Present evaluation returns
\(z\), so these coordinates have a fixed Banach model space
\(\mathscr K_0\).  To avoid confusion with the Hermite-collar support
constant used elsewhere, put
\begin{equation}
 c_{\mathscr H}:=\frac{2}{\alpha e}.                 \label{eq:anchor-first-integral-normalization}
\end{equation}
On the declared chart,
\begin{equation}
 D_Y\{c_{\mathscr H}\mathscr H_\alpha(0,Y)\}
 =1+O(\delta)\ne0.                                  \label{eq:anchor-first-integral-local-invertibility}
\end{equation}
The Banach inverse-function theorem therefore gives an exact,
parameter-trivialized tubular chart
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak C_{0,p}:B_{\mathscr K_0}(0,r_0)\times(-u_0,u_0)
   \longrightarrow\mathcal M_{\Sigma_0}^2,\qquad
 \phi=\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,u),                    \label{eq:anchor-central-history-chart}
\end{equation}
which is \(C^2\) in \((\psi,u)\), \(C^1\) in \(p\), and is characterized by
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak n_p(\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,u))=\psi,
 \qquad
 c_{\mathscr H}\mathscr H_\alpha
   (z(\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,u)))=u.                \label{eq:anchor-central-chart-identities}
\end{equation}
Thus varying \(u\) holds the entire normal/history coordinate fixed.  At
the singular base point put
\(\jmath_0^u=D_u\mathfrak C_{0,p}(0,0)\); this is the normalized transported
scalar column.

Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_{0\to{\rm ent},p}:\mathcal U_{0,p}
       \longrightarrow\mathcal U_{{\rm ent},p}       \label{eq:anchor-forward-central-entry-holonomy}
\end{equation}
be the first-hit map from \(\Sigma_0\) to
\(\Sigma_-^{\rm ent}\), defined only on the declared physical tube by
forward RFDE evolution and event recutting.  The travel contains the three
reserved delay buffers, so this map is \(C^2\) in the compatible state chart
and \(C^1\) in \(p\).  If \(\mathfrak m_{{\rm ent},p}\) denotes the exact
entry membership function of the intrinsic half-line sheet, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak M_{0,p}(\psi,u)
 =\mathfrak m_{{\rm ent},p}\!\left(
    \mathcal P_{0\to{\rm ent},p}
       (\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,u))\right).           \label{eq:anchor-forward-preimage-membership}
\end{equation}
No inverse of \(\mathcal P_{0\to{\rm ent},p}\) occurs.  The exact
transport-column splitting alone would give only conditional transversality
at a zero.  The missing nonemptiness is supplied by the following
same-equation shooting result.

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic stable-sheet shooting at a generated cut]
\label[proposition]{prop:anchor-central-finite-shooting}
Fix \(0<\chi_b<1<c_b\).  Instantiate the central and outer constructions
with Gaussian exponents \(p_c,p_o\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
 p_c=\left(\frac{c_b}{\chi_b}\right)^2p_o,\qquad
 S_\iota=\sqrt{2p_\iota\log(1/\delta)}\quad(\iota=c,o),
 \qquad
 s_b^\#=\chi_bS_c=c_bS_o.                            \label{eq:anchor-main-reserved-scales}
\end{equation}
The common physical cut is
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_b=\{r=-\rho_b\},\qquad
 \rho_b=\frac{\delta s_b^\#}{2\alpha}.               \label{eq:anchor-main-generated-cut}
\end{equation}
Its complete delay enlargement lies strictly inside the unmodified central
history tube, while the independently constructed outer reference has
generated its own literal history there.

Let the selected central repelling trace start at
\begin{equation}
 Z^{c}_{p,0}
 =\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\bar\psi_p,\bar u_p),             \label{eq:anchor-reference-central-data}
\end{equation}
and denote its history at \(\Sigma_b\) by \(C_{b,p}\).  Let \(O_{b,p}\)
be the independently constructed exact outer reference at that section.
There is one compatible chart
\[
 \Phi_{b,p}(\psi_b,u_b),\qquad \Phi_{b,p}(0,0)=O_{b,p},
\]
whose scalar column is the oriented outer unstable current column.  In this
chart the histories whose forward orbits stay in the declared channel and
converge to \(E_N^-\) form the intrinsic graph
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_b^-(p)
 =\{\Phi_{b,p}(\psi_b,F_{b,p}^-(\psi_b))\}.           \label{eq:anchor-main-intrinsic-b-sheet}
\end{equation}
It is \(C^2\) in \(\psi_b\), has the selected first \(p\)-jet, and is
defined by the canonically normalized membership function
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak m_{b,p}(\Phi_{b,p}(\psi_b,u_b))
 =u_b-F_{b,p}^-(\psi_b),\qquad
 \lambda_{b,p}^o:=D\mathfrak m_{b,p}.                \label{eq:anchor-main-b-membership}
\end{equation}
This graph is obtained from a literal-history mixed problem whose terminal
condition is membership in the exact future-decaying sheet.  It is therefore
intrinsic to the fixed anchored RFDE and does not depend on a finite
completion or terminal selector.

At a later cut still inside the exact preparation--physical agreement
region, place an affine terminal row through the selected repelling trace.
Pulling that row back over the common physical core produces a finite graph
\(F_{b,p}^{\rm sel}\) which contains \(C_{b,p}\) exactly.  The intrinsic
and finite graphs satisfy, for \(J_p^1\in\{1,\partial_\nu,D_\eta\}\),
\begin{equation}
 \|J_p^1(F_{b,p}^--F_{b,p}^{\rm sel})\|_{C^1}
 \le\varepsilon_{bc,\delta}\le\delta^{Q_*+M_{\rm gl}+4}.          \label{eq:anchor-main-finite-intrinsic-comparison}
\end{equation}
Here \(Q_*\) is any prescribed finite order after the preparation exponent
is chosen.  The assertion concerns the difference of two graphs; neither
graph is claimed to have small curvature.

The forward hit \(\mathcal P_{0\to b,p}\) stays inside the exact central
tube.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}(\psi,u)
 :=\mathfrak m_{b,p}\!\left(
   \mathcal P_{0\to b,p}\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,u)\right).          \label{eq:anchor-main-intrinsic-membership}
\end{equation}
At the selected reference its scalar derivative has the positive
 action factor
\begin{align}
 A_{0b,p}
 &:=D\mathfrak m_{b,p}(C_{b,p})
       D\mathcal P_{0\to b,p}
       D_u\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\bar\psi_p,\bar u_p),\notag\\
 c_E\frac{\delta}{s_b^\#}E_b
 &\le A_{0b,p}\le C_E\frac{\delta}{s_b^\#}E_b,\qquad
 E_b:=e^{(s_b^\#)^2/2}=\delta^{-c_b^2p_o},\qquad
 |D_p^{\rm sel}\log A_{0b,p}|\le C\delta^{-M_A}.     \label{eq:anchor-main-projective-action}
\end{align}
Choose \(Q_*>M_{\rm gl}+12\), then choose \(p_o\) after all finite
jet-loss exponents so that \cref{eq:anchor-main-finite-intrinsic-comparison}
and \(c_b^2p_o>M_{\rm gl}+8\) hold.  There are a fixed
\(M_{\rm sh}<\infty\) and radii
\begin{equation}
 r_{{\rm sh},\delta}\ge c\delta^{M_{\rm sh}},\qquad
 c\delta^{Q_*}\le\rho_{{\rm sh},\delta}
 \le C\delta^{Q_*},                                  \label{eq:anchor-main-shooting-radii}
\end{equation}
such that, with
\begin{equation}
 \xi=A_{0b,p}(u-\bar u_p),\qquad
 \|\psi-\bar\psi_p\|<r_{{\rm sh},\delta},\quad
 |\xi|<\rho_{{\rm sh},\delta},                       \label{eq:anchor-finite-shooting-strip}
\end{equation}
the whole strip lies in the central and intrinsic-sheet charts and
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\xi\mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}\ge\kappa_0>0. \label{eq:anchor-finite-shooting-orientation}
\end{equation}
The amplified unstable displacement at the \(b\)-section associated with the
whole action strip is
\begin{equation}
 E_b\rho_{{\rm sh},\delta}A_{0b,p}^{-1}
 \le C\frac{s_b^\#}{\delta}\delta^{Q_*}
 <\tfrac14\delta^\kappa,                            \label{eq:anchor-main-action-strip-containment}
\end{equation}
so the strip remains strictly inside the retained central tube.

The two strip faces have opposite signs.  Hence
\(\mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}=0\) has one and only one graph
 \begin{equation}
 u=F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,p}(\psi),\qquad
 A_{0b,p}|F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,p}(\bar\psi_p)-\bar u_p|
 \le C\delta^{Q_*},                                  \label{eq:anchor-main-intrinsic-root-distance}
\end{equation}
which is \(C^2\) in state and has the selected first \(p\)-jet.  Moreover,
 \begin{equation}
 \left|J_p^1\{F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,p}(\bar\psi_p)-\bar u_p\}\right|
 \le C\delta^{Q_*-M_{\rm gl}}\frac{s_b^\#}{\delta E_b}
 =o(\delta^8).                                       \label{eq:anchor-main-intrinsic-root-flatness}
\end{equation}
At every zero, equality in \cref{eq:anchor-main-intrinsic-b-sheet} is literal
equality of the full voltage history and current resource.  Forward
uniqueness therefore gives one exact orbit converging to \(E_N^-\).  Finally,
for every prescribed finite \(Q_{\rm in}\), after the preparation exponent is
chosen beyond the finite trace losses, the incoming normal coordinate obeys
\begin{equation}
 \|\psi_A-\bar\psi_p\|
 \le C\delta^{Q_{\rm in}}
 <\tfrac12r_{{\rm sh},\delta}.                       \label{eq:anchor-incoming-in-shooting-ball}
\end{equation}
Thus the graph domain contains the actual incoming history used below.
\end{proposition}

The proof of
\cref{prop:anchor-central-finite-shooting} first constructs the intrinsic
sheet at \(\Sigma_b\), then proves a sign-changing bracket for
\(\mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}\), and only afterwards differentiates its
unique zero.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}(\psi,u)=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \partial_u\mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}(\psi,u)
 \ge c_E\frac{\delta}{2s_b^\#}E_b>0.               \label{eq:anchor-forward-membership-transversality}
\end{equation}
Membership in the sheet at \(b\), convergence to \(E_N^-\), and membership
in the later intrinsic entry sheet describe the same forward orbit.
Consequently the entry pullback \(\mathfrak M_{0,p}\) in
\cref{eq:anchor-forward-preimage-membership} has exactly the same zero graph,
and
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak M_{0,p}(\psi,F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,p}(\psi))=0.             \label{eq:anchor-central-graph-preimage-identity}
\end{equation}
Thus the graph in \cref{eq:anchor-stable-sheet-graph} is an intrinsic
stable-set germ, rather than a graph selected by a finite terminal row.

Its normalized conormal as a covector on the physical history section is
\begin{equation}
 \lambda^-_{0,p}
 =\{\dd u-D_\psi F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,p}(\psi)\}
      \circ D\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,F^{-,0}(\psi))^{-1},
 \qquad
 \lambda^-_{0,p}
   (D_u\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,F^{-,0}(\psi))1)=1.     \label{eq:anchor-central-physical-conormal}
\end{equation}
Coordinate and history covectors are therefore not identified without the
displayed pullback.  More precisely, at
\(\phi_0=\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi,F^{-,0}(\psi))\), differentiation of the
intrinsic membership identity gives the exact forward covariance law
\begin{equation}
 D\mathfrak m_{b,p}(\mathcal P_{0\to b,p}\phi_0)
   \circ D\mathcal P_{0\to b,p}(\phi_0)
 =\partial_u\mathcal M_{0,p}^{\rm int}
     (\psi,F^{-,0}(\psi))\,\lambda^-_{0,p}.           \label{eq:anchor-central-conormal-forward-pullback}
\end{equation}
This is an ordinary covector pullback by a forward map; it contains no
inverse history holonomy.

Write the incoming hit as
\[
 A^0=\mathfrak C_{0,p}(\psi_A,u_A)
\]
and pair it with the unique point of the stable sheet having the same
complete normal/history coordinate,
\begin{equation}
 B^0=\mathfrak C_{0,p}
   (\psi_A,F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(\psi_A)).     \label{eq:anchor-paired-stable-point}
\end{equation}
Let \(z^a(0)\) and \(z^r(0)\) be the exact planar core coordinates of
\(A^0\) and \(B^0\), respectively.  Both have \(X=0\).  Define
\begin{equation}
 G^{\rm anc}_{N,\delta}(\nu,\eta)
 :=c_{\mathscr H}\{\mathscr H_\alpha(z^a(0))
               -\mathscr H_\alpha(z^r(0))\}.       \label{eq:anchor-physical-gap}
\end{equation}
Let \(\widehat G(\psi,u;\nu,\eta)\) denote the same first-integral
difference with the stable-sheet point paired at fixed \(\psi\).  The chart
identities give, exactly,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G(\psi,u;\nu,\eta)
 =u-F^{-,0}_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(\psi),\qquad
 \partial_u\widehat G=1 .                           \label{eq:anchor-gap-coordinate-transversality}
\end{equation}
Hence this is the exact local membership coordinate.  Its orientation is
fixed by \cref{eq:anchor-central-chart-identities}; no unspecified nonzero
multiplier remains.
The tubular chart is an auxiliary proof device: another admissible local
normal-history chart may change \(G^{\rm anc}\) away from its zero fiber.
Because both are \(C^1\) defining functions with the same exact membership
fiber, they differ locally by a nowhere-zero \(C^1\) factor.  The physical
root and its conormal line are therefore chart independent; no equality of
the two off-zero gap functions is asserted.

\begin{proposition}[Exact zero fiber and anchored finite-gap bridge]
\label[proposition]{prop:anchor-gap-comparison}
The map \(G^{\rm anc}_{N,\delta}\) is a scalar \(C^1\) defining function,
and
\begin{equation}
 G^{\rm anc}_{N,\delta}(\nu,\eta)=0                \label{eq:anchor-gap-zero}
\end{equation}
if and only if the inward branch of \(W^u(E_N^+)\) belongs to
\(W^s(E_N^-)\) after the phase is fixed.  Equivalently, there is an anchored
RFDE orbit \(Z^{\rm het}\), unique in the declared tube and modulo time
translation, for which
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to-\infty}Z^{\rm het}_t=E_N^+,
 \qquad
 \lim_{t\to+\infty}Z^{\rm het}_t=E_N^-             \label{eq:anchor-heteroclinic-limits}
\end{equation}
in the corresponding history norms.

The scalar normalization in \cref{eq:anchor-physical-gap} is the same one
used for the selected gap.  The two-sided common-core comparison gives, for
any prescribed finite \(Q\),
\begin{equation}
 \bigl|J_p^1\{G^{\rm anc}_{N,\delta}
                 -\mathscr D_N^{\rm sel}\}\bigr|
 \le C\delta^Q,
 \qquad J_p^1\in\{1,\partial_\nu,D_\eta\}.          \label{eq:anchor-total-comparison-error}
\end{equation}
The exact selected gap is only a proof comparator; it does not define the
zero fiber on the left.  Choose \(Q>6\).  The already proved selected-gap
Melnikov estimates and \cref{eq:anchor-total-comparison-error} then give
\begin{align}
 \frac{G^{\rm anc}}\delta
 &=\sqrt{2\pi}(\nu-\nu_{0,N})+O(\delta),\notag\\
 \partial_\nu G^{\rm anc}
 &=\delta\sqrt{2\pi}+O(\delta^2),\notag\\
 \|D_\eta G^{\rm anc}
       +\delta^2\sqrt{2\pi}\Lambda_N\|
 &\le C\{\delta^3+\delta^2\|\eta\|\}.             \label{eq:anchor-physical-gap-estimates}
\end{align}
All constants are independent of \(N\); the flat term has been absorbed
after decreasing \(\delta_0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-model heteroclinic-canard root and intrinsic conormal]
\label[theorem]{thm:anchored-physical-root-conormal}
There are common \(c_0,\eta_0,\delta_0>0\) such that, for every admitted
network and \(0<\delta<\delta_0\), the anchored physical equation has a
unique root
\begin{equation}
 \mu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)
 =\delta^2\nu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,\eta),
 \qquad |\nu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}-\nu_{0,N}|<c_0,       \label{eq:anchor-physical-root}
\end{equation}
at which the complete-history heteroclinic
\cref{eq:anchor-heteroclinic-limits} exists.  The root is a property of the
fixed anchored RFDE and is independent of every preparation used to prove
it.  It is jointly \(C^1\) in the structural ball and satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|D_\eta\mu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)
       -\delta^3\Lambda_N\|
 &\le C\{\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|\},             \label{eq:anchor-physical-root-jets}\\
 |\mu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)
  -\mu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,0)
  -\delta^3\Lambda_N(\eta)|
 &\le C\{\delta^4\|\eta\|
               +\delta^3\|\eta\|^2\}.            \label{eq:anchor-physical-root-increment}
\end{align}
The derivative estimate holds in the full dual operator norm and is
uniform in \(N\).

For this statement, let \(\mathcal Q^+_{N,\delta,p}\) be the exact incoming
history curve obtained by recutting the inward branch of \(W^u(E_N^+)\), and
let \(\mathcal Q^-_{N,\delta,p}\) be the exact negative chart-center history
curve used in the half-line construction of
\cref{thm:anchor-annulus-flat-forgetting}; on a regular subannulus both are
parameterized by the physical coordinate \(r\).  These are specified RFDE
orbits, not unnamed slow manifolds.  The connecting orbit is a canard in the
following quantitative sense.  On each fixed attracting or repelling anchored
subannulus, after matching the common \(r\)-section, its compatible voltage
history is within \(C\delta^2\), in the same capped fading norm used in the
half-line and annulus constructions, of \(\mathcal Q^+\) or
\(\mathcal Q^-\), respectively.  It remains in each such subannulus for at least
\(c\delta^{-2}\) units of original time.  In the central fold window
\(|s|\le S_\delta\), its solved planar core satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|z^{\rm het}(s)-\gamma_0(s)\|
 \le C\delta e^{c|s|}\langle s\rangle^M,            \label{eq:anchor-heteroclinic-canard-tracking}
\end{equation}
and all reconstructed transverse histories obey the corresponding graph
bound.  Thus “canard” refers to a proved attracting-to-repelling
slow-history passage, not merely to the existence of an arbitrary
heteroclinic.

Define
\begin{equation}
 \Xi^{\rm anc}_{N,\delta}(\eta)
 =\delta^{-3}\{\mu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)
                   -\mu^{\rm anc}_{c,N}(\delta,0)\}.          \label{eq:anchor-weighted-physical-graph}
\end{equation}
Then the intrinsic weighted conormal satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|(1,-D_\eta\Xi^{\rm anc}_{N,\delta}(0))
       -(1,-\Lambda_N)\|\le C\delta.               \label{eq:anchor-physical-conormal-limit}
\end{equation}
It inherits the hidden-return factorization, the sharp two-delay right
inverse and dual reconstruction, the merged-single-delay leading no-go, and
the asynchronous nonvanishing examples already proved for \(\Lambda_N\).
Thus a redistribution that is exactly invisible under the stationary
projection moves an exact, globally defined complete-history heteroclinic
canard at order \(\delta^3\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Naturality, intermediate cuts, and anchor universality]
\label[proposition]{prop:anchor-physical-naturality-composition}
The conormal line in
\cref{eq:anchor-physical-conormal-limit} is unchanged by a phase shift, by
evaluating the same constructed orbit and future-asymptotic sheet on another
regular section reached by forward holonomy, and by multiplying
\(G^{\rm anc}\) by a nowhere-zero \(C^1\) scalar.  This assertion concerns
the common local zero locus in parameter space; it does not assert that an
RFDE holonomy is invertible on an open history set.  Under a fixed nonsingular
parameter change the line pulls back as a covector.  If the physical passage
is split by finitely many intermediate sections, the derivative of the
forward composite is the ordered product of the derivatives of its factors,
and the final parameter-space conormal is independent of the cuts.

For two fixed anchor multipliers satisfying
\cref{eq:anchor-multiplier}, the exact finite-\(\delta\) baselines may differ.
After each physical locus is centered at its own baseline, their weighted
first conormals are each within \(C\delta\) of
\((1,-\Lambda_N)\), uniformly over the bounded completion class.  Hence
two such conormals differ by at most \(2C\delta\) and have the same uniform
limit.  No pairwise flat comparison and no equality of the two exact roots
is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Proof map and logical dependence}

For readability, the concise estimates are collected in the supplement; the
dependence needed to audit the flagship theorem is short.  The literal
central identity and the two equilibria in \cref{prop:anchor-central-identity-equilibria}
follow directly from the support of \(g_{\rm anc}-1\) and from cancellation of
all current-minus-delay terms on constant histories.  The bordered
characteristic determinant is then counted in the anchored history-object
construction summarized by \cref{lem:lean-anchor-history-objects}.  Its
homotopy never reverses the RFDE semiflow and, together with the scaled
Lyapunov--Perron charts built there, proves
\cref{thm:anchor-indices-manifolds}.

The global history objects are constructed in
\cref{lem:lean-anchor-history-objects,lem:lean-anchor-generated-cut-sheets}.
On the positive side one evolves the
one-dimensional unstable branch only forward.  On the negative side the
mixed half-line inverse propagates the stable complement forward and inverts
only the scalar unstable cocycle.  Nonlinear contraction, event recutting,
and capped-action comparison give
\cref{thm:anchor-annulus-flat-forgetting}; no inverse of the ambient RFDE
semiflow is used.

Finally, the intrinsic sheet at the generated cut is compared with a finite
selected sheet only over the interval on which both use the identical
physical RFDE; this is \cref{lem:lean-anchor-generated-cut-sheets}.  The
action-balanced rectangle in \cref{lem:lean-anchor-action-intersection}
proves existence and uniqueness of the intrinsic sheet intersection before
any implicit differentiation.  The two-sided comparison in
\cref{lem:lean-anchor-gap-comparison} then transfers the already proved
selected Melnikov estimates to the intrinsic physical gap; it does not
transfer the selected zero fiber.  That fiber comes from stable-sheet
membership and forward uniqueness.  The strict \(\nu\)-slope yields the
unique root, and differentiating the comparison gives
\cref{eq:anchor-physical-root-jets}, and the tracking bounds already built
into the two half-lines give \cref{eq:anchor-heteroclinic-canard-tracking}.
Forward holonomy and the ordinary covector chain rule then prove
\cref{prop:anchor-physical-naturality-composition}.
