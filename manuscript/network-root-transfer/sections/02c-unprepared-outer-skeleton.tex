\section{Preparation-independent critical-layer geometry}
\label{sec:unprepared-outer-skeleton}

The finite-section noncanonicity result raises a sharper question: which
outer features are nevertheless fixed by the physical equation?  The
following result answers this at the critical-layer level and shows precisely
why it does not select a complete history.  The splitting is for the leading
frozen-resource fast-voltage matrix obtained by omitting the
\(O(\delta^2)\) current and delayed variational terms; the critical curve is
not a finite-\(\delta\) slow solution.

Put
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_N=D(P_N-\Id),\qquad
 \mathcal N_N(x)=c_N\circ x^{\circ2}
              +\frac\beta3x^{\circ3}.                 \label{eq:outer-fast-nonlinearity}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform critical curve and leading frozen-voltage splitting]
\label[proposition]{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton}
Assume the common Markov, curvature, and operator bounds in
\cref{eq:shared-markov,eq:shared-curvature,eq:shared-layer-bounds}.
There are \(r_0,C,C_P>0\), independent of \(N\) and of the delay layers,
with the following properties.

For \(|r|\le r_0\), there is a solution
\(z_{0,N}(r)\in E_N\) of
\begin{equation}
 A_Nz_{0,N}(r)
 =P_{\perp,N}\mathcal N_N
       \bigl(r\mathbf1+z_{0,N}(r)\bigr),
 \qquad \|z_{0,N}(r)\|_N\le Cr^2,                  \label{eq:outer-critical-transverse}
\end{equation}
and it is the unique solution in the displayed \(Cr^2\)-ball.
Define
\begin{align}
 x_{0,N}(r)&=r\mathbf1+z_{0,N}(r),\notag\\
 V_{0,N}(r)&=\mathbf1+x_{0,N}(r),\notag\\
 w_{0,N}(r)&=\frac23-\pi_N^T\mathcal N_N(x_{0,N}(r)).
                                                               \label{eq:outer-critical-curve}
\end{align}
Then the constant history with value \(V_{0,N}(r)\), at frozen resource
\(w_{0,N}(r)\), is an exact zero of the unprepared voltage field for every
positive \(\delta\), every admitted redistribution, and either recovery law.  In
particular, this critical curve uses no cutoff or completion.  Uniformly for
\(0\le j\le3\),
\begin{align}
 z_{0,N}(r)
 &=g_Nr^2+g_{3,N}r^3+R^z_N(r),
 &\|\partial_r^jR^z_N(r)\|_N&\le C|r|^{4-j},\notag\\
 w_{0,N}(r)
 &=\frac23-\alpha r^2-\kappa_Nr^3+R^w_N(r),
 &|\partial_r^jR^w_N(r)|&\le C|r|^{4-j},             \label{eq:outer-critical-expansion}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
 g_{3,N}=2A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}(c_N\circ g_N).         \label{eq:outer-g3}
\end{equation}
The two curve components are \(C^\infty\) in \(r\).  More precisely, for
every fixed finite \(s\),
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\le j\le s}\sup_{|r|\le r_0}
 \left\{\|\partial_r^jz_{0,N}(r)\|_N
             +|\partial_r^jw_{0,N}(r)|\right\}
 \le C_s,                                           \label{eq:outer-critical-high-regularity}
\end{equation}
where \(C_s\) is independent of \(N\).

The leading frozen-resource fast-voltage matrix along this curve is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J^{\rm fv}_{N}(r)=\mathcal A_N-\diag\!\left(
       2c_N\circ x_{0,N}(r)+\beta x_{0,N}(r)^{\circ2}
                                  \right).            \label{eq:outer-layer-jacobian}
\end{equation}
It has a real simple eigenvalue \(\lambda_{c,N}(r)\), continued from the
collective zero eigenvalue at the fold, and corresponding right eigenvector
and left eigenfunctional \(e_{c,N}(r)\), \(\ell_{c,N}(r)\), normalized by
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{c,N}(r)\mathbf1=1,
 \qquad \ell_{c,N}(r)e_{c,N}(r)=1.                  \label{eq:outer-eigen-normalization}
\end{equation}
Writing \(O_{\rm unif}\) for a remainder whose bound is independent of
\(N\), they obey, in the vector and dual norms,
\begin{align}
 e_{c,N}(r)&=\mathbf1+2rg_N+O_{\rm unif}(r^2),\notag\\
 \ell_{c,N}(r)&=\pi_N^T
   +2r\,\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}+O_{\rm unif}(r^2),\notag\\
 \lambda_{c,N}(r)&=-2\alpha r-3\kappa_Nr^2+O_{\rm unif}(r^3),
                                                               \label{eq:outer-eigen-expansion}
\end{align}
where all remainder constants are independent of \(N\).  If
\(P^s_N(r)=\Id-e_{c,N}(r)\ell_{c,N}(r)\), then
\begin{equation}
 \|e^{\mathcal J^{\rm fv}_{N}(r)t}P^s_N(r)\|
 \le C_Pe^{-D\gamma t/2},\qquad t\ge0.              \label{eq:outer-stable-layer-semigroup}
\end{equation}
Consequently the leading frozen-resource voltage layer is attracting for
\(0<r\le r_0\), whereas for \(-r_0\le r<0\) it has exactly one unstable
direction.  This statement excludes both the resource zero direction and all
positive-\(\delta\) current and delayed variational terms.

There is also an exact sign identity.  With
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_N(r)=\ell_{c,N}(r)V_{0,N}'(r),               \label{eq:outer-theta}
\end{equation}
one has \(\Theta_N(r)=1+O_{\rm unif}(r^2)\); after decreasing \(r_0\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac12\le\Theta_N(r)\le\frac32,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{c,N}(r)=\frac{w_{0,N}'(r)}{\Theta_N(r)}.  \label{eq:outer-eigen-identity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Truncated action for the reduced critical-layer equation]
\label[corollary]{cor:outer-reduced-actions}
Retain \cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton}.
For the shared-resource and sensed-recovery laws, respectively, set
\begin{align}
 \sigma_N^{\rm sh}(r)&=r,\notag\\
 \sigma_N^{\rm rec}(r)&=\pi_N^T\!\left[
   \varpi_N\circ x_{0,N}(r)
   +d_N^{\rm rec}\circ x_{0,N}(r)^{\circ2}\right]. \label{eq:outer-recovery-source}
\end{align}
For the second formula, assume additionally \cref{eq:sensing-bounds}.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_N^{\rm rec}(r)=r+\chi_N^{\rm rec}r^2+O_{\rm unif}(r^3).
                                                               \label{eq:outer-recovery-expansion}
\end{equation}
Fix \(p>4\) and \(\nu_*>0\), restrict to \(|\nu|\le\nu_*\), and let
\begin{equation}
 S_\delta=\sqrt{2p\log(1/\delta)},\qquad
 \rho_\delta=\frac{\delta S_\delta}{2\alpha},       \label{eq:outer-matching-radius}
\end{equation}
and use slow time \(T=\delta^2t\).  There are fixed
\(0<r_{\rm out}<r_0\) and \(\delta_0>0\), depending only on
\(p,\nu_*\) and the common bounds, such that, for either
\(\diamond\in\{\mathrm{sh},\mathrm{rec}\}\) and every
\(0<\delta<\delta_0\),
\begin{equation}
 q^\diamond_{N,\delta,\nu}(r)
 :=\frac{\sigma_N^\diamond(r)-\delta^2\nu}{w_{0,N}'(r)}
 =-\frac1{2\alpha}
   +O_{\rm unif}\!\left(|r|+\frac{\delta^2}{|r|}\right)       \label{eq:outer-reduced-speed}
\end{equation}
on \(\rho_\delta\le|r|\le r_{\rm out}\), and
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{3}{2\alpha}\le q^\diamond_{N,\delta,\nu}(r)
 \le-\frac1{6\alpha}.                               \label{eq:outer-speed-sign}
\end{equation}
The two truncated reduced actions
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A^a_{N,\diamond}
 &=\int_{\rho_\delta}^{r_{\rm out}}
      \frac{\lambda_{c,N}(r)}
           {q^\diamond_{N,\delta,\nu}(r)}\,\dd r,\notag\\
 \mathcal A^r_{N,\diamond}
 &=-\int_{-r_{\rm out}}^{-\rho_\delta}
      \frac{\lambda_{c,N}(r)}
           {q^\diamond_{N,\delta,\nu}(r)}\,\dd r       \label{eq:outer-normal-actions}
\end{align}
are positive for \(0<\delta<\delta_0\).  These are truncated actions of the
formal reduced critical-curve equation, not actions of finite-\(\delta\)
invariant RFDE histories.  If \(\rho_\delta\le r_{\rm out}/2\), then, uniformly in
\(N\), \(\nu\), and the two recovery laws,
\begin{equation}
 \min\{\mathcal A^a_{N,\diamond},
        \mathcal A^r_{N,\diamond}\}
 \ge \frac{\alpha^2r_{\rm out}^2}{6}=:\mathcal A_*>0,
                                                               \label{eq:outer-action-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A^{a/r}_{N,\diamond}
 =2\alpha^2(r_{\rm out}^2-\rho_\delta^2)
   +O_{\rm unif}(r_{\rm out}^3+\delta^2r_{\rm out}). \label{eq:outer-action-expansion}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The preceding matrix splitting does have a positive-\(\delta\),
voltage-history-space consequence, provided the resource coordinate is frozen.
This is the first ambient RFDE estimate needed by an outer-history
construction.  It is convenient to use fold time \(s=\delta t\), for which
the history length is fixed rather than \(O(\delta^{-1})\).

For
\(\eta=(E_0,\ldots,E_m)\in\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\), write
\(L_{k,N}(\eta)=B_{k,N}+E_k\), and put
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr X_N=C([-\theta_m,0];\R^N),\qquad
 \mathfrak a_N(r)=
 \left\{\frac1{|\lambda_{c,N}(r)|}
             +\frac2{D\gamma}\right\}^{-1}.         \label{eq:frozen-history-a}
\end{equation}
Fix \(0<\vartheta<1/4\).  For \(0<\delta<e^{-1}\) and \(r\ne0\), define
the smooth capped rate and its associated fading-history norm by
\begin{align}
 \kappa_{N,\delta}(r)
 &=\vartheta\left\{
       \frac{\delta}{\mathfrak a_N(r)}
       +\frac{\theta_m}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\}^{-1},
                                                        \label{eq:frozen-history-rate}\\
 \|\phi\|_{N,r,\delta}^{\sharp}
 &=\sup_{-\theta_m\le\theta\le0}
       e^{2\kappa_{N,\delta}(r)\theta}\|\phi(\theta)\|_N.
                                                        \label{eq:frozen-history-norm}
\end{align}
The harmless case \(\theta_m=0\) is interpreted by deleting the second
term in braces and reducing the history equation to an ODE.

\begin{proposition}[Frozen-resource voltage-history splitting]
\label[proposition]{prop:frozen-voltage-history-splitting}
Retain \cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton,cor:outer-reduced-actions} and
the structural-ball layers \cref{eq:structural-ball-layers}.  After
decreasing \(r_{\rm out},\delta_0,\eta_0>0\), independently of \(N\), the
following holds whenever
\[
 0<\delta<\delta_0,\qquad
 \rho_\delta\le |r|\le r_{\rm out},\qquad
 \|\eta\|_{\mathfrak T_N}<\eta_0.
\]
On \(\mathscr X_N\), consider the frozen-resource voltage RFDE
\begin{equation}
 u'(s)=\delta^{-1}\mathcal J_N^{\rm fv}(r)u(s)
 +\delta K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
       \{u(s)-u(s-\theta_k)\}.                      \label{eq:frozen-voltage-rfde}
\end{equation}
Let \(\mathcal T_{N,\delta,r,\eta}(s)\) denote its solution semigroup.

If \(r>0\), the whole history space is exponentially stable and
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal T_{N,\delta,r,\eta}(s)\phi\|_{N,r,\delta}^{\sharp}
 \le C e^{-\kappa_{N,\delta}(r)s}
       \|\phi\|_{N,r,\delta}^{\sharp},\qquad s\ge0.  \label{eq:frozen-attracting-history-estimate}
\end{equation}
If \(r<0\), there is an invariant splitting
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr X_N=\mathscr W^s_{N,\delta,r,\eta}
                 \oplus\mathscr W^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta},
 \qquad \operatorname{codim}\mathscr W^s=1,
 \qquad \dim\mathscr W^u=1,                         \label{eq:frozen-repelling-history-splitting}
\end{equation}
whose projections have norm at most \(C\) in
\cref{eq:frozen-history-norm}.  On the stable space,
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal T(s)\phi^s\|_{N,r,\delta}^{\sharp}
 \le C e^{-\kappa_{N,\delta}(r)s}
       \|\phi^s\|_{N,r,\delta}^{\sharp},\qquad s\ge0.          \label{eq:frozen-repelling-stable-estimate}
\end{equation}
The unstable space is spanned by
\begin{equation}
 h^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta}(\theta)
 =e^{\lambda^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta}\theta}
       e^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta},\qquad -\theta_m\le\theta\le0, \label{eq:frozen-unstable-history}
\end{equation}
where \(\ell_{c,N}(r)e^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta}=1\) and
\begin{equation}
 \left|\lambda^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta}
       -\delta^{-1}\lambda_{c,N}(r)\right|\le C\delta,
 \qquad
 \|e^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta}-e_{c,N}(r)\|_N\le C\delta^2,       \label{eq:frozen-unstable-root-estimate}
\end{equation}
and the inverse restricted to this line satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal T(-s)|_{\mathscr W^u}\|
 \le C e^{-\lambda^u_{N,\delta,r,\eta}s},\qquad s\ge0.     \label{eq:frozen-unstable-inverse-estimate}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal T(-s)|_{\mathscr W^u}\) is only the inverse on the
one-dimensional invariant line; no backward RFDE semiflow is asserted.

The bundles, projections, unstable root, and normalized unstable history are
twice Fr\'{e}chet differentiable in \(\eta\), in fixed graph charts, with
common operator-norm bounds through order two.  The proof is controlled by
the dimension-free quantity
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{N,\delta}(r)
 :=C\frac{\delta^2
       \{1+e^{2\kappa_{N,\delta}(r)\theta_m}\}}
          {\mathfrak a_N(r)}
 \le C\frac{\delta^{1-2\vartheta}}{S_\delta}=o(1).  \label{eq:frozen-history-smallness}
\end{equation}
In particular, the attracting equation has no characteristic root with
real part greater than \(-\kappa_{N,\delta}(r)\); on the repelling branch the
only such root is the real simple root in
\cref{eq:frozen-unstable-root-estimate}.
\end{proposition}

The fading factor \(2\kappa\) in the phase norm and the smaller decay rate
\(\kappa\) in the conclusion are intentional.  The margin controls the
translation of prescribed old history before one full delay has elapsed;
using the same exponent in both places would leave no perturbative gap.
For clarity, this is a fading-norm estimate: its comparison with the ordinary
supremum norm is
\begin{equation}
 \|\phi\|^\sharp_{N,r,\delta}\le\|\phi\|_\infty
 \le e^{2\kappa_{N,\delta}(r)\theta_m}
       \|\phi\|^\sharp_{N,r,\delta}
 \le\delta^{-2\vartheta}\|\phi\|^\sharp_{N,r,\delta}.         \label{eq:frozen-history-norm-equivalence}
\end{equation}
The cap is also necessary at the level of this model class: long-delay weak
modes need not retain a \(\delta\)-independent fast-time decay rate.

\begin{remark}[Why the resource direction must be phase-quotiented]
\label[remark]{rem:frozen-resource-phase-quotient}
\Cref{prop:frozen-voltage-history-splitting} cannot be upgraded by simply
adjoining the frozen resource equation and retaining the phrase ``one
unstable direction.''  In the admissible homogeneous, delay-free collective
subclass \(c_N=\alpha\mathbf1\), the collective voltage--resource block in
the original time is
\[
 \dot u=\lambda_{c,N}(r)u-\omega,
 \qquad \dot\omega=\delta^2u,
\]
and hence has characteristic polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \lambda^2-\lambda_{c,N}(r)\lambda+\delta^2.         \label{eq:full-frozen-resource-polynomial}
\end{equation}
For \(r<0\) with \(|r|\ge\rho_\delta\), both roots are real and positive
for all sufficiently small \(\delta\).  Thus the unquotiented full frozen
system has two unstable collective--resource roots on the repelling branch.
The additional slow root signals a degree of freedom that can be related to
the longitudinal bundle only after a genuine slow history has been
constructed.  A physical outer theorem must then prove either a
stable--longitudinal--strong-unstable trichotomy or, equivalently, a
stable/one-strong-unstable dichotomy for the correctly gauged quotient
process.  The raw full frozen system has neither of those already-identified
structures.
\end{remark}

The true outer-history problem can be written without a completion or
cutoff.  The next proposition records the exact equation, including the
state-dependent backtrack generated by the physical slow speed.
On the fixed fold-time phase space put
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr Y_N=C([-\theta_m,0];\R^N)\times\R,
 \qquad
 \varrho_N(\phi,\omega)=\pi_N^T\phi(0).             \label{eq:outer-stationary-gauge-row}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact uncut outer-history equation]
\label[proposition]{prop:exact-outer-history-equation}
Fix \(N,\delta,\nu\), restrict to the shared-resource law, take
\(\eta\in\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\), use
\(L_{k,N}(\eta)=B_{k,N}+E_k\), and let \(J\subset\R\) be an interval.
Suppose \(q\in C^1(J)\) is nonzero and its scalar flow
\(\Phi_q^T\) is defined for the required backward times.  Put
\begin{equation}
 r_k(r)=\Phi_q^{-\delta\theta_k}(r),                 \label{eq:exact-outer-backtrack}
\end{equation}
and assume \(r_k(r)\in J\) for every retained \(r\) and \(k\).  Let
\[
 V(r)=\mathbf1+r\mathbf1+z(r),\qquad
 z(r)\in E_N,\qquad w(r)\in\R,
\]
with \(z,w\in C^1(J)\).  Then the following are equivalent:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em]
\item for every retained \(r_0\), these functions generate a physical
solution of the unmodified structural-ball RFDE by
\begin{equation}
 r(T)=\Phi_q^T(r_0),\qquad
 \bar v(t)=V(r(\delta^2t)),\qquad
 \bar w(t)=w(r(\delta^2t))                          \label{eq:exact-outer-generated-solution}
\end{equation}
on every orbit segment for which the orbit and all delayed backtracks remain
in \(J\);
\item the identities
\begin{align}
 \delta^2q(r)\{\mathbf1+z'(r)\}
 ={}&\{2/3-w(r)\}\mathbf1
 -\mathcal N_N(r\mathbf1+z(r))+A_Nz(r)\notag\\
 &+\delta^2K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
 \bigl[\{r-r_k(r)\}\mathbf1+z(r)-z(r_k(r))\bigr],
                                                        \label{eq:exact-outer-voltage-invariance}\\
 q(r)w'(r)={}&r-\delta^2\nu.                         \label{eq:exact-outer-resource-invariance}
\end{align}
hold at every retained \(r\).
\end{enumerate}
Every \(C^1\) solution of these identities is automatically \(C^2\) on the
interior of the retained set.

Equivalently, the full fold-time history over the curve is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{V,w,q}(r)
 =\left(
   \theta\longmapsto V(\Phi_q^{\delta\theta}(r)),
   \;w(r)\right)\in\mathscr Y_N.                    \label{eq:exact-outer-history-embedding}
\end{equation}
It satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \varrho_N\partial_r\mathcal H_{V,w,q}(r)=1.        \label{eq:exact-outer-history-phase}
\end{equation}
Along a generated orbit define \(r_*(s)=r(\delta s)\).  Then
\(r_*'(s)=\delta q(r_*(s))\), and the normalized
time-tangent history in
\cref{eq:physical-normalized-tangent} is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \tau_*(s)=
 \partial_r\mathcal H_{V,w,q}(r_*(s)).              \label{eq:exact-outer-history-tangent}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The state-dependent backtrack in
\cref{eq:exact-outer-backtrack} has to be differentiated in the tracker
problem.  The next estimate makes explicit the only relative loss: the delay
shift divided by the distance from the fold.

For an interval \(J\) contained in one component of \(\R\setminus\{0\}\),
put
\begin{equation}
 \|f\|_{k,\rho;J}
 =\max_{0\le j\le k}\sup_{r\in J}
       |r|^j\|\partial_r^jf(r)\|.                  \label{eq:backtrack-weighted-norm}
\end{equation}
The definition applies to scalar-, vector-, and operator-valued functions.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted calculus for the physical backtrack]
\label[proposition]{prop:physical-backtrack-calculus}
Fix an integer \(k\ge2\), \(0<q_-<q_+\), and \(M>0\).  Let
\(I\Subset J\) be compact intervals in one component of
\(\R\setminus\{0\}\) such that
\begin{equation}
 |r|\ge\rho\quad(r\in I),
 \qquad |r|\ge\rho/2\quad(r\in J).                \label{eq:backtrack-collar-geometry}
\end{equation}
Suppose \(q\in C^{k+2}(J)\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 q_-\le-q(r)\le q_+,
 \qquad \|q\|_{k+2,\rho;J}\le M,                 \label{eq:backtrack-q-class}
\end{equation}
Suppose moreover that one fixed interval \(J_0\Subset J\) contains the whole
backward flow tube
\begin{equation}
 \left\{\Phi_q^{-\tau}(r):
       r\in\overline I,\ 0\le\tau\le\delta\theta_m\right\}
 \subset J_0.                                      \label{eq:backtrack-flow-tube}
\end{equation}
Put \(r_j(r)=\Phi_q^{-\delta\theta_j}(r)\).  The collars and \(\rho\) may
vary with \(\delta\).  There are \(\epsilon_0,C>0\), depending only on
\(k,\theta_m,q_-,q_+\), and \(M\), but not on \(N,\delta,\rho\), or the
locations and lengths of the displayed intervals, such that, whenever
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_\rho:=\frac{\delta\theta_m}{\rho}
 \le\epsilon_0,                                    \label{eq:backtrack-relative-shift}
\end{equation}
the following estimates hold for every \(j\):
\begin{align}
 |r_j(r)-r|&\le q_+\delta\theta_j,
 &r_j'(r)&=\frac{q(r_j(r))}{q(r)},                  \label{eq:backtrack-basic-identities}\\
 \|r_j-\Id\|_{k,\rho;I}&\le C\delta\theta_j.    \label{eq:backtrack-map-bound}
\end{align}
On the open subset of \(C^{k+2}(J)\), endowed with
\cref{eq:backtrack-weighted-norm}, whose swept flow tube remains in
\(\operatorname{int}J\), the map
\[
 q\longmapsto r_j-\Id:
 C^{k+2}(J)\longrightarrow C^k(I)
\]
is three times Fr\'{e}chet differentiable.  For
\(h,h_1,h_2,h_3\in C^{k+2}(J)\),
\begin{align}
 D_qr_j[h]
 &=q(r_j)\int_r^{r_j}\frac{h(u)}{q(u)^2}\,\dd u,  \label{eq:backtrack-first-variation}\\
 \|D_qr_j[h]\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\delta\theta_j\|h\|_{k+2,\rho;J},\notag\\
 \|D_q^2r_j[h_1,h_2]\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\delta\theta_j
       \|h_1\|_{k+2,\rho;J}\|h_2\|_{k+2,\rho;J},\notag\\
 \|D_q^3r_j[h_1,h_2,h_3]\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\delta\theta_j
       \prod_{a=1}^3\|h_a\|_{k+2,\rho;J}.
                                                               \label{eq:backtrack-frechet-bounds}
\end{align}
If \(f\in C^{k+3}(J;X)\), where \(X\) is any Banach space, then
\begin{align}
 \|f\circ r_j\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\|f\|_{k,\rho;J},                           \label{eq:backtrack-composition-bound}\\
 \|f\circ r_j-f\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\epsilon_\rho\|f\|_{k+1,\rho;J},          \label{eq:backtrack-composition-difference}\\
 \|D_q(f\circ r_j)[h]\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\epsilon_\rho\|f\|_{k+1,\rho;J}
                    \|h\|_{k+2,\rho;J},\notag\\
 \|D_q^2(f\circ r_j)[h_1,h_2]\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\epsilon_\rho\|f\|_{k+2,\rho;J}
       \|h_1\|_{k+2,\rho;J}\|h_2\|_{k+2,\rho;J},\notag\\
 \|D_q^3(f\circ r_j)[h_1,h_2,h_3]\|_{k,\rho;I}
 &\le C\epsilon_\rho\|f\|_{k+3,\rho;J}
       \prod_{a=1}^3\|h_a\|_{k+2,\rho;J}.
                                                               \label{eq:backtrack-composition-frechet}
\end{align}
More precisely, on any product neighborhood with one common flow tube, put
\[
 \mathcal C_j(f,q)=f\circ r_j(q),\qquad
 \Delta_j(f,q)=f\circ r_j(q)-f|_I.
\]
Both maps from
\(C^{k+3}(J;X)\times C^{k+2}(J)\) to \(C^k(I;X)\) are three times
Fr\'{e}chet differentiable.  If \(1\le b\le3\) and
\((g_a,h_a)\) are product-space directions, then
\begin{align}
 &\|D^b\Delta_j(f,q)
       [(g_1,h_1),\ldots,(g_b,h_b)]\|_{k,\rho;I}\notag\\
 &\quad\le C\epsilon_\rho\left\{
      \|f\|_{k+3,\rho;J}\prod_{a=1}^b\|h_a\|_{k+2,\rho;J}
      +\sum_{\ell=1}^b\|g_\ell\|_{k+3,\rho;J}
          \prod_{a\ne\ell}\|h_a\|_{k+2,\rho;J}\right\}.
                                                               \label{eq:backtrack-joint-difference}
\end{align}
The same estimate without the factor \(\epsilon_\rho\) holds for
\(D^b\mathcal C_j\).  Thus a parameter-dependent pair \((f,q)\), with a
common flow tube and common \(C^1_\nu C^2_\eta\) bounds in these two Banach
spaces, produces delayed compositions and delayed differences with the same
rectangular parameter regularity, including the mixed
\(\partial_\nu\partial_{\eta\eta}\) derivative.  The same conclusion holds
for every rectangular jet of total order at most three, in particular also
for \(C^2_\nu C^1_\eta\) and the mixed
\(\partial_{\nu\nu}\partial_\eta\) derivative.  A raw backtrack variation has
the absolute factor \(C\delta\theta_j\) in
\cref{eq:backtrack-frechet-bounds}; once it enters a weighted delayed
composition, its relative factor is \(C\epsilon_\rho\).  At
\(\rho=\rho_\delta\), this factor is
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{\rho_\delta}\le\frac{C}{S_\delta}.       \label{eq:backtrack-logarithmic-loss}
\end{equation}
All constants in these conclusions are uniform in \(N,\delta\), and the
admissible collar family just specified.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is an exact reduction, not an existence assertion.
In particular, imposing boundary data on
\cref{eq:exact-outer-voltage-invariance,eq:exact-outer-resource-invariance}
can still select different representatives.  The missing theorem must
construct a nonempty physical representative class and control that
dependence in full-history parameter norms.

The remaining principal tracker linearization has an apparent derivative
coupling from the transverse curve into the slow speed.  The next result
removes that singularity exactly and proves the endpoint Green splitting
that is available before compatible complete collars have been constructed.
It is restricted to the shared-resource law.  Put
\begin{align}
 B_N(r)
 &=A_N-P_{\perp,N}D\mathcal N_N(x_{0,N}(r))|_{E_N},\notag\\
 \ell_N(r)
 &=\pi_N^TD\mathcal N_N(x_{0,N}(r))|_{E_N}.        \label{eq:principal-tracker-coefficients}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint Green splitting for the principal tracker]
\label[proposition]{prop:principal-tracker-endpoint-green}
Retain \cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton,cor:outer-reduced-actions}, use
the shared-resource speed \(q_0=q^{\rm sh}_{N,\delta,\nu}\), fix an integer
regularity order \(s\ge1\), and put
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon=\delta^2,\qquad
 I_\delta^a=[\rho_\delta,r_{\rm out}],\qquad
 I_\delta^r=[-r_{\rm out},-\rho_\delta].           \label{eq:principal-green-intervals}
\end{equation}
After decreasing \(r_{\rm out}\) and then \(\delta_0\), independently of
\(N\), the following holds for \(|\nu|\le\nu_*\) and
\(0<\delta<\delta_0\).  On either interval,
\begin{align}
 -q_+\le q_0(r)\le-q_-<0,\qquad
 &c_w|r|\le|w_{0,N}'(r)|\le C_w|r|,\qquad
 rw_{0,N}'(r)<0,                                   \label{eq:principal-green-signs}\\
 \max_{0\le j\le s+1}|r|^j
 \bigl\{\|\partial_r^j(B_N-A_N)\|
 &+\|\partial_r^jz_{0,N}'\|_N
  +\|\partial_r^j\ell_N\|
  +|\partial_r^jw_{0,N}'|\bigr\}
 \le C_s|r|,                                      \label{eq:principal-green-coefficients}\\
 \max_{0\le j\le s+1}|r|^j
 |\partial_r^jq_0(r)|&\le C_s.                    \label{eq:principal-green-q-bounds}
\end{align}
All displayed constants are dimension uniform.

For
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{N,\delta}\binom ZQ
 =\binom{
   \varepsilon q_0Z'-B_NZ+\varepsilon z_{0,N}'Q}
  {-\varepsilon q_0Q'+w_{0,N}'Q-q_0(\ell_NZ)'}
 =\binom fg,                                       \label{eq:principal-tracker-operator}
\end{equation}
set
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak p=\varepsilon Q+\ell_NZ.                \label{eq:principal-green-p-coordinate}
\end{equation}
For \(I\in\{I_\delta^a,I_\delta^r\}\), define
\begin{align}
 \|Z\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^s(I)}
 &=\varepsilon^{-1}\max_{0\le j\le s}\sup_{r\in I}
       |r|^j\|\partial_r^jZ(r)\|_N,\notag\\
 \|\mathfrak p\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^s(I)}
 &=\varepsilon^{-1}\max_{0\le j\le s}\sup_{r\in I}
       |r|^{j-1}|\partial_r^j\mathfrak p(r)|,\notag\\
 \|Q\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^j(I)}
 &=\varepsilon^{-1}\max_{0\le i\le j}\sup_{r\in I}
       |r|^{i+1}|\partial_r^iQ(r)|,\notag\\
 \|(f,g)\|_{\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^s(I)}
 &=\varepsilon^{-1}\max_{0\le j\le s}\sup_{r\in I}|r|^j
       \bigl\{\|\partial_r^jf(r)\|_N
                    +|\partial_r^jg(r)|\bigr\}.    \label{eq:principal-green-spaces}
\end{align}
The factor \(|r|^{-1}\) at order zero in the
\(\mathsf P_\varepsilon^s\)-norm is intentional.

There are linear slow Green operators
\(\mathcal G^{a/r}_{N,\delta}\) such that, writing
\((Z_{\rm sl},Q_{\rm sl})=\mathcal G^{a/r}_{N,\delta}(f,g)\) and
\(\mathfrak p_{\rm sl}=\varepsilon Q_{\rm sl}+\ell_NZ_{\rm sl}\),
\begin{equation}
 \|Z_{\rm sl}\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^s}
 +\|\mathfrak p_{\rm sl}\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^s}
 +\|Q_{\rm sl}\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^{s-1}}
 \le C_s\|(f,g)\|_{\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^s}.       \label{eq:principal-green-slow-bound}
\end{equation}
If \((f,g)\in\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^{s+1}\) and the coefficient bounds are
retained through order \(s+2\), then
\begin{equation}
 \|Q_{\rm sl}\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^s}
 \le C_s\|(f,g)\|_{\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^{s+1}}.  \label{eq:principal-green-extra-q-derivative}
\end{equation}

The endpoint trace maps are
\begin{align}
 \Gamma_a(Z,Q)
 &=\bigl(Z(r_{\rm out}),
   \varepsilon Q(r_{\rm out})
       +\ell_N(r_{\rm out})Z(r_{\rm out})\bigr),\notag\\
 \Gamma_r(Z,Q)
 &=\bigl(Z(-\rho_\delta),
   \varepsilon Q(-r_{\rm out})
       +\ell_N(-r_{\rm out})Z(-r_{\rm out})\bigr).
                                                               \label{eq:principal-green-traces}
\end{align}
There are homogeneous boundary lifts
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_a,\mathcal B_r:E_N\times\R
          \longrightarrow\Ker\mathcal L_{N,\delta},
 \qquad \Gamma_a\mathcal B_a=\Gamma_r\mathcal B_r=\Id.       \label{eq:principal-green-lifts}
\end{equation}
Consequently the unique solution with endpoint datum \(b\) is
\begin{equation}
 \binom ZQ
 =\mathcal G^{a/r}_{N,\delta}(f,g)
  +\mathcal B_{a/r}\left[
      b-\Gamma_{a/r}\mathcal G^{a/r}_{N,\delta}(f,g)\right]. \label{eq:principal-green-augmented-inverse}
\end{equation}

The boundary lifts are estimated in \((Z,\mathfrak p)\), not in the top
slow \(Q\)-norm.  Let \(r_a(T)\) solve
\(\dot r=q_0(r)\), \(r_a(0)=r_{\rm out}\), and let \(r_r(T)\) solve
\(\dot r=q_0(r)\), \(r_r(0)=-\rho_\delta\), reaching
\(-r_{\rm out}\) at \(T=T_r\).  Put
\begin{align}
 \Phi_a(T)&=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_0^T
        |w_{0,N}'(r_a(\sigma))|\,\dd\sigma,\notag\\
 \Phi_s(T)&=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_0^T
        w_{0,N}'(r_r(\sigma))\,\dd\sigma,\notag\\
 \Phi_u(T)&=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_T^{T_r}
        w_{0,N}'(r_r(\sigma))\,\dd\sigma.          \label{eq:principal-green-actions}
\end{align}
If \((Z_b,\mathfrak p_b)\) is a boundary lift, then, for
\(0\le j\le s\),
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon^j\bigl\{
  \|\partial_T^jZ_b(T)\|_N
       +|\partial_T^j\mathfrak p_b(T)|\bigr\}
 &\le C_se^{-c\Phi_a(T)}(\|b_z\|_N+|b_p|)          \label{eq:principal-green-attracting-layer}
\end{align}
on the attracting branch.  On the repelling branch write the trace datum
as \(b=(b_s,b_u)\in E_N\times\R\); then
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon^j\bigl\{
  \|\partial_T^jZ_b(T)\|_N
       +|\partial_T^j\mathfrak p_b(T)|\bigr\}
 \le C_s\bigl\{
 e^{-c\Phi_s(T)}\|b_s\|_N
 +e^{-c\Phi_u(T)}|b_u|\bigr\}.                     \label{eq:principal-green-repelling-layer}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
 \Phi_a(T)&\asymp
     \frac{r_{\rm out}^2-r_a(T)^2}{\varepsilon},&
 \Phi_s(T)&\asymp
     \frac{r_r(T)^2-\rho_\delta^2}{\varepsilon},&
 \Phi_u(T)&\asymp
     \frac{r_{\rm out}^2-r_r(T)^2}{\varepsilon},  \label{eq:principal-green-action-comparison}
\end{align}
with dimension-uniform comparison constants.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[No uniform gain of a full slow derivative]
\label[remark]{rem:principal-green-no-derivative-gain}
The endpoint theorem deliberately does not assert a uniform map
\(\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^{s-1}\to
\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^s\times\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^s\).
That assertion is false even for constant coefficients.  On a fixed interior
interval consider
\[
 -\varepsilon Z'+Z=f_\varepsilon,\qquad
 h_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2,\qquad
 f_\varepsilon(r)=\varepsilon h_\varepsilon^{s-1}
                         e^{\mathrm i r/h_\varepsilon}.
\]
The \(\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^{s-1}\)-norm of the forcing is bounded, but the
bounded particular solution has
\(|\partial_r^sZ|\asymp1\), so its slow order-\(s\) norm is
\(\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}\).

The one-order loss in \(Q\) is also genuine.  Freeze
\(B=-1\), \(q_0=-1\), \(z_0'=0\), \(\ell=r_*>0\), and
\(w_0'=-r_*\), and take
\(f_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{s+1}e^{\mathrm i r/\varepsilon}\),
\(g=0\).  The particular solution has
\(|Z|\asymp\varepsilon^{s+1}\) and
\(|Q|\asymp r_*\varepsilon^s\).  Its slow \(Q\)-norm is bounded through
order \(s-1\), and its input \(\mathsf Y_\varepsilon^s\)-norm is bounded,
whereas the order-\(s\) \(Q\)-norm grows like
\(c\varepsilon^{-1}\).  Taking real or imaginary parts gives the same
examples in the real Banach spaces.  They use the exact traces of the
particular solutions and therefore are not endpoint boundary layers.
Thus the coefficient hypotheses in the proposition alone cannot yield a
same-order estimate for \(Q\).
\end{remark}

The principal cancellation coordinate has an exact nonlinear counterpart.
It is the resource defect itself, rather than a formal change of variables.
For a nonvanishing speed \(q\), define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal D_N(r,z,q,\eta)
 =K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
 \bigl[\{r-r_k^q(r)\}\mathbf1
       +z(r)-z(r_k^q(r))\bigr],                   \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-delay}\\
 \mathcal D_{c,N}&=\pi_N^T\mathcal D_N,
 &\mathcal D_{\perp,N}&=P_{\perp,N}\mathcal D_N,\notag
\end{align}
where \(r_k^q=\Phi_q^{-\delta\theta_k}\).  Put
\begin{equation}
 F_N(r,z)=\pi_N^T\mathcal N_N(r\mathbf1+z).        \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-F}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact nonlinear resource-defect normal form]
\label[proposition]{prop:nonlinear-resource-defect-normal-form}
Retain the shared-resource equation and the intervals of
\cref{prop:principal-tracker-endpoint-green}.  Let
\(z=z_{0,N}+Z\), \(q=q_0+Q\), and suppose all backtracks remain in one
common collar.  Set
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_N^{(2)}(r,Z)
 &={\mathcal N}_N(x_{0,N}+Z)-{\mathcal N}_N(x_{0,N})
      -D{\mathcal N}_N(x_{0,N})Z,\notag\\
 h_{c,N}&=\pi_N^T\mathcal R_N^{(2)},\qquad
 h_{\perp,N}=P_{\perp,N}\mathcal R_N^{(2)},\notag\\
 \mathcal D_{0,N}&=\mathcal D_N(r,z_{0,N},q_0,\eta),\notag\\
 \Delta\mathcal D_N
 &=\mathcal D_N(r,z_{0,N}+Z,q_0+Q,\eta)-\mathcal D_{0,N},\notag\\
 R_{z,N}&=\varepsilon
   \{q_0z_{0,N}'-\mathcal D_{0,\perp,N}\},\notag\\
 R_{q,N}&=\varepsilon q_0
   (\mathcal D_{0,c,N}-q_0)',\notag\\
 \mathcal C_N(r)&=B_N(r)+z_{0,N}'(r)\ell_N(r).   \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-data}
\end{align}
Define the seed resource and the nonlinear resource defect by
\begin{align}
 w_{\rm seed,N}
 &=w_{0,N}+\varepsilon\mathcal D_{0,c,N}-\varepsilon q_0,\notag\\
 \mathfrak p_{\rm nl}
 &=w_{\rm seed,N}-w\notag\\
 &=\varepsilon Q+\ell_NZ+h_{c,N}
       -\varepsilon\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}.        \label{eq:nonlinear-resource-defect}
\end{align}
Then the two projected identities in
\cref{eq:exact-outer-voltage-invariance,eq:exact-outer-resource-invariance}
are equivalent to
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon qZ'
 ={}&\mathcal C_NZ-z_{0,N}'\mathfrak p_{\rm nl}-R_{z,N}
       +z_{0,N}'h_{c,N}-h_{\perp,N}\notag\\
 &+\varepsilon\{
       \Delta\mathcal D_{\perp,N}
       -z_{0,N}'\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}\},         \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-Z-normal-form}\\
 \varepsilon q\mathfrak p_{\rm nl}'
 ={}&w_{0,N}'\mathfrak p_{\rm nl}-w_{0,N}'\ell_NZ
       -w_{0,N}'h_{c,N}\notag\\
 &+\varepsilon w_{0,N}'\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}
       +\varepsilon^2q(\mathcal D_{0,c,N}-q_0)',   \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-p-normal-form}\\
 \varepsilon Q
 ={}&\mathfrak p_{\rm nl}-\ell_NZ-h_{c,N}
       +\varepsilon\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N},
 &q={}&q_0+Q,                                      \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-Q-reconstruction}\\
 w={}&w_{\rm seed,N}-\mathfrak p_{\rm nl}.        \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-w-reconstruction}
\end{align}
In particular, no derivative of \(h_{c,N}\),
\(\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}\), or \(Q\) occurs in the nonlinear forcing.
At the origin the right-hand sides have affine value
\((-R_{z,N},\varepsilon R_{q,N})\).  If
\[
 \mathscr K_{\perp/c,N}[\widehat Z,\widehat Q]
 =D\Delta\mathcal D_{\perp/c,N}(0,0)
       [\widehat Z,\widehat Q],
\]
then the exact differential is
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon q_0\widehat Z'
 &=\mathcal C_N\widehat Z-z_{0,N}'\widehat p
   +\varepsilon(\mathscr K_{\perp,N}
                  -z_{0,N}'\mathscr K_{c,N}),\notag\\
 \varepsilon q_0\widehat p'
 &=w_{0,N}'\widehat p-w_{0,N}'\ell_N\widehat Z
   +\varepsilon w_{0,N}'\mathscr K_{c,N}
   +\varepsilon^2(\mathcal D_{0,c,N}-q_0)'\widehat Q,\notag\\
 \varepsilon\widehat Q
 &=\widehat p-\ell_N\widehat Z
   +\varepsilon\mathscr K_{c,N}.                 \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-full-differential}
\end{align}
Thus \cref{eq:principal-green-transformed-system} is the retained leading
differential block; the full linearization additionally contains the
displayed \(\varepsilon\)-weighted delay/backtrack terms.

Around each admissible base point, after shrinking the product neighborhood,
the algebraic relation
\cref{eq:nonlinear-tracker-Q-reconstruction} locally determines \(Q\) from
\((Z,\mathfrak p_{\rm nl})\) in \(C^0\).  More precisely, on every common
flow tube on which \(-q\) is bounded above and below and
\(\|\mathbf1+z_{0,N}'+Z'\|_N\le M_X\),
\begin{equation}
 \|D_Q\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}[H]\|_{C^0}
 \le C\delta M_X\|H\|_{C^0}.                     \label{eq:nonlinear-tracker-Q-contraction}
\end{equation}
Hence the coordinate change is one-to-one for small \(\delta\), uniformly
in \(N\).  The reduced structural directions have no direct contribution to
\(\mathcal D_{c,N}\), because \(\pi_N^TE_k=0\).
\end{proposition}

The remaining endpoint object is a genuine RFDE history, not an independent
function \(q_{\rm old}\).  The next result constructs its strong compatibility
jets without adding rows to the Green boundary operator.  In fold time write
the raw fields as
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak F_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(\phi,\omega)
 ={}&\delta^{-1}\bigl[
  (2/3-\omega)\mathbf1
  -\mathcal N_N(\phi(0)-\mathbf1)
  +\mathcal A_N\{\phi(0)-\mathbf1\}\bigr]\notag\\
 &+\delta K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
       \{\phi(0)-\phi(-\theta_k)\},\notag\\
 \mathfrak G_{N,\delta,\nu}(\phi)
 ={}&\delta\{\pi_N^T\phi(0)-1-\delta^2\nu\}.       \label{eq:raw-fold-time-fields}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Compatible endpoint-jet collar chart]
\label[proposition]{prop:compatible-endpoint-jet-collar}
Fix an integer \(h\ge3\).  Suppose at least one delay is positive, put
\(\theta_+=\min\{\theta_k:\theta_k>0\}\), and choose
\(c_H>0\) and small \(\delta\) so that \(4c_H\delta<\theta_+\).
Decrease \(r_{\rm out}\le1\) if necessary.
For an endpoint \(e\) satisfying
\(\rho_\delta\le|e|\le r_{\rm out}\), set
\(\kappa_e=\kappa_{N,\delta}(e)\) and define
\begin{equation}
 \|\psi\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e}
 =\max_{0\le j\le h}\delta^j
   \sup_{-\theta_m\le\theta\le0}
    e^{2\kappa_e\theta}\|\partial_\theta^j\psi(\theta)\|_N.   \label{eq:strong-collar-norm}
\end{equation}
There is a dimension-uniform linear Hermite operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\delta,h}(a_0,\ldots,a_h)(\theta)
 =\sum_{j=0}^h\delta^ja_j\chi_j(\theta/\delta),    \label{eq:collar-Hermite-lift}
\end{equation}
where the scalar one-sided \(C^\infty(({-}\infty,0])\) functions \(\chi_j\)
are supported, relative to that half-line, in \([-c_H,0]\) and satisfy
\(\chi_j^{(i)}(0)=\delta_{ij}\).  It obeys
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal E_{\delta,h}a\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e}
 \le C_h\max_{0\le j\le h}\delta^j\|a_j\|_N.      \label{eq:collar-Hermite-bound}
\end{equation}
Put
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr K_{N,\delta}^h
 =\{\widehat\gamma\in C^h([-\theta_m,0];\R^N):
        \partial_\theta^j\widehat\gamma(0)=0,\ 0\le j\le h\}.
                                                               \label{eq:collar-core-space}
\end{equation}

On every fixed bounded data ball whose endpoint values lie in a sufficiently
small weighted affine neighborhood of a reference critical collar, every
\begin{equation}
 (Z_e,p_e,\widehat\gamma;\nu,\eta)
 \in E_N\times\R\times\mathscr K_{N,\delta}^h
       \times\R\times\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}       \label{eq:collar-chart-data}
\end{equation}
determines a unique history--resource pair
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_e^h(Z_e,p_e,\widehat\gamma;\nu,\eta)
   =(\phi_e,\omega_e)                               \label{eq:collar-chart}
\end{equation}
of the form \(\phi_e=\widehat\gamma+\mathcal E_{\delta,h}a\), satisfying
\begin{align}
 \phi_e(0)&=V_{0,N}(e)+Z_e,&
 \omega_e&=w_{\rm seed,N}(e)-p_e,                 \label{eq:collar-current-data}\\
 \partial_\theta^j\phi_e(0)
 &=\mathfrak V_{j,N}(\phi_e,\omega_e;\delta,\nu,\eta),
 &1&\le j\le h.                                   \label{eq:collar-compatibility}
\end{align}
Here \(\mathfrak V_{1,N}=\mathfrak F_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}\), and
\(\mathfrak V_{j,N}\) is obtained by differentiating the two raw equations
\(j-1\) times, using \(\mathfrak G\) for every resource derivative.
Thus \cref{eq:collar-compatibility} is precisely the order-\(h\) raw-RFDE
compatibility condition.  The recursion for the endpoint jets is triangular:
the right-hand side at order \(j\) uses only endpoint jets of order below
\(j\) and the prescribed core jets at the positive delays.

For fixed parameters, two chart values satisfy the dimension-uniform tame
estimate
\begin{align}
 \|\phi_e-\widetilde\phi_e\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e}
 +\frac{|\omega_e-\widetilde\omega_e|}{|e|}
 \le C_h\biggl\{
  \|Z_e-\widetilde Z_e\|_N
  +\frac{|p_e-\widetilde p_e|}{|e|}
  +\|\widehat\gamma-\widetilde{\widehat\gamma}\|_{
        \mathrm{cap},h,e}\biggr\}.                \label{eq:collar-chart-tame-bound}
\end{align}
The chart, as a map into the fixed \(\theta\)-history space, is three times
Fr\'{e}chet differentiable in all its arguments, with analogous triangular
tame bounds, uniformly in \(N\).  In particular it supports every
rectangular parameter jet in
\((C_\nu^1C_\eta^2)\cap(C_\nu^2C_\eta^1)\).

If the collective collar coordinate
\begin{equation}
 r_{\phi_e}(\theta)=\pi_N^T\{\phi_e(\theta)-\mathbf1\}         \label{eq:collar-collective-coordinate}
\end{equation}
is monotone and
\(-q_+\le\delta^{-1}r_{\phi_e}'\le-q_-\), its image carries the unique
old-speed function
\begin{equation}
 q_{\rm old}(r_{\phi_e}(\theta))
 =\delta^{-1}r_{\phi_e}'(\theta).                  \label{eq:collar-derived-old-speed}
\end{equation}
It belongs to \(C^{h-1}\), includes its complete endpoint jet, and is not
an additional boundary datum.  More precisely,
\begin{equation}
 r_{\phi_e}(\theta)=\Phi_{q_{\rm old}}^{\delta\theta}(e),
 \qquad r_{\phi_e}(0)=e,                           \label{eq:collar-backtrack-bridge}
\end{equation}
and, with
\[
 \mathcal D_{c,N}[\phi_e]
 =K\sum_{k=0}^m\pi_N^TL_{k,N}(\eta)
       \{\phi_e(0)-\phi_e(-\theta_k)\},
\]
the first compatibility row gives the exact endpoint bridge
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon\{q_{\rm old}(e)-q_0(e)\}
 =p_e-\ell_N(e)Z_e-h_{c,N}(e,Z_e)
  +\varepsilon\{\mathcal D_{c,N}[\phi_e]
                    -\mathcal D_{0,c,N}(e)\}.     \label{eq:collar-speed-defect-bridge}
\end{equation}
Thus the right-hand side is exactly the reconstruction of
\(\varepsilon Q_e\) in
\cref{eq:nonlinear-tracker-Q-reconstruction}.  The exact nonlinear scalar
trace is simply
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak p_{\rm nl}(e)=w_{\rm seed,N}(e)-\omega_e=p_e.      \label{eq:collar-nonlinear-trace}
\end{equation}
Consequently the finite Green trace count remains
\((N-1)+1=N\).  On the repelling branch the inner value \(p_e\) may be the
output of the mixed BVP; evaluating \(\Gamma_e^h\) at that value imposes no
second scalar boundary row.  The core variable \(\widehat\gamma\) is a
collar gauge, not an additional Fredholm row.

The pullback
\(q_{\rm old}\circ r_{\phi_e}=\delta^{-1}r_{\phi_e}'\) carries the same
three parameter jets in the fixed \(\theta\)-chart.  No lossless Eulerian
fixed-\(r\) assertion is made: on a common image interval, a parameter
derivative of total order \(0\le b\le\min\{3,h-1\}\) generally has only
\(D_\lambda^bq_{\rm old}\in C^{h-1-b}\); a full third Eulerian parameter
jet therefore requires \(h\ge4\).  Nor is
\(\Gamma_e^h\) a compatible right inverse for arbitrary Green sources; it
is a chart of raw-compatible histories, and matching it to such a right
inverse is a separate hybrid step.  If there is no positive delay, the
statement reduces to the corresponding finite-dimensional jet recursion.
\end{proposition}

The strong collar chart does not make every endpoint Green lift slow.  The
following scalar mode identifies the remaining hybrid boundary-layer gate.

\begin{proposition}[Finite endpoint scale does not control the slow speed]
\label[proposition]{prop:endpoint-scale-not-slow-speed}
Let \(I=[\rho,R]\) with fixed \(R>0\), take
\(q_0=-c\), \(w_0'(r)=-r/c\), \(\ell=0\), and \(Z=0\), where \(c>0\).
For every fixed \(0<A<c/2\), the homogeneous scalar equation has the solution
\begin{equation}
 Q_A(r)=A\exp\left\{\frac{r^2-R^2}{2c^2\varepsilon}\right\},
 \qquad \mathfrak p_A(r)=\varepsilon Q_A(r).       \label{eq:speed-boundary-layer}
\end{equation}
It retains the strict speed sign \(q_0+Q_A\le-c/2\) and has endpoint datum
\(\mathfrak p_A(R)=\varepsilon A\), hence the finite endpoint scale is
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}|\mathfrak p_A(R)|=A/R.    \label{eq:speed-boundary-layer-trace-scale}
\end{equation}
Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
 \|Q_A\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^0}
 \ge\frac{RA}{\varepsilon},
 \qquad R|Q_A'(R)|=\frac{R^2A}{c^2\varepsilon},    \label{eq:speed-boundary-layer-blowup}
\end{equation}
so it does not belong to the uniform slow-speed class of
\cref{prop:physical-backtrack-calculus}.  If \(w'=r/(q_0+Q_A)\), then
\begin{equation}
 |w'(R)-w_0'(R)|=\frac{RA}{c(c-A)},                \label{eq:speed-boundary-layer-resource}
\end{equation}
which is not \(O(\varepsilon)\).  Thus the finite endpoint scale alone does
not supply the slow-speed hypothesis.  Coupling the collar chart to the
finite endpoint theorem still requires a slow/action hybrid backtrack
estimate, or an explicit first-delay buffer, before it can close the
nonlinear tracker.
\end{proposition}

The obstruction is caused by using the slow coordinate inside an endpoint
layer, not by the physical RFDE.  In fold time the delay is fixed, the
compatible collar is an ordinary initial history, and the nonlinear speed
does not multiply a differentiated boundary layer.  The following exact
reformulation identifies the correct space in which the remaining mixed BVP
has to be solved.

\begin{proposition}[Exact fixed-delay tracker normal form in fold time]
\label[proposition]{prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form}
Retain \cref{prop:nonlinear-resource-defect-normal-form} and the raw
fold-time fields in \cref{eq:raw-fold-time-fields}.  Put
\[
 I_s=[s_-,s_+],\qquad J_s=[s_--\theta_m,s_+],
\]
and suppose
\[
 r\in C(J_s)\cap C^1(I_s),\qquad
 Z\in C(J_s;E_N)\cap C^1(I_s;E_N),\qquad
 p\in C^1(I_s).
\]
All equations below are imposed for \(s\in I_s\).  Suppose every current
and delayed value of \(r\), as well as every \(q_0\)-backtrack used to
define \(\mathcal D_{0,N}(r(s))\), lies in one coefficient collar.  Put
\begin{align}
 \mathcal D_N^{\rm ft}[r,Z](s)
 ={}&K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
 \bigl[\{r(s)-r(s-\theta_k)\}\mathbf1\notag\\
 &\hspace{29mm}+z_{0,N}(r(s))+Z(s)
       -z_{0,N}(r(s-\theta_k))-Z(s-\theta_k)\bigr],\notag\\
 \Delta\mathcal D_N^{\rm ft}[r,Z](s)
 ={}&\mathcal D_N^{\rm ft}[r,Z](s)
       -\mathcal D_{0,N}(r(s)).                   \label{eq:fold-time-tracker-delay}
\end{align}
Write \(h_{c/\perp,N}(s)=h_{c/\perp,N}(r(s),Z(s))\), and evaluate every
undisplayed coefficient below at \(r(s)\).  Define
\begin{align}
 Q(s)
 ={}&\varepsilon^{-1}
       \{p-\ell_NZ-h_{c,N}\}
       +\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}^{\rm ft}[r,Z](s),\notag\\
 q(s)={}&q_0(r(s))+Q(s).                          \label{eq:fold-time-speed-reconstruction}
\end{align}
Assume \(-q_+\le q(s)\le-q_-<0\).  Then the following statements are
equivalent:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em]
\item \((r,Z,p)\) satisfies the fixed-delay defect system
\begin{align}
 r_s={}&\delta q,                                  \label{eq:fold-time-tracker-r}\\
 \delta Z_s
 ={}&\mathcal C_NZ-z_{0,N}'p-R_{z,N}
       +z_{0,N}'h_{c,N}-h_{\perp,N}\notag\\
 &+\varepsilon\{\Delta\mathcal D_{\perp,N}^{\rm ft}
       -z_{0,N}'\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}^{\rm ft}\},
                                                        \label{eq:fold-time-tracker-Z}\\
 \delta p_s
 ={}&w_{0,N}'p-w_{0,N}'\ell_NZ-w_{0,N}'h_{c,N}
       +\varepsilon w_{0,N}'\Delta\mathcal D_{c,N}^{\rm ft}\notag\\
 &+\varepsilon^2q(\mathcal D_{0,c,N}-q_0)'.       \label{eq:fold-time-tracker-p}
\end{align}

\item The reconstruction
\begin{equation}
 v(s)=\mathbf1+r(s)\mathbf1+z_{0,N}(r(s))+Z(s),
 \qquad
 w(s)=w_{\rm seed,N}(r(s))-p(s)                  \label{eq:fold-time-tracker-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
is a classical solution of the unmodified shared-resource RFDE written in
fold time, with current history
\(v_s^{\rm hist}(\theta)=v(s+\theta)\), and
\[
 r(s)=\pi_N^T\{v(s)-\mathbf1\},\qquad
 p(s)=w_{\rm seed,N}(r(s))-w(s).
\]
\end{enumerate}

The equivalence itself places no orbit condition on the prescribed old
history \(J_s\setminus I_s\).  If, in addition,
\begin{equation}
 r\in C^2(J_s),\qquad Z\in C^1(J_s;E_N),\qquad
 -q_+^{\rm ext}\le\delta^{-1}r_s
       \le-q_-^{\rm ext}<0\quad\hbox{on }J_s,     \label{eq:fold-time-old-history-speed}
\end{equation}
define the extended speed on the image of \(r\) by
\[
 q_{\rm ext}(r(\sigma))=\delta^{-1}r_\sigma(\sigma),
 \qquad \sigma\in J_s.
\]
Then \(q_{\rm ext}=q\) on the current interval and
\begin{equation}
 r(s-\theta_k)=\Phi_{q_{\rm ext}}^{-\delta\theta_k}(r(s)).
                                                        \label{eq:fold-time-backtrack-identification}
\end{equation}
Under this additional hypothesis, reparameterization by \(r\) recovers exactly
\cref{eq:nonlinear-tracker-Z-normal-form,eq:nonlinear-tracker-p-normal-form}
on the current \(r\)-image, with every delayed voltage evaluation represented
by the extended curve.  It also recovers the voltage-history evaluation
formula in \cref{eq:exact-outer-history-embedding}.  This does not assert that
the prescribed old history is itself a past physical orbit.  That stronger
interpretation requires an old resource extension satisfying the raw RFDE.
Conversely, every monotone physical outer orbit gives this fixed-delay system.
Without \cref{eq:fold-time-old-history-speed}, the same reparameterization
conclusion holds after the first-delay window, whenever
\(s-\theta_k\ge s_-\) for every active delay.

In particular, \cref{eq:fold-time-tracker-Z,eq:fold-time-tracker-p} contains
neither a state-dependent backtrack nor a product of \(Q\) with \(Z_s\) or
\(p_s\).  If
\((\phi_e,\omega_e)=\Gamma_e^h(Z_e,p_e,\widehat\gamma;\nu,\eta)\), its
raw-compatible initial data for this system are
\begin{align}
 r_-(\theta)&=\pi_N^T\{\phi_e(\theta)-\mathbf1\},\notag\\
 Z_-(\theta)&=P_{\perp,N}\{\phi_e(\theta)-\mathbf1\}
                  -z_{0,N}(r_-(\theta)),
 &-\theta_m\le\theta\le0,\notag\\
 p(s_-)&=w_{\rm seed,N}(e)-\omega_e=p_e.          \label{eq:fold-time-collar-interface}
\end{align}
Thus \(\Gamma_e^h\) is an ordinary initial-history chart for the fixed-delay
system, with no resource history and no extra scalar row.  It need not
describe a past orbit; even \cref{eq:fold-time-old-history-speed} alone only
provides an \(r\)-parameterization of its voltage history.  A past-orbit
claim additionally requires that the old history satisfy the raw RFDE with
a compatible resource extension.  This proposition is an exact equivalence,
not an existence theorem for the mixed BVP.
\end{proposition}

The first invertible block of that mixed BVP can be isolated without
assuming a physical tracker.  The qualification ``fixed phase'' is
essential: deleting the collective equation while allowing its speed
correction to move the current phase would not give a linearization of the
raw RFDE.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-phase \(C^0\) complete-history Green--collar inverse]
\label[proposition]{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer}
Retain \cref{prop:principal-tracker-endpoint-green,%
prop:compatible-endpoint-jet-collar,%
prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form}.  Let \(r_a(T)\) and \(r_r(T)\) be the
reduced paths in \cref{prop:principal-tracker-endpoint-green}, and let
\(T_a\) be the first time at which \(r_a(T_a)=\rho_\delta\).  Set
\begin{equation*}
 \bar r_a(s)=r_a(\delta s),\quad
 \bar r_r(s)=r_r(\delta s),\quad
 S_a=T_a/\delta,\quad S_r=T_r/\delta.
\end{equation*}
Thus \(\partial_s\bar r_\sigma=\delta q_0(\bar r_\sigma)\), the attracting
path runs from \(r_{\rm out}\) to \(\rho_\delta\), and the repelling path
runs from \(-\rho_\delta\) to \(-r_{\rm out}\).  All coefficients below
are evaluated on one prescribed path \(\bar r_\sigma\), and the current
collective variation is fixed to zero.  Extend each reduced path backward
by one maximal delay with the \(q_0\)-flow inside the common coefficient
collar.  Work on the retained parameter ball
\[
 |\nu|\le\nu_*,\qquad
 \eta\in\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red},\qquad
 \|\eta\|_{\mathfrak T_N}<\eta_0.
\]

Let \(\gamma\in C([ -\theta_m,0];\R^N)\) satisfy
\(\gamma(0)\in E_N\).  For a current transverse path \(Z\), assemble the
full voltage direction
\begin{equation}
 \widehat u_{\gamma,Z}(s)=
 \begin{cases}
  \gamma(s),&-\theta_m\le s<0,\\
  Z(s),&s\ge0,
 \end{cases}
 \quad
 \mathscr D_N^{\rm lin}[\gamma,Z](s)
 =K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
 \{\widehat u_{\gamma,Z}(s)
       -\widehat u_{\gamma,Z}(s-\theta_k)\}.       \label{eq:fixed-phase-collar-delay}
\end{equation}
Write \(\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}=\pi_N^T\mathscr D_N^{\rm lin}\),
\(\mathscr D_{\perp,N}^{\rm lin}=P_{\perp,N}\mathscr D_N^{\rm lin}\), and
\(\mathfrak d_N=(\mathcal D_{0,c,N}-q_0)'\).  For continuous sources with
\(F(s)\in E_N\) and \(H(s)\in\R\), consider
\begin{align}
 \delta Z_s={}&\mathcal C_NZ-z_{0,N}'p+F
   +\varepsilon\{\mathscr D_{\perp,N}^{\rm lin}
                    -z_{0,N}'\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}\},\notag\\
 \delta p_s={}&w_{0,N}'p-w_{0,N}'\ell_NZ+H
   +\varepsilon w_{0,N}'\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}
   +\varepsilon^2\mathfrak d_NQ,\notag\\
 \varepsilon Q={}&p-\ell_NZ
                  +\varepsilon\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}.  \label{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block}
\end{align}
This is the \((Z,p)\) diagonal normal restriction of the differential of
the fold-time system at the reduced path after the current collective
variation has been fixed.  It is not a Schur complement and not the full
variational equation.

For \(\sigma\in\{a,r\}\), put \(I_\sigma=[0,S_\sigma]\) and
\begin{align}
 \|(Z,p)\|_{\mathsf X_{\sigma,0}}
 &=\sup_{s\in I_\sigma}
   \left\{\|Z(s)\|_N+\frac{|p(s)|}{|\bar r_\sigma(s)|}\right\},\notag\\
 \|(F,H)\|_{\mathsf Y_{\sigma,0}}
 &=\sup_{s\in I_\sigma}
   \left\{\|F(s)\|_N+\frac{|H(s)|}{|\bar r_\sigma(s)|^2}\right\}. \label{eq:fixed-phase-C0-spaces}
\end{align}
Use the endpoint fading norm
\(\|\gamma\|_{N,e,\delta}^{\sharp}\) from
\cref{eq:frozen-history-norm}, with \(e=r_{\rm out}\) on the attracting
branch and \(e=-\rho_\delta\) on the repelling branch.

After decreasing \(\delta_0\), independently of \(N\), the following holds
whenever \(0<\delta<\delta_0\): the boundary problems below have unique mild
solutions, classical for \(s>0\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2em]
\item on the attracting branch, prescribe the complete old history
\(\gamma\), impose \(Z(0)=\gamma(0)\), and set \(p(0)=b_p\);
\item on the repelling branch, prescribe the complete old history
\(\gamma\), impose \(Z(0)=\gamma(0)\), and set \(p(S_r)=b_u\).
Only the scalar future Green line is used;
no backward RFDE evolution is asserted.
\end{enumerate}
Put \(b_a=b_p\) and \(b_r=b_u\).  They obey the dimension-uniform estimate
\begin{equation}
 \|(Z,p)\|_{\mathsf X_{\sigma,0}}
 \le C\left\{
   \|\gamma\|_{N,e,\delta}^{\sharp}
   +\frac{|b_\sigma|}{r_{\rm out}}
   +\|(F,H)\|_{\mathsf Y_{\sigma,0}}\right\}.     \label{eq:fixed-phase-C0-inverse}
\end{equation}

Fix \(C_\Gamma\).  After decreasing \(\delta_0\) further, the repelling
result also permits the compatible feedback for a bounded linear map
\(\mathfrak C_e:\R\to C([ -\theta_m,0];\R^N)\),
\begin{equation}
 \gamma=\gamma_0+\mathfrak C_ep(0),\qquad
 (\mathfrak C_ec)(0)=0\quad(c\in\R),\qquad
 \|\mathfrak C_ec\|_{N,e,\delta}^{\sharp}
       \le C_\Gamma\frac{|c|}{|e|}.               \label{eq:fixed-phase-collar-feedback}
\end{equation}
The solution remains unique and satisfies
\cref{eq:fixed-phase-C0-inverse} with \(\gamma_0\) on the right.  In
particular, at every base point in the fixed bounded chart ball, the
\(p_e\)-column of the voltage-history component of \(D\Gamma_e^h\), with
\(Z_e\), the core gauge, and the parameters fixed, is an operator of this
type evaluated at the unknown resource-defect trace \(p(0)\).  Since
\((\mathfrak C_ec)(0)=0\), the finite boundary rows remain
\((Z(0),p(S_r))\in E_N\times\R\); compatibility creates no additional
Fredholm row.

More explicitly, splitting the delayed term into current and old-history
parts, let \(\mathbb G_\sigma^0\) be the zero-trace Green operator of the
principal block, and let \(\mathbb V_{\rm cur}\) and
\(\mathbb V_{\rm old}\) be the current-path and old-history perturbing
sources obtained after eliminating \(Q\) in
\cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block}.  With \(e_a=r_{\rm out}\) and
\(e_r=-\rho_\delta\), define
\begin{align*}
 \beta_{\rm cur}
 &=\max_{\sigma\in\{a,r\}}
   \|\mathbb G_\sigma^0\mathbb V_{\rm cur}\|_{
      \mathcal L(\mathsf X_{\sigma,0})},\\
 \beta_{\rm col}
 &=\max_{\sigma\in\{a,r\}}
   \sup_{\gamma\ne0}
   \frac{\|\mathbb G_\sigma^0\mathbb V_{\rm old}\gamma\|_{
          \mathsf X_{\sigma,0}}}
        {\|\gamma\|_{N,e_\sigma,\delta}^{\sharp}}.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align}
 \beta_{\rm cur}
 &\le C\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta}
                 +\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\rho_\delta^2}\right\},\notag\\
 \beta_{\rm col}
 &\le Ce^{2\kappa_e\theta_m}
       \left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta}
                 +\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\rho_\delta^2}\right\}
 \le C\frac{\delta^{1-2\vartheta}}{S_\delta}=o(1). \label{eq:fixed-phase-C0-Neumann-number}
\end{align}
The second line bounds the response to every prescribed old history; its
smallness as an operator is needed only for
\cref{eq:fixed-phase-collar-feedback}.

This proposition gives neither an order-\(h\) slow inverse nor a tracker.
The missing collective variation satisfies a differential phase equation
and can retain a displacement created by a short collar pulse.  It must be
closed together with an event row.  Likewise, one maximal delay removes
direct reads of \(\gamma\), but does not erase the delayed echoes already
generated in \((Z,p)\); their high-order slow bootstrap is a separate
finite-generation estimate.
\end{proposition}

The deleted collective equation has a separate, explicitly invertible
phase--event core.  Its natural event-time unknown is scaled by \(\delta\).
This is also the precise reason that a phase displacement cannot be included
in the finite-generation echo contraction.

\begin{proposition}[Exact scaled phase--event core]
\label[proposition]{prop:scaled-phase-event-core}
Retain \cref{prop:principal-tracker-endpoint-green,%
prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form}.  On either reduced branch let
\(e_{\sigma,0}\) and \(e_{\sigma,1}\) denote its entry and exit values:
\begin{equation*}
 (e_{a,0},e_{a,1})=(r_{\rm out},\rho_\delta),\qquad
 (e_{r,0},e_{r,1})=(-\rho_\delta,-r_{\rm out}).
\end{equation*}
Let \(\bar r_\sigma:[0,S_\sigma]\to I_\sigma\) be the corresponding
reduced fold-time path, defined by
\begin{equation*}
 \partial_s\bar r_\sigma=\delta q_0(\bar r_\sigma),\qquad
 \bar r_\sigma(0)=e_{\sigma,0},\qquad
 \bar r_\sigma(S_\sigma)=e_{\sigma,1}.
\end{equation*}
Write \(I_\sigma\) for the closed interval between these endpoints.  Norm
integrals over \(I_\sigma\) are the usual nonoriented integrals; only
primitives with explicit endpoints are oriented from \(e_{\sigma,0}\).
Fix an integer \(j\ge0\), retain the coefficient estimates through order
\(j+1\), and endow
\(\mathsf H_\phi^j(I_\sigma)=C^j(I_\sigma)\) and
\(\mathsf A_\phi^{j+1}(I_\sigma)=C^{j+1}(I_\sigma)\) with
\begin{align}
 \|h\|_{\mathsf H_{\phi}^j(I_\sigma)}
 &=\int_{I_\sigma}|h(r)|\,\dd r
   +\max_{0\le i\le j}\sup_{r\in I_\sigma}
          |r|^{i+1}|\partial_r^ih(r)|,\notag\\
 \|a\|_{\mathsf A_{\phi}^{j+1}(I_\sigma)}
 &=\sup_{r\in I_\sigma}|a(r)|
   +\int_{I_\sigma}|a'(r)|\,\dd r
   +\max_{1\le i\le j+1}\sup_{r\in I_\sigma}
          |r|^i|\partial_r^ia(r)|.               \label{eq:phase-event-spaces}
\end{align}
Then the bordered operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}(a,\widehat\tau)
 =\bigl(q_0^2a',\ a(e_{\sigma,0}),\
              a(e_{\sigma,1})+\widehat\tau\bigr) \label{eq:phase-event-operator}
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
 \mathsf A_{\phi}^{j+1}(I_\sigma)\times\R
 \longrightarrow
 \mathsf H_{\phi}^{j}(I_\sigma)\times\R^2
\end{equation*}
with dimension-uniform inverse.  For data \((h,b_0,b_E)\), that inverse is
\begin{align}
 a(r)&=b_0+\int_{e_{\sigma,0}}^r
                  \frac{h(u)}{q_0(u)^2}\,\dd u,\notag\\
 \widehat\tau&=b_E-a(e_{\sigma,1}),              \label{eq:phase-event-explicit-inverse}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 \|a\|_{\mathsf A_{\phi}^{j+1}}+|\widehat\tau|
 \le C_j\bigl\{
       \|h\|_{\mathsf H_{\phi}^{j}}+|b_0|+|b_E|\bigr\}.    \label{eq:phase-event-inverse-bound}
\end{equation}

More precisely, let \(\xi\) be the collective variation of \(r\) along
the prescribed reduced path and let \(\widehat Q\) be the variation of the
reconstructed speed, pulled back to the \(r\)-interval along that monotone
path when it is written as \(\widehat Q(r)\).  The collective differential of
\cref{eq:fold-time-tracker-r} is
\begin{equation}
 \xi_s=\delta\{q_0'(\bar r_\sigma)\xi+\widehat Q\}.
                                                               \label{eq:phase-event-xi-equation}
\end{equation}
With \(a=\xi/q_0(\bar r_\sigma)\), this is exactly
\begin{equation}
 a_s=\delta\frac{\widehat Q}{q_0(\bar r_\sigma)},
 \qquad q_0(r)^2a'(r)=\widehat Q(r).              \label{eq:phase-event-a-equation}
\end{equation}
If \(\Delta S\) varies the terminal fold time and \(b_{\rm raw}\) is the
linearized terminal event datum, then
\begin{equation}
 \xi(S_\sigma)+\delta q_0(e_{\sigma,1})\Delta S
 =b_{\rm raw}.                                   \label{eq:phase-event-raw-row}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\widehat\tau=\delta\Delta S\) and
\(b_E=b_{\rm raw}/q_0(e_{\sigma,1})\) give precisely the last row of
\cref{eq:phase-event-operator}.  Using the unscaled \(\Delta S\) instead
would make the corresponding inverse bound lose a factor \(\delta^{-1}\).
The physical entry datum is
\(b_0=\xi(0)/q_0(e_{\sigma,0})\).
This loss is sharp: with \(\widehat Q=0\), zero entry phase, and
\(b_{\rm raw}=1\), one has
\(|\Delta S|=|\delta q_0(e_{\sigma,1})|^{-1}\).

The entry phase row is also essential.  If it is removed, the homogeneous
operator has the one-dimensional kernel
\((a,\widehat\tau)=(c,-c)\).

If \(\widehat Q\) vanishes after a compact collar interval, then \(a\) is
constant thereafter, not decaying.  Hence the collective phase and every
phase-induced delayed term must remain in the bordered block; no power of a
strict-delay generation operator can contract this persistent component.
The proposition inverts only this scalar core.  It does not prove the full
coupled phase--normal inverse or a nonlinear moving-event chart.

The phase source norm is compatible with the existing slow Green ladder:
for \(\widehat Q\in\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^j(I_\sigma)\),
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat Q\|_{\mathsf H_\phi^j}
 \le C\varepsilon
   \left\{1+\log\frac{r_{\rm out}}{\rho_\delta}\right\}
   \|\widehat Q\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^j}.      \label{eq:phase-event-Q-embedding}
\end{equation}
In fold time, the same identity gives, on a pulse interval \([s_-,s_+]\),
\begin{equation}
 |a(s_+)-a(s_-)|
 \le C\delta\int_{s_-}^{s_+}|\widehat Q(s)|\,\dd s.            \label{eq:phase-event-pulse-bound}
\end{equation}
The pulse may be small, but its phase shift remains constant after the pulse.
\end{proposition}

The top slow derivative of a boundary-layer speed correction need not be
bounded, but its phase action is still small at the natural normal scale.
This is the first off-diagonal estimate needed for the coupled border.

\begin{proposition}[Normal-to-phase action coupling]
\label[proposition]{prop:normal-to-phase-action-coupling}
Retain \cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
prop:scaled-phase-event-core} with \(j=0\).  For either branch, let
\((Z,p,Q)\) solve \cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block} with prescribed old
history \(\gamma\), and define its normalized hybrid size by
\begin{equation}
 \|(\gamma,Z,p)\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf X}_{\sigma,0}}
 =\varepsilon^{-1}\left\{
   \|\gamma\|_{N,e_\sigma,\delta}^{\sharp}
       +\|(Z,p)\|_{\mathsf X_{\sigma,0}}\right\}.  \label{eq:normalized-hybrid-normal-size}
\end{equation}
Here \(e_a=r_{\rm out}\), \(e_r=-\rho_\delta\), and on the repelling branch
\(\gamma\) may be the actual total history after the feedback in
\cref{eq:strong-compatible-feedback-problem} has been closed.  Pull \(Q\)
back by the monotone coordinate \(r=\bar r_\sigma(s)\).  The seam identity
\(Z(0)=\gamma(0)\) makes the assembled delay row, and hence this \(Q\),
continuous on \(I_\sigma\).  Then the
algebraically reconstructed speed direction satisfies
\begin{align}
 |Q(r)|
 &\le C\left\{|r|+\varepsilon
             e^{2\kappa_{e_\sigma}\theta_m}\right\}
       \|(\gamma,Z,p)\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf X}_{\sigma,0}},
                                                               \label{eq:normal-phase-pointwise-Q}\\
 \|Q\|_{\mathsf H_\phi^0(I_\sigma)}
 &\le C\left\{r_{\rm out}^2
       +\varepsilon e^{2\kappa_{e_\sigma}\theta_m}\right\}
       \|(\gamma,Z,p)\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf X}_{\sigma,0}}\notag\\
 &\le C\left\{r_{\rm out}^2
                   +\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\right\}
       \|(\gamma,Z,p)\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf X}_{\sigma,0}}. \label{eq:normal-phase-H0-coupling}
\end{align}
Consequently, if \((a_Q,\widehat\tau_Q)\) is the phase--event response to
the data \((h,b_0,b_E)=(Q,0,0)\), then
\begin{equation}
 \|a_Q\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)}
      +|\widehat\tau_Q|
 \le C\left\{r_{\rm out}^2+\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\right\}
       \|(\gamma,Z,p)\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf X}_{\sigma,0}}. \label{eq:normal-phase-response-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus the normal-to-phase off-diagonal is dimension-uniform and becomes
small by first fixing \(r_{\rm out}\) sufficiently small and then decreasing
\(\delta\).

This estimate includes endpoint and collar boundary layers; it does not
place them in \(\mathsf H_\phi^j\) for \(j\ge1\).  It also proves only the
normal-to-phase half of the coupled border.  No invertibility of the full
phase--normal block is asserted until the phase-to-normal collar and moving
event columns have been defined and estimated.
\end{proposition}

The reverse off-diagonal has an exact cancellation before any estimate is
made.  Write
\begin{equation}
 e_{0,N}(r)=\mathbf1+z_{0,N}'(r).                  \label{eq:reduced-flow-voltage-tangent}
\end{equation}
Extend the prescribed reduced path backward by one delay with the
\(q_0\)-flow, as in \cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer}.

\begin{lemma}[Exact reduced-flow phase-defect column]
\label[lemma]{lem:reduced-flow-phase-defect}
Retain \cref{prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form,%
prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
prop:scaled-phase-event-core}.  Let
\(a\in\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)\), pull it to the current fold-time
interval by \(a(s)=a(\bar r_\sigma(s))\), and use the constant formal
phase extension
\begin{equation}
 \widehat a(s)=
 \begin{cases}
  a(e_{\sigma,0}),&-\theta_m\le s<0,\\
  a(\bar r_\sigma(s)),&0\le s\le S_\sigma.
 \end{cases}                                      \label{eq:formal-phase-collar-extension}
\end{equation}
For \(s\ge0\), put
\begin{align}
 (\mathscr P_{\sigma,N}a)(s)
 =K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)\,
 &q_0(\bar r_\sigma(s-\theta_k))
 e_{0,N}(\bar r_\sigma(s-\theta_k))\notag\\
 &\times\{\widehat a(s)-\widehat a(s-\theta_k)\}.
                                                        \label{eq:reduced-flow-phase-delay-defect}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 D_r\Delta\mathcal D_N^{\rm ft}[\bar r_\sigma,0]
       [q_0(\bar r_\sigma)\widehat a]
 =\mathscr P_{\sigma,N}a.                          \label{eq:exact-phase-delay-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Here \(D_r\) is the Fr\'echet derivative with respect to the complete
collective history, not merely the current scalar value.
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr P_{\sigma,N}c=0\qquad(c\in\R).           \label{eq:constant-phase-delay-annihilation}
\end{equation}
Writing
\(\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}=\pi_N^T\mathscr P_{\sigma,N}\) and
\(\mathscr P_{\perp,\sigma,N}=P_{\perp,N}\mathscr P_{\sigma,N}\), one has
\begin{align}
 \|\mathscr P_{\sigma,N}a(s)\|_N
 &\le C\frac{\delta}{|\bar r_\sigma(s)|}
              \|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)},             \label{eq:phase-delay-pointwise-bound}\\
 \|\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a\|_{\mathsf H_\phi^0(I_\sigma)}
 &\le C\delta\left\{1+
       \log\frac{r_{\rm out}}{\rho_\delta}\right\}
              \|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)}.             \label{eq:phase-delay-action-bound}
\end{align}
All constants are uniform in \(N\).

More precisely, linearize the full fold-time defect system at the prescribed
reduced path \((\bar r_\sigma,0,0)\), and write the collective variation as
\(\xi=q_0(\bar r_\sigma)a\).  If
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm n}
 =\varepsilon^{-1}(p-\ell_NZ)
       +\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}[\gamma,Z]        \label{eq:phase-column-normal-speed}
\end{equation}
is the fixed-phase normal speed direction, then the full differential of the
speed correction and the phase row are exactly
\begin{align}
 \widehat Q_{\rm full}
 &=Q_{\rm n}+\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a,\notag\\
 q_0^2a'
 &=Q_{\rm n}+\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a.             \label{eq:full-delayed-phase-row}
\end{align}
The differential of the total speed \(q=q_0(r)+Q\) is instead
\(q_0'q_0a+\widehat Q_{\rm full}\).
Relative to \cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block}, the two phase-induced normal
sources are exactly
\begin{align}
 \mathscr C_{Z,\sigma}a
 ={}&-q_0R_{z,N}'a
   +\varepsilon\{\mathscr P_{\perp,\sigma,N}a
           -z_{0,N}'\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a\},\notag\\
 \mathscr C_{p,\sigma}a
 ={}&\varepsilon q_0R_{q,N}'a
   +\varepsilon\{w_{0,N}'+\varepsilon\mathfrak d_N\}
             \mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a,             \label{eq:exact-phase-normal-source-column}
\end{align}
where every coefficient is evaluated at the current \(r\).
Consequently a constant phase leaves only
\begin{equation}
 (\mathscr C_{Z,\sigma}c,\mathscr C_{p,\sigma}c)
 =c(-q_0R_{z,N}',\,\varepsilon q_0R_{q,N}').       \label{eq:constant-phase-residual-column}
\end{equation}
These are precisely the derivatives of the affine normal residual
\((-R_{z,N},\varepsilon R_{q,N})\) along the reduced flow.  They need not
vanish because the preparation-independent critical curve is not a
finite-\(\delta\) physical tracker.
Nevertheless, in the fixed-phase normal source norm,
\begin{align}
 \|(\mathscr C_{Z,\sigma}a,\mathscr C_{p,\sigma}a)\|_{
       \mathsf Y_{\sigma,0}}
 &\le C\frac{\delta}{S_\delta^2}
       \|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)},         \label{eq:phase-normal-source-bound}\\
 \|(\mathscr C_{Z,\sigma}c,\mathscr C_{p,\sigma}c)\|_{
       \mathsf Y_{\sigma,0}}
 &\le C\left\{\delta^2+\frac{\delta}{S_\delta^3}\right\}|c|.
                                                        \label{eq:constant-phase-normal-source-bound}
\end{align}
Thus both the full formal phase column and its constant affine-residual
part are \(o(1)\) as normal sources, uniformly on both branches and in
network size.

The extension \cref{eq:formal-phase-collar-extension} is the formal
\(q_0\)-flow phase column, not the endpoint derivative of the raw-compatible
collar chart.  The lemma does not identify those two columns and does not
bound the resulting normal response in the scaled nonlinear tracker space.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Raw-compatible moving-core collar]
\label[lemma]{lem:raw-compatible-moving-core-collar}
Retain \cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton,%
prop:principal-tracker-endpoint-green,%
prop:physical-backtrack-calculus,%
prop:compatible-endpoint-jet-collar} at a fixed order \(h\ge3\).
For every endpoint
\(\rho_\delta\le |e|\le r_{\rm out}\), define the formal moving critical
core
\begin{align}
 r_e^0(\theta)&=\Phi_{q_0}^{\delta\theta}(e),
 &\phi_e^0(\theta)&=V_{0,N}(r_e^0(\theta)),\notag\\
 a_{j,e}^0&=\partial_\theta^j\phi_e^0(0),
 &\widehat\gamma_e^0
   &=\phi_e^0-\mathcal E_{\delta,h}
                   (a_{0,e}^0,\ldots,a_{h,e}^0).             \label{eq:moving-critical-core}
\end{align}
Then \(\widehat\gamma_e^0\in\mathscr K_{N,\delta}^h\), and the
raw-compatible collar with the same current voltage and seed resource is
well defined by
\begin{equation}
 (\phi_e^{\rm rc},\omega_e^{\rm rc})
 =\Gamma_e^h(0,0,\widehat\gamma_e^0;\nu,\eta).
                                                               \label{eq:raw-compatible-moving-core}
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}
 b_{j,e}=\partial_\theta^j\phi_e^{\rm rc}(0)-a_{j,e}^0,
 \qquad \mathcal L_e=q_0(e)\partial_e,             \label{eq:moving-core-jet-defect}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{align}
 \phi_e^{\rm rc}-\phi_e^0
 &=\mathcal E_{\delta,h}(b_{0,e},\ldots,b_{h,e}),
 &b_{0,e}&=0,                                      \label{eq:moving-core-exact-correction}\\
 \max_{0\le j\le h}\delta^j
  \{\|b_{j,e}\|_N+\|\mathcal L_eb_{j,e}\|_N\}
 &\le C_h\varepsilon.                              \label{eq:moving-core-jet-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \|\phi_e^{\rm rc}-\phi_e^0\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e}
 +\|\mathcal L_e\phi_e^{\rm rc}
          -\mathcal L_e\phi_e^0\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e}
 \le C_h\varepsilon.                              \label{eq:moving-core-cap-bound}
\end{equation}
All constants are uniform in the network size, the admissible topology,
the retained parameters, and the two signs of \(e\).

The leading compatibility defects are not merely estimated: they are
exactly
\begin{align}
 b_{1,e}&=-\delta^{-1}R_{z,N}(e),\notag\\
 c_{1,e}^{\rm rc}-c_{1,e}^{0}
 &=-\delta R_{q,N}(e),                            \label{eq:moving-core-first-defects}
\end{align}
where
\(c_{1,e}^{0}=\partial_\theta
  [w_{\rm seed,N}(r_e^0(\theta))]_{\theta=0}\)
and \(c_{1,e}^{\rm rc}\) is the first resource jet forced by the raw
compatibility recursion.  In particular, with
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_e^{\rm ph}
 =\mathcal L_e\phi_e^{\rm rc}-\mathcal L_e\phi_e^0,
                                                               \label{eq:moving-core-phase-collar}
\end{equation}
let \(\varpi_e^{\rm rc}\) be the resource endpoint jet germ forced by raw
compatibility, let
\(\varpi_e^0(\theta)=w_{\rm seed,N}(r_e^0(\theta))\), and define the
resource-defect phase jet by
\(
 p_e^{\rm ph}=-\mathcal L_e(\varpi_e^{\rm rc}-\varpi_e^0)
\).
Then the seam identities are
\begin{align}
 \gamma_e^{\rm ph}(0)&=0,
 &\delta\partial_\theta\gamma_e^{\rm ph}(0)
   &=-q_0(e)R_{z,N}'(e),\notag\\
 p_e^{\rm ph}(0)&=0,
 &\delta\partial_\theta p_e^{\rm ph}(0)
   &=\varepsilon q_0(e)R_{q,N}'(e).               \label{eq:moving-core-seam-match}
\end{align}
Thus the collar column matches the constant affine-residual bulk column in
\cref{eq:constant-phase-residual-column}; it cannot be discarded at the
entry seam.

The order in \cref{eq:moving-core-jet-bound} is generally sharp.  For
example, if \(\nu=0\), \(\mathcal D_{0,\perp,N}=0\), and
\(z_{0,N}(r)=g_Nr^2+O(r^3)\) with \(g_N\ne0\), then at
\(e=-\rho_\delta\)
\begin{equation}
 \delta\mathcal L_eb_{1,e}
 =-2\delta^2q_0(0)^2g_N+o(\delta^2).              \label{eq:moving-core-sharpness}
\end{equation}
Hence \(\varepsilon^{-1}\gamma_e^{\rm ph}\) is uniformly bounded but is
not, in general, \(o(1)\).  This lemma constructs a raw-compatible seam
collar, not a past physical orbit, and does not by itself close the full
phase--normal Schur loop.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing base raw-compatible collar correction]
\label[lemma]{lem:raw-compatible-base-collar-refinement}
Retain \cref{lem:raw-compatible-moving-core-collar} at the fixed order
\(h\ge3\), and write
\[
 d_{j,e}=c_{j,e}^{\rm rc}-c_{j,e}^{0},
 \qquad 0\le j\le h,
\]
for the resource-jet correction used in its proof.  After decreasing
\(\delta_0\),
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\le j\le h}\delta^j
 \bigl\{\|b_{j,e}\|_N+|d_{j,e}|\bigr\}
 \le C_h\varepsilon(|e|+\delta)
 \le C_h\varepsilon|e|                         \label{eq:base-collar-vanishing-jets}
\end{equation}
uniformly for
\(\rho_\delta\le |e|\le r_{\rm out}\).  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \|\phi_e^{\rm rc}-\phi_e^0\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e}
 \le C_h\varepsilon(|e|+\delta)
 \le C_h\varepsilon|e|.                         \label{eq:base-collar-vanishing-cap}
\end{equation}
At either inner endpoint this becomes
\begin{equation}
 \|\phi_{\pm\rho_\delta}^{\rm rc}
             -\phi_{\pm\rho_\delta}^0\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,\pm\rho_\delta}
 \le C_h\varepsilon\rho_\delta
 =C_h\frac{\delta^3S_\delta}{2\alpha}.          \label{eq:base-collar-inner-scale}
\end{equation}
The improvement is only for the base correction.  The moving-endpoint
column in \cref{eq:moving-core-cap-bound} is generally only
\(O(\varepsilon)\), as witnessed by
\cref{eq:moving-core-sharpness}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Delayed zeroth-order phase--event inverse]
\label[proposition]{prop:delayed-phase-event-core}
Under \cref{lem:reduced-flow-phase-defect}, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm del}(a,\widehat\tau)
 =\bigl(q_0^2a'-\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a,\,
       a(e_{\sigma,0}),\,a(e_{\sigma,1})+\widehat\tau\bigr).
                                                        \label{eq:delayed-phase-event-operator}
\end{equation}
After decreasing \(\delta_0\), this is an isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
 \mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)\times\R
 \longrightarrow \mathsf H_\phi^0(I_\sigma)\times\R^2
\end{equation*}
with dimension-uniform inverse.  More explicitly,
\begin{equation}
 \|(\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm del})^{-1}\|
 \le\frac{\|\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{-1}\|}
 {1-C\delta\{1+\log(r_{\rm out}/\rho_\delta)\}},   \label{eq:delayed-phase-event-inverse-bound}
\end{equation}
where the denominator is positive for small \(\delta\).  Thus the
phase-induced delayed speed is now included in the scalar bordered block;
the persistent constant phase mode remains in the bordered variable and is
not treated as a delay generation.

The proposition still uses the formal \(q_0\)-flow collar and fixed
\(r\)-endpoint rows.  It proves neither the raw-compatible phase collar, the
affine-residual normal column in
\cref{eq:constant-phase-residual-column}, nor the scaled moving-event normal
row, and hence not the full phase--normal inverse.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Event-aligned fixed-section normal traces]
\label[proposition]{prop:event-aligned-normal-traces}
Retain \cref{prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form,%
prop:scaled-phase-event-core}, and fix an integer \(d\ge1\).  Let
\[
 \lambda\longmapsto
 (r_\lambda,Z_\lambda,p_\lambda,S_\lambda)
\]
be jointly \(C^1\) in \((\lambda,s)\) on a common interval enlarged by one
delay, based at \((r_*,Z_*,p_*,S)\), and assume the normal components have \(d+1\)
fold-time derivatives and depend \(C^1\) on \(\lambda\) in that local
\(C^{d+1}\) topology near every trace below.  A dot denotes
\(\partial_\lambda|_{\lambda=0}\), a prime denotes \(\partial_s\), and
\(\xi=\dot r\).  Suppose
\begin{equation}
 r_\lambda(S_\lambda)
 =e_1+\lambda b_{\rm raw}+o(\lambda),\qquad
 r_*'(S)=\delta q_e,\qquad |q_e|\ge q_->0,          \label{eq:event-aligned-section-row}
\end{equation}
and put \(\widehat\tau=\delta\dot S\).  For fixed \(L\ge0\) such that
all points \(S_\lambda-L+\theta\) below lie in the common domain, define
\[
 (\mathcal T_{S_\lambda,L}Z_\lambda)(\theta)
 =Z_\lambda(S_\lambda-L+\theta),
 \qquad-\theta_m\le\theta\le0.
\]
Then the raw moving-trace derivatives are
\begin{align}
 D\{p_\lambda(S_\lambda)\}
 &=\dot p(S)+\delta^{-1}p_*'(S)\widehat\tau,\notag\\
 D\{\mathcal T_{S_\lambda,L}Z_\lambda\}(\theta)
 &=\dot Z(S-L+\theta)
       +\delta^{-1}Z_*'(S-L+\theta)\widehat\tau.   \label{eq:raw-moving-normal-traces}
\end{align}
The history identity is an equality in \(C^d([-\theta_m,0];E_N)\).
Define the event-aligned normal traces
\begin{align}
 n_p(S)
 &=\dot p(S)-\frac{p_*'(S)}{\delta q_e}\xi(S),\notag\\
 n_{Z,S,L}(\theta)
 &=\dot Z(S-L+\theta)
   -\frac{Z_*'(S-L+\theta)}{\delta q_e}\xi(S).     \label{eq:event-aligned-normal-quotient}
\end{align}
The phase row and the normal traces then triangularize exactly:
\begin{align}
 \xi(S)+q_e\widehat\tau&=b_{\rm raw},\notag\\
 D\{p_\lambda(S_\lambda)\}
 &=n_p(S)+\frac{p_*'(S)}{\delta q_e}b_{\rm raw},\notag\\
 D\{\mathcal T_{S_\lambda,L}Z_\lambda\}
 &=n_{Z,S,L}
   +\frac{Z_*'(S-L+\cdot)}{\delta q_e}b_{\rm raw}.
                                                        \label{eq:event-aligned-triangular-rows}
\end{align}
In particular, for the homogeneous fixed-\(r\) event, the unknown
\(\widehat\tau\) disappears from every normal terminal row.  At the
prescribed reduced base \(Z_*=p_*=0\), the raw moving-event normal column
itself is identically zero.  This last statement concerns only the
chain-rule trace column; it does not annihilate the phase-induced bulk
response or the affine-residual column.

More generally, let \(e_h\) be a fixed regular \(r\)-section and let
\(s_h(\lambda)\) be its unique nearby crossing:
\(r_\lambda(s_h(\lambda))=e_h\).  Then
\begin{align}
 D\{Z_\lambda(s_h(\lambda)+\cdot)\}
 &=\dot Z(s_h+\cdot)
   -\frac{Z_*'(s_h+\cdot)}{r_*'(s_h)}\xi(s_h),\notag\\
 D\{p_\lambda(s_h(\lambda))\}
 &=\dot p(s_h)
   -\frac{p_*'(s_h)}{r_*'(s_h)}\xi(s_h).          \label{eq:fixed-r-handoff-traces}
\end{align}
Thus a fixed-\(r\) handoff has no separate moving-\(S\) column.  For the
reduced terminal branch, the terminal-relative coordinate
\(\varsigma=s-S\) has reference path
\begin{equation}
 r^\#(\varsigma)=\Phi_{q_0}^{\delta\varsigma}(e_1).             \label{eq:terminal-relative-reduced-path}
\end{equation}
For any chosen buffer length \(L_b>0\), choosing
\(e_h=\Phi_{q_0}^{-\delta L_b}(e_1)\) makes its crossing
\(\varsigma=-L_b\).  At this prescribed reduced base, the
terminal-relative component operator is conjugate by simultaneous
translation to the fixed component operator, so its Green kernels and norm
estimates are unchanged.  In this reference linearization
\(\widehat\tau\) occurs only in the scalar phase--event border.  This
statement does not extend the flushing estimates to a moving nonlinear
family, for which the fixed-\(r\) crossing can move in the
\(\varsigma\)-coordinate.

There is a genuine regularity cost if one instead differentiates the raw
moving trace.  With
\begin{equation}
 \|x\|_{d,\delta}
 =\max_{0\le j\le d}\delta^j
       \|\partial_s^jx\|_\infty,                  \label{eq:moving-trace-strong-norm}
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
 \|\delta^{-1}x'\|_{d,\delta}
 \le\delta^{-2}\|x\|_{d+1,\delta},                \label{eq:moving-trace-one-order-loss}
\end{equation}
and the power \(\delta^{-2}\) is sharp.  Translation is not in general
Fr\'echet differentiable from the continuous piecewise
\(W^{d,\infty}\) space to itself.  Thus the event-aligned formulation
avoids an otherwise unavoidable extra derivative in the component
flushing argument.

This proposition is exact trace calculus.  It does not construct the
nonlinear family, identify the formal collar with the raw-compatible
collar, estimate the affine-residual column, or prove the full bordered
inverse.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Terminal-relative event-aligned trace-scale coordinates]
\label[lemma]{lem:terminal-relative-trace-scale-chart}
Fix \(d\ge0\), \(L\ge0\), and \(\ell>0\), translate the reference terminal
time to zero, and put
\[
 K=[-L-\theta_m,0],\qquad
 K^\ell=[-L-\theta_m-\ell,\ell].
\]
Let
\[
 Y_*=(r_*,Z_*,p_*)\in
 C^{d+2}(K^\ell;\R\times E_N\times\R)
\]
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 r_*(0)=e_1,\qquad r_*'(0)=\delta q_e,\qquad
 |q_e|\ge q_->0.                                  \label{eq:trace-scale-reference-event}
\end{equation}
Assume \(r_*\) has a scalar inverse \(\chi_*\) near \(e_1\), normalized by
\(\chi_*(e_1)=0\).  For
\[
 Y=(r,Z,p)\in C^{d+2}(K^\ell;\R\times E_N\times\R),
 \qquad t_{\widehat\tau}=\widehat\tau/\delta,
\]
restrict to the open neighborhood on which
\[
 |t_{\widehat\tau}|<\ell/2,\qquad
 r(t_{\widehat\tau})\in\mathcal D(\chi_*),\qquad
 |\alpha(Y,\widehat\tau)|<\ell/2,
\]
where
\begin{equation}
 \alpha(Y,\widehat\tau)
 =\chi_*\!\left(r(t_{\widehat\tau})\right).       \label{eq:trace-scale-phase-match}
\end{equation}
Define, for \(\varsigma\in K\),
\begin{align}
 E(Y,\widehat\tau)
 &=r(t_{\widehat\tau})-e_1,\notag\\
 r^\#(\varsigma)
 &=r(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma),\notag\\
 N_Z^\#(\varsigma)
 &=Z(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma)
      -Z_*(\alpha(Y,\widehat\tau)+\varsigma),\notag\\
 N_p^\#(\varsigma)
 &=p(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma)
      -p_*(\alpha(Y,\widehat\tau)+\varsigma).
                                                        \label{eq:trace-scale-chart}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 (Y,\widehat\tau)\longmapsto
   (\widehat\tau,r^\#,N_Z^\#,N_p^\#)             \label{eq:trace-scale-map}
\end{equation}
is \(C^2\) from the displayed strong neighborhood to
\[
 \R\times C^d(K;\R)
       \times C^d(K;E_N)\times C^d(K;\R).
\]
In these coordinates the fixed physical-section event row is the bounded
functional
\begin{equation}
 E=r^\#(0)-e_1.                                    \label{eq:trace-scale-event-row}
\end{equation}
It is algebraically lossless on the retained moving window: once
\((\widehat\tau,r^\#,N_Z^\#,N_p^\#)\) is known, put
\(\alpha=\chi_*(r^\#(0))\) and reconstruct, for
\(\varsigma\in K\),
\begin{align}
 r(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma)&=r^\#(\varsigma),\notag\\
 Z(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma)
 &=N_Z^\#(\varsigma)+Z_*(\alpha+\varsigma),\notag\\
 p(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma)
 &=N_p^\#(\varsigma)+p_*(\alpha+\varsigma).
                                                        \label{eq:trace-scale-reconstruction}
\end{align}
This is a strong-to-weak moving-window coordinate package, not a
same-space Banach diffeomorphism or a chart of the unretained part of
\(K^\ell\).

At \((Y_*,0)\), let a direction be
\[
 H_i=(\xi_i,\mathsf z_i,\mathsf p_i,\sigma_i),
 \qquad
 B_i=\xi_i(0)+q_e\sigma_i,\qquad
 A_i=\frac{B_i}{\delta q_e}.                      \label{eq:trace-scale-first-data}
\]
The raw translated terminal traces
\[
 Z_{\rm raw}^\#(\varsigma)=Z(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma),
 \qquad
 p_{\rm raw}^\#(\varsigma)=p(t_{\widehat\tau}+\varsigma)
\]
have derivatives
\begin{align}
 DE[H_i]&=B_i,\notag\\
 DZ_{\rm raw}^\#[H_i]
 &=\mathsf z_i+\delta^{-1}Z_*'\sigma_i,\notag\\
 Dp_{\rm raw}^\#[H_i]
 &=\mathsf p_i+\delta^{-1}p_*'\sigma_i.           \label{eq:trace-scale-raw-first}
\end{align}
Thus the nonzero-base terminal columns are retained.  The event-aligned
coordinates instead satisfy the exact triangularization
\begin{align}
 DN_Z^\#[H_i]
 &=\mathsf z_i-\frac{Z_*'}{\delta q_e}\xi_i(0),
 &DZ_{\rm raw}^\#[H_i]
 &=DN_Z^\#[H_i]+\frac{Z_*'}{\delta q_e}DE[H_i],
 \notag\\
 DN_p^\#[H_i]
 &=\mathsf p_i-\frac{p_*'}{\delta q_e}\xi_i(0),
 &Dp_{\rm raw}^\#[H_i]
 &=DN_p^\#[H_i]+\frac{p_*'}{\delta q_e}DE[H_i].
                                                        \label{eq:trace-scale-first-triangularization}
\end{align}
All unmarked reference functions in these formulas are evaluated at
\(\varsigma\).

For the second derivative, put
\begin{align}
 R_{12}
 &=\delta^{-1}\xi_1'(0)\sigma_2
   +\delta^{-1}\xi_2'(0)\sigma_1
   +\delta^{-2}r_*''(0)\sigma_1\sigma_2,\notag\\
 A_{12}
 &=\frac{R_{12}-r_*''(0)A_1A_2}{\delta q_e}.
                                                        \label{eq:trace-scale-second-data}
\end{align}
The second event derivative is
\begin{equation}
 D^2E[H_1,H_2]=R_{12}.                              \label{eq:trace-scale-event-second}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 D^2N_Z^\#[H_1,H_2]
={}&\delta^{-1}\{
   \mathsf z_1'\sigma_2+\mathsf z_2'\sigma_1\}
 +\delta^{-2}Z_*''\sigma_1\sigma_2\notag\\
 &{}-Z_*'A_{12}-Z_*''A_1A_2,                     \label{eq:trace-scale-Z-second}\\
 D^2N_p^\#[H_1,H_2]
={}&\delta^{-1}\{
   \mathsf p_1'\sigma_2+\mathsf p_2'\sigma_1\}
 +\delta^{-2}p_*''\sigma_1\sigma_2\notag\\
 &{}-p_*'A_{12}-p_*''A_1A_2.                     \label{eq:trace-scale-p-second}
\end{align}
In particular, the pure \(\widehat\tau^2\) reference column cancels
identically, but the mixed path--event columns do not.

With the scaled strong norm in
\cref{eq:moving-trace-strong-norm}, raw translation obeys
\begin{align}
 \|D_{\widehat\tau}\mathcal T x\|_{d,\delta}
 &\le\delta^{-2}\|x\|_{d+1,\delta},\notag\\
 \|D_{\widehat\tau}^2\mathcal T x\|_{d,\delta}
 &\le\delta^{-4}\|x\|_{d+2,\delta}.              \label{eq:trace-scale-translation-losses}
\end{align}
The first power is sharp by
\cref{prop:event-aligned-normal-traces}; the second follows from the same
rescaling.  The alignment cancels pure reference translations but does not
turn these raw strong-to-weak losses into same-space bounds.

This lemma constructs only \(C^2\) moving-window coordinates for an already
available strong terminal buffer.  It does not produce that buffer, a parameterized RFDE
solution map, a fixed-chart nonlinear residual, a moving-event inverse, a
physical endpoint family, or a connection root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Exact phase-delay shift and state-norm obstruction]
\label[proposition]{prop:phase-delay-action-shift}
Retain \cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
lem:reduced-flow-phase-defect,prop:event-aligned-normal-traces}.
For \(a\in\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)\), write
\begin{equation}
 P=\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a,\qquad
 P_\perp=\mathscr P_{\perp,\sigma,N}a,\qquad
 p^\sharp=p+\varepsilon P,\qquad
 \widehat Q=Q_{\rm n}+P.                          \label{eq:phase-action-shift}
\end{equation}
Then the full formal phase column and the fixed-phase normal block are
equivalent, exactly, to
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon\widehat Q
 &=p^\sharp-\ell_NZ
       +\varepsilon\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}[\gamma,Z],\notag\\
 \delta Z_s
 &=\mathcal C_NZ-z_{0,N}'p^\sharp+F
   +\varepsilon\{\mathscr D_{\perp,N}^{\rm lin}[\gamma,Z]
       -z_{0,N}'\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}[\gamma,Z]\}
   +F_\phi^\sharp,\notag\\
 \delta p_s^\sharp
 &=\{w_{0,N}'+\varepsilon\mathfrak d_N\}
    \{p^\sharp-\ell_NZ
       +\varepsilon\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}[\gamma,Z]\}
       +H+H_\phi^\sharp,                          \label{eq:shifted-phase-normal-system}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
 F_\phi^\sharp=-q_0R_{z,N}'a+\varepsilon P_\perp,
 \qquad
 H_\phi^\sharp=\varepsilon q_0R_{q,N}'a
                    +\delta\varepsilon\partial_sP.           \label{eq:shifted-phase-structured-sources}
\end{equation}
The delay columns are continuous and piecewise \(W^{1,\infty}\) on the
finite delay-seam partition, with
\begin{align}
 |P(s)|+\|P_\perp(s)\|_N+|\partial_sP(s)|
 &\le C\frac{\delta}{|\bar r_\sigma(s)|}
          \|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)},\notag\\
 \|F_\phi^\sharp(s)\|_N
 &\le C\varepsilon\|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)},
 &|H_\phi^\sharp(s)|
 &\le C\frac{\varepsilon^2}{|\bar r_\sigma(s)|}
          \|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)}.   \label{eq:shifted-phase-source-profile}
\end{align}
No seam measure occurs in \(\partial_sP\).

For the zero normal-data phase response, the scalar boundary rows become
\begin{align}
 p^\sharp(0)&=0 &&(\sigma=a),\notag\\
 p^\sharp(S_r)&=\varepsilon P(S_r) &&(\sigma=r).  \label{eq:shifted-phase-boundary-rows}
\end{align}
The repelling compatible-history feedback remains indexed by
\(p^\sharp(0)=p(0)\), because the formal entry extension gives \(P(0)=0\).

The shift is necessary, not cosmetic.  If one instead asks for a uniform
bound of the formal phase response in the old normalized state norm, then
in general
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0<\delta<\delta_0}
 \varepsilon^{-1}
 \|G_\sigma(0,0,\mathscr C_{Z,\sigma}a,
                       \mathscr C_{p,\sigma}a)\|_{
                       \mathsf X_{\sigma,0}}=\infty           \label{eq:normalized-phase-response-obstruction}
\end{equation}
for a bounded family in \(\mathsf A_\phi^1\), whenever a nonzero collective
delayed layer is present.  Thus the coupled inverse must be formulated in a
graph/action norm based on \(\widehat Q\), not by declaring the reverse
off-diagonal bounded in the old normalized state space.  This proposition
does not yet estimate the action of \(\widehat Q\).
\end{proposition}

We next isolate the strong block on which delay generations can actually be
counted.  A zero-delay difference is deleted as one intact zero term.  For a
positive delay, its current evaluation belongs to the generator and only its
shifted evaluation is a generation edge.

\begin{lemma}[Current-absorbed positive-delay splitting]
\label[lemma]{lem:current-absorbed-delay-splitting}
Fix \(d\ge0\), put \(k_d=\max\{2,d+1\}\), and retain
\cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,prop:physical-backtrack-calculus},
with the backtrack proposition used at order \(k_d\) and the principal
coefficient estimates taken through order \(k_d+2\).  Put
\[
 \mathcal I_+=\{k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}:\theta_k>0\}.
\]
Along either prescribed reduced path, with all undisplayed coefficients
evaluated at \(\bar r_\sigma(s)\), define
\begin{align}
 \mathscr R_{k,N}^{\rm cur}(s)
 &=K\{P_{\perp,N}-z_{0,N}'\pi_N^T\}L_{k,N}(\eta),
 &c_{k,N}(s)&=K\pi_N^TL_{k,N}(\eta),\notag\\
 b_{k,N}(s)
 &=\{\varepsilon w_{0,N}'(s)
       +\varepsilon^2\mathfrak d_N(s)\}c_{k,N}(s),
 &&k\in\mathcal I_+,                              \label{eq:current-absorbed-delay-rows}\\
 \mathbb A_{\rm cur}(s)\binom Zp
 &=
  \binom{\displaystyle
  \mathcal C_NZ-z_{0,N}'p
       +\varepsilon\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}
                    \mathscr R_{k,N}^{\rm cur}Z}
 {\displaystyle
  w_{0,N}'p-w_{0,N}'\ell_NZ
       +\varepsilon\mathfrak d_N(p-\ell_NZ)
       +\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}b_{k,N}Z},\notag\\
 \mathbb B_k(s)u
 &=
 \binom{-\varepsilon\mathscr R_{k,N}^{\rm cur}(s)u}
       {-b_{k,N}(s)u}.
                                                               \label{eq:current-absorbed-delay-blocks}
\end{align}
After eliminating \(Q\), \cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block} is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \delta x_s
 =\mathbb A_{\rm cur}(s)x(s)
  +\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}
       \mathbb B_k(s)\widehat Z(s-\theta_k)
  +\binom FH,\qquad x=(Z,p),                       \label{eq:current-absorbed-delay-equation}
\end{equation}
where \(\widehat Z=\gamma\) before \(s=0\) and \(\widehat Z=Z\) afterwards.
No term with \(\theta_k=0\) occurs.

On an attracting outer buffer
\([0,L_b]\subset[0,S_a]\) for which
\begin{equation}
 \frac12r_{\rm out}\le\bar r_a(s)\le r_{\rm out},
 \qquad 0\le s\le L_b,                            \label{eq:attracting-outer-buffer-range}
\end{equation}
let \(\mathbb U_{a,\rm cur}(s,u)\) be the evolution family of
\(\delta x_s=\mathbb A_{\rm cur}(s)x\).  In the norm
\[
 \|(Z,p)\|_{a,\rm out}=\|Z\|_N+\frac{|p|}{r_{\rm out}},
\]
it satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \max_{i+j\le d}\delta^{i+j}
 \|\partial_s^i\partial_u^j
       \mathbb U_{a,\rm cur}(s,u)\|_{\mathcal L(E_N\times\R)}
 \le C_d e^{-c(s-u)/\delta},
 \qquad 0\le u\le s\le L_b.                       \label{eq:current-absorbed-attracting-evolution}
\end{equation}
Here the operator norm is induced by \(\|\cdot\|_{a,\rm out}\).
The constants may depend on \(d\), the fixed \(r_{\rm out}\), and the fixed
delay support, but are uniform in \(N,\delta,\nu,\eta\) on the retained
balls.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Attracting finite-generation collar flushing]
\label[proposition]{prop:attracting-finite-generation-flushing}
Retain
\cref{prop:compatible-endpoint-jet-collar,%
prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
lem:current-absorbed-delay-splitting}.  Fix an integer \(d\ge1\), take
\(h\ge\max\{3,d\}\), and choose an integer \(M=M_d\) such that
\begin{equation}
 M(1-2\vartheta)>d.                               \label{eq:attracting-flush-generation-count}
\end{equation}
 Fix \(A_d>0\), put
\begin{equation}
 L_0=A_dS_\delta,\qquad
 L_b=(M+1)\theta_m+L_0+1,                        \label{eq:attracting-flush-buffer-length}
\end{equation}
and decrease \(\delta_0\).  Then \(L_b<S_a\) and
\cref{eq:attracting-outer-buffer-range} hold.

Let \(\gamma\in C^h([-\theta_m,0];\R^N)\) satisfy
\(\gamma(0)\in E_N\), and let \(b_p\in\R\).  Let \((Z,p,Q)\) be the
attracting solution of \cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block} with \(F=H=0\),
old voltage history \(\gamma\), and
\[
 Z(0)=\gamma(0),\qquad p(0)=b_p.
\]
On the exit interval
\begin{equation*}
 J_b=\bar r_a([L_b-\theta_m,L_b]),
\end{equation*}
use \(r=\bar r_a(s)\) to write the restrictions as
\((Z_b,p_b,Q_b)\).  Then
\begin{align}
 &\|Z_b\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^d(J_b)}
 +\|p_b\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^d(J_b)}
 +\|Q_b\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^{d-1}(J_b)}
 \notag\\
 &\quad\le C_d\left\{
   \delta^{-2d}e^{-cL_0/\delta}
   +\delta^{M(2-2\vartheta)-2d}\right\}
 \varepsilon^{-1}
 \left\{\|\gamma\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,r_{\rm out}}
             +\frac{|b_p|}{r_{\rm out}}\right\}.  \label{eq:attracting-finite-generation-flush}
\end{align}
More precisely, if the input is given the normalized norm
\begin{equation*}
 \|(\gamma,b_p)\|_{a,\mathrm{in}}
 =\varepsilon^{-1}
   \left\{\|\gamma\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,r_{\rm out}}
                  +\frac{|b_p|}{r_{\rm out}}\right\},
\end{equation*}
then the operator norm of this collar-to-exit trace tends to zero with
\(\delta\), uniformly in \(N\).  No decay from the corresponding
\emph{unnormalized} input norm is asserted.  For every fixed \(\mu>0\),
\[
 \delta^{-2d}e^{-cA_dS_\delta/\delta}
 \le C_{d,\mu}\delta^\mu.
\]

The estimate is fixed-parameter.  It neither differentiates the inverse in
\((\nu,\eta)\) nor treats the repelling mixed orientation.  It also supplies
no phase--normal coupling, nonlinear contraction, speed sign, or tracker.
\end{proposition}

The repelling branch cannot be treated by taking powers of its full mixed
Green operator.  Its scalar line is selected from the future, so that full
operator has precursors and no causal support filtration.  The correct
iteration counts only strict delayed evaluations of \(Z\), while resumming
the current \(Z\)--\(p\) loop inside each generation.

\begin{lemma}[Repelling component kernel and decorated delay generations]
\label[lemma]{lem:repelling-component-generations}
Retain \cref{lem:current-absorbed-delay-splitting} at order \(d\ge0\), use
\cref{prop:physical-backtrack-calculus} at order \(d+1\), take the principal
coefficient estimates through order \(d+3\), and work on
\(I_r=[0,S_r]\).  Put
\begin{align}
 \mathbb A_Z
 &=\mathcal C_N+\varepsilon\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}
                 \mathscr R_{k,N}^{\rm cur},
 &a_p&=w_{0,N}'+\varepsilon\mathfrak d_N,\notag\\
 C_{Zp}&=z_{0,N}',
 &C_{pZ}&=a_p\left(\ell_N-\varepsilon
                  \sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}c_{k,N}\right),\notag\\
 B_k^Z&=-\varepsilon\mathscr R_{k,N}^{\rm cur},
 &B_k^p&=-b_{k,N}=-\varepsilon a_pc_{k,N}.          \label{eq:repelling-component-coefficients}
\end{align}
Then \cref{eq:current-absorbed-delay-equation}, with \(F=H=0\), is
\begin{align}
 \delta Z_s
 &=\mathbb A_ZZ-C_{Zp}p
       +\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}B_k^Z\widehat Z(s-\theta_k),\notag\\
 \delta p_s
 &=a_pp-C_{pZ}Z
       +\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}B_k^p\widehat Z(s-\theta_k).
                                                               \label{eq:repelling-component-equations}
\end{align}
Write \(r_*=|r|\).  Uniformly on the repelling branch,
\begin{equation}
 c r_*\le a_p(r)\le C r_*,\qquad
 \|C_{Zp}(r)\|\le Cr_*,\qquad
 \|C_{pZ}(r)\|\le Cr_*^2.                         \label{eq:repelling-component-sizes}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for \(0\le j\le d\),
\begin{align}
 r_*^j|\partial_r^j\mathfrak d_N(r)|
 &\le C_d,                                                   \label{eq:repelling-d-weighted-jets}\\
 r_*^j\|\partial_r^j(\mathbb A_Z-A_N)\|
 &\le C_dr_*,\notag\\
 r_*^j\bigl\{|\partial_r^ja_p|
              +\|\partial_r^jC_{Zp}\|\bigr\}
 &\le C_dr_*,\notag\\
 r_*^j\|\partial_r^jC_{pZ}\|
 &\le C_dr_*^2,\notag\\
 r_*^j\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}\|\partial_r^jB_k^Z\|
 &\le C_d\varepsilon,\notag\\
 r_*^j\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_+}\|\partial_r^jB_k^p\|
 &\le C_d\varepsilon r_*,                                  \label{eq:repelling-weighted-block-jets}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 r_*^{j+1}\left\{
  |\partial_r^j(a_p^{-1})|
  +\left|\partial_r^j\!\left(\frac{q_0}{a_p}\right)\right|
 \right\}\le C_d.                                  \label{eq:repelling-inverse-coefficient-jets}
\end{equation}

Let \(U_Z(s,u)\) be the forward evolution of
\(\delta Z_s=\mathbb A_Z(s)Z\), and, for \(s\le u\), put
\begin{align}
 u_p(s,u)
 &=\exp\left\{-\delta^{-1}\int_s^ua_p(v)\,\dd v\right\},
 &\Psi_p(s,u)&=\delta^{-1}\int_s^ua_p(v)\,\dd v. \label{eq:repelling-component-kernels}
\end{align}
For \(i+j\le d\),
\begin{align}
 \delta^{i+j}\|\partial_s^i\partial_u^jU_Z(s,u)\|
 &\le C_de^{-c(s-u)/\delta},&&u\le s,\notag\\
 \delta^{i+j}|\partial_s^i\partial_u^ju_p(s,u)|
 &\le C_de^{-c\Psi_p(s,u)},&&s\le u.             \label{eq:repelling-component-kernel-bounds}
\end{align}
Define
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal G_Zf)(s)
 &=\delta^{-1}\int_0^sU_Z(s,u)f(u)\,\dd u,\notag\\
 (\mathcal G_p^+g)(s)
 &=\delta^{-1}\int_s^{S_r}u_p(s,u)g(u)\,\dd u,\notag\\
 \mathcal K_{Zp}&=\mathcal G_ZC_{Zp},
 &\mathcal K_{pZ}&=\mathcal G_p^+C_{pZ}.          \label{eq:repelling-component-Green-operators}
\end{align}
Thus the mixed current problem with component sources \((f_Z,f_p)\) and
traces \((b_s,b_u)\) is exactly
\begin{align}
 Z&=U_Z(\,\cdot\,,0)b_s-\mathcal K_{Zp}p
                         +\mathcal G_Zf_Z,\notag\\
 p&=u_p(\,\cdot\,,S_r)b_u+\mathcal K_{pZ}Z
                         -\mathcal G_p^+f_p.       \label{eq:repelling-component-LP}
\end{align}

Let \(\Sigma_r\) be the finite set of all sums of positive delays lying in
\([0,S_r]\), and use the continuous, piecewise \(W^{d,\infty}\) space on
the induced partition, with norm
\begin{equation}
 \|x\|_{\mathfrak S_{r,d}}
 =\max_{0\le j\le d}\delta^j
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0\le s\le S_r}
  \left\{\|\partial_s^jZ(s)\|_N
       +\frac{|\partial_s^jp(s)|}{|\bar r_r(s)|}\right\}.     \label{eq:repelling-component-strong-space}
\end{equation}
After the fixed choice of small \(r_{\rm out}\),
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal K_{Zp}\|\le Cr_{\rm out}^2,\qquad
 \|\mathcal K_{pZ}\|\le C,\qquad
 \|\mathcal K_{Zp}\mathcal K_{pZ}\|\le\frac14              \label{eq:repelling-current-loop-bound}
\end{equation}
on this space.  The action statement used below is local and oriented.  Fix
small \(\kappa,\kappa_{\rm out}>0\) and attach to a forward transverse edge
and a future scalar edge, respectively, the two-point weights
\begin{equation}
 W_Z(s,u)=e^{\kappa(s-u)/\delta}\quad(u\le s),\qquad
 W_p(s,u)=e^{\kappa\Psi_p(s,u)}\quad(s\le u).        \label{eq:repelling-oriented-edge-weights}
\end{equation}
If both endpoints and the intervening scalar edge lie in an outer interval
on which \(a_p\ge c_{\rm out}>0\), one may in addition use
\begin{equation}
 W_{p,{\rm out}}(s,u)=e^{\kappa_{\rm out}(u-s)/\delta}.
                                                               \label{eq:repelling-terminal-edge-weight}
\end{equation}
For \(i+j\le d\), the corresponding precise estimates are
\begin{align}
 \delta^{i+j}W_Z(s,u)
       \|\partial_s^i\partial_u^jU_Z(s,u)\|&\le C_d,\notag\\
 \delta^{i+j}W_p(s,u)
       |\partial_s^i\partial_u^ju_p(s,u)|&\le C_d,\notag\\
 \delta^{i+j}W_{p,{\rm out}}(s,u)
       |\partial_s^i\partial_u^ju_p(s,u)|&\le C_d,             \label{eq:repelling-weighted-kernel-bounds}
\end{align}
where the last line is used only under the outer-interval condition just
stated.
After expanding the current-loop inverse, the kernel of every decorated
component word, including every boundary-face word obtained by at most
\(d\) differentiations, remains bounded after multiplication by the product
of its applicable edge weights.  Each additional current loop contributes
a factor at most \(q_{\rm loop}<1\), uniformly in \(N\), and the loop
multiplicities sum geometrically.  No global terminal-fast weight for the
scalar component is asserted away from such an outer interval.

Let \(h\ge\max\{3,d\}\), let
\(\gamma\in C^h([-\theta_m,0];\R^N)\) satisfy
\(\gamma(0)\in E_N\), and let \(b_u\in\R\).  Define
\begin{align}
 D_\gamma^\diamond(s)
 &=\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathcal I_+\\0\le s<\theta_k}}
       B_k^\diamond(s)\gamma(s-\theta_k),\notag\\
 (\mathscr S_\diamond y)(s)
 &=\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathcal I_+\\s\ge\theta_k}}
       B_k^\diamond(s)y(s-\theta_k),
 \qquad \diamond\in\{Z,p\}.                     \label{eq:repelling-old-and-strict-sources}
\end{align}
Let \(x^{[0]}=(Z^{[0]},p^{[0]})\) be the unique current-loop solution
\begin{align*}
 Z^{[0]}&=U_Z(\,\cdot\,,0)\gamma(0)
     -\mathcal K_{Zp}p^{[0]}+\mathcal G_ZD_\gamma^Z,\\
 p^{[0]}&=u_p(\,\cdot\,,S_r)b_u
     +\mathcal K_{pZ}Z^{[0]}-\mathcal G_p^+D_\gamma^p,
\end{align*}
and, recursively, let \(x^{[n+1]}\) have zero finite boundary rows and solve
\begin{align}
 Z^{[n+1]}&=-\mathcal K_{Zp}p^{[n+1]}
                  +\mathcal G_Z\mathscr S_ZZ^{[n]},\notag\\
 p^{[n+1]}&=\mathcal K_{pZ}Z^{[n+1]}
                  -\mathcal G_p^+\mathscr S_pZ^{[n]}.
                                                               \label{eq:repelling-component-generation-recursion}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 (Z,p)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty(Z^{[n]},p^{[n]})           \label{eq:repelling-component-generation-series}
\end{equation}
converges absolutely in \(\mathfrak S_{r,d}\) to the unique solution of
\cref{eq:repelling-component-equations} with
\(Z(0)=\gamma(0)\) and \(p(S_r)=b_u\).  Moreover,
\begin{align}
 \|x^{[0]}\|_{\mathfrak S_{r,d}}
 &\le C_d\left\{\|\gamma\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta}
                         +\frac{|b_u|}{r_{\rm out}}\right\},\notag\\
 \|x^{[n+1]}\|_{\mathfrak S_{r,d}}
 &\le\beta_{r,\delta}\|x^{[n]}\|_{\mathfrak S_{r,d}},
 &\beta_{r,\delta}
 &\le C_d\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta}
  \le C_d\frac{\delta^{1-2\vartheta}}{S_\delta}=o(1).       \label{eq:repelling-generation-number}
\end{align}
Each recursion index \(n\) counts exactly \(n\) strict delayed evaluations
of the current path after the root insertion.  Endpoint and terminal words
therefore have \(n\) strict positive-delay edges.  An old-history word has,
in addition, its direct collar edge in \(D_\gamma\); equivalently that edge
is absorbed into a current insertion label in \([0,\theta_m]\), after which
the word has \(n\) recursive edges.  The arbitrarily many current
\(Z\)--\(p\) loops have already been summed by
\cref{eq:repelling-current-loop-bound}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Structured phase-column action Green estimate]
\label[proposition]{prop:structured-phase-action-green}
Retain \cref{prop:phase-delay-action-shift,%
lem:current-absorbed-delay-splitting,%
lem:repelling-component-generations} at order zero.  Put

 m_\delta(r)=|r|+\frac{\varepsilon}{|r|^2}.         \label{eq:phase-action-weight}
\end{equation}
On either branch, let \((Z,p^\sharp)\) solve the current-absorbed form of
\cref{eq:shifted-phase-normal-system}, with zero exogenous normal data,
the structured sources in
\cref{eq:shifted-phase-structured-sources}, and the formal boundary rows
\begin{align*}
 &\gamma=0,\quad Z(0)=0,\quad p^\sharp(0)=0
       &&(\sigma=a),\\
 &Z(0)=0,\quad p^\sharp(S_r)=\varepsilon P(S_r),\quad
   \gamma=\mathfrak C_ep^\sharp(0)
       &&(\sigma=r),
\end{align*}

 \sup_{s\in I_\sigma}\left\{
   \frac{\|Z(s)\|_N}{\varepsilon}
   +\frac{|p^\sharp(s)|}

 \le C\|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)}.         \label{eq:structured-phase-state-bound}
\end{equation}

\begin{align}
 |\widehat Q(r)|
 &\le C\left\{|r|+\frac{\varepsilon}{|r|^2}\right\}

             +\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta}\right\}

\end{align}

Equivalently, if \(T_\sigma^{\rm form}a\) denotes the returned normal
response in the original variable
\(p=p^\sharp-\varepsilon P\), then the true action operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm form}a
 =Q_{\rm n}[T_\sigma^{\rm form}a]
       +\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a                 \label{eq:formal-true-action-operator}
\end{equation}
maps \(\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)\) to
\(\mathsf H_\phi^0(I_\sigma)\) with norm at most the last constant in
\cref{eq:structured-phase-action-bound}.  The proposition does not assert
that \(T_\sigma^{\rm form}\) is uniformly bounded in the old normalized

need not be.
\end{proposition}

prop:structured-phase-action-green}.  Put

 e_a=r_{\rm out},\qquad e_r=-\rho_\delta,\qquad

 \gamma=c\gamma_a^{\rm ph},\qquad p(0)=0,

with
\begin{equation}
 p(S_r)=0,\qquad

\end{equation}

\begin{align}
 \|(c\gamma_a^{\rm ph},\mathfrak R_{a,\rm col}c)\|_{
       \widetilde{\mathsf X}_{a,0}}
 &\le C|c|,\notag\\
 \|(\gamma_{r,c}^{\rm tot},\mathfrak R_{r,\rm col}c)\|_{
       \widetilde{\mathsf X}_{r,0}}
 &\le C|c|,                                      \label{eq:raw-phase-collar-response-bound}\\
 \|Q_{\sigma,\rm col}c\|_{\mathsf H_\phi^0(I_\sigma)}
 &\le C\{r_{\rm out}^2+\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\}|c|.
                                                        \label{eq:raw-phase-collar-action-bound}
\end{align}

Define the raw-compatible returned phase column and its true action by
\begin{align}
 T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a
 &=T_\sigma^{\rm form}a
       +\mathfrak R_{\sigma,\rm col}E_{0,\sigma}a,\notag\\
 \mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm rc}a
 &=\mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm form}a
       +Q_{\sigma,\rm col}E_{0,\sigma}a\notag\\
 &=Q_{\rm n}[T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a]
       +\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a.                \label{eq:raw-compatible-true-action}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm rc}\|_{
   \mathcal L(\mathsf A_\phi^1,\mathsf H_\phi^0)}
 \le C\left\{r_{\rm out}^2+\frac{\delta}{S_\delta}
                     +\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\right\}.
                                                        \label{eq:raw-compatible-action-smallness}
\end{equation}
The old-history voltage boundary column is exactly the differential of the
raw-compatible collar.  More explicitly, its phase part is
\begin{equation}
 E_{0,\sigma}a\,\mathcal L_{e_\sigma}\phi_{e_\sigma}^0
 +E_{0,\sigma}a\,\gamma_{e_\sigma}^{\rm ph}
 =E_{0,\sigma}a\,\mathcal L_{e_\sigma}
                         \phi_{e_\sigma}^{\rm rc}.           \label{eq:raw-phase-collar-exact-assembly}
\end{equation}
On the repelling branch the closed feedback
\(\mathfrak C_ep(0)\) is added to this history.  The additive collar phase
column creates no extra scalar boundary row because its voltage and resource
normal traces vanish at the seam.  The total repelling value \(p(0)\) need
not vanish; it is the mixed-boundary output that drives the feedback.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Raw-compatible zeroth-order phase--normal inverse]
\label[theorem]{thm:raw-compatible-phase-normal-inverse}
Retain \cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
prop:scaled-phase-event-core,%
prop:normal-to-phase-action-coupling,%
lem:raw-phase-boundary-column-assembly}.  A normal response is a hybrid
triple \(y=(\gamma_y,Z_y,p_y)\), with
\[
 Q_y=\varepsilon^{-1}(p_y-\ell_NZ_y)
          +\mathscr D_{c,N}^{\rm lin}[\gamma_y,Z_y].
\]
Let \(F_y,H_y\) be the residuals obtained by moving every displayed
non-source term in \cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block} to the left.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr N_a y=(\gamma_y,p_y(0),F_y,H_y),\qquad
 \mathscr N_r y=(\gamma_y-\mathfrak C_ep_y(0),
                         p_y(S_r),F_y,H_y).         \label{eq:fixed-phase-normal-boundary-operator}
\end{equation}
Thus on the repelling branch the first component is the prescribed baseline
history, whereas \(\gamma_y\) is the actual total history.  For a normal-data package
\(f=(\gamma_0,b_\sigma,F,H)\), use the scaled norm
\begin{equation}
 \|f\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf Y}_{\sigma,0}}
 =\varepsilon^{-1}\left\{
   \|\gamma_0\|_{N,e_\sigma,\delta}^{\sharp}
   +\frac{|b_\sigma|}{r_{\rm out}}
   +\|(F,H)\|_{\mathsf Y_{\sigma,0}}\right\}.     \label{eq:raw-schur-normal-data-norm}
\end{equation}
Here \(b_a=p(0)\), \(b_r=p(S_r)\), and
\(e_a=r_{\rm out}\), \(e_r=-\rho_\delta\).
Let \(\mathcal D(\mathscr N_\sigma)\) be the continuous finite-seam hybrid
responses with piecewise \(W^{1,\infty}\) current part, seam condition
\(Z_y(0)=\gamma_y(0)\), algebraic row \(Q_y\), and residual in
\(\widetilde{\mathsf Y}_{\sigma,0}\).  Equip it with
\begin{equation}
 \|y\|_{\mathcal D(\mathscr N_\sigma)}
 =\|y\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf X}_{\sigma,0}}

\end{equation}
Then \(\mathscr N_\sigma\) is closed, and
\[
 G_\sigma:\widetilde{\mathsf Y}_{\sigma,0}

is the solution operator supplied by
\cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer}.  Put

 \mathfrak D_\sigma y=Q_{\rm n}[y]

 \mathscr S_\sigma^{\rm rc}
 =\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}-
       (\mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm rc},0,0).
\]

\begin{equation}
 \|\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{-1}\|

\end{equation}
so \(\mathscr S_\sigma^{\rm rc}\) is an isomorphism from
\(\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)\times\R\) to

 \mathscr N_\sigma\{x-T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a\}&=f,\notag\\
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}(a,\widehat\tau)

       +\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a,0,0\bigr)&=g             \label{eq:raw-compatible-coupled-block}
\end{align}

 y&=G_\sigma f,\notag\\
 (a,\widehat\tau)
  &=(\mathscr S_\sigma^{\rm rc})^{-1}
       \{(\mathfrak D_\sigma y,0,0)+g\},\notag\\
x&=T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a+y.                       \label{eq:raw-compatible-schur-inverse}

\begin{align}
 \mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rc}
 &=\{(x,a,\widehat\tau):
       x-T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a\in\mathcal D(\mathscr N_\sigma)\},\notag\\
 \|(x,a,\widehat\tau)\|_{\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rc}}

 \|(x,a,\widehat\tau)\|_{\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rc}}
 \le C\{\|f\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf Y}_{\sigma,0}}
                 +\|g\|_{\mathsf H_\phi^0\times\R^2}\}.      \label{eq:raw-compatible-schur-bound}
\end{equation}

normalized state norm of \(x\) alone.  For an arbitrary trial \(a\), the
lemma above identifies the raw boundary column; full seam compatibility is
asserted only after the coupled phase equation in
\cref{eq:raw-compatible-coupled-block} is imposed.

This theorem closes the fixed-reduced-base, zeroth-order raw-compatible
phase--normal border.  It does not construct a nonlinear moving tracker,
control parameter or higher slow jets, preserve the sign of the physical

 \mathsf A_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}^1(I_\sigma)

\begin{lemma}[Inner-anchored nonlinear reduced-flow phase chart]
\label[lemma]{lem:inner-anchored-nonlinear-phase-chart}
Let
\[
 I_a=[\rho_\delta,r_{\rm out}],\qquad
 I_r=[-r_{\rm out},-\rho_\delta],\qquad
 \varepsilon=\delta^2,
\]
and put
\[
 e_{a,*}=\rho_\delta,\qquad e_{r,*}=-\rho_\delta,\qquad
 m_\delta(r)=|r|+\frac{\varepsilon}{|r|^2}.
\]
For \(\chi>0\), put
\[
 \mathcal C_{\sigma,\chi}
 :=\left\{u:\ \text{there exists }r\in I_\sigma
       \text{ with }|u-r|<\chi|r|\right\}.
\]
Fix \(0<\chi_0<1/8\), and assume that each \(I_\sigma\) has a
same-sign open enlargement \(J_\sigma\) satisfying
\[
 \mathcal C_{\sigma,4\chi_0}
 \subset J_\sigma .
\]
Assume on \(J_\sigma\), uniformly in all retained indices, that
\begin{equation}
 0<q_-\le-q_0(r)\le q_+,\qquad
 |r|\,|q_0'(r)|\le C_q,                            \label{eq:anchored-phase-q-class}
\end{equation}
There is \(c_\phi>0\), independent of \(N,\delta\) and the branch, such
that the open set
\[
 \mathcal U_{\sigma,\phi}
 =\{a\in\mathsf A_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}^1(I_\sigma):
          \|a\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}<c_\phi\}
\]
has the following property.  For \(a\in\mathcal U_{\sigma,\phi}\), set
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_a(r)=\Phi_{q_0}^{a(r)}(r).                 \label{eq:nonlinear-phase-chart}
\end{equation}
Then \(\Theta_a(I_\sigma)\subset J_\sigma\), and the map
\[
 a\longmapsto\Theta_a:
   \mathcal U_{\sigma,\phi}\to C^1(I_\sigma)
\]
is \(C^1\).  Its differential and the exact phase identities are
\begin{align}
 D_a\Theta_a[h](r)
 &=q_0(\Theta_a(r))h(r),                           \label{eq:nonlinear-phase-chart-a-derivative}\\
 \Theta_a'(r)
 &=\frac{q_0(\Theta_a(r))}{q_0(r)}
       \{1+q_0(r)a'(r)\},                          \label{eq:nonlinear-phase-chart-derivative}\\
 q_0(r)\Theta_a'(r)-q_0(\Theta_a(r))
 &=q_0(r)q_0(\Theta_a(r))a'(r).                    \label{eq:nonlinear-phase-row-identity}
\end{align}
Define the nonlinear phase action by
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm nl}(a)(r)
 =q_0(r)q_0(\Theta_a(r))a'(r).                     \label{eq:anchored-nonlinear-phase-action}
\end{equation}
The map
\[
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm nl}:
 \mathcal U_{\sigma,\phi}\longrightarrow
             \mathsf H_{\phi,\infty}^0(I_\sigma)
\]
is \(C^1\), and its differential is
\begin{align}
 D\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm nl}(a)h
 ={}&q_0(r)q_0(\Theta_a(r))h'(r)\notag\\
 &+q_0(r)q_0'(\Theta_a(r))q_0(\Theta_a(r))
             a'(r)h(r),                            \label{eq:anchored-nonlinear-phase-action-derivative}\\
 D\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm nl}(0)h
 ={}&q_0(r)^2h'(r).                                \label{eq:anchored-nonlinear-phase-linearization}
\end{align}
Consequently the full anchored scalar map
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma,*}^{\rm nl}(a)
 =\bigl(\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm nl}(a),
             a(e_{\sigma,*})\bigr)                 \label{eq:anchored-nonlinear-phase-full-map}
\end{equation}
is \(C^1\) from \(\mathcal U_{\sigma,\phi}\) to
\(\mathsf H_{\phi,\infty}^0(I_\sigma)\times\R\), and
\begin{equation}
 D\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma,*}^{\rm nl}(0)h
 =\bigl(q_0^2h',h(e_{\sigma,*})\bigr).             \label{eq:anchored-nonlinear-full-linearization}
\end{equation}
In particular, let \(\widehat Q:I_\sigma\to\R\) denote a speed
residual already pulled back to the fixed reduced coordinate.  If
\[
 \partial_s\bar r(s)=\delta q_0(\bar r(s)),
 \qquad
 r_a(s)=\Theta_a(\bar r(s)),
\]
then
\[
 \partial_sr_a(s)
 =\delta\left\{q_0(r_a(s))
          +\widehat Q(\bar r(s))\right\}
\]
is equivalent, since \(\bar r\) covers \(I_\sigma\), to
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma}^{\rm nl}(a)(r)
 =\widehat Q(r).                                   \label{eq:anchored-nonlinear-phase-equation}
\end{equation}

There are constants \(c_*,C_*,D_*>0\), depending only on
\(q_\pm,C_q,\chi_0\) and the common collar geometry, with the following
property.  In particular they are independent of
\(N,\delta,\nu,\eta\) and of the branch.  If
\(\widehat Q\in C(I_\sigma)\) and
\begin{equation}
 |\widehat Q(r)|\le Dm_\delta(r),\qquad
 D\Lambda_\delta\le D_*,\qquad
 \Lambda_\delta=r_{\rm out}
       +\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta^2},          \label{eq:anchored-phase-speed-class}
\end{equation}
then the anchored problem
\begin{equation}
 q_0(r)q_0(\Theta_a(r))a'(r)=\widehat Q(r),\qquad
 a(e_{\sigma,*})=0,                               \label{eq:anchored-phase-IVP}
\end{equation}
has a unique solution \(a\in\mathcal U_{\sigma,\phi}\).  It satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|a\|_{\mathsf A_\phi^1(I_\sigma)}
 &\le C_*DM_\delta,
 &M_\delta&=r_{\rm out}^2
                  +\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta},
                                                               \label{eq:anchored-phase-A1-bound}\\
 \sup_{r\in I_\sigma}\frac{|a(r)|}{|r|}
 +\sup_{r\in I_\sigma}|q_0(r)a'(r)|
 &\le C_*D\Lambda_\delta,                          \label{eq:anchored-phase-relative-bound}\\
 \sup_{r\in I_\sigma}\frac{|\Theta_a(r)-r|}{|r|}
 +\sup_{r\in I_\sigma}|\Theta_a'(r)-1|
 &\le C_*D\Lambda_\delta.                          \label{eq:anchored-phase-chart-smallness}
\end{align}
After decreasing \(D_*\) if necessary,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{sgn}\Theta_a(r)=\operatorname{sgn}r,\qquad
 c_*\le\Theta_a'(r)\le C_*,
 \qquad r\in I_\sigma,                             \label{eq:anchored-phase-orientation}
\end{equation}
so \(\Theta_a\) is an orientation-preserving \(C^1\) diffeomorphism onto
its same-sign image.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 q_0(\Theta_a(r))+\widehat Q(r)
 =q_0(r)\Theta_a'(r)<0.                            \label{eq:anchored-phase-speed-sign}
\end{equation}
Thus the reconstructed scalar reduced speed, not yet a speed along a
constructed RFDE tracker, retains its sign.
Since \(a(e_{\sigma,*})=0\),
\[
 \Theta_a(e_{\sigma,*})=e_{\sigma,*}.
\]
Thus the attracting chart is fixed at its inner terminal section
\(e_{a,*}=\rho_\delta\), whereas the repelling chart is fixed at its
inner entry section \(e_{r,*}=-\rho_\delta\).

Since \(\rho_\delta=\delta S_\delta/(2\alpha)\),
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_\delta=r_{\rm out}+\frac{4\alpha^2}{S_\delta^2},
 \qquad
 M_\delta=r_{\rm out}^2+\frac{2\alpha\delta}{S_\delta}.       \label{eq:anchored-phase-small-parameters}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every fixed dimension-uniform upper bound
\(D\le D_0\), condition
\cref{eq:anchored-phase-speed-class} follows by first choosing
\(r_{\rm out}\) so that \(D_0r_{\rm out}\) is sufficiently small and
then decreasing \(\delta\).
\end{lemma}

The exponent in \(\Theta_a=\Phi_{q_0}^{a}\) is \(a\), not
\(\delta a\).  Indeed, a fold-time displacement \(\Delta S\) produces the
\(q_0\)-flow time \(\delta\Delta S=\widehat\tau\), while the phase
coordinate satisfies \(a=\xi/q_0=\widehat\tau\).  Replacing \(a\) by
\(\delta a\) would incorrectly linearize the action as
\(\delta q_0^2a'\).  The two inner anchors are a nonlinear gauge choice;
they are not silently identified with the branch-entry row of
\cref{prop:scaled-phase-event-core}.  The next proposition instead
constructs the corresponding one-row linear inverse directly.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-recut obstruction for the inner-anchored phase core]
\label[proposition]{prop:inner-anchor-endpoint-recut-obstruction}
Retain \cref{lem:inner-anchored-nonlinear-phase-chart}, fix a sufficiently
small \(r_{\rm out}>0\), and put
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\delta^{\rm ep}=r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-2}.
                                                        \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-lambda}
\end{equation}
There is a dimension-uniform \(d_0>0\) such that, for all sufficiently
small \(\delta\), the admissible pulled-back scalar source
\begin{equation}
 \widehat Q_{\sigma,\delta}(r)
 =D_\delta|r|,\qquad
 D_\delta=d_0\lambda_\delta^{\rm ep},             \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-source}
\end{equation}
belongs to the speed class in
\cref{eq:anchored-phase-speed-class}.  Let \(a_{\sigma,\delta}\) be the
corresponding inner-anchored solution.  With
\begin{equation*}
 e_{a,o}=r_{\rm out},\qquad e_{r,o}=-r_{\rm out},
\end{equation*}
one has
\begin{align}
 a_{a,\delta}(e_{a,o})&>0,
 &a_{r,\delta}(e_{r,o})&<0,                       \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-phase-signs}\\
 cD_\delta(r_{\rm out}^2-\rho_\delta^2)
 &\le |a_{\sigma,\delta}(e_{\sigma,o})|
 \le CD_\delta(r_{\rm out}^2-\rho_\delta^2),      \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-phase-size}\\
 \Theta_{a_{a,\delta}}(r_{\rm out})&<r_{\rm out},
 &\Theta_{a_{r,\delta}}(-r_{\rm out})&>-r_{\rm out}.
                                                        \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-sidedness}
\end{align}
For these two admissible inner-anchored solutions, neither image
\(\Theta_{a_{\sigma,\delta}}(I_\sigma)\) contains its original outer
endpoint.  The attracting forward scalar path starts at
\(\Theta_{a_{a,\delta}}(r_{\rm out})<r_{\rm out}\) and ends at
\(\rho_\delta\), whereas the repelling path starts at \(-\rho_\delta\) and
ends at \(\Theta_{a_{r,\delta}}(-r_{\rm out})>-r_{\rm out}\).  The strict
scalar speed sign in \cref{eq:anchored-phase-speed-sign} does not repair this
failure.  On the attracting side the missing endpoint lies behind the
available forward segment; on the repelling side it requires an as yet
unconstructed forward continuation.

The same obstruction is visible in the bordered rows.  The unique constant
shift preserving \(a'\) and imposing the attracting causal-entry row, and
the associated algebraic scalar event-row corrections, are
\begin{align}
 \widetilde a_{a,\delta}
 &=a_{a,\delta}-a_{a,\delta}(r_{\rm out}),
 &\widehat\tau_{a,\delta}
 &=a_{a,\delta}(r_{\rm out}),\notag\\
 \widetilde a_{r,\delta}
 &=a_{r,\delta},
 &\widehat\tau_{r,\delta}
 &=-a_{r,\delta}(-r_{\rm out}).                  \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-row-reanchoring}
\end{align}
They satisfy
\begin{align}
 \widetilde a_{a,\delta}(r_{\rm out})&=0,
 &\widetilde a_{a,\delta}(\rho_\delta)
       +\widehat\tau_{a,\delta}&=0,\notag\\
 \widetilde a_{r,\delta}(-\rho_\delta)&=0,
 &\widetilde a_{r,\delta}(-r_{\rm out})
       +\widehat\tau_{r,\delta}&=0.              \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-rows}
\end{align}
For fixed \(r_{\rm out}\), as \(\delta\downarrow0\),
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\sigma\in\{a,r\}}
 \frac{|\widehat\tau_{\sigma,\delta}|}{\rho_\delta}
 \longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \min_{\sigma\in\{a,r\}}
 \frac{|\widehat\tau_{\sigma,\delta}|/\delta}{S_\delta}
 \longrightarrow\infty.                         \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-scale-divergence}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \|\widetilde a_{a,\delta}\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}
 \ge
 \frac{|\widehat\tau_{a,\delta}|}{\rho_\delta}
 \longrightarrow\infty.                         \label{eq:endpoint-obstruction-relative-norm}
\end{equation}
Here \(\widehat\tau_{\sigma,\delta}\) is scalar bordered-row bookkeeping, and
\(\widehat\tau_{\sigma,\delta}/\delta\) is only its represented fold-time
scale; no nonlinear physical moving-event time has been constructed.
Indeed, \(\widetilde a_{a,\delta}\) need not solve the same nonlinear phase
action equation.  Hence the present weighted speed class, inner-anchored
relative inverse, strict speed sign, and an \(O(S_\delta)\) terminal window
do not imply a literal original-endpoint recut.  Such a theorem must instead
solve a new causal-entry plus terminal-event moving-duration boundary
problem, or impose an additional endpoint cancellation
\(|\widehat\tau|=O(\rho_\delta)\) together with the missing one-sided
attachment.

This is a scalar phase-core nonimplication result.  The source in
\cref{eq:endpoint-obstruction-source} is not asserted to be the residual of
a full RFDE tracker, and the proposition does not exclude additional
model-specific cancellation.  It excludes only the attempted deduction of
original-endpoint recutting from the scalar phase-core hypotheses and
estimates of
\cref{lem:inner-anchored-nonlinear-phase-chart} alone.
\end{proposition}

The obstruction is a fixed-outer-radius statement about an inner-anchored
reference chart.  It does not say that a scalar causal endpoint problem has
no solution.  For a given Eulerian speed, the correct phase is the
difference of two flight-time coordinates.  This gives an exact nonlinear
terminal row and also identifies a receding outer scale on which its
duration fits the \(O(S_\delta)\) endpoint window.

\begin{theorem}[Receding-collar causal flight time and terminal Schur row]
\label[theorem]{thm:receding-collar-causal-flight-time}
Retain the common same-sign scalar-speed bounds in
\cref{eq:anchored-phase-q-class}.  Fix \(C_Q>0\).  There is
\(0<\varkappa_{\rm ep}^*<1\), depending only on \(C_Q\) and the common
scalar collar bounds, with the following property.  For every fixed
\(0<\varkappa_{\rm ep}\le\varkappa_{\rm ep}^*\), there is
\(\delta_{\rm ep}(\varkappa_{\rm ep})>0\).  Whenever
\(0<\delta<\delta_{\rm ep}(\varkappa_{\rm ep})\), set
\begin{equation}
 r_{{\rm out},\delta}
 =\varkappa_{\rm ep}(\delta S_\delta^3)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \lambda_\delta^{\rm ep}
 =r_{{\rm out},\delta}+S_\delta^{-2},
 \qquad
 M_\delta^{\rm ep}
 =r_{{\rm out},\delta}^2+\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_\delta}.
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-scales}
\end{equation}
In this theorem \(r_{\rm out}=r_{{\rm out},\delta}\), and
\[
 (e_{a,0},e_{a,1})=(r_{{\rm out},\delta},\rho_\delta),
 \qquad
 (e_{r,0},e_{r,1})=(-\rho_\delta,-r_{{\rm out},\delta}).
\]
Write \(I_\sigma\) for the closed interval between these endpoints and put
\[
 m_\delta(r)=|r|+\frac{\varepsilon}{|r|^2}.
\]
Let \(\widehat Q_\sigma\in C(I_\sigma)\) be an
\emph{Eulerian} speed correction: its argument is the current physical
scalar coordinate, not a fixed-reference pullback.  Assume
\begin{equation}
 |\widehat Q_\sigma(r)|\le D_\delta m_\delta(r),
 \qquad
 0\le D_\delta\le C_Q\lambda_\delta^{\rm ep}.     \label{eq:receding-flight-source-class}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 q_\sigma(r)=q_0(r)+\widehat Q_\sigma(r)
 \le-\frac{q_-}{2}<0
\]
on \(I_\sigma\).  Define
\begin{align}
 \theta_\sigma(r)
 &=\int_{e_{\sigma,0}}^r\frac{\dd u}{q_\sigma(u)},
 &
 \theta_\sigma^0(r)
 &=\int_{e_{\sigma,0}}^r\frac{\dd u}{q_0(u)},\notag\\
 \beta_\sigma(r)
 &=\theta_\sigma^0(r)-\theta_\sigma(r),
 &
 \widehat\tau_\sigma
 &=\theta_\sigma(e_{\sigma,1})
       -\theta_\sigma^0(e_{\sigma,1}).            \label{eq:receding-flight-coordinates}
\end{align}
The pair \((\beta_\sigma,\widehat\tau_\sigma)\) is the unique solution of
the exact nonlinear bordered problem
\begin{align}
 \mathscr B_{\sigma}^{\rm tof}(\beta_\sigma)(r)
 &:=\frac{q_0(r)^2\beta_\sigma'(r)}
          {1-q_0(r)\beta_\sigma'(r)}
   =\widehat Q_\sigma(r),\notag\\
 \beta_\sigma(e_{\sigma,0})&=0,\qquad
 \beta_\sigma(e_{\sigma,1})
       +\widehat\tau_\sigma=0.                   \label{eq:receding-flight-bordered-problem}
\end{align}
In particular,
\begin{align}
 \beta_\sigma'(r)
 &=\frac{\widehat Q_\sigma(r)}
       {q_0(r)\{q_0(r)+\widehat Q_\sigma(r)\}},
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-beta-prime}\\
 \widehat\tau_\sigma
 &=\int_{e_{\sigma,1}}^{e_{\sigma,0}}
    \frac{\widehat Q_\sigma(r)}
       {q_0(r)\{q_0(r)+\widehat Q_\sigma(r)\}}\,\dd r.
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-duration}
\end{align}
Its exact source differential is
\begin{equation}
 D_{\widehat Q}\widehat\tau_\sigma[H]
 =\int_{e_{\sigma,1}}^{e_{\sigma,0}}
       \frac{H(r)}
        {\{q_0(r)+\widehat Q_\sigma(r)\}^2}\,\dd r.
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-duration-differential}
\end{equation}
The following estimates are uniform in \(N,\delta\), the branch, and
\(0<\varkappa_{\rm ep}\le\varkappa_{\rm ep}^*\):
\begin{align}
 \sup_{I_\sigma}
   \frac{|\beta_\sigma'(r)|}{m_\delta(r)}
 &\le CD_\delta,\notag\\
 \sup_{I_\sigma}\frac{|\beta_\sigma(r)|}{|r|}
   +\frac{|\widehat\tau_\sigma|}{\rho_\delta}
 &\le C C_Q\{\varkappa_{\rm ep}^2+S_\delta^{-4}\},
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-relative-bounds}\\
 \frac{|\widehat\tau_\sigma|}{\delta}
 &\le C C_Q\{\varkappa_{\rm ep}^2S_\delta
                         +S_\delta^{-3}\}.        \label{eq:receding-flight-fold-time-bound}
\end{align}

The nonlinear terminal row has a uniform action-weighted Schur inverse.
Put
\begin{align}
 \|h\|_{\mathsf H_{\sigma,{\rm ep}}}
 &=\frac1{\lambda_\delta^{\rm ep}}
   \sup_{r\in I_\sigma}\frac{|h(r)|}{m_\delta(r)},\notag\\
\|b\|_{\mathsf A_{\sigma,{\rm ep}}}
 &=\sup_{r\in I_\sigma}\frac{|b(r)|}{|r|}
   +\frac1{\lambda_\delta^{\rm ep}}
      \sup_{r\in I_\sigma}\frac{|b'(r)|}{m_\delta(r)}.
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-linear-norms}
\end{align}
The rational bulk map \(\mathscr B_\sigma^{\rm tof}\) is understood on the
connected component of zero in the open set
\begin{equation}
 \left\{\beta\in C^1(I_\sigma):
  \inf_{r\in I_\sigma}|1-q_0(r)\beta'(r)|>0\right\}.
                                                        \label{eq:receding-flight-rational-domain}
\end{equation}
On the source ball defined by the strict form of
\cref{eq:receding-flight-source-class}, the map
\[
 \widehat Q_\sigma\longmapsto
       (\beta_\sigma,\widehat\tau_\sigma)
\]
is real analytic from \(C(I_\sigma)\), with its \(C^0\) topology, into
\(\mathsf A_{\sigma,{\rm ep}}\times\mathbb R\).  At every such solution, the
differential of
\begin{equation}
 (\beta,\widehat\tau)\longmapsto
 \left(\mathscr B_\sigma^{\rm tof}(\beta),
       \beta(e_{\sigma,0}),
       \beta(e_{\sigma,1})+\widehat\tau\right)    \label{eq:receding-flight-bordered-map}
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism.  If its data are \((h,b_0,b_E)\) and its unknown
variation is \((b,\zeta)\), then
\begin{equation}
 \|b\|_{\mathsf A_{\sigma,{\rm ep}}}
       +\frac{|\zeta|}{\rho_\delta}
 \le C\left\{
   \|h\|_{\mathsf H_{\sigma,{\rm ep}}}
       +\frac{|b_0|+|b_E|}{\rho_\delta}\right\}.  \label{eq:receding-flight-bordered-inverse}
\end{equation}
The uniform assertion in
\cref{eq:receding-flight-bordered-inverse} concerns the inverse; no uniform
forward trace bound in these norms is claimed.

Finally, let
\[
 S_\sigma
 =\delta^{-1}\theta_\sigma(e_{\sigma,1})
 =S_\sigma^0+\frac{\widehat\tau_\sigma}{\delta},
 \qquad
 S_\sigma^0
 =\delta^{-1}\theta_\sigma^0(e_{\sigma,1}).
\]
The scalar initial-value problem
\begin{equation}
 r_\sigma'(s)=\delta q_\sigma(r_\sigma(s)),
 \qquad r_\sigma(0)=e_{\sigma,0},                \label{eq:receding-flight-physical-ivp}
\end{equation}
has a unique strictly decreasing solution on \([0,S_\sigma]\), and
\[
 r_\sigma(S_\sigma)=e_{\sigma,1}.
\]
Thus the prescribed scalar causal entry, the hit at the original
scalar-coordinate terminal endpoint, its exact duration, and the normalized
scalar terminal Schur row are closed on the receding collar.  This theorem
does not put the existing fixed-section
RFDE zeros on the diagonal family
\(r_{\rm out}=r_{{\rm out},\delta}\).  It also does not construct an
arbitrary-source normal inverse, a raw-compatible endpoint history, or a
past physical orbit.  Those are separate requirements for an
original-endpoint outer tracker and a complete-history connection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The exact endpoint moment is nonlinear]
\label[remark]{rem:exact-endpoint-flight-moment}
For a fixed \(r_{\rm out}\), strict negativity of
\(q_0+\widehat Q_\sigma\) already gives a unique scalar causal hit.  What
can fail is the normalized terminal window.  By
\cref{eq:receding-flight-duration}, the condition
\[
 |\widehat\tau_\sigma|\le C\rho_\delta
\]
is equivalent to the exact nonlinear flight-time moment bound
\begin{equation}
 \left|
 \int_{e_{\sigma,1}}^{e_{\sigma,0}}
 \frac{\widehat Q_\sigma(r)}
      {q_0(r)\{q_0(r)+\widehat Q_\sigma(r)\}}\,\dd r
 \right|
 \le C\rho_\delta.                               \label{eq:exact-endpoint-flight-moment}
\end{equation}
Cancelling only its first variation is insufficient, because the exact
identity
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\tau_\sigma
 =\int_{e_{\sigma,1}}^{e_{\sigma,0}}
       \frac{\widehat Q_\sigma}{q_0^2}\,\dd r
 -\int_{e_{\sigma,1}}^{e_{\sigma,0}}
       \frac{\widehat Q_\sigma^2}
        {q_0^2(q_0+\widehat Q_\sigma)}\,\dd r    \label{eq:exact-endpoint-flight-quadratic-remainder}
\end{equation}
has a sign-definite second term whenever the speed remains negative.  Thus
a sign-changing nonzero source can have zero linear moment while retaining
a nonzero quadratic duration.  The receding scale in
\cref{thm:receding-collar-causal-flight-time} controls the full nonlinear
moment, not merely its linearization.
\end{remark}

The RFDE phase row does not initially produce an Eulerian source.  It
produces a source pulled back to the fixed reduced coordinate.  The next
result closes this coordinate seam.  A terminal flow tube is indispensable:
the reference endpoint that maps to the fixed physical endpoint need not
belong to the original closed interval.

\begin{theorem}[Reference-pulled causal phase and moving endpoint]
\label[theorem]{thm:reference-pulled-causal-endpoint}
Retain \cref{thm:receding-collar-causal-flight-time} and write
\[
 R_\delta=r_{{\rm out},\delta},\qquad
 \lambda_\delta=\lambda_\delta^{\rm ep},\qquad
 M_\delta=M_\delta^{\rm ep}.
\]
Choose a fixed \(c_T>0\) so small that \(q_+c_T<1/4\), and put
\begin{equation}
 I_\sigma^\sharp
 =\operatorname{hull}\left(
 I_\sigma\cup
 \{\Phi_{q_0}^{t}(e_{\sigma,1}):|t|\le c_T\rho_\delta\}
 \right).                                      \label{eq:reference-pulled-terminal-tube}
\end{equation}
After decreasing the common same-sign collar, and then taking
\(\delta\) sufficiently small for each fixed
\(\varkappa_{\rm ep}>0\), one has
\begin{equation}
 I_\sigma^\sharp\Subset J_\sigma,\qquad
 |r|\ge\tfrac12\rho_\delta\quad(r\in I_\sigma^\sharp),
 \qquad
 \int_{I_\sigma^\sharp}m_\delta(r)\,\dd r\le CM_\delta.
                                                        \label{eq:reference-pulled-tube-geometry}
\end{equation}

Fix \(C_Q>0\).  There is
\(0<\varkappa_{\rm rp}^*\le\varkappa_{\rm ep}^*\) such that, whenever
\(0<\varkappa_{\rm ep}\le\varkappa_{\rm rp}^*\), then for sufficiently
small \(\delta\), every
\(Q_{\sigma}^{\rm ref}\in C(I_\sigma^\sharp)\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
 |Q_{\sigma}^{\rm ref}(r)|\le D_\delta m_\delta(r),
 \qquad 0\le D_\delta\le C_Q\lambda_\delta             \label{eq:reference-pulled-source-class}
\end{equation}
determines a unique pair
\begin{equation*}
 (a_\sigma,\tau_\sigma)\in
 C^1(I_\sigma^\sharp)\times
 (-c_T\rho_\delta,c_T\rho_\delta)
\end{equation*}
in the small component of zero that solves
\begin{align}
 q_0(r)q_0(\Theta_{a_\sigma}(r))a_\sigma'(r)
 &=Q_{\sigma}^{\rm ref}(r),
 &a_\sigma(e_{\sigma,0})&=0,                   \label{eq:reference-pulled-causal-phase}\\
 a_\sigma(r_{\sigma,\tau})+\tau_\sigma&=0,
 &r_{\sigma,\tau}
 &=\Phi_{q_0}^{\tau_\sigma}(e_{\sigma,1}),     \label{eq:reference-pulled-terminal-row}
\end{align}
where
\(\Theta_a(r)=\Phi_{q_0}^{a(r)}(r)\).  It satisfies
\begin{align}
 \sup_{I_\sigma^\sharp}
       \frac{|a_\sigma'(r)|}{m_\delta(r)}
 &\le CD_\delta,\notag\\
 \frac{\|a_\sigma\|_\infty+|\tau_\sigma|}{\rho_\delta}
 &\le C D_\delta\frac{M_\delta}{\rho_\delta}
 \le CC_Q\{\varkappa_{\rm ep}^2+S_\delta^{-4}\},
                                                        \label{eq:reference-pulled-relative-bound}\\
 \frac{|\tau_\sigma|}{\delta}
 &\le CC_Q\{\varkappa_{\rm ep}^2S_\delta+S_\delta^{-3}\}.
                                                        \label{eq:reference-pulled-fold-time-bound}
\end{align}

This reference-pulled problem is exactly conjugate to the Eulerian
flight-time problem.  On the image of \(\Theta_{a_\sigma}\), define
\begin{equation}
 Q_\sigma^E(R)
 =Q_\sigma^{\rm ref}(\Theta_{a_\sigma}^{-1}(R)),
 \qquad q_\sigma^E(R)=q_0(R)+Q_\sigma^E(R).       \label{eq:reference-pulled-Eulerian-source}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 q_\sigma^E(\Theta_{a_\sigma}(r))
 =q_0(r)\Theta_{a_\sigma}'(r).                  \label{eq:reference-pulled-speed-conjugacy}
\end{equation}
If
\begin{align*}
 \theta_0(r)&=\int_{e_{\sigma,0}}^r\frac{\dd u}{q_0(u)},&
 \theta_E(R)&=\int_{e_{\sigma,0}}^R\frac{\dd U}{q_\sigma^E(U)},
\end{align*}
then
\begin{align}
 \theta_E(\Theta_{a_\sigma}(r))&=\theta_0(r),&
 \theta_0(\Theta_{a_\sigma}(r))-\theta_0(r)&=a_\sigma(r).
                                                        \label{eq:reference-pulled-flight-conjugacy}
\end{align}
Consequently the Eulerian flight defect
\(\beta_\sigma=\theta_0-\theta_E\) obeys
\begin{equation}
 \beta_\sigma(\Theta_{a_\sigma}(r))=a_\sigma(r),
 \qquad
 \beta_\sigma(e_{\sigma,1})+\tau_\sigma=0.      \label{eq:reference-pulled-beta-conjugacy}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 R_\sigma(s)=\Theta_{a_\sigma}
   (\Phi_{q_0}^{\delta s}(e_{\sigma,0}))         \label{eq:reference-pulled-physical-path}
\end{equation}
solves
\(R_\sigma'=\delta q_\sigma^E(R_\sigma)\), starts at
\(e_{\sigma,0}\), and reaches \(e_{\sigma,1}\) at
\begin{equation}
 S_\sigma=S_\sigma^0+\frac{\tau_\sigma}{\delta}.          \label{eq:reference-pulled-duration}
\end{equation}

The nonlinear bordered differential has a uniform inverse in normalized
causal--endpoint norms.  Define
\begin{align}
 \|k\|_{\mathsf H_{\sigma,{\rm ce}}}
 &=\frac1{\lambda_\delta}
      \sup_{I_\sigma^\sharp}\frac{|k(r)|}{m_\delta(r)},\notag\\
 \|h\|_{\mathsf A_{\sigma,{\rm ce}}}
 &=\frac{\|h\|_\infty}{\rho_\delta}
   +\frac1{\lambda_\delta}
      \sup_{I_\sigma^\sharp}\frac{|h'(r)|}{m_\delta(r)}.
                                                        \label{eq:reference-pulled-linear-norms}
\end{align}
At a solution, put
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak F_\sigma(a,\tau)
 =\left(q_0q_0(\Theta_a)a',\ a(e_{\sigma,0}),
    a(\Phi_{q_0}^{\tau}(e_{\sigma,1}))+\tau\right).       \label{eq:reference-pulled-bordered-map}
\end{equation}
Then \(D\mathfrak F_\sigma(a_\sigma,\tau_\sigma)\) is an isomorphism.
For data \((k,b_0,b_E)\) and inverse image \((h,\zeta)\),
\begin{equation}
 \|h\|_{\mathsf A_{\sigma,{\rm ce}}}
      +\frac{|\zeta|}{\rho_\delta}
 \le C\left\{\|k\|_{\mathsf H_{\sigma,{\rm ce}}}
      +\frac{|b_0|+|b_E|}{\rho_\delta}\right\}.           \label{eq:reference-pulled-bordered-inverse}
\end{equation}
Its exact terminal coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 c_{\sigma,{\rm ter}}
 =1+q_0(r_{\sigma,\tau})a_\sigma'(r_{\sigma,\tau})
 =\frac{q_\sigma^E(e_{\sigma,1})}{q_0(e_{\sigma,1})},
 \qquad \frac12\le c_{\sigma,{\rm ter}}\le\frac32.       \label{eq:reference-pulled-terminal-coefficient}
\end{equation}

Thus a fixed-reference source, a causal entry, the shifted reference
endpoint, and the original physical endpoint are now one exact scalar
object.  The theorem does not construct the RFDE moving-duration column.
In the full phase--normal border that column changes
\cref{eq:reference-pulled-terminal-coefficient} by an off-diagonal Schur
term; the scalar coefficient above cannot be substituted for that still
unproved RFDE estimate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Inner-anchored phase Schur inverse]
\label[proposition]{prop:inner-anchored-relative-phase-schur}
Retain
\cref{thm:raw-compatible-phase-normal-inverse,%
lem:inner-anchored-nonlinear-phase-chart} and the spaces in
\cref{eq:relative-phase-spaces}.  On
\(\mathsf H_{\phi,\infty}^0(I_\sigma)\times\R\), use the
inner-row data norm
\begin{equation}
 \|(h,b)\|_{\phi,*}
 =\|h\|_{\phi,\infty}+\frac{|b|}{\rho_\delta}.     \label{eq:relative-phase-data-norm}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma,*}^0a
 =\bigl(q_0^2a',a(e_{\sigma,*})\bigr)              \label{eq:inner-anchored-phase-linear-operator}
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism
\[
 \mathsf A_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}^1(I_\sigma)
 \longrightarrow
 \mathsf H_{\phi,\infty}^0(I_\sigma)\times\R
\]
with a dimension-uniform inverse.
The scalar \(b\) is included to make the linear map onto; the nonlinear
anchored chart in \cref{eq:anchored-phase-IVP} uses the slice \(b=0\).

The fixed-base linear raw-compatible true-speed action from
\cref{eq:raw-compatible-true-action}, restricted to the relative space,
satisfies the stronger pointwise estimate
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm rc}a\|_{\phi,\infty}
 \le C r_{\rm out}\left\{
        r_{\rm out}+\frac1{S_\delta^2}
                    +\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\right\}
       \|a\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}.                  \label{eq:relative-raw-action-smallness}
\end{equation}
Consequently, after first decreasing \(r_{\rm out}\) and then
\(\delta_0\),
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr S_{\sigma,*}^{\rm rc}
 =\mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma,*}^0
       -(\mathfrak A_\sigma^{\rm rc},0)            \label{eq:inner-anchored-relative-schur}
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism between the same spaces, with dimension-uniform inverse.

Put
\[
 \mathfrak D_\sigma^\infty y=Q_{\rm n}[y]
       \in\mathsf H_{\phi,\infty}^0(I_\sigma).
\]
For
\(f\in\widetilde{\mathsf Y}_{\sigma,0}\) and
\(g\in\mathsf H_{\phi,\infty}^0(I_\sigma)\times\R\), the coupled problem
\begin{align}
 \mathscr N_\sigma\{x-T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a\}&=f,\notag\\
 \mathscr B_{\phi,\sigma,*}^0a
  -\bigl(\mathfrak D_\sigma^\infty x
       +\mathscr P_{c,\sigma,N}a,0\bigr)&=g       \label{eq:inner-anchored-coupled-block}
\end{align}
has the unique solution
\begin{align}
 y&=G_\sigma f,\notag\\
 a&=(\mathscr S_{\sigma,*}^{\rm rc})^{-1}
       \{(\mathfrak D_\sigma^\infty y,0)+g\},\notag\\
 x&=T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a+y.                         \label{eq:inner-anchored-schur-inverse}
\end{align}
If
\begin{align}
 \mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{*,\rm rc}
 &=\{(x,a):a\in\mathsf A_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}^1,\
       x-T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a\in\mathcal D(\mathscr N_\sigma)\},\notag\\
 \|(x,a)\|_{\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{*,\rm rc}}
 &=\|x-T_\sigma^{\rm rc}a\|_{\mathcal D(\mathscr N_\sigma)}
       +\|a\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}},                 \label{eq:inner-anchored-graph-norm}
\end{align}
then this is a Banach graph space and
\begin{equation}
 \|(x,a)\|_{\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{*,\rm rc}}
 \le C\left\{\|f\|_{\widetilde{\mathsf Y}_{\sigma,0}}
                    +\|g\|_{\phi,*}\right\}.       \label{eq:inner-anchored-schur-bound}
\end{equation}

On the attracting branch the inner anchor does not set the outer entry
value \(a(r_{\rm out})\) to zero; rather
\(|a(r_{\rm out})|\le r_{\rm out}\|a\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}\), which is
the extra small factor in the raw collar term of
\cref{eq:relative-raw-action-smallness}.  This proposition fixes the
fold-side phase and removes the constant-mode obstruction at the scalar
phase/fixed-base linear level.  It introduces no nonlinear normal residual
or moving terminal event and proves no nonlinear tracker or physical
complete-history connection.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Exact nonlinear reduced-flow phase-delay defect]
\label[lemma]{lem:nonlinear-reduced-flow-phase-delay}

\cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton,%
prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
lem:reduced-flow-phase-defect,%

and the common layer-sum bounds.  Use the \(C^3\) critical-curve bounds and

 |q_0'(r)|\le C,\qquad |r|\,|q_0''(r)|\le C.       \label{eq:nonlinear-phase-delay-two-jets}

\(\bar r_\sigma:[-\theta_m,S_\sigma]\to J_\sigma\) be the backward
one-delay \(q_0\)-extension of the reduced path, so that

 \partial_s\bar r_\sigma=\delta q_0(\bar r_\sigma),
 \qquad e_{\sigma,0}=\bar r_\sigma(0).

 \widehat a_\sigma(s)

 \begin{cases}

 \Sigma_\sigma

 \|F\|_{\mathrm{pw},1}

 \Pi_{\sigma,N}(0)=0,\qquad

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\Pi,\sigma,N}(a)

\end{equation}

 \|\Pi_{\sigma,N}(a)\|_{\mathrm{pw},1}

\begin{lemma}[Second-jet raw-compatible history assembler]
\label[lemma]{lem:second-jet-raw-history-assembler}
Retain
\cref{prop:compatible-endpoint-jet-collar,%
lem:raw-compatible-moving-core-collar,%
lem:raw-compatible-base-collar-refinement,%

Choose \(\chi>0\) so small that
\begin{equation}
 2q_+\chi<\min\{4\chi_0,1/2\},                   \label{eq:raw-assembler-relative-width}
\end{equation}
and, for \(\sigma\in\{a,r\}\), put
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde I_{\sigma,\chi}
 =\overline{\{e:\ \text{there is }r\in I_\sigma
                    \text{ with }|e-r|<2q_+\chi|r|\}}
 \Subset J_\sigma .                              \label{eq:raw-assembler-enlarged-collar}
\end{equation}
After decreasing \(\delta_0\), the finite collar construction in the three
results above, including its critical, backtrack, and compatibility-defect
estimates, extends to these same-sign relative endpoint collars.  For the
voltage and resource compatibility defects \(b_{j,e}\) and \(d_{j,e}\)
on that extension, one has
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\le j\le h}\delta^j
 \left\{\|\mathcal L_e^\ell b_{j,e}\|_N
             +|\mathcal L_e^\ell d_{j,e}|\right\}
 \le C_h\varepsilon |e|^{1-\ell},
 \qquad \ell=0,1,2,                              \label{eq:second-endpoint-collar-jets}
\end{equation}
uniformly for \(e\in\widetilde I_{a,\chi}\cup
\widetilde I_{r,\chi}\), where
\(\mathcal L_e=q_0(e)\partial_e\).  Thus, if
\(\Delta\phi_e=\phi_e^{\rm rc}-\phi_e^0\), then for every reference
\(e_*\in I_\sigma\) and every \(e\) in its relative collar with
\(|e|\asymp|e_*|\), in the fixed-reference cap norm introduced below,
\begin{equation}
 \|\Delta\phi_e\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}
 +|e|\,\|\mathcal L_e\Delta\phi_e\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}
 +|e|^2\,\|\mathcal L_e^2\Delta\phi_e\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}
 \le C_h\varepsilon|e|.                           \label{eq:second-endpoint-collar-cap}
\end{equation}

More precisely, fix \(e_*\in I_\sigma\) and put
\[
 e_c=\Phi_{q_0}^{c}(e_*),\qquad |c|\le\chi|e_*|.
\]
The whole tube
\begin{equation}
 \left\{\Phi_{q_0}^{t}(e_*),\
   \Phi_{q_0}^{-\tau}(\Phi_{q_0}^{t}(e_*)):
   |t|\le\chi|e_*|,\ 0\le\tau\le\delta\theta_m\right\}
                                                        \label{eq:raw-assembler-flow-backtrack-tube}
\end{equation}
lies in \(\widetilde I_{\sigma,\chi}\), and
\begin{equation}
 \frac34|e_*|\le|\Phi_{q_0}^{t}(e_*)|
       \le\frac54|e_*|,
 \qquad |t|\le\chi|e_*|.                        \label{eq:raw-assembler-relative-flow-bound}
\end{equation}
This includes the cases \(e_*=\pm\rho_\delta\) and
\(e_*=\pm r_{\rm out}\); the shifted endpoint need not remain in the
original closed interval.

All cap norms in the rest of the statement use the single reference weight
\(\kappa_{e_*}\), not the moving weight \(\kappa_{e_c}\).  For
\[
 U=(Z,p,\zeta)\in
 E_N\times\mathbb R\times\mathscr K_{N,\delta}^h
\]
set
\begin{equation}
 \|U\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}
 =\|Z\|_N+\frac{|p|}{|e_*|}
       +\|\zeta\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}.            \label{eq:nonlinear-collar-data-norm}
\end{equation}
The finite triangular recursion defining \(\Gamma_{e_c}^h\) has a unique
extension to an open, dimension-uniform ball
\(\|U\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}<r_h\) in this fixed-reference norm;
write its value at the displayed affine-core data as
\(\Gamma_{e_c;e_*}^{h,\mathrm{fr}}(U;\nu,\eta)\).  This extension is
defined directly by the compatibility recursion, not by comparing the two
fading weights, and it agrees with \(\Gamma_{e_c}^h\) wherever both are
defined.  On that open ball define the exact centered voltage-history
assembler
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)
 =\operatorname{pr}_\phi
   \Gamma_{e_c;e_*}^{h,\mathrm{fr}}(U;\nu,\eta)
       -\phi_{e_c}^0.                             \label{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-assembler}
\end{equation}
It is \(C^2\) into the fixed-reference \(C^h\) history space; the
anisotropic derivatives needed below have dimension-uniform bounds.  At
its base point,
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(0,0)=\Delta\phi_{e_*},
 \qquad
 \partial_c\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(0,0)
       =\gamma_{e_*}^{\rm ph}.                    \label{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-first-jets}
\end{equation}
There is \(0<r_h'<r_h\), dimension-uniform, such that for
\(|c|\le\chi|e_*|\), \(\|U\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}\le r_h'\), and data
directions \(H,H_1,H_2\),
\begin{align}
 \|\partial_c^2\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,0)
       \|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}
 &\le C_h\frac{\varepsilon}{|e_*|},\notag\\
 \|\partial_cD_U\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)H
       \|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}
 &\le \frac{C_h}{|e_*|}\|H\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*},\notag\\
 \|D_U^2\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)[H_1,H_2]
       \|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}
 &\le C_h\prod_{i=1}^2\|H_i\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}. \label{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-C2-bounds}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 &\|\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)
      -\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(0,0)
      -c\gamma_{e_*}^{\rm ph}
      -D_U\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(0,0)U
        \|_{\mathrm{cap},h,e_*}\notag\\
 &\qquad\le C_h\left[
   \varepsilon|e_*|
       \left(\frac{|c|}{|e_*|}\right)^2
 +\frac{|c|}{|e_*|}\|U\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}
  +\|U\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}^2\right].             \label{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-taylor}
\end{align}
Here and below the remainder is exactly the four-term difference
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Rem}\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)
 :={}&\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)
      -\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(0,0)
      -c\gamma_{e_*}^{\rm ph}\notag\\
 &{}-D_U\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(0,0)U.          \label{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-remainder-definition}
\end{align}
In particular, if
\(\|U\|_{\mathrm{col},e_*}\le\varepsilon R\le r_h'\), then
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon^{-1}
 \|\operatorname{Rem}\mathscr H_{e_*}^{\rm rc}(c,U)
       \|_{N,e_*,\delta}^{\sharp}
 \le C_h\left(\frac{|c|}{|e_*|}+R\right)^2.       \label{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-graph-remainder}
\end{equation}
The full moving formal collar is subtracted in
\cref{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-assembler}; subtracting only its tangent
would not give
\cref{eq:nonlinear-raw-history-graph-remainder}.  The construction uses a
fixed \(\theta\)-history chart and never translates an arbitrary
\(C^0\) history.  It constructs an order-\(h\) compatible old-history
assembler, not a past orbit, a nonlinear tracker, or a physical connection.
\end{lemma}

 \mathscr J_r g_r
 &=\left(
  D_U\mathscr H_{-\rho_\delta}^{\rm rc}(0,0)
       (\beta_r,0,\widehat\gamma_r),
       b_r,0,0;\,0,0\right).                     \label{eq:raw-gauge-linear-injections}
\end{align}
The semicolon separates the normal and phase data.  There are uniform
constants \(g_*,\lambda_g,C_g>0\) and
\(0<R_{\rm G}\le R_{\rm u}\) such that, whenever
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\delta+\|g_\sigma\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}
 <\lambda_g,
 \qquad
 \|g_\sigma\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}<g_*,          \label{eq:raw-gauge-smallness}
\end{equation}
the residual \(\mathcal F_\sigma^{g_\sigma}\) has a unique zero
\begin{equation}
 u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g_\sigma}
 \in\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rga}
 \quad\hbox{in the open \(R_{\rm G}\)-ball}.     \label{eq:raw-gauge-zero}
\end{equation}
It satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g_\sigma}\|_
       {\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rga}}
 &\le C_g\left(\lambda_\delta
       +\|g_\sigma\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}\right),
                                                        \label{eq:raw-gauge-zero-size}\\
 \left\|u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g_\sigma}
     +L_\sigma^{-1}\mathcal F_\sigma(0,0)
     -L_\sigma^{-1}\mathscr J_\sigma g_\sigma\right\|
 &\le C_g\left(\lambda_\delta
       +\|g_\sigma\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}\right)^2,
                                                        \label{eq:raw-gauge-first-jet}\\
 \bigl\|[D_u\mathcal F_\sigma^{g_\sigma}
       (u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g_\sigma})]^{-1}\bigr\|
 &\le2M.                                           \label{eq:raw-gauge-zero-inverse}
\end{align}
On a smaller common gauge ball the zero is \(C^1\) in \(g_\sigma\), with
\begin{equation}
 D_g u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g}
 =-[D_u\mathcal F_\sigma^g(u_{\sigma,\delta}^g)]^{-1}
       D_g\mathcal F_\sigma^g(u_{\sigma,\delta}^g),
 \qquad
 \|D_g u_{\sigma,\delta}^g\|\le C_g,           \label{eq:raw-gauge-zero-derivative}
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
 \|u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g_1}-u_{\sigma,\delta}^{g_2}\|
 \le C_g\|g_1-g_2\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}.       \label{eq:raw-gauge-zero-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
The zero at \(g_\sigma=0\) is the canonical zero in
\cref{cor:fixed-section-canonical-zero}.

At a gauge zero, the exact boundary identities are
\begin{align}
 \gamma_x&=\mathscr H_{a,g_a}^{\rm raw}(u),&
 Z_x(0)&=\beta_a,&p_x(0)&=b_a &&(\sigma=a),\notag\\
 \gamma_x&=\mathscr H_{r,g_r}^{\rm raw}(u),&
 Z_x(0)&=\beta_r,&p_x(S_r)&=b_r &&(\sigma=r).     \label{eq:raw-gauge-exact-boundary-identities}
\end{align}
They therefore reconstruct exact monotone forward solution segments of the
unmodified RFDE, with the strict speed bound in
\cref{eq:fixed-section-zero-speed-sign}, uniformly on the gauge ball.
This proves a nonempty, infinite-dimensional gauge-indexed class of
raw-compatible finite-branch representatives.  It does not prove that a gauge history is
cut from a past orbit, compare two gauges after a physical endpoint recut,
construct a parameterized moving-event family, or produce a stable sheet,
connection, or root.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Generated-interior raw-gauge tracker representatives]
\label[corollary]{cor:generated-interior-canonical-tracker}
Retain \cref{thm:raw-gauge-fixed-section-zero}.  At any one of its
gauge-indexed zeros,
write its phase component as \(a_{\sigma,\delta}\), and
write the reconstructed forward solution as
\[
 v_\sigma(s),\quad -\theta_m\le s\le S_\sigma,
 \qquad
 w_\sigma(s),\quad 0\le s\le S_\sigma,
\]
in fold time, and put
\[
 r_\sigma(s)=\pi_N^T\{v_\sigma(s)-\mathbf1\}
 =\Theta_{a_{\sigma,\delta}}(\bar r_\sigma(s)).
\]
After decreasing \(\delta_0(r_{\rm out})\) once more,
\(S_\sigma>2\theta_m\).  Choose
\begin{equation}
 2\theta_m<s_0<s_1\le S_\sigma,\qquad
 J_\sigma^{\rm gen}=r_\sigma([s_0-\theta_m,s_1]),
 \qquad I_\sigma^{\rm gen}=r_\sigma([s_0,s_1]).   \label{eq:generated-interior-intervals}
\end{equation}
Then \(r_\sigma\) is a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism from
\([s_0-\theta_m,s_1]\) onto \(J_\sigma^{\rm gen}\).  If
\(s_\sigma=(r_\sigma|_{[s_0-\theta_m,s_1]})^{-1}\), define
\begin{equation}
 V_\sigma(r)=v_\sigma(s_\sigma(r)),\qquad
 W_\sigma(r)=w_\sigma(s_\sigma(r)),\qquad
 q_\sigma(r)=\delta^{-1}r_\sigma'(s_\sigma(r)).   \label{eq:generated-interior-tracker}
\end{equation}
There are uniform constants \(c_q,C_q,c_w,C_w,C_V>0\) such that
\begin{align}
 -C_q\le q_\sigma(r)&\le-c_q<0,
       &&r\in J_\sigma^{\rm gen},                 \label{eq:generated-interior-q-bounds}\\
 c_w|r|\le|W_\sigma'(r)|&\le C_w|r|,
       &&r\in J_\sigma^{\rm gen},                \label{eq:generated-interior-wprime-bounds}\\
 \|V_\sigma'(r)\|_N&\le C_V,
       &&r\in J_\sigma^{\rm gen}.                \label{eq:generated-interior-Vprime-bound}
\end{align}
For every \(r\in I_\sigma^{\rm gen}\) and every delay atom,
\begin{equation}
 r_{\sigma,k}(r)
 :=r_\sigma(s_\sigma(r)-\theta_k)
 =\Phi_{q_\sigma}^{-\delta\theta_k}(r)
 \in J_\sigma^{\rm gen}.                         \label{eq:generated-interior-backtracks}
\end{equation}
The triple \((V_\sigma,W_\sigma,q_\sigma)\) exactly satisfies
\cref{eq:exact-outer-voltage-invariance,eq:exact-outer-resource-invariance}
on \(I_\sigma^{\rm gen}\), with the backtracks in
\cref{eq:generated-interior-backtracks}.  Its complete-history embedding is
not a formal collar: it is exactly the generated forward window
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{V_\sigma,W_\sigma,q_\sigma}(r)
 =\left(
   \theta\longmapsto v_\sigma(s_\sigma(r)+\theta),\;
   w_\sigma(s_\sigma(r))
   \right).                                      \label{eq:generated-interior-history-window}
\end{equation}
Consequently the stationary-coordinate quotient in
\cref{prop:physical-history-phase-quotient} and the exact resource-gauge
normal equation in \cref{prop:exact-resource-gauge-quotient} apply on this
generated interior.  In particular, the latter has its exact Volterra
realization and
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal K_s\|\le \frac{C}{S_\delta},\qquad
 \|(\Id-\mathcal K_s)^{-1}\|\le2.                \label{eq:generated-interior-volterra-bound}
\end{equation}

This corollary constructs an exact generated-interior tracker
representative carried by every zero in the raw-gauge ball.  The preceding
theorem proves Lipschitz dependence of the fixed-section zeros on the gauge;
this corollary makes no common-chart comparison of the reparameterized
trackers.  It proves existence, not flat forgetting or raw-gauge independence.
The result does not prescribe both physical branch endpoints,
construct a moving-event or parameter family, extend the initial collar to
a past-complete orbit, produce a repelling stable sheet, or prove a physical
connection or root.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Common generated buffer and fixed physical-section hit]
\label[proposition]{prop:raw-gauge-physical-hit}
Retain
\cref{thm:raw-gauge-fixed-section-zero,%
cor:generated-interior-canonical-tracker}
at order \(h\ge3\), pointwise at one fixed admissible parameter pair.  Put
\begin{equation}
 r_h=\frac12r_{\rm out},\qquad
 e_{a,h}=r_h,\qquad e_{r,h}=-r_h,                 \label{eq:physical-hit-sections}
\end{equation}
and fix \(0<\kappa_h<1/8\).  After decreasing \(\delta_0\), assume
\(\rho_\delta<r_{\rm out}/4\).  Define the reduced bracket times by
\begin{equation}
 \bar r_\sigma(T_{\sigma,-})=e_{\sigma,h}+\kappa_hr_h,
 \qquad
 \bar r_\sigma(T_{\sigma,+})=e_{\sigma,h}-\kappa_hr_h.
                                                        \label{eq:physical-hit-reference-bracket}
\end{equation}
Then \(T_{\sigma,-}<T_{\sigma,+}\).  After one further decrease of
\(\delta_0\),
\begin{equation}
 3\theta_m<T_{\sigma,-}<T_{\sigma,+}<S_\sigma,
 \qquad
 [T_{\sigma,-}-\theta_m,T_{\sigma,+}]
 \quad\hbox{is generated interior}.              \label{eq:physical-hit-common-buffer}
\end{equation}

There are uniform \(g_h,\lambda_h,C_h>0\) such that, whenever
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\delta<\lambda_h,
 \qquad
 \|g_\sigma\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}<g_h,          \label{eq:physical-hit-gauge-ball}
\end{equation}
the raw-gauge zero gives the complete physical history
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(s)
 =\left(\theta\longmapsto v_{\sigma,g}(s+\theta),
                         w_{\sigma,g}(s)\right)\in\mathscr Y_N,
 \qquad s\in[T_{\sigma,-},T_{\sigma,+}].          \label{eq:physical-hit-history-family}
\end{equation}
The map \((s,g)\mapsto\mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(s)\) is jointly \(C^1\)
into \(\mathscr Y_N\), and
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{s,g}\left\{
  \|D_g\mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(s)\|+
  \|\tau_{\sigma,g}(s)\|_{\mathscr Y_N}\right\}\le C_h, \label{eq:physical-hit-uniform-history-jets}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\sigma,g}(s)
 =\frac1{r_{\sigma,g}'(s)}
   \left(\theta\longmapsto v_{\sigma,g}'(s+\theta),
                         w_{\sigma,g}'(s)\right),
 \qquad \varrho_N\tau_{\sigma,g}(s)=1.            \label{eq:physical-hit-normalized-tangent}
\end{equation}
The first norm in \cref{eq:physical-hit-uniform-history-jets} is the
operator norm from the normalized gauge space to the ordinary product norm
on \(\mathscr Y_N\).  Thus this is a dimension-uniform weak complete-history
buffer estimate, not a \(C^2\) strong-buffer estimate.

For every gauge in \cref{eq:physical-hit-gauge-ball} there is a unique
selected hit
\begin{equation}
 S_{\sigma,h}(g)\in(T_{\sigma,-},T_{\sigma,+}),
 \qquad r_{\sigma,g}(S_{\sigma,h}(g))=e_{\sigma,h}.           \label{eq:physical-hit-time}
\end{equation}
It is the only hit of this section on the monotone forward branch segment.
The event time, its natural scaled displacement, and the complete-history
hit
\begin{align}
 \widehat\tau_{\sigma,h}(g)
 &=\delta\{S_{\sigma,h}(g)-S_{\sigma,h}(0)\},\notag\\
 \mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g)
 &=\mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(S_{\sigma,h}(g))
   \in\Sigma_{N,e_{\sigma,h}}.                    \label{eq:physical-hit-map}
\end{align}
are \(C^1\) in \(g\).  Here
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_{N,e}=\{Y\in\mathscr Y_N:\varrho_NY=1+e\}.          \label{eq:physical-hit-section}
\end{equation}
For a gauge direction \(k\), all fixed-time
derivatives below are evaluated at \(s=S_{\sigma,h}(g)\), and
\begin{align}
 DS_{\sigma,h}(g)k
 &=-\frac{\varrho_ND_g\mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(s)k}
          {r_{\sigma,g}'(s)},\notag\\
 D\widehat\tau_{\sigma,h}(g)k
 &=-\frac{\varrho_ND_g\mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(s)k}
          {q_{\sigma,g}(e_{\sigma,h})},           \label{eq:physical-hit-time-derivative}\\
 D\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g)k
 &=\bigl(\Id-\tau_{\sigma,g}(s)\varrho_N\bigr)
       D_g\mathcal Z_{\sigma,g}(s)k.              \label{eq:physical-hit-history-derivative}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \|D\widehat\tau_{\sigma,h}(g)\|
 +\|D\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g)\|\le C_h,          \label{eq:physical-hit-map-uniform-bound}
\end{equation}
whereas the unscaled derivative \(DS_{\sigma,h}\) may lose the sharp
factor \(\delta^{-1}\).  The projection in
\cref{eq:physical-hit-history-derivative} is exactly the
stationary-coordinate phase quotient in
\cref{eq:physical-quotient-process} at the hit.

This proposition constructs a common weak generated buffer and a fixed
interior physical-section hit map for the fixed-parameter gauge family.  It
does not construct the \(C^2\) strong terminal buffer required by
\cref{lem:terminal-relative-trace-scale-chart}, recut either outer branch
endpoint, prove gauge flatness, produce parameter jets or a repelling sheet,
or construct a physical connection or root.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Common strong generated buffer at the physical hit]
\label[proposition]{prop:raw-gauge-strong-hit-buffer}
Retain \cref{prop:raw-gauge-physical-hit} at order \(h\ge3\), still
pointwise at one fixed admissible parameter pair, and put
\begin{equation}
 B_{\sigma,h}
 =[T_{\sigma,-}-\theta_m,T_{\sigma,+}],\qquad
 Y_{\sigma,g}=(r_{\sigma,g},Z_{\sigma,g},p_{\sigma,g}).
                                                        \label{eq:strong-hit-fixed-buffer}
\end{equation}
For \(Y=(r,Z,p)\in C^3(B_{\sigma,h})\), define
\begin{equation}
 \|Y\|_{\mathfrak S_{\sigma,h}^3}
 =\max_{0\le j\le3}\delta^j
   \sup_{s\in B_{\sigma,h}}
   \left\{
    \frac{|\partial_s^jr(s)|}{r_h}
   +\frac{\|\partial_s^jZ(s)\|_N}{\varepsilon}
   +\frac{|\partial_s^jp(s)|}{\varepsilon r_h}
   \right\}.                                    \label{eq:strong-hit-buffer-norm}
\end{equation}
After decreasing the common gauge ball and
\(\delta_0(r_{\rm out})\), there are uniform constants
\(c_{\rm buf},C_{\rm buf}>0\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \min\{S_{\sigma,h}(g)-(T_{\sigma,-}-\theta_m),
          T_{\sigma,+}-S_{\sigma,h}(g)\}
 \ge \frac{c_{\rm buf}}{\delta}                 \label{eq:strong-hit-time-margin}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 g\longmapsto Y_{\sigma,g}|_{B_{\sigma,h}}
 \quad\hbox{is \(C^1\) from \(\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h\)
 into \(\mathfrak S_{\sigma,h}^3\)},             \label{eq:strong-hit-C1-map}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 \sup_g\left\{
  \|Y_{\sigma,g}\|_{\mathfrak S_{\sigma,h}^3}
  +\|D_gY_{\sigma,g}\|_{\mathcal L(
       \mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h,
       \mathfrak S_{\sigma,h}^3)}
       \right\}\le C_{\rm buf}.                \label{eq:strong-hit-C1-bound}
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of \(N\), \(\delta\), the branch, and the
fixed parameter point in the retained structural ball.

More explicitly, let \(L_\delta,\ell_\delta\ge0\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \delta\{L_\delta+\theta_m+\ell_\delta\}
 \le\frac12c_{\rm buf},                         \label{eq:strong-hit-window-budget}
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}
 K_\delta=[-L_\delta-\theta_m,0],\qquad
 K_\delta^{\ell}
 =[-L_\delta-\theta_m-\ell_\delta,\ell_\delta].
                                                        \label{eq:strong-hit-moving-windows}
\end{equation}
If \(S_{\sigma,h}^0=S_{\sigma,h}(0)\), then the fixed-reference buffer
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Y_{\sigma,h}(g)(\varsigma)
 =Y_{\sigma,g}(S_{\sigma,h}^0+\varsigma),\qquad
 \varsigma\in K_\delta^{\ell},                 \label{eq:strong-hit-reference-buffer}
\end{equation}
is \(C^1\) in \(g\) into the scaled \(C^3\) space.  If, in addition,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_g|S_{\sigma,h}(g)-S_{\sigma,h}^0|
 <\frac12\ell_\delta,                            \label{eq:strong-hit-chart-shift-budget}
\end{equation}
then every translated window sampled by
\(\widehat\tau_{\sigma,h}(g)\) stays in the same generated buffer.
Consequently \cref{lem:terminal-relative-trace-scale-chart}, with
\(d=1\), applies to
\((\mathcal Y_{\sigma,h}(g),\widehat\tau_{\sigma,h}(g))\).
It produces a \(C^1\) family of event-aligned \(C^1\) retained traces in
the fixed physical section.  For a product trace \(X=(x,z,p)\) on
\(K_\delta\), write
\begin{equation*}
 \|X\|_{C^1_{\delta,\mathrm{ord}}(K_\delta)}
 =\max_{0\le j\le1}\delta^j
   \sup_{\varsigma\in K_\delta}
       \bigl\{|\partial_\varsigma^jx|
              +\|\partial_\varsigma^jz\|_N
              +|\partial_\varsigma^jp|\bigr\}.
\end{equation*}
Thus, in this ordinary unnormalized product space, its first gauge
differential has the safe operator bound
\begin{equation}
 \|D_g(r^\#,N_Z^\#,N_p^\#)\|_{\mathcal L(
       \mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h,
       C^1_{\delta,\mathrm{ord}}(K_\delta))}
 \le C_{\rm buf}\delta^{-2}.                    \label{eq:strong-hit-recut-loss}
\end{equation}
This is a supplied strong input for the trace chart, not a claim that raw
translation is uniformly bounded on one same strong space.

The two window conditions are nonempty on one fixed gauge ball: after
choosing \(L_\delta=O(S_\delta)\), one may take
\(\ell_\delta=c_{\rm buf}/(4\delta)\) and decrease the gauge radius so
that \cref{eq:strong-hit-chart-shift-budget} holds.

The proposition concerns time regularity and first gauge dependence.  It
does not make the gauge map \(C^2\), estimate an
\(\varepsilon\)-normalized terminal phase-to-normal Schur column, prove
gauge forgetting, construct a hybrid source inverse, take parameter
derivatives, or produce a repelling sheet, physical connection, or root.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Repelling component-kernel finite-generation flushing]
\label[proposition]{prop:repelling-component-flushing}
Retain \cref{lem:repelling-component-generations}.  Fix \(d\ge1\), take
\(h\ge\max\{3,d\}\), and choose \(M=M_d\) so that
\begin{equation}
 M(1-2\vartheta)>d.                                \label{eq:repelling-flush-generation-count}
\end{equation}
There is \(A_d^*>0\) such that, for fixed \(A_d>A_d^*\),
\begin{equation}
 L_0=A_dS_\delta,\qquad
 L_b=(M+1)\theta_m+L_0+1,                         \label{eq:repelling-flush-buffer-length}
\end{equation}
and sufficiently small \(\delta\), one has
\(2L_b+\theta_m<S_r\).  Set
\begin{align}
 J_{r,\rm in}
 &=\bar r_r([L_b-\theta_m,L_b]),\notag\\
 J_{r,\rm u}
 &=\bar r_r([S_r-L_b-\theta_m,S_r-L_b]).          \label{eq:repelling-flush-windows}
\end{align}

Split the solution linearly as
\((Z,p,Q)=(Z_{\rm in},p_{\rm in},Q_{\rm in})
 +(Z_{\rm u},p_{\rm u},Q_{\rm u})\), where the inner component has data
\((\gamma,b_u)=(\gamma,0)\) and the terminal component has data
\((\gamma,b_u)=(0,b_u)\).  On the two windows use
\(r=\bar r_r(s)\).  Then
\begin{align}
 &\|Z_{\rm in}\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm in})}
 +\|p_{\rm in}\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm in})}
 +\|Q_{\rm in}\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^{d-1}(J_{r,\rm in})}
 \notag\\
 &\quad\le C_{d,A_d}\mathcal E_{r,\rm in}(\delta)
             \varepsilon^{-1}
             \|\gamma\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta},      \label{eq:repelling-inner-flush}\\
 &\|Z_{\rm u}\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm u})}
 +\|p_{\rm u}\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm u})}
 +\|Q_{\rm u}\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^{d-1}(J_{r,\rm u})}
 \notag\\
 &\quad\le C_{d,A_d}\mathcal E_{r,\rm u}(\delta)
             \varepsilon^{-1}\frac{|b_u|}{r_{\rm out}},      \label{eq:repelling-terminal-flush}
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_{r,\rm in}(\delta)
 &=\left(\frac{S_\delta}{\delta}\right)^d
       \left\{e^{-cL_0/\delta}+e^{-cS_\delta L_0}\right\}
   +\delta^{M(1-2\vartheta)-d}S_\delta^{d-M},\notag\\
 \mathcal E_{r,\rm u}(\delta)
 &=\delta^{-2d}e^{-cL_0/\delta}
   +\delta^{M(2-2\vartheta)-2d}.                  \label{eq:repelling-flush-errors}
\end{align}
The number \(A_d^*\) is chosen so that the second inner action term tends to
zero; this is possible because \(S_\delta^2=2p\log(1/\delta)\).  Hence both
errors tend to zero.  These are normalized component trace estimates, not
support-vanishing statements.

The result is fixed-parameter and fixed-phase.  It uses no backward RFDE
evolution and does not include the compatible \(p(0)\)-to-collar feedback.
It proves neither the coupled phase--normal inverse, a nonlinear tracker,
the speed sign, a repelling invariant history sheet, nor a physical root.
\end{proposition}

The omitted feedback is not another infinite-dimensional boundary
condition.  It is a scalar feedback through the already constructed
compatible collar chart.  The next result closes that feedback in the
strong history class and records the extra cross-column estimate needed at
the terminal handoff.

\begin{proposition}[High-order compatible repelling-collar closure]
\label[proposition]{prop:compatible-repelling-collar-closure}
Retain \cref{prop:fixed-phase-green-collar-buffer,%
prop:compatible-endpoint-jet-collar,%
prop:repelling-component-flushing}.  Fix \(d\ge1\), take
\(h\ge\max\{3,d\}\), and now choose the generation number so that
\begin{equation}
 M(1-2\vartheta)>2d.                              \label{eq:compatible-repelling-generation-count}
\end{equation}
Use the same \(L_0,L_b,J_{r,\rm in},J_{r,\rm u}\) as in
\cref{eq:repelling-flush-buffer-length,eq:repelling-flush-windows}.  Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak C_e:\R\longrightarrow
 C^h([ -\theta_m,0];\R^N)                         \label{eq:strong-compatible-feedback-map}
\end{equation}
be a bounded linear map satisfying, uniformly on the retained chart ball,
\begin{align}
 (\mathfrak C_ec)(0)&=0,\notag\\
 \|\mathfrak C_ec\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta}
 &\le C_\Gamma\frac{|c|}{\rho_\delta}.           \label{eq:strong-compatible-feedback-bound}
\end{align}
For every
\(\gamma_0\in C^h([ -\theta_m,0];\R^N)\) with
\(\gamma_0(0)\in E_N\), and every \(b_u\in\R\), the mixed problem
\cref{eq:fixed-phase-normal-block} with \(F=H=0\),
\begin{equation}
 Z(0)=\gamma(0),\qquad p(S_r)=b_u,\qquad
 \gamma=\gamma_0+\mathfrak C_ep(0),               \label{eq:strong-compatible-feedback-problem}
\end{equation}
has a unique solution with \((Z,p)\in\mathfrak S_{r,d}\).  It splits
linearly as
\begin{equation}
 (Z,p,Q)=(Z_{\rm c},p_{\rm c},Q_{\rm c})
          +(Z_{\rm u},p_{\rm u},Q_{\rm u}),       \label{eq:compatible-repelling-column-split}
\end{equation}
where the first closed-loop column has data \((\gamma_0,b_u)=(\gamma_0,0)\)

 \gamma_{\rm c}=\gamma_0+\mathfrak C_ep_{\rm c}(0),
 \qquad
 \gamma_{\rm u}=\mathfrak C_ep_{\rm u}(0),
\end{equation*}
and obey
\begin{align}
 \|\gamma_{\rm c}\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta}
 &\le C\|\gamma_0\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta},\notag\\
 \|\gamma_{\rm u}\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta}
 &\le C\frac{|b_u|}{r_{\rm out}}.                 \label{eq:compatible-repelling-history-columns}
\end{align}

The collar column retains the inner handoff estimate
\begin{align}
 &\|Z_{\rm c}\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm in})}
 +\|p_{\rm c}\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm in})}
 +\|Q_{\rm c}\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^{d-1}(J_{r,\rm in})}\notag\\
 &\qquad\le C_{d,A_d}\mathcal E_{r,\rm in}(\delta)
       \varepsilon^{-1}
       \|\gamma_0\|_{\mathrm{cap},h,-\rho_\delta}.          \label{eq:compatible-repelling-inner-column}
\end{align}
For the terminal column put
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{r,\rm cross}(\delta)
 =\delta^{-2d}e^{-cL_0/\delta}
   +\delta^{M(1-2\vartheta)-2d}S_\delta^{-M}.      \label{eq:compatible-repelling-cross-error}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 &\|Z_{\rm u}\|_{\mathsf Z_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm u})}
 +\|p_{\rm u}\|_{\mathsf P_\varepsilon^d(J_{r,\rm u})}
 +\|Q_{\rm u}\|_{\mathsf Q_\varepsilon^{d-1}(J_{r,\rm u})}\notag\\
 &\qquad\le C_{d,A_d}
   \{\mathcal E_{r,\rm u}(\delta)
                  +\mathcal E_{r,\rm cross}(\delta)\}
       \varepsilon^{-1}\frac{|b_u|}{r_{\rm out}}.            \label{eq:compatible-repelling-terminal-column}
\end{align}
Both error factors on the last line tend to zero, uniformly in \(N\).

At every base point of the compatible collar chart,
\(\mathfrak C_e=D_{p_e}(\operatorname{pr}_\phi\Gamma_e^h)\), with
\(Z_e,\widehat\gamma,\nu,\eta\) fixed, satisfies
\cref{eq:strong-compatible-feedback-bound}.  Thus the proposition applies
to the actual linearized raw-compatibility feedback, not merely to an
abstract collar map.  It remains a fixed-parameter, fixed-phase result: the
coupled phase--normal border, nonlinear tracker, speed sign, and physical
root are not asserted.
\end{proposition}

There is nevertheless an intrinsic quotient once an actual physical history
has been constructed.  The point is to quotient by the transported tangent
line, rather than by an eigenvector of a frozen voltage--resource matrix; a
fixed complement is only a coordinate gauge for that quotient.

\begin{proposition}[Stationary-coordinate quotient along a physical history]
\label[proposition]{prop:physical-history-phase-quotient}
Fix \(N,\delta,\nu,\eta\), and in the raw equation replace
\(B_{k,N}(\zeta)\) by the structural-ball layer \(L_{k,N}(\eta)\).
Let \((\bar v(t),\bar w(t))\) be a classical solution of this physical
equation and rescale it to fold time by
\[
 v_*(s)=\bar v(s/\delta),\qquad w_*(s)=\bar w(s/\delta).
\]
Thus the fold-time delays are \(\theta_k\).  Let
\(Z_*(s)=(v_{*,s},w_*(s))\in\mathscr Y_N\) be compatible and \(C^2\) on an
interval enlarged by one delay, and let
\[
 r_*(s)=\pi_N^T\{v_*(s)-\mathbf1\}.
\]
Suppose \(r_*'(s)\ne0\) throughout that interval, and denote the variational
solution process of the rescaled RFDE along \(Z_*\) by
\(\mathcal U_*(s,u)\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 \tau_*(s)=\frac1{r_*'(s)}
 \left(\theta\longmapsto v_*'(s+\theta),\;w_*'(s)\right)
 \in\mathscr Y_N                                      \label{eq:physical-normalized-tangent}
\end{equation}
is the unique vector in the time-tangent line satisfying
\(\varrho_N\tau_*(s)=1\).  Its line is transported exactly:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_*(s,u)\tau_*(u)
 =\frac{r_*'(s)}{r_*'(u)}\tau_*(s).                  \label{eq:physical-tangent-transport}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 Q_*(s)=\Id-\tau_*(s)\varrho_N,
 \qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal U}_*(s,u)
 =Q_*(s)\mathcal U_*(s,u)|_{\ker\varrho_N}          \label{eq:physical-quotient-process}
\end{equation}
defines an exact linear process on the fixed stationary-coordinate gauge
section
\(\ker\varrho_N\): for \(u\le t\le s\),
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal U}_*(s,t)
 \widehat{\mathcal U}_*(t,u)
 =\widehat{\mathcal U}_*(s,u).                      \label{eq:physical-quotient-cocycle}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the intrinsic object is the variational process induced on
\(\mathscr Y_N/\operatorname{span}\{\tau_*(s)\}\), written in the physical
stationary-coordinate gauge.  Another bounded transversal gives a conjugate
representation of the same quotient process provided it is uniformly
transverse to the tangent line and its projections, quotient
identifications, and inverse identifications have common bounds.  In every
history norm for which
\(\varrho_N\) is bounded,
\begin{equation}
 \|Q_*(s)\|\le1+\|\tau_*(s)\|\,\|\varrho_N\|.       \label{eq:physical-quotient-bound}
\end{equation}
The construction is unchanged if the time tangent is multiplied by a
nonzero scalar before imposing \(\varrho_N\tau=1\); no eigenvector or
spectral projection of a frozen full system enters it.
\end{proposition}

The row \(\varrho_N\) need not be dual-covariant, and
\(\ker\varrho_N\) need not be invariant; the projection at every step in
\cref{eq:physical-quotient-process} is essential.  A dual-covariant phase
functional annihilating the stable and strong-unstable bundles could be
defined only after the missing normal dichotomy has been proved.  Thus the
proposition constructs neither such a phase functional nor an invariant
normal bundle.

The monotonicity in \cref{eq:outer-speed-sign} is therefore the correct
leading input for phase removal, but it is not yet the hypothesis of
\cref{prop:physical-history-phase-quotient}: one must first construct a true
positive-\(\delta\) history with \(r_*'\ne0\), and then prove dimension-uniform
bounds and the required parameter jets for
\cref{eq:physical-normalized-tangent,eq:physical-quotient-process}.  In
particular, the fixed row in
\cref{prop:backward-asymptotic-nonselection} can normalize the tangent of
each already selected member, but the row alone selects neither that physical
history nor its transported tangent line.  The present proposition is not a
global anchor or history-selection theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Exact resource-gauge normal equation]
\label[proposition]{prop:exact-resource-gauge-quotient}
Retain \cref{prop:exact-outer-history-equation} for the shared-resource law,
and let \(r_s\) be a generated orbit in fold time:
\[
 r_s'=\delta q(r_s),\qquad
 Z_*(s)=\mathcal H_{V,w,q}(r_s).
\]
Assume \(w'(r_s)\ne0\) on a delay-enlarged orbit interval, and denote the
variational process along \(Z_*\) by \(\mathcal U_*(s,\sigma)\).  The
automatic \(C^2\) regularity in
\cref{prop:exact-outer-history-equation} applies.  Write
\begin{align}
 r_{s+\theta}&=\Phi_q^{\delta\theta}(r_s),&
 \chi_s(\theta)&=\frac{q(r_{s+\theta})}{q(r_s)},\notag\\
 e_s(\theta)&=\chi_s(\theta)V'(r_{s+\theta}),&
 E_s^c&=(e_s,w'(r_s)).                              \label{eq:resource-gauge-center-history}
\end{align}
Then \(E_s^c=\partial_r\mathcal H_{V,w,q}(r_s)\), and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_*(s,\sigma)E_\sigma^c
 =\frac{q(r_s)}{q(r_\sigma)}E_s^c.                 \label{eq:resource-gauge-center-cocycle}
\end{equation}
In particular, the genuine time-translation tangent is
\(\delta q(r_s)E_s^c\), not a frozen slow eigenvector.

Let \((\xi_s,\omega(s))\) solve the full variational equation along \(Z_*\).
Define
\begin{equation}
 a(s)=\frac{\omega(s)}{w'(r_s)},\qquad
 \psi_s(\theta)=\xi(s+\theta)-e_s(\theta)a(s),       \label{eq:resource-gauge-coordinates}
\end{equation}
and put
\begin{equation}
 h_s(\theta)=\frac{e_s(\theta)}{w'(r_s)},\qquad
 H_s=h_s(0)=\frac{V'(r_s)}{w'(r_s)}.                \label{eq:resource-gauge-h}
\end{equation}
At each fixed \(s\), the history \(\psi_s\) is unchanged when any multiple of
\(E_s^c\) is added to the full variation.  With \(u(s)=\psi_s(0)\), the exact triangular
quotient equations are
\begin{align}
 a'(s)&=\delta q'(r_s)a(s)
       +\frac{\delta}{w'(r_s)}\pi_N^Tu(s),           \label{eq:resource-gauge-a}\\
 (\partial_s-\partial_\theta)\psi_s(\theta)
 &=-\delta h_s(\theta)\pi_N^Tu(s),                  \label{eq:resource-gauge-transport}\\
 u'(s)&=\delta^{-1}\mathcal J_*(s)u(s)
 +\delta K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
       \{u(s)-\psi_s(-\theta_k)\}
 -\delta H_s\pi_N^Tu(s),                            \label{eq:resource-gauge-boundary}
\end{align}
where the transport identity is for
\(-\theta_m\le\theta<0\), in the mild characteristic sense for continuous
histories; \(\theta=0\) is governed by
\cref{eq:resource-gauge-boundary}.  Here
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_*(s)=\mathcal A_N-\diag\!\left(
 2c_N\circ\{V(r_s)-\mathbf1\}
 +\beta\{V(r_s)-\mathbf1\}^{\circ2}\right).         \label{eq:resource-gauge-current-matrix}
\end{equation}

This transport--boundary system has an exact realization on an ordinary
voltage history.  For \(u_s(\sigma)=u(s+\sigma)\), define the Volterra
operator
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal K_s\phi)(\theta)
 =\delta\int_\theta^0
 h_{s+\sigma}(\theta-\sigma)\pi_N^T\phi(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma.
                                                               \label{eq:resource-gauge-volterra}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \psi_s=(\Id-\mathcal K_s)u_s,                       \label{eq:resource-gauge-history-conjugacy}
\end{equation}
and \(u\) satisfies the standard nonautonomous RFDE
\begin{align}
 u'(s)={}&\delta^{-1}\mathcal J_*(s)u(s)
 +\delta K\sum_{k=0}^mL_{k,N}(\eta)
 \bigl[u(s)-u(s-\theta_k)
       +(\mathcal K_su_s)(-\theta_k)\bigr]\notag\\
 &-\delta H_s\pi_N^Tu(s).                           \label{eq:resource-gauge-standard-rfde}
\end{align}

Suppose, on the whole delay collar, that \(q\), its reciprocal, and \(V'\)
have common bounds, and that
\begin{equation}
 |w'(r)|\ge c_w|r|,\qquad |r|\ge c_\rho\rho_\delta. \label{eq:resource-gauge-collar-bounds}
\end{equation}
For every \(\kappa_*\ge0\), use
\[
 \|\phi\|^\sharp_{\kappa_*}
 =\sup_{-\theta_m\le\theta\le0}
 e^{2\kappa_*\theta}\|\phi(\theta)\|_N.
\]
Then, uniformly in \(N\) and the orbit point,
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal K_s\|
 \le C\frac{\delta}{\rho_\delta}
 \le\frac{C}{S_\delta}.                             \label{eq:resource-gauge-volterra-bound}
\end{equation}
There is \(\delta_0>0\) such that, for \(0<\delta<\delta_0\),
\begin{equation}
 \|(\Id-\mathcal K_s)^{-1}\|\le2,\qquad
 \|(\Id-\mathcal K_s)^{-1}-\Id\|\le\frac{C}{S_\delta}.
                                                               \label{eq:resource-gauge-volterra-inverse}
\end{equation}
Conversely, on this range
\begin{equation}
 u_s=(\Id-\mathcal K_s)^{-1}\psi_s,                 \label{eq:resource-gauge-history-inverse}
\end{equation}
so \cref{eq:resource-gauge-history-conjugacy} is a two-sided coordinate
equivalence between the quotient transport--boundary system and
\cref{eq:resource-gauge-standard-rfde}; the scalar \(a\) is then recovered
from \cref{eq:resource-gauge-a}.
The full moving norm
\begin{equation}
 \|(\xi_s,\omega)\|_{*,s}
 =\|\xi_s\|^\sharp_{\kappa_*}
   +\frac{|\omega|}{|w'(r_s)|}                      \label{eq:resource-gauge-anisotropic-norm}
\end{equation}
is uniformly equivalent to
\(\|\psi_s\|^\sharp_{\kappa_*}+|a(s)|\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Local two-sided quotient flushing at a physical hit]
\label[proposition]{prop:physical-hit-quotient-flushing}
Retain
\cref{prop:raw-gauge-strong-hit-buffer,%
prop:exact-resource-gauge-quotient}
on the shared-resource law, still pointwise at one fixed admissible
parameter pair.  Fix \(A>0\), put
\begin{equation}
 L_\delta=AS_\delta,\qquad
 \kappa_{\sigma,h}
 =\kappa_{N,\delta}(e_{\sigma,h}),                \label{eq:hit-flushing-length-rate}
\end{equation}
and equip \(\mathscr X_N\) with the fixed hit norm
\begin{equation}
 \|\phi\|_{\sigma,h}^{\sharp}
 =\sup_{-\theta_m\le\theta\le0}
   e^{2\kappa_{\sigma,h}\theta}\|\phi(\theta)\|_N.
                                                               \label{eq:hit-fixed-fading-norm}
\end{equation}
For every raw gauge in the common ball, write
\[
 s_h=S_{\sigma,h}(g),\qquad
 I_{\sigma,h}(g)=[s_h-L_\delta,s_h+L_\delta].
\]
After decreasing \(\delta_0(A,r_{\rm out})\), this interval and its
one-delay enlargement lie in the common generated buffer.  Every solution
\(u\) of the exact standard quotient RFDE
\cref{eq:resource-gauge-standard-rfde} along that already constructed
tracker obeys
\begin{align}
 \|u_{s_h}\|_{a,h}^{\sharp}
 &\le C e^{-c\kappa_{a,h}L_\delta}
       \|u_{s_h-L_\delta}\|_{a,h}^{\sharp},
                                                        \label{eq:hit-attracting-interior-flush}\\
 \|u_{s_h}\|_{r,h}^{\sharp}
 &\le C e^{-c\kappa_{r,h}L_\delta}
   \left\{\|u_{s_h-L_\delta}\|_{r,h}^{\sharp}
          +\|u_{s_h+L_\delta}\|_{r,h}^{\sharp}\right\}.
                                                        \label{eq:hit-repelling-interior-flush}
\end{align}
Uniformly in \(N\) and the retained indices,
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{\sigma,h}\ge c_\kappa\log(1/\delta),
 \qquad
 e^{2\kappa_{\sigma,h}\theta_m}\le\delta^{-2\vartheta}.
                                                        \label{eq:hit-fading-rate-bounds}
\end{equation}
Thus the right-hand sides of
\cref{eq:hit-attracting-interior-flush,%
eq:hit-repelling-interior-flush}
are superalgebraically small relative to uniformly bounded endpoint
histories.

The repelling estimate uses a stable-left/one-dimensional-unstable-right
current Green representation; its backward factor is only the inverse on
the transported finite-dimensional unstable line.  This is a local,
fixed-parameter interior estimate for already constructed physical branch
segments, not a global invariant normal bundle or a new boundary-value
inverse.  It does not extend either segment through the fold, identify a
repelling invariant history sheet, take parameter derivatives, relate the
two branches, or construct a physical connection or root.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[First-order raw-gauge forgetting at fixed physical hits]
\label[theorem]{thm:physical-hit-first-order-flatness}
Retain \cref{prop:physical-hit-quotient-flushing} and use the
\(\varepsilon\)-normalized event norm
\begin{equation}
 \|(\phi,\omega)\|_{\mathrm{ev},h}
 =\varepsilon^{-1}\left\{
       \|\phi\|_{\infty,N}+\frac{|\omega|}{r_h}\right\},
 \qquad
 \|\phi\|_{\infty,N}
 =\sup_{-\theta_m\le\theta\le0}\|\phi(\theta)\|_N,
 \qquad \varepsilon=\delta^2.                    \label{eq:physical-hit-event-normal-norm}
\end{equation}
For every \(P>0\) there are \(C_P,\delta_P>0\), independent of \(N\),
the branch, the fixed admissible parameter point, and the gauges in the
common convex ball, such that
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\sigma\in\{a,r\}}\sup_g
 \|D_g\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g)\|_
   {\mathcal L(\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h,
               (\mathscr Y_N,\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{ev},h}))}
 \le C_P\delta^P,
 \qquad 0<\delta<\delta_P.                       \label{eq:physical-hit-gauge-flat-derivative}
\end{equation}
In particular, for any two gauges in that ball,
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g_1)
       -\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g_2)\|_{\mathrm{ev},h}
 \le C_P\delta^P
       \|g_1-g_2\|_{\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}.          \label{eq:physical-hit-gauge-flat-pair}
\end{equation}
Hence each physical section carries a well-defined raw-gauge-independent
flat class
\begin{equation}
 [\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}]_{\mathrm{flat}}
 :=
 [\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(g)]_{\mathcal O(\delta^\infty)}
 \quad\hbox{in }\Sigma_{N,e_{\sigma,h}}.           \label{eq:physical-hit-flat-class}
\end{equation}
Here \(g=g(\delta)\) may be any uniformly bounded gauge family in the
common ball, and the equivalence relation is the all-orders estimate
\cref{eq:physical-hit-gauge-flat-pair}.

The class in \cref{eq:physical-hit-flat-class} is asymptotic: it does not
assert exact gauge independence at a fixed positive \(\delta\).
The event quotient removes the time tangent, so the theorem does not make
the hit time \(S_{\sigma,h}(g)\) or its scaled displacement
\(\widehat\tau_{\sigma,h}(g)\) flat.  It constructs two separate
raw-gauge-independent hit classes, not a branch-to-branch relation.  In
particular, it
does not prove fold passage, membership in a repelling stable sheet,
parameter-jet flatness, a signed connection gap, transversality, or a
physical root.
\end{theorem}

The fixed-parameter qualifier in the preceding theorem cannot be removed by
compactness alone: superalgebraic closeness of histories does not control
their parameter derivatives.  We next construct the required common
parameter chart and then differentiate the quotient argument itself.  Put
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{\rm phys}
 =\{(i,j):0\le i\le1,\ 0\le j\le2\}
   \cup\{(i,j):0\le i\le2,\ 0\le j\le1\},       \label{eq:physical-rectangular-index-set}
\end{equation}
and, for a Banach-valued map $F(\nu,\eta)$, write
\begin{equation}
 \|F\|_{\mathcal J_{\rm phys};X}
 =\max_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I_{\rm phys}}
   \sup_{(\nu,\eta)}
   \|\partial_\nu^iD_\eta^jF(\nu,\eta)\|_{
      \mathcal L^j(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red};X)}.   \label{eq:physical-rectangular-jet-norm}
\end{equation}
Thus

\[
 \mathcal J_{\rm phys}
 =(C_\nu^1C_\eta^2)\cap(C_\nu^2C_\eta^1),
\]

including the mixed third derivatives
$\partial_\nu D_{\eta\eta}^2$ and
$\partial_{\nu\nu}^2D_\eta$, but not a fourth-order
$\partial_{\nu\nu}^2D_{\eta\eta}^2$ slot.

For the gauge variable we retain exactly the staircase needed to compose
with a parameter-dependent gauge field:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_{\rm phys}
 =\{(\ell,i,j):0\le\ell\le1,
                  \ (i,j)\in\mathcal I_{\rm phys}\}
  \cup\{(\ell,i,j):2\le\ell\le3,
                  \ \ell+i+j\le3\}.             \label{eq:physical-gauge-staircase}
\end{equation}
Thus one gauge derivative may be followed by the complete rectangular
parameter jet, while the higher gauge derivatives needed by the
third-order chain rule are retained through total order three.  This is
not a claim of full $C_g^3\mathcal J_{\rm phys}$ product regularity.

The rough current graph cannot be trivialized by translating or rescaling
its time interval: translation is not Fr\'{e}chet differentiable on the
piecewise $W^{1,\infty}$ graph space.  We instead freeze the reference
fold-time equation itself.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-reference rough residual chart]
\label[lemma]{lem:fixed-reference-parameter-residual}
Retain
\cref{prop:fold-time-tracker-normal-form,%
thm:raw-compatible-phase-normal-inverse,%
thm:raw-gauge-fixed-section-zero}
at order $h\ge8$, and fix an admissible reference parameter
$\lambda^\circ=(\nu^\circ,\eta^\circ)$.  Let
$\bar r_\sigma^\circ:[0,S_\sigma^\circ]\to I_\sigma$ be its reduced
fold-time path.  Use this one interval, its fixed-delay enlargement, and
the graph and range norms at $\lambda^\circ$ for every nearby parameter.
Here $\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rga,\circ}$,
$\mathcal Y_{\sigma,\delta}^{\circ}$, and
$\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^{h,\circ}$ denote the resulting fixed copies
of, respectively, the domain, the product target of
\cref{eq:raw-gauge-residual}, and the normalized raw-gauge space.
There are common neighborhoods and an exact residual
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal F}_{\sigma,\lambda^\circ}:
 \mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rga,\circ}
 \times\mathcal P_N^\circ
 \times\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^{h,\circ}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal Y_{\sigma,\delta}^{\circ}          \label{eq:fixed-reference-parameter-residual}
\end{equation}
with the following properties.

For $u=(x,a)$, reconstruct on the fixed interval
\begin{equation}
 r_u(s)=\Theta_a^\circ(\bar r_\sigma^\circ(s)),\qquad
 v_u(s)=\mathbf1+r_u(s)\mathbf1+z_{0,N}(r_u(s))+Z_x(s),
 \qquad
 w_u(s)=w_{\rm seed,N}(r_u(s))-p_x(s),            \label{eq:fixed-reference-physical-fields}
\end{equation}
At a zero, the inner phase row fixes exactly one end of each phase path:
\begin{equation}
 r_{a,u}(S_a^\circ)=\rho_\delta,
 \qquad
 r_{r,u}(0)=-\rho_\delta.                       \label{eq:fixed-reference-inner-endpoints}
\end{equation}
The opposite, outer endpoint is free.  Thus the fixed duration does not
impose two endpoint conditions on either branch.

For clarity, the fixed row operation used below is part of the definition.
Let $H_{\sigma,\lambda,g}(u)$ be the actual-parameter compatible old
collar returned by the raw history package, let $\gamma_x$ be the trial
old-history coordinate, and put
\begin{equation}
 R_\gamma=\gamma_x-H_{\sigma,\lambda,g}(u).       \label{eq:fixed-reference-collar-mismatch}
\end{equation}
Write $R_{\rm bulk}^{H}$ for the two unmodified raw fold-time residuals
when $H_{\sigma,\lambda,g}(u)$ is inserted in every negative-time delayed
slot.  If the raw voltage residual is written as left side minus right
side, the fixed-reference old-history injection has unprojected voltage
and resource components
\begin{equation}
 (\mathbf J_{\lambda^\circ}^{\rm old}\rho)(s)
 =\delta K\!\sum_{k:\,s-\theta_k<0}
       L_{k,N}(\eta^\circ)\rho(s-\theta_k),
 \qquad
 (\mathbf J_{\lambda^\circ}^{\rm old,w}\rho)(s)=0. \label{eq:fixed-reference-old-history-injection}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal J_{\lambda^\circ}^{\rm old}$ denote
\cref{eq:fixed-reference-old-history-injection} after the fixed
collective--transverse row reduction at $\lambda^\circ$.  On the collar
and bulk rows apply the triangular isomorphism
\begin{align}
 \mathscr U_{\lambda^\circ}(R_\gamma,R_{\rm bulk}^{H})
 &=\bigl(R_\gamma,
       R_{\rm bulk}^{H}+\mathcal J_{\lambda^\circ}^{\rm old}R_\gamma\bigr),
                                                        \notag\\
 \mathscr U_{\lambda^\circ}^{-1}(R_\gamma,\widetilde R_{\rm bulk})
 &=\bigl(R_\gamma,
       \widetilde R_{\rm bulk}
          -\mathcal J_{\lambda^\circ}^{\rm old}R_\gamma\bigr).
                                                        \label{eq:fixed-reference-row-isomorphism}
\end{align}
The seam, finite boundary, and inner phase rows are left unchanged.
Both maps in \cref{eq:fixed-reference-row-isomorphism} are bounded in the
normalized fixed-reference product norms, uniformly in $N$ and $\delta$.

Use the raw-compatible collar at the actual parameter and gauge on the
old fixed-delay interval.  The residual in
\cref{eq:fixed-reference-parameter-residual} is obtained by substituting
these fields into the two unmodified raw fold-time equations
\cref{eq:raw-fold-time-fields}, together with the same seam, finite
boundary, and inner phase rows as in
\cref{eq:raw-gauge-residual}, and then applying the fixed bounded row
operations at $\lambda^\circ$.  Thus its zero is equivalent to the
unmodified forward RFDE on the fixed interval, compatibility of its
initial history, and the displayed finite boundary and phase rows; it does
not assert a past orbit.  Indeed, at a zero $R_\gamma=0$, so
$R_{\rm bulk}^{H}=0$ by
\cref{eq:fixed-reference-row-isomorphism}.  No moving trial path or
parameter-dependent terminal evaluation enters the definition.  At the
reference parameter the identity
$R_{\rm bulk}^{\gamma}=R_{\rm bulk}^{H}
 +\mathcal J_{\lambda^\circ}^{\rm old}R_\gamma$
makes it exactly
$\mathcal F_\sigma^g$ in the fixed graph coordinates, and
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\bigl[D_u\widehat{\mathcal F}_{\sigma,\lambda^\circ}
       (u;\lambda,g)\bigr]^{-1}\right\|\le4M       \label{eq:fixed-reference-parameter-inverse}
\end{equation}
on a smaller common product ball.
At a zero, the physical collective speed is represented kinematically by
\begin{equation}
 q_{\sigma,\lambda,g}(r_u(s))
 =q_0^\circ(\bar r_\sigma^\circ(s))
       (\Theta_a^\circ)'(\bar r_\sigma^\circ(s)). \label{eq:fixed-reference-speed-identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently the common sign follows from the reference sign and the
orientation margin of the phase chart; it is not inferred by identifying
the reference speed with the changed physical parameter.

The map is four times Fr\'{e}chet differentiable on that rough product
ball, with derivatives uniform in $N$, $\delta$, and the branch in the
normalized parameter and gauge norms.  More precisely, it has every derivative required by
\cref{eq:physical-gauge-staircase}.  This regularity is strong-to-weak only
where a time derivative is spent: the fixed translations
$s\mapsto s-\theta_k$ are bounded linear maps, while the finite
compatibility recursion and the nonlinear phase Nemytskii maps supply the
remaining derivatives.  The lemma makes no same-space differentiability
claim for a moving translation on piecewise $W^{1,\infty}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Parameter-coherent physical-hit families]
\label[proposition]{prop:parameter-coherent-physical-hit-family}
Retain
\cref{thm:raw-gauge-fixed-section-zero,%
prop:raw-gauge-strong-hit-buffer,%
lem:fixed-reference-parameter-residual,%
lem:terminal-relative-trace-scale-chart}
at one fixed order $h\ge8$.  After decreasing the common structural ball
and the raw-gauge ball, the following conclusions hold on one common open
parameter box
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_N=(-\nu_*,\nu_*)
   \times B_{\eta_*}^{\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}}(0)\subset
       \R\times\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}.          \label{eq:physical-hit-parameter-box}
\end{equation}

First, use the single fixed-reference residual chart in
\cref{lem:fixed-reference-parameter-residual}, centered at the middle of
the displayed box.  This freezes the interval and the fixed-delay graph
before the parameter is varied; it is not a translation or rescaling of a
rough trial path.  Its unique zero
\begin{equation}
 (\nu,\eta,g)\longmapsto
 u_{\sigma,\delta}(\nu,\eta,g)
 \in\mathcal G_{\sigma,\delta}^{\rm rga}          \label{eq:parameter-coherent-raw-zero}
\end{equation}
has every mixed derivative
\begin{equation}
 D_g^\ell\partial_\nu^iD_\eta^j

 (\ell,i,j)\in\mathcal K_{\rm phys},             \label{eq:physical-hit-mixed-derivative-class}
\end{equation}
and these derivatives are dimension uniformly bounded as multilinear maps
from the normalized gauge and parameter spaces.  The inverse at every zero
satisfies \cref{eq:fixed-reference-parameter-inverse}.  In particular, the exact reconstructed
RFDE segments have the rectangular parameter jets, the fixed-parameter
$C_g^3$ jets, and precisely the mixed staircase in
\cref{eq:physical-gauge-staircase}.  They retain, uniformly on the box,
\begin{equation}
 q_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}\le-c_q<0,
 \qquad
 c_w|r|\le|W_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}'(r)|\le C_w|r|,
 \qquad
 \|V_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}'(r)\|_N\le C_V          \label{eq:parameter-coherent-tracker-signs}
\end{equation}
on the common generated collar containing the physical hit.
Moreover, with
\(\mathbf y=(r/r_h,Z/\varepsilon,p/(\varepsilon r_h))\), the zero family
has the triangular strong-to-weak ledger
\begin{equation}
 \sup_s\delta^d
 \left\|\partial_s^d
 D_g^\ell\partial_\nu^iD_\eta^j\mathbf y(s)\right\|
 \le C_{\ell,i,j,d},
 \qquad
 (\ell,i,j)\in\mathcal K_{\rm phys},\quad
 0\le d\le h-(\ell+i+j).                         \label{eq:parameter-coherent-time-jet-ledger}
\end{equation}
The supremum is on the common generated subbuffer used below, including
the delay collar.  In particular, since $h\ge8$, every mixed slot retained
here has the extra time derivatives needed to translate it into a scaled
$C^3$ event window.

Second, fix $A>0$, put $L_\delta=AS_\delta$, and use the event-relative
window
\begin{equation}
 K_{\delta,h}=[-L_\delta-\theta_m,L_\delta].       \label{eq:parameter-coherent-event-window}
\end{equation}
The fixed-chart endpoints straddle the physical section with a uniform
coordinate margin: for some $c_{\rm end}>0$,
\begin{equation}
 r_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}(0)-e_{\sigma,h}
       \ge c_{\rm end}r_{\rm out},
 \qquad
 e_{\sigma,h}-r_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}(S_\sigma^\circ)
       \ge c_{\rm end}r_{\rm out}.              \label{eq:parameter-coherent-endpoint-gap}
\end{equation}
Consequently there is a unique hit $S_{\sigma,h}$ and, for a uniform
$c_{\rm hit}>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \min\{S_{\sigma,h}-\theta_m,
            S_\sigma^\circ-S_{\sigma,h}\}
 \ge \frac{c_{\rm hit}}{\delta}.                \label{eq:parameter-coherent-hit-margin}
\end{equation}
For the exact tracker triple $Y=(r,Z,p)$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Y_{\sigma,h}^{\#}(\nu,\eta,g)(\varsigma)
 =Y_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}
    (S_{\sigma,h}(\nu,\eta,g)+\varsigma),
 \qquad \varsigma\in K_{\delta,h}.              \label{eq:parameter-coherent-event-buffer}
\end{equation}
After decreasing \(\delta_0(A,r_{\rm out})\), this is a common generated
buffer for all retained arguments.  If
\(b=\ell+i+j\) and the derivative belongs to
\cref{eq:physical-hit-mixed-derivative-class}, then
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\|D_g^\ell\partial_\nu^iD_\eta^j
     \mathcal Y_{\sigma,h}^{\#}\bigr\|_{
     \mathcal L^{\ell+j}(\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h,
       \mathfrak T_N^{\rm red};\mathfrak S^3(K_{\delta,h}))}
 \le C_b\delta^{-2b}.                            \label{eq:parameter-coherent-event-buffer-jets}
\end{equation}
Here the notation on the left means the corresponding mixed multilinear
operator, with the scalar \(\nu\)-directions suppressed, and
$\mathfrak S^3(K_{\delta,h})$ is the norm in
\cref{eq:strong-hit-buffer-norm} with $B_{\sigma,h}$ replaced by
$K_{\delta,h}$.  The exponent is a safe strong-to-weak translation loss,
not an assertion that every slot actually attains it.  Writing
\begin{equation}
 v(s)=\mathbf1+r(s)\mathbf1+z_{0,N}(r(s))+Z(s),
 \qquad w(s)=w_{\rm seed,N}(r(s))-p(s),           \label{eq:parameter-coherent-physical-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
restriction of the event-relative buffer to $[-\theta_m,0]$ gives the
complete-history hit map with the same staircase regularity,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(\nu,\eta,g)
 =\left(\theta\longmapsto
       v_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}
          (S_{\sigma,h}(\nu,\eta,g)+\theta),
       \ w_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}
          (S_{\sigma,h}(\nu,\eta,g))\right)
 \in\Sigma_{N,e_{\sigma,h}}.                     \label{eq:parameter-coherent-hit-map}
\end{equation}
All constants are independent of $N$, $\delta$, the two branches, and the
point of the retained parameter box.

This proposition constructs a parameter-coherent family on the fixed
physical interior sections relative to the chosen reference chart
$\lambda^\circ$.  It proves no covariance between two different
fixed-reference charts.  It does not yet estimate the differentiated
quotient, identify the original outer endpoints, or construct a terminal
Schur inverse, repelling history sheet, branch-to-branch relation, or root.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Rectangular-jet raw-gauge forgetting at physical hits]
\label[theorem]{thm:physical-hit-rectangular-flatness}
Retain
\cref{prop:parameter-coherent-physical-hit-family,%
prop:physical-hit-quotient-flushing,%
thm:physical-hit-first-order-flatness}.
For every $P>0$ and every
$(\ell,i,j)\in\mathcal K_{\rm phys}$ with
\begin{equation}
 \ell\ge1,                                      \label{eq:physical-hit-flat-mixed-index}
\end{equation}
there are $C_{P,\ell,i,j},\delta_{P,\ell,i,j}>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\sigma,\nu,\eta,g}
 \bigl\|D_g^\ell\partial_\nu^iD_\eta^j
       \mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(\nu,\eta,g)\bigr\|_{
  \mathcal L^{\ell+j}(\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h,
       \mathfrak T_N^{\rm red};
       (\mathscr Y_N,\|\cdot\|_{\rm ev,h}))}
 \le C_{P,\ell,i,j}\delta^P.                   \label{eq:physical-hit-mixed-flatness}
\end{equation}
The supremum is over the common gauge and parameter boxes, and the constants
are independent of $N$.

More generally, for $a=1,2$ let
$(g_{a,N,\delta,\sigma})_{N,\delta,\sigma}$ be a family of maps from
$\mathcal P_N$ to $\mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h$ taking values in the
common convex gauge ball and having tame rectangular jets.  Assume that
the following constants are independent of $N$, $\delta$, and the branch;
we suppress those three indices below:
\begin{equation}
 \|g_a\|_{\mathcal J_{\rm phys};
             \mathfrak B_{\sigma,\delta}^h}
 \le C_{12}\delta^{-M_{12}}e^{c_{12}S_\delta},
 \qquad a=1,2.                                   \label{eq:physical-hit-tame-gauge-fields}
\end{equation}
Then for every $P>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\|\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(\cdot,g_1(\cdot))
       -\mathcal E_{\sigma,h}(\cdot,g_2(\cdot))
       \bigr\|_{\mathcal J_{\rm phys};
             (\mathscr Y_N,\|\cdot\|_{\rm ev,h})}
 \le C_{P,12}\delta^P.                          \label{eq:physical-hit-rectangular-pair-flatness}
\end{equation}
Thus each of the two physical sections carries a well-defined
\(\mathcal J_{\rm phys}\)-flat complete-history class relative to the
chosen fixed-reference chart, independent of the raw gauge within the
stated tame class.  The constant $C_{P,12}$ is likewise independent of
$N$, $\delta$, and the branch.

The conclusion concerns differences between physical representatives.
Parameter derivatives of one representative are generally nonzero and are
not flushed away; those derivatives contain the response that a later
connection conormal must retain.  The theorem still gives two separate hit
classes.  It does not make the hit times flat, supply an
\(\varepsilon\)-normalized full phase--normal RFDE terminal Schur inverse,
pass through the fold,
construct a repelling sheet or signed membership gap, or prove a physical
root.  Nor does it identify the classes produced from two different
reference charts.
\end{theorem}

Thus the apparently large term \(-\omega\mathbf1/\delta\) in the raw
fold-time variational equation is removed exactly only after the genuine
tracker tangent is known.  The remaining Volterra coordinate correction is
\(O(S_\delta^{-1})\), but
\cref{prop:exact-resource-gauge-quotient} still assumes the tracker and the
collar bounds; it does not prove them.  Uniform parameter derivatives further
require relative bounds on the jets of \(w'\), not merely absolute bounds.

The proofs, including the dimension-uniform block graph construction, are in
\cref{sec:supplementary-outer-skeleton}.  Three qualifications are essential.
First, \cref{eq:outer-stable-layer-semigroup} concerns the leading
frozen-resource voltage matrix \(\mathcal J^{\rm fv}_{N}(r)\).  Its
positive-\(\delta\) frozen-resource RFDE upgrade is precisely
\cref{prop:frozen-voltage-history-splitting}; neither result includes the
resource tangent or a nonautonomous slow history.  Second, the action is that of the principal
normal equation \(\delta^2n_T=\lambda_{c,N}(r(T))n\), not yet an action
estimate for an invariant RFDE slow-history graph.  Third, at
\(r=\pm\rho_\delta\), so that \(X=r/\delta\), the critical curve has
\begin{equation}
 d_{\rm crit}
 =\frac{2/3-w_{0,N}(r)}{\delta^2}-\alpha X^2
      +\frac1{2\alpha}
 =\frac1{2\alpha}+O_{\rm unif}(\delta S_\delta^3),            \label{eq:outer-critical-fold-offset}
\end{equation}
whereas the retained canard tube has \(|d|<\delta^\kappa\).  Thus, in the
present \(r\)-parameterization and fold chart, a physical outer-history
theorem must at least produce the leading finite-\(\delta\) correction
\begin{equation}
 w^{{\rm slow},\diamond,\pm}_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}(\pm\rho_\delta)
 =w_{0,N}(\pm\rho_\delta)+\frac{\delta^2}{2\alpha}
   +E^{\diamond,\pm}_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}.          \label{eq:outer-required-resource-correction}
\end{equation}
A sufficient resource-coordinate remainder for entry into
\(|d|<\delta^\kappa\) would be
\(E^{\diamond,\pm}_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}
=o(\delta^{2+\kappa})\), uniformly in the displayed indices on a fixed
admissible compact \((\nu,\zeta)\)-window.  Even that
would not prove exact overlap without the corresponding voltage and full
history estimates.

Nor can such a theorem obtain a unique history merely by asking for a
bounded complete backward extension converging in an unnormalized history
norm to a reference branch.
\Cref{prop:backward-asymptotic-nonselection} gives an explicit RFDE
counterexample, even for families which are superalgebraically close in a
strong history norm and share one phase row.  These qualitative conditions
alone prove neither uniqueness nor the required weighted parameter jets.
Any complete-history construction for this RFDE must therefore specify an
additional model-dependent selection or normalization and prove its uniform
jets.
