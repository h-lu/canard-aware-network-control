\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

In a retarded differential equation, an orbit is represented at each time by
an entire history segment.  This matters near a canard connection: matching
two current states need not match the delayed information that subsequently
returns to the critical direction.  In this paper a \emph{selected
complete-history root} is a parameter value at which an attracting and a
repelling one-sided trace, constructed on the same invariant history graph,
agree on a fixed phase section.  The traces are exact RFDE histories on the
retained graph, while their outer tail levels and cutoffs are generated by a
fixed canonical preparation datum.  The resulting local root is a selected
object and is not asserted to be independent of that datum.

\subsection{Related work and precise novelty}

The local fold geometry used below originates in planar canard theory
\citep{krupa2001relaxation,krupa2001extending}.  Canard explosions and
complex canard-induced oscillations in delay equations have been established
in concrete scalar models
\citep{campbell2009delay,krupa2016complex,krupa2016explosion}, and
\citet{avitabile2020local} developed a local theory for spatio-temporal
canards with infinite-dimensional fast dynamics.  These results establish
delayed canard mechanisms; they do not supply the network-uniform,
phase-fixed comparison of complete RFDE histories used here.

The contraction tools are also classical.  The Dobrushin coefficient is the
contraction ratio of a stochastic operator in the oscillation seminorm
\citep{gaubert2015dobrushin}.  Exponential consensus and robustness to
transmission delays have been proved for several directed or scalable network
classes \citep{moreau2004consensus,munz2010delay,atay2013consensus,
haskovec2021consensus,proskurnikov2023delay}.  Our companion result is not a
general delay-consensus theorem.  It treats the specific nonautonomous,
two-component delayed FitzHugh--Nagumo transverse family, permits delay
layers that are not left balanced, and gives both an explicit evolution
estimate and a causal complete-line Green inverse with constants independent
of network size and admitted topology.

Lin reductions and Fredholm range characterizations for retarded and
mixed-type functional differential equations are likewise established
\citep{hale1986heteroclinic,lin1986exponential,malletparet1999fredholm,
hupkes2009lin}.  The block elimination below is an operator-valued Schur
reduction, not a new Fredholm principle.  Its network-specific content is
that row-mass preservation of the delayed layers does not preserve the
stationary transverse space.  The resulting upper-triangular defect changes
the full cokernel; after the correct codomain row operation, collective
matching data give an exact identity between the scalar and network gaps.

Canards and canard-type tipping on networks have been studied on consensus
manifolds, nonlinear diffusion networks, adaptive mean-field networks, and
through collective reductions
\citep{jardon2020consensus,bonetto2024nonlinear,qin2023tipping,
balzer2024cascading,laurence2019spectral,thibeault2020threefold,
masuda2022dimension,ma2023generalized,liu2026predicting}.  The principal
theorem has a different form: it constructs a preparation-indexed,
complete-history root uniformly over a finite directed graph class, derives
an explicit response to atomwise row-neutral delay redistribution, and gives
a heterogeneous witness family with no nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal.
It does not claim the first network canard, a preparation-independent maximal
canard, or a physical pulse threshold.

We ask whether such a root can be constructed with estimates uniform over
finite directed networks, whether a structural redistribution of delayed
coupling has a nonzero uniform response, and whether that response can persist
when heterogeneous node fields eliminate every nontrivial synchrony quotient.

Two elementary reductions do not answer this question.  First, copying a
scalar orbit on the synchronous line proves invariance but gives no inverse
for nonsynchronous histories.  Second, row-mass preservation leaves the
collective line invariant, yet delayed coupling layers need not preserve the
transverse complement determined by a stationary measure.  The linearized
network operator is then upper triangular rather than an orthogonal direct
sum.  A current-state projection would remove precisely the delayed
transverse contribution that feeds back into the collective matching row.

Our estimates use oscillation norms and a common Dobrushin gap; no eigenbasis,
reversibility, or simultaneous diagonalization of the coupling layers is
required.

\subsection{Principal result}

The main theorem, \cref{thm:shared-resource}, concerns a shared-resource RFDE
on a finite directed Markov network.  Node curvatures may be heterogeneous,
the delay support is fixed, and the perturbation of each delay atom is
neutral with respect to the stationary row.  After the fold scaling
\(\varepsilon=\delta^2\), one canonical preparation datum, fixed across the
network family, selects an attracting and a repelling phase-fixed trace.
Their normalized complete-history gap has, for every finite network size,
exactly one root in a common local parameter window.  If
\(\mu=\delta^2\nu\) and \(\zeta\) denotes the structural
perturbation, then
\begin{equation}
 \mu^{\rm can}_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)
 =\mu^{\rm can}_{c,N}(\delta,0)
   +\mathscr C_N\delta^3\zeta
   +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),                 \label{eq:intro-response}
\end{equation}
uniformly in \(N\), with \(\inf_N|\mathscr C_N|>0\).  The hidden constant
may depend on the fixed preparation datum.  At the root the two selected
retained histories are equal, not merely their present states.  An explicit
family with pairwise-distinct curvatures has the same nonzero coefficient for
every \(N\ge2\) and admits no nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal.

The superscript ``can'' in \cref{eq:intro-response} records this selection.
The datum fixes the cutoffs, normal extension, tail levels, and phase
convention used to select the two one-sided traces.  The theorem proves that
the leading structural coefficient is determined by the physical low-order
jets and that the remainder is uniform in \(N\) for this fixed selection.  It
does not compare arbitrary graph preparations or identify the root with an
independently selected geometric or physical outer canard.

\subsection{Mechanism and companion transfer results}

The proof of the main theorem has four steps.  A Dobrushin estimate gives a
dimension-free exponential inverse on the transverse network space.  An
exact fold chart then produces a common contraction for an invariant history
graph, including the required state and parameter jets.  One-sided Gaussian
Green operators solve the trace, phase, and endpoint equations with bounds
uniform in network size.  Only after this range problem is solved do we pair
the reduced field with its adjoint.  Atomwise row neutrality cancels the
direct structural delay term, leaving a mixed-curvature contribution that
produces \(\mathscr C_N\).  Strict monotonicity of the normalized gap then
gives the unique root.  The decisive bridge is the injectivity of the history
graph: equality in the scalar reduced gap becomes equality of the retained
complete RFDE histories.
\Cref{fig:root-mechanism} separates the selected fold geometry from the
algebraic response chain through the delay moment, transverse resolvent,
curvature pairing, and gap derivative.

Two companion results place this mechanism in a broader operator setting.
For the leaky
FitzHugh--Nagumo network in \cref{eq:leaky-network-v,eq:leaky-network-w},
\cref{thm:transverse} gives dimension- and topology-uniform transverse decay
and a causal complete-line Green bound.  When the endpoint, phase, parameter,
and gap data are collective, \cref{cor:canonical-transfer} uses a bounded
codomain row operation to reduce the upper-triangular Lin operator to the
scalar/transverse direct sum.  Consequently any supplied scalar simple root
has exactly the same root, slope, and orientation in every admitted finite
network.

The companion leaky result is a transfer theorem, not an existence theorem
for its scalar root.  The main shared-resource result constructs its own
selected histories and root, but only for a common Dobrushin gap, fixed delay
support, an atomwise row-neutral perturbation direction, and the fixed
canonical preparation.  Signed coupling, moving delay atoms, closing mixing
gaps, infinite networks, comparison of arbitrary graph preparations, and
preparation-independent canard selection lie outside the proved scope.

\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:model-results} states the shared-resource theorem and the companion
network results.  \Cref{sec:network-mechanism} proves the complete-line
inverse and triangular transfer, followed by the invariant-history graph,
trace range, normalized gap expansion, and explicit non-synchrony witness
needed for the principal theorem.  The final section records the exact
mathematical boundary of the resulting root theory.
