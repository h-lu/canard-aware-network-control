\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Suppose a parameter changes a delayed network while leaving a prescribed
projected vector field exactly unchanged at every full history.  Can that
parameter nevertheless be detected by a dynamical matching measurement?  If
the projection closes the dynamics, the quotient system answers the question.
If it does not, an invisible perturbation can first displace unresolved
histories and later return to a resolved matching condition.  This paper shows
that, for a class of finite retarded networks, the returned information is not
only detectable: two separated delays make the entire compressed transverse
return functional recoverable, with estimates independent of network size.

The setting is a family of directed Markov networks with a common Dobrushin
gap and finitely many fixed delay locations.  A structural parameter
redistributes coupling between delay layers in stationary-row-neutral
directions.  If \(\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}\) is the full history vector
field and \(\Pi_N(x,y)=(\pi_N^Tx,y)\), then
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}(\Phi)
 =\Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,0}(\Phi)              \label{eq:intro-blindness}
\end{equation}
for every unreduced history \(\Phi\).  This is equality at the same full
history, not exact lumpability and not equality of projected trajectories.
Nevertheless, a selected complete-history fold-matching root has a nonzero
leading response.  The mechanism is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \text{blind delay redistribution}
  &\longrightarrow \text{transverse source}
   \longrightarrow \text{stable history lift}\\
  &\longrightarrow \text{collective cokernel}
   \longrightarrow \text{root response}.
 \end{aligned}                                      \label{eq:intro-return-chain}
\end{equation}

The perturbations have a concrete network interpretation.  A designer may
redistribute a fixed aggregate interaction between communication, transport,
or feedback channels with different latencies while preserving stationary
balance at every channel.  An observer that records only the stationary
average then sees the same projected right-hand side, even though hidden
disagreement modes retain the latency information.  Near a fold, small
hidden displacements can change where attracting and repelling slow-passage
histories meet.  The selected root studied here is a rigorous local
transition sensitivity for that meeting problem.  It is not asserted to be
a biological onset or a globally defined switching threshold; its value is
that it tests whether an aggregate-only description has discarded
dynamically recoverable delay information.

\paragraph{Reusable verification interface.}
To transport \cref{eq:intro-return-chain} to another RFDE, one must verify
five independent inputs: exact blindness on the full history space; a
factored source \(\mathsf S_N\) with a dimension-uniform right inverse on
the admitted probe space; a dimension-uniform transverse history inverse
\(L_{\perp,N}^{-1}\); a return map \(\mathsf B_N\) into a collective
matching operator with one-dimensional cokernel and nonzero parameter class;
and a \(C^2\) history lift, zero-fiber identification, and finite-section
remainder bounds.  The abstract two-atom source criterion in
\cref{prop:abstract-two-atom-source}, the hidden-return result
\cref{thm:hidden-return-schur}, and the range-to-root result
\cref{thm:abstract-root-response} separate these checks.  The two concrete
models below verify the same source and range interfaces but realize
inequivalent return covectors.

\subsection{Main theorem}

The next statement collects the operator dichotomy, the RFDE range
construction, and the nonlinear measurement in one result for the two model
families treated here.  It is a model-family synthesis theorem, not a
replacement for the abstract portability criteria just listed.  Its
constituent statements are separated in later sections so that the source
theorem and the range-to-root theorem can be reused independently.

For this statement, call a model family \emph{uniformly admissible} when the
following data are fixed independently of \(N\): constants
\(D,\gamma,\alpha,\beta>0\), \(K\ne0\), and finitely many merged delay
locations; row-stochastic \(P_N\) with strictly positive stationary
probability \(\pi_N\) and \(\tau(P_N)\le1-\gamma\); curvature vectors with
\(0<c_-\le c_{i,N}\le c_+\) and \(\pi_N^Tc_N=\alpha\); and delay layers
and stationary-row-neutral pure-redistribution directions satisfying one
common sum of operator-norm bounds.  The physical RFDE is either
\cref{eq:shared-rfde-v,eq:shared-rfde-w} or
\cref{eq:shared-rfde-v,eq:sensed-recovery-law}; in the second case the
sensing coefficients also obey \cref{eq:sensing-bounds}.  A compact
\(\nu\)-interval contains all corresponding fold centers with one positive
boundary distance.  Finally, one selection datum is fixed: it agrees with
the physical history equation on the retained fold tube, supplies the stated
uniformly bounded smooth history completion, and fixes the endpoint and
phase rows; the full, nonempty class is specified by \textup{(S1)}--\textup{(S4)}
in \cref{def:admissible-preparation}.

\begin{theorem}[Projection-blind two-delay fold-response readout]
\label[theorem]{thm:flagship-synthesis}
Consider a uniformly admissible family in the preceding sense.  Let
\(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\) be the stationary-row-neutral
pure-redistribution space, let
\(\mathsf S_N:\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\to E_N\) be the first-delay-moment
source, and put \(\mathsf T_N=A_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N\).  If
\(\Theta\) is the merged delay support, set
\(\Delta_\theta=\max\Theta-\min\Theta\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Every admitted redistribution is exactly invisible to the stationary
projection at every unreduced history:
\[
 \Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}
 =\Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,0}.
\]
If \(\Delta_\theta>0\), then \(\mathsf S_N\) is onto \(E_N\), has an explicit
two-layer right inverse \(\mathsf Q_N\), and
\[
 \|\mathsf S_N\|=\frac{|K|\Delta_\theta}{4\alpha},
 \qquad
 \|\mathsf Q_N\|=\frac{4\alpha}{|K|\Delta_\theta}.
\]
If the merged support contains only one location, then
\(\mathsf S_N=0\) on \(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\).
This last assertion is a no-go statement only for the leading
first-delay-moment source on the pure-redistribution space; it does not
exclude higher-order responses or responses outside
\(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\).

\item For each uniformly bounded family
\(\mathbf R_N\in\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\), there exist
\(\delta_0,\zeta_0,c_0,C>0\), depending on the common model bounds, the
common direction bound, and the fixed preparation datum, but not on \(N\),
such that for every \(N\ge2\), \(0<\delta<\delta_0\), and
\(|\zeta|<\zeta_0\), the corresponding selected gap has exactly one zero
\(\nu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}(\delta,\zeta)\) in
\[
 |\nu-\nu_{0,N}^{\rm mod}|<c_0,
\]
where \(\nu_{0,N}^{\rm mod}=\nu_{0,N}\) in the curvature model and
\(\nu_{0,N}^{\rm mod}=\nu_{0,N}^{\rm rec}\) in the recovery-sensing
model.  No assertion is made about zeros outside this window.  At this zero
the two retained complete RFDE histories agree.  Put
\(\mu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}
=\delta^2\nu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}\).  With
\(\mathfrak r_N\in E_N^*\) denoting the curvature or recovery return
covector and
\(\Lambda_N=\mathfrak r_N\mathsf T_N\),
\[
 \Delta_{N,\mathbf R}^{\mathbf p}(\delta,\zeta)
 :=\mu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}(\delta,\zeta)
   -\mu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}(\delta,0),
 \qquad
 \left|\Delta_{N,\mathbf R}^{\mathbf p}(\delta,\zeta)
   -\delta^3\zeta\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\right|
 \le C\{\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2\},
\]
on one parameter box with one constant for all \(N\).

\item In the case \(\Delta_\theta>0\), the observation map
\(\mathcal O_N\mathfrak r_N=\mathfrak r_N\mathsf T_N\) is injective and
\[
 \mathfrak r_N(z)
  =(\mathcal O_N\mathfrak r_N)(\mathsf Q_NA_Nz),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{cond}(\mathcal O_N|_{\operatorname{Ran}\mathcal O_N})
 \le\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma},
\]
where the range has the inherited dual norm.  Consequently limiting
normalized root increments on a probe family whose transverse images span
\(E_N\) determine the compressed return covector.  Finite-scale increments
approximate the corresponding covector evaluations with error
\(O(\delta+|\zeta|)\).  Under the additional irreducibility,
compatible-base-layer, and common unit-probe preparation hypotheses of
\cref{cor:curvature-tomography,cor:recovery-tomography}, that error bound is
uniform in \(N\) and over \(\|d_N\|_N\le1\).

\item For either model, after fixing the physical model, stationary
projection, phase and endpoint conditions, and normalization
\(\mu=\delta^2\nu\), baseline-subtracted increments from any two fixed
admissible preparation data satisfy
\[
 |\Delta^{\mathbf p_1}_{N,\mathbf R}
   -\Delta^{\mathbf p_2}_{N,\mathbf R}|
 \le C_{\mathbf p_1,\mathbf p_2}
       \{\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2\}
\]
on their common parameter box.  Here
\(C_{\mathbf p_1,\mathbf p_2}\) is independent of \(N\), but may depend on
the fixed pair of preparations.  Hence they define the same
dimension-uniform leading response germ
\([\,\delta^3\zeta\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\,]_{\rm resp}\).  For each fixed
preparation and each uniformly bounded direction family, the normalized
derivative converges to the limiting covector evaluation
\(\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\) uniformly in \(N\); no uniformity over the whole
preparation class is asserted.  Equivalently, after each selected connection curve is
centered at its own \(\zeta=0\) root and its physical-parameter difference is
divided by \(\delta^3\), its baseline conormal converges to
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\dd\xi
       -\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\,\dd\zeta\}.
\]
For the shared-resource family, if the selection rule is structural-ball
admissible in the sense of
\cref{def:structural-ball-preparation}, these raywise loci are restrictions of
one \(C^2\) Fr\'{e}chet graph on a common ball in
\(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\), and its \(\dd\xi\)-calibrated baseline conormal
satisfies
\[
 \|(1,-D_\eta\Xi^{\mathbf p}_{N,\delta}(0))-(1,-\Lambda_N)\|
 \le C\delta
\]
in the full dual norm, with one constant for all \(N\).  This conclusion is
proved by a uniform affine-line Picard-jet argument and polarization, not by
identifying compatible directional derivatives.
This weighted conormal is a statement about the selected complete-history
connection relation, not a physical outer maximal canard.  For the
shared-resource family, moreover, the exact
finite-\(\delta\) roots are not preparation independent: at every fixed
\(N_0\) and sufficiently small fixed target, an admissible localized change
of the outer completion can move the selected baseline root.
\end{enumerate}

No preparation-independent maximal-canard claim, full-network reconstruction,
or \(N\)-uniform inversion from an arbitrary finite noisy probe frame is part
of the theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the shared-resource model, exact blindness is
\cref{prop:projection-blindness}; for the recovery-sensing model the same
voltage identity holds and the modified recovery law has no direct
\(\zeta\)-dependence.  The source dichotomy and dual reconstruction are
\cref{thm:hidden-return-tomography}.  The two model-specific root statements
are \cref{thm:shared-resource,thm:sensed-recovery-response}.  The finite-scale
generator-probe conclusions, under their additional hypotheses, are
\cref{cor:curvature-tomography,cor:recovery-tomography}.  Preparation
universality for the shared-resource model is
\cref{cor:preparation-universality,thm:canonical-response-germ}; the
recovery-sensing germ is \cref{eq:sensing-canonical-germ}, the raywise
weighted-conormal realization is \cref{thm:weighted-connection-jet}, and the
shared-resource full-ball realization is
\cref{thm:joint-redistribution-conormal}.  The final
finite-section noncanonicity assertion, as stated here for the
shared-resource family, is \cref{prop:finite-section-noncanonicity}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Blind controllability and dual recovery}

Let \(E_N=\ker\pi_N^T\) be the transverse space and let
\(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\) contain delay-layer directions
\(\mathbf R=(R_0,\ldots,R_m)\) satisfying
\(\pi_N^TR_k=0\) for each layer and \(\sum_kR_k=0\).  The second identity
keeps the aggregate coupling fixed.  The leading transverse source and its
stable lift are
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S_N\mathbf R
 =\frac{K}{2\alpha}P_{\perp,N}
   \left(\sum_k\theta_kR_k\right)\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 \mathsf T_N\mathbf R=A_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N\mathbf R. \label{eq:intro-source-lift}
\end{equation}
Thus the zeroth delay moment is erased exactly, while the first moment may
survive.

Our first main result, \cref{thm:hidden-return-tomography}, proves a sharp
dichotomy.  If two delay locations differ by
\(\Delta_\theta>0\), then \(\mathsf S_N\) maps the projection-blind
redistributions onto all of \(E_N\).  An explicit two-layer right inverse
\(\mathsf Q_N\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S_N\mathsf Q_N=\Id_{E_N},
 \qquad
 \|\mathsf S_N\|\,\|\mathsf Q_N\|=1,               \label{eq:intro-sharp-source}
\end{equation}
with each norm given exactly and independently of \(N\).  After coincident
atoms are merged, a single-point delay support instead gives
\(\mathsf S_N=0\) on \(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\).  This is a sharp no-go
statement for the leading source generated by pure delay redistributions; it
does not concern moving delays, general row-neutral perturbations, or
higher-order mechanisms.  Dependence of stability on delay distributions and
their moments has a separate history \citep{kiss2010stability}; what is sharp
here is the projection-blind source map on matrix redistributions.  We use
``blind controllability'' only for this
algebraic ability of admissible parameter directions to span the leading
transverse source, not for time-dependent state control in the classical
sense \citep{hermann1977nonlinear}.

Let \(\mathfrak r_N\in E_N^*\) be the transverse return covector compressed
by the collective matching equation.  The observed response covector is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_N=\mathcal O_N\mathfrak r_N
 :=\mathfrak r_N\mathsf T_N.
\end{equation}
The same theorem gives the coordinate-free reconstruction
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak r_N(z)=\Lambda_N(\mathsf Q_NA_Nz),
 \qquad z\in E_N,                                   \label{eq:intro-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
and bounds the condition number of \(\mathcal O_N\), as an isomorphism onto
its range with the inherited dual norm, by \((2-\gamma)/\gamma\).  Once the
right inverse is known, injectivity and \cref{eq:intro-reconstruction} are
standard duality.  The mathematical content is the exactly
projection-blind source realization, its sharp two-delay inverse, and the
dimension-uniform return bounds---not a new principle of Banach-space duality.

\subsection{Nonlinear complete-history readout}

The operator statement becomes a dynamical measurement only after an
infinite-dimensional range problem is solved.  Near a collective fold we
construct an invariant RFDE history graph and attracting and repelling
one-sided traces.  A zero of their scalar gap is equality of the retained
complete RFDE histories on the phase section, not merely equality of a
projected observable.  Fix a uniformly bounded blind direction family
\(\mathbf R=(\mathbf R_N)\).  The root theorem proves
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{N,\mathbf R}^{\mathbf p}(\delta,\zeta)
 :=\mu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}(\delta,\zeta)
     -\mu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}(\delta,0)
 =\delta^3\zeta\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)
   +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),             \label{eq:intro-response}
\end{equation}
with one parameter box and one remainder constant for all admitted network
sizes and every bounded probe family.  Consequently, normalized finite-scale
root increments recover the pairings of \(\mathfrak r_N\) with error
\(C(\delta+|\zeta|)\).  This is the nonlinear bridge from
\cref{eq:intro-reconstruction} to an input--output quantity.

For generator-supported probes \(\mathsf G_N(d)\), the two model
realizations give the explicit pairings
\begin{align}
 \Lambda_N^{\rm curv}(\mathsf G_N(d))
 &=\frac{K\Delta_\theta}{2\alpha^2D}
   \langle c_N-\alpha\mathbf1,d\rangle_{\pi_N},
                                                               \label{eq:intro-curvature-pairing}\\
 \Lambda_N^{\rm rec}(\mathsf G_N(d))
 &=-\frac{K\Delta_\theta}{2\alpha D}
   \left\langle\varpi_N-\frac{c_N}{\alpha},d
   \right\rangle_{\pi_N}.                         \label{eq:intro-recovery-pairing}
\end{align}
Thus any \(N-1\) probes spanning \(E_N\) determine the relevant centered
return profile at fixed \(N\).  The pairing estimates are uniform over the
unit probe ball.  Without a uniform lower frame bound, however, we do not
claim an \(N\)-uniform condition number for reconstructing a coordinate
vector from finitely many noisy pairings.  Likewise, an absolute measurement
error in the root increment is amplified by
\(\delta^{-3}|\zeta|^{-1}\); the theorem establishes asymptotic
identifiability, not noise robustness as \(\delta\downarrow0\).

We use ``tomography'' descriptively for the recovery of this one compressed
return covector from directional root responses.  The network skeleton,
stationary weights, delay locations, and scalar coefficients are treated as
known.  We neither reconstruct the full hidden network nor infer all
coefficients of the RFDE.

\subsection{What is intrinsic, and what is not}

The scalar readout in \cref{eq:intro-response} is selected by finite
endpoints and an outer planar completion.  It would be incorrect simply to
rename its exact finite-\(\delta\) zero an intrinsic maximal canard.
For the shared-resource family,
\cref{prop:finite-section-noncanonicity} constructs localized admissible
changes of the artificial completion which leave the physical RFDE, retained
fold tube, phase row, and endpoint equation fixed but move that exact root.
This is an obstruction result, not a missing uniqueness proof.

There is nevertheless a relative-canonical object within a precisely fixed
comparison class.  Hold fixed the physical RFDE family, stationary projection, phase and
endpoint conditions, and normalization \(\mu=\delta^2\nu\).  Subtract each
admissible preparation's own \(\zeta=0\) baseline.  For any two fixed
preparations, \cref{thm:canonical-response-germ} gives
\begin{equation}
 [\Delta_{N,\mathbf R}^{\mathbf p}]_{\rm resp}
 =[(\delta,\zeta)\mapsto
    \delta^3\zeta\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)]_{\rm resp}, \label{eq:intro-canonical-germ}
\end{equation}
on their common parameter box, and
\(\delta^{-3}\partial_\zeta
\mu_{c,N}^{\mathbf p,\mathbf R_N}(\delta,0)
\to\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\)
uniformly in \(N\).  In the weighted coordinate
\[
 \xi=\delta^{-3}\{\mu-\mu^{\mathbf p}_{c,N}(\delta,0)\},
\]
the selected connection graph satisfies
\(\xi=\zeta\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)
 +O(\delta|\zeta|+\zeta^2)\); its baseline tangent has slope
\(\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)+O(\delta)\), and its conormal converges to
\(\operatorname{span}\{\dd\xi-
\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\dd\zeta\}\).  For the shared-resource family,
\cref{thm:joint-redistribution-conormal} proves, rather than infers from those
raywise limits, that before restriction to a ray the tangent hyperplane is
the kernel of the cokernel-valued map
\[
 (\xi,R)\longmapsto
 \xi[f_{\nu,N}]
 +[\mathsf B_NL_{\perp,N}^{-1}\mathsf S_NR].
\]
This formulation removes the arbitrary scalar representative of the
collective cokernel.  The baseline-subtracted response germ, limiting
covector, and weighted conormal are preparation independent in the stated
relative sense; the finite-section representative root is not.  They remain
objects of the selected local connection problem until physical outer
history families are constructed and compared with it.  The quantitative
criterion \cref{prop:physical-weighted-c1-transfer} isolates the direct
obligation: a preparation-free physical root whose weighted structural
derivative agrees with that of the selected root.  One sufficient gap-level
route additionally asks that the two root locations converge and that their
defining gaps differ by \(o(\delta)\) in the \(\nu\)-gap slope and by
\(o(\delta^2)\) in the structural derivative.

\subsection{Two inequivalent return channels}

The shared-resource model returns the transverse history through heterogeneous
fast curvature:
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak r_N^{\rm curv}(h)
 =-\frac1\alpha\pi_N^T(c_N\circ h).
                                                               \label{eq:intro-curvature-return}
\end{equation}
Its response is nonzero on the full blind redistribution space exactly when
the node curvatures are not stationary-homogeneous.  The second RFDE family
changes the recovery law and has
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak r_N^{\rm rec}(h)
 =\left\langle\varpi_N-\frac{c_N}{\alpha},h
   \right\rangle_{\pi_N}.                           \label{eq:intro-sensing-return}
\end{equation}
With \(c_N=\alpha\mathbf1\), the curvature channel vanishes identically
while heterogeneous recovery sensing remains active.  For each fixed \(N\),
varying the positive sensing weights realizes every nonzero ray in \(E_N^*\).
This makes the second family a genuinely different return operator, not a
renaming of the first model.

The generator probes are supported on \(\Id+P_N\) and therefore preserve the
support of the chosen base layers
\(B_{0,N}=B_{m,N}=(\Id+P_N)/2\).  For sufficiently small signed
amplitudes the perturbed layers remain entrywise nonnegative; for an open
dense set of directions the nonzero probe layers have rank \(N-1\).  Sparse directed
reset--cycle networks then give \(O(N)\)-edge realizations with common
Dobrushin gap, uniformly nonzero response, and no nontrivial synchrony
polydiagonal in the curvature family.  Thus the result is not an arbitrary-size
copy of a lower-dimensional synchronous fold-matching problem.

\subsection{Relation to existing theory and the novelty boundary}

Exact lumpability requires the full vector field to descend to a closed
quotient, equivalently to intertwine full and quotient flows under appropriate
hypotheses \citep{horstmeyer2016lumpability}.
\Cref{eq:intro-blindness} is weaker in a different direction: it compares two
projected vector-field functionals at the same unreduced history, but the
common functional need not factor through the projected history.  We prove
projection blindness, not exact lumpability.  Nor does same-history equality
assert invariance of an entire measured output trajectory under parameter
changes, as in structural unidentifiability or dynamical compensation
\citep{bearup2013inputoutput,villaverde2017dynamical}.  Mori--Zwanzig theory supplies a
broad conceptual precedent for unresolved modes returning through memory
\citep{chorin2002optimal,herrera2020tractable}; here the return is instead a
deterministic first-delay-moment source, a stable transverse inverse, and a
one-dimensional matching cokernel.

Writing a first-order splitting displacement as a normal-bundle section is
already a canonical construction in Melnikov theory
\citep{lomeli2008canonical}.  Accordingly,
\cref{thm:weighted-connection-jet} is not presented as a new principle merely
because it replaces a scalar defining function by a conormal line.  Its role
is to show that the nonlinear selected connection curves realize the same
cokernel-valued object as the blind-source factorization and that this object
retains the two-delay and network-size-uniform recovery structure.  A
preparation-free physical connection manifold would require the additional
outer-history theorem and weighted \(C^1\) selected-to-physical comparison.
\Cref{prop:physical-weighted-c1-transfer} records the formal naturality under
an exact parameter-coherent section holonomy; constructing and verifying such
a physical holonomy for this model remain outside the present theorem.

Adjoint sensitivity already represents scalar response derivatives as
covector pairings, including for functional differential systems with
time lags \citep{cacuci1981sensitivity,rihan2003sensitivity}, and sufficiently many
directional measurements can of course recover a finite-dimensional
functional.  Network reconstruction from response dynamics
\citep{timme2007revealing}, from delayed noisy interactions
\citep{zhang2019reconstruction}, and from long noisy time series
\citep{pereira2025robust} addresses the more ambitious problem of recovering
interaction structure, delays, or dynamics.  We make no such claim.  The
principal conjunction here is narrower: admissible probes annihilate the entire
stationary projection at every delay atom and preserve aggregate coupling;
two separated delays nevertheless span the transverse source with sharp
dimension-free cost; and a nonlinear complete-history root reads out the
resulting covector with a uniform remainder.  The Schur elimination,
one-dimensional cokernel projection, and dual reconstruction after a right
inverse are standard operations and are not presented as new principles.

Observability and parameter identifiability for nonlinear delay systems ask
whether states, histories, or model parameters can be recovered from an
input--output behavior
\citep{xia2002analysis,zhang2006parameter,anguelova2010analytic,verduynlunel2001parameter}.
Our result is not an observability test and does not recover the delay layers,
delay locations, or an arbitrary RFDE state.  The network skeleton and
coefficients are known; the probes recover only the finite-dimensional return
covector compressed by the chosen fold-matching equation.  Equality of the
two retained complete histories at a selected root is a zero-fiber property
of that matching construction, not analytic observability of the underlying
delay system.

The fold chart and singular orbit are classical planar canard objects
\citep{krupa2001relaxation,krupa2001extending}.  Canard explosions and more
complicated canard-induced oscillations occur in delay equations
\citep{campbell2009delay,krupa2016complex,krupa2016explosion}, and local
infinite-dimensional fast--slow theory includes DDE examples
\citep{avitabile2020local}.  Network canards arise in consensus,
nonlinear-diffusion, adaptive mean-field, and tipping settings
\citep{jardon2020consensus,bonetto2024nonlinear,balzer2024cascading,qin2023tipping}.
We do not claim a first network canard, a preparation-independent maximal
canard, or a new infinite-dimensional center-manifold theorem.
\Cref{prop:unprepared-outer-skeleton} is a model-specific,
dimension-uniform critical-layer calculation.
\Cref{prop:frozen-voltage-history-splitting} upgrades its voltage layer to a
positive-\(\delta\), fixed-history-space splitting with a capped rate; the
resource is frozen, and the theorem is neither a nonautonomous RFDE slow
manifold nor a physical outer-history construction.  The exact uncut
invariance equation and the quotient identities in
\cref{prop:exact-outer-history-equation,prop:physical-history-phase-quotient,%
prop:exact-resource-gauge-quotient} show what changes once a genuine monotone
history is available: time translation supplies the transported longitudinal
line, and an adapted resource gauge removes the singular resource coupling
through an \(O(S_\delta^{-1})\) Volterra history correction.  These identities
are conditional at the level of an arbitrary tracker.  The canonical
fixed-section zero is only the zero-gauge member of a larger class:
\cref{thm:raw-gauge-fixed-section-zero} admits a nonempty,
infinite-dimensional gauge ball of small raw-compatible attracting and
repelling boundary data, proves a uniform zero for every such gauge, and
gives its first gauge jet, fixed-graph Lipschitz dependence, strict speed
sign, and exact forward RFDE reconstruction.  For every zero in that ball,
\cref{cor:generated-interior-canonical-tracker} verifies the tracker
hypotheses on each generated subsegment whose full delay collar lies beyond
the first maximal delay.  Thus the quotient and Volterra coordinates there
belong to an exact RFDE orbit rather than a formal history graph.  No common
chart is yet used to compare the reparameterized trackers for two gauges.
This interior realization does not supply flat forgetting, physical branch
endpoints, a parameter-coherent moving-event family, a past-complete history,
or a connection between the two branches.

The dimension-uniform range estimates use Dobrushin contraction in the
oscillation seminorm \citep{gaubert2015dobrushin}, in the context of a broad
literature on delayed consensus
\citep{moreau2004consensus,munz2010delay,atay2013consensus,haskovec2021consensus,proskurnikov2023delay}.
Fredholm and Lin methods already provide finite-codimension matching theories
for RFDEs and mixed-type equations
\citep{hale1986heteroclinic,lin1986exponential,malletparet1999fredholm,hupkes2009lin}.
Our range construction is model specific: it combines a Dobrushin-controlled
complete-history graph, selected traces, and a finite-section estimate.  It is
not a new general Fredholm or Lin theorem.  Synchrony polydiagonals are
organized by balanced relations and common invariant subspaces
\citep{golubitsky2005patterns,neuberger2020invariant}; the sparse curvature
family rules them out by distinct node curvatures.

\subsection{Proof architecture and organization}

The proof has five load-bearing steps.  First, stationary-row cancellation
gives exact blindness.  Second, the Dobrushin gap supplies a dimension-free
transverse inverse, while two delay atoms give the sharp source right inverse.
Third, a special-flow transform constructs an injective invariant graph of
complete RFDE histories and two selected traces.  Fourth, Gaussian cokernel
pairings and a finite-section error estimate transfer the whole-line return
coefficient to the nonlinear root.  Fifth, the structural-ball Fr\'{e}chet
estimate identifies the selected shared-resource connection hyperplane with
the kernel of the Schur map and its limiting conormal with
\((1,-\Lambda_N)\).  The two model calculations identify the curvature and
recovery covectors, while the localized bump construction proves exact-root
noncanonicity for the shared-resource family.

\Cref{sec:model-results} defines the shared-resource model and states the
blind-controllability, root-readout, tomography, and obstruction results.
\Cref{sec:abstract-response} records the supporting Schur and range-to-root
machinery.  \Cref{sec:sensed-recovery} supplies the second return channel.
\Cref{sec:network-mechanism} proves the model-specific graph, trace, and
coefficient calculations.  After the main proof,
\cref{sec:unprepared-outer-skeleton} records a preparation-independent
critical curve, a positive-\(\delta\) frozen-voltage full-history splitting,
truncated reduced actions, the exact uncut tracker equation, and two exact
realizations of the quotient along any genuine monotone tracker.  It also
shows why the unquotiented voltage--resource system has the wrong unstable
count, and gives an exact counterexample showing why convergence in an
unnormalized history norm and a phase row do not by themselves select a tame
RFDE history.  The same section proves the fixed-delay fold-time normal form,
a compatible endpoint-history chart, and a fixed-phase \(C^0\)
Green--collar inverse for a prescribed RFDE history segment.  Its boundary
traces are \((Z(0),p(0))\) on the attracting branch and
\((Z(0),p(S_r))\) on the repelling branch.  The last result is only the
\((Z,p)\) normal block.  The scalar phase--event core has an explicit
dimension-uniform inverse in the naturally scaled event-time variable, but
the phase displacement is not part of a causal echo contraction.  On the
attracting outer buffer, an exact current/positive-delay splitting and a
finite-generation argument now flush arbitrary strong collar data into a
genuine order-\(d\) slow exit trace, with a dimension-uniform operator norm
tending to zero.  On the repelling branch, the current loop is first
resummed into forward transverse and future scalar component kernels; a
decorated-word/first-exit argument then flushes both the inner-history and
terminal-scalar components into genuine order-\(d\) handoff histories, again
with normalized operator norms tending to zero uniformly in \(N\).
This closes the fixed-parameter, fixed-phase component bootstrap on both
outer branches.  The raw-compatible repelling \(p(0)\)-to-collar feedback
is now closed in the same high-order handoff class, including the
feedback-generated history cross-column at the terminal window.  Moreover,
the algebraically reconstructed normal speed maps into the zeroth-order
phase-action space with norm
\(O(r_{\rm out}^2+\delta^{2-2\vartheta})\), even for endpoint boundary
layers.  In the reverse direction, exact differentiation of the reduced
delay row produces a phase-delay operator of size
\(O(\delta\{1+\log(r_{\rm out}/\rho_\delta)\})\) in phase action; it
annihilates constant phase exactly and gives a dimension-uniform inverse for
the formal delayed phase--event core.  The same calculation identifies the
two phase-to-normal sources, including the nonzero affine-residual column.
The exact shift and structured Green estimate in
\cref{prop:phase-delay-action-shift,prop:structured-phase-action-green}
show that this reverse phase column need not be uniformly bounded in the old
normalized state norm, while its reconstructed true-speed action is
\(O(r_{\rm out}^2+\delta/S_\delta)\).
An event-aligned fixed-\(r\) trace theorem removes the scaled event-time
unknown from the normal terminal rows at the reduced base and proves the
sharp \(\delta^{-2}\) strong-norm loss incurred by differentiating a raw
moving trace instead.
At a nonzero terminal base,
\cref{lem:terminal-relative-trace-scale-chart} upgrades this calculation to
an exact nonlinear strong-to-weak \(C^2\) moving-window coordinate package.
It retains the event displacement as a coordinate, makes the fixed physical
section a derived event row, preserves the nonzero terminal columns, and
records all mixed second derivatives together with the
\(\delta^{-2}\)/\(\delta^{-4}\) translation losses.  It assumes an already
available strong terminal buffer and does not construct a parameterized
solution family or a moving-event inverse.
\Cref{lem:raw-phase-boundary-column-assembly,%
thm:raw-compatible-phase-normal-inverse} then assemble the raw-compatible
entry-collar column, retain the affine-residual bulk response, and close a
dimension-uniform zeroth-order phase--normal inverse on each fixed reduced
branch in a graph/action norm.  The total Schur action is
\(O(r_{\rm out}^2+\delta/S_\delta+\delta^{2-2\vartheta})\).
Independently, a preparatory refinement shows that the base raw-collar
correction itself is smaller:
\(O(\varepsilon|e|)\), hence \(O(\delta^3S_\delta)\) at either inner
section, although its moving-endpoint derivative remains sharply
\(O(\varepsilon)\).  This base estimate is not used to replace the sharp
moving-endpoint column in the Schur inverse.  To remove the constant-phase
obstruction in the scalar phase coordinate, an inner-anchored
\(q_0\)-flow chart is constructed next.
Its phase action is exact, it preserves the sign and orientation of both
outer branches for a prescribed pulled-back scalar speed residual in the
natural weighted class, and its one-row relative-phase linearization has a
dimension-uniform fixed-base Schur inverse.  In that relative norm the
fixed-base linear raw true-speed action is
\[
 O\!\left(r_{\rm out}
   \{r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-2}+\delta^{2-2\vartheta}\}\right).
\]
Finally, the delay profile itself is \(O(\delta)\) in the relative phase
norm.  Feeding its exact original-variable sources through the closed normal
Green inverse recovers the previously unavailable normalized state and
pointwise true action, both with the sharper factor
\(O(r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-1})\).  This yields a uniformly equivalent
relative graph--action norm.  The unrestricted old-state obstruction remains
valid; the recovery uses essentially the fold-side anchor
\(|a(r)|\le |r|\|a\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}\).
For the same formal \(q_0\)-flow history, the exact nonlinear delay
functional is then identified.  Its linearization is precisely the delayed
phase column, while its value and piecewise derivative have a two-point
quadratic remainder of size
\(O(\delta|r|\|a\|_{\phi,\mathrm{rel}}^2)\).
Together these results remove the constant-phase obstruction at the
fixed-base linear level and close the formal phase-only delay nonlinearity.
\Cref{lem:second-jet-raw-history-assembler} then constructs the nonlinear
raw-compatible old-history assembler directly in a fixed-reference chart.
It covers the four closed endpoint collars, supplies the second endpoint
jet, agrees with the original moving-weight collar chart on their overlap,
and has a dimension-uniform normalized quadratic graph remainder.  It is a
compatible history segment, not a past orbit.
\Cref{thm:fixed-section-nonlinear-graph-residual} then assembles this
boundary chart with the exact phase-delay functional and the nonlinear
resource-defect equations on one common graph--action space.  The resulting
fixed-parameter, inner-anchored residual is \(C^1\), its differential is
exactly the already inverted raw-compatible Schur block, its base defect is
\(O(r_{\rm out}+S_\delta^{-2})\), and its nonlinear remainder satisfies a
dimension-uniform two-point quadratic estimate.
\Cref{cor:fixed-section-canonical-zero} solves that residual pointwise at
each fixed admissible parameter pair.  On each outer branch it gives a
unique small zero in the canonical graph slice, its first Newton jet, a
strict speed sign, and an exact finite forward segment of the unmodified
RFDE.  \Cref{thm:raw-gauge-fixed-section-zero} replaces the zero-core boundary
choice by a nonempty, infinite-dimensional ball of small raw-compatible
attracting and repelling gauges.  Uniform contraction and the gauge implicit
function theorem give one zero in every gauge slice, its first gauge jet,
fixed-graph Lipschitz dependence, inverse persistence, the same speed sign,
and exact raw-RFDE reconstruction.  Each sufficiently deep generated
subsegment is then reparameterized as an exact tracker by
\cref{cor:generated-interior-canonical-tracker}.  This is a pointwise
statement on a gauge-dependent interval, not a common-chart comparison or a
flat-forgetting theorem.  It supplies neither parameter derivatives nor a
moving-event family, and no compatible collar is shown to be cut from a past
orbit.  Thus no cross-branch handoff, physical connection, or canard root
follows.  The phase-quotiented nonautonomous normal dichotomy also remains
open.  Detailed history-graph and finite-section bookkeeping is placed in
the supplementary material.
