\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

In a retarded differential equation, the state at time \(t\) is an entire
history segment.  This distinction becomes decisive in an orbit-matching
problem near a fold: two present states can agree while the delayed
information that later returns to the critical direction remains different.
We therefore call a parameter value a \emph{selected complete-history root}
when an attracting and a repelling one-sided trace, constructed on the same
invariant history graph, agree as retained RFDE histories on a fixed phase
section.  Their outer tail levels and cutoffs are fixed by an admissible
preparation datum.  No uniqueness is built into that datum: the
finite-parameter root is consequently a selected
object; the result below shows that its leading response coefficient is shared
by any two fixed admissible preparations even though their baseline roots need
not agree.  The labels attracting and repelling refer to the two singular
fold sides used in this local selection; they do not assert that the traces
come from independently prescribed outer slow manifolds.

The phenomenon studied here is a failure of projection identifiability.  A
structural perturbation changes each delay layer in a direction annihilated by
the stationary row of a directed Markov network.  Hence, at every full vector
history, the stationary projection of the RFDE right-hand side is exactly the
same before and after perturbation.  Nevertheless, the selected root moves.
The information lost under projection first enters the stable transverse
history, passes through a dimension-uniform Markov resolvent, and is then
returned to the scalar matching equation by heterogeneous curvature.

\subsection{Relation to existing canard and network theory}

The fold chart and singular orbit used below come from planar canard theory
\citep{krupa2001relaxation,krupa2001extending}.  Canard explosions and more
complicated canard-induced oscillations have been established in concrete
delay equations \citep{campbell2009delay,krupa2016complex,krupa2016explosion},
and \citet{avitabile2020local} developed a local theory for spatio-temporal
canards with infinite-dimensional fast dynamics.  Our question is different:
can two network families with identical stationary-projected vector-field
functionals have different complete-history matching roots?

Canards and canard-type tipping on networks have been studied on consensus
manifolds, nonlinear diffusion networks, adaptive mean-field networks, and
through collective reductions
\citep{jardon2020consensus,bonetto2024nonlinear,qin2023tipping,
balzer2024cascading,laurence2019spectral,thibeault2020threefold,
masuda2022dimension,ma2023generalized,liu2026predicting}.  Collective
reductions are powerful when the discarded modes are slaved without returning
new information to the selected observable.  Our result isolates an
obstruction to that premise: stationary projection and invariant-history
reduction need not commute.  The explicit family has pairwise distinct node
curvatures, no nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal, and a transverse amplitude
bounded below along the matched root orbit.  Thus the conclusion is not a
synchronous copy of a scalar canard.

There is a complementary warning in projection-based model reduction.
Eliminating unresolved network variables generally produces memory terms,
and nonlinear projection methods can use those terms to recover transients
that a memoryless reduction loses 
\citep{herrera2020tractable}.  That literature reconstructs reduced dynamics;
our question is an identifiability question for a parameter selected by an
orbit-matching problem.  We impose the stronger identity that the projected
vector-field functional itself is unchanged at every full history, and then
show that the selected root still changes.  Thus the new issue is not merely
that projection creates memory, but that two structurally different memory
channels can be indistinguishable to the projected vector field and yet be
distinguished by a complete-history root.

Synchrony polydiagonals and the associated quotient networks are classically
organized by balanced equivalence relations or, more generally, common
invariant synchrony subspaces of the coupling matrices
\citep{golubitsky2005patterns,neuberger2020invariant}.  We use this standard
notion in the explicit witness: pairwise-distinct curvature terms violate
tangency to every polydiagonal containing two different nodes, even though
the coupling itself has a collective Markov component.

The analytic tools have classical ancestors.  The Dobrushin coefficient is
the contraction ratio of a stochastic operator in the oscillation seminorm
\citep{gaubert2015dobrushin}, and delayed consensus estimates are available
for several directed and scalable network classes
\citep{moreau2004consensus,munz2010delay,atay2013consensus,
haskovec2021consensus,proskurnikov2023delay}.  Fredholm and Lin-type matching
for functional differential equations is also established
\citep{hale1986heteroclinic,lin1986exponential,malletparet1999fredholm,
hupkes2009lin}.  We use these ideas as tools rather than claim a new
contraction or Fredholm principle.  The main result package couples four
facts: exact blindness of the stationary-projected RFDE, construction
of the complete-history root, a transverse-return coefficient with uniform
remainder, and a sharp algebraic classification of the perturbation
directions for which that coefficient vanishes.

\subsection{Projection blindness and the main theorem}

Let \(\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}\) be the full history vector field and
\(\Pi_N(x,y)=(\pi_N^Tx,y)\) the stationary output projection.  Atomwise row
neutrality gives the exact functional identity
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}
 =\Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,0}.                    \label{eq:intro-blindness}
\end{equation}
This is stronger than cancellation of a first-order coefficient: it holds
for every full history and every admitted \(\zeta\).  It does not make the
projected dynamics closed, because the projected nonlinear field still
depends on the transverse components of that history.

Under a common Dobrushin gap, fixed delay support, uniformly bounded positive
curvatures, and one fixed admissible preparation datum, \cref{thm:shared-resource}
constructs exactly one root in a common local window for every finite network
size.  If \(\mu=\delta^2\nu\), then
\begin{equation}
 \mu^{\rm can}_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)
 =\mu^{\rm can}_{c,N}(\delta,0)
   +\mathscr C_N\delta^3\zeta
   +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),                \label{eq:intro-response}
\end{equation}
uniformly in \(N\), where
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr C_N
 =-\frac{K}{2\alpha^2}\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}
     \left(\sum_k\theta_kR_{k,N}\right)\mathbf1.      \label{eq:intro-coefficient}
\end{equation}
When \(\inf_N|\mathscr C_N|>0\),
\cref{eq:intro-blindness,eq:intro-response} exhibit two systems with the
same projected vector-field functional and different selected roots.  This is
the precise projection non-identifiability conclusion.

The coefficient is not imposed as a hypothesis of the existence theorem.
\Cref{thm:response-directions} classifies it.  For a fixed heterogeneous
network, the leading coefficient's kernel in the stationary-row-neutral
direction space is a closed codimension-one hyperplane; the complementary
nonzero directions are open and dense.  The same is true under the additional pure-redistribution constraint
\(\sum_kR_{k,N}=0\) when two delays are distinct.  An explicit witness gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr C_N=-\frac{K}{2\alpha^2}
 \left\{\pi_N^T(c_N^{\circ2})-\alpha^2\right\},        \label{eq:intro-variance}
\end{equation}
so curvature heterogeneity is exactly what allows a hidden transverse history
to return to the selected scalar gap at leading order in this tangent class.

The explicit sequence following \cref{thm:response-directions} strengthens
this fixed-network genericity in two ways.  Its coefficient is the same
nonzero number for every \(N\), and it stays uniformly separated from zero
on a fixed-radius ball in the supremum of the network direction norms.  At
slow time \(s=-1\), the transverse part of the matched voltage history has
size at least \(\sigma\delta^2/(4D)\), uniformly in \(N\).  Hence the algebraic
response is realized by a genuinely asynchronous orbit, not only by a formal
derivative at a collective state.

The word ``selected'' remains essential.  The fixed datum fixes the
tail levels, cutoffs, normal extension, and phase convention.  We do not prove
that two preparations have the same finite-\(\delta\) baseline root.  We do
prove in \cref{cor:preparation-universality} that any two fixed admissible
preparations have the same leading increment in \(\zeta\), up to the uniform
remainder in \cref{eq:intro-response}.  The response coefficient is therefore
preparation universal at leading order even though the selected baseline is
not claimed to be.

\subsection{Proof mechanism and scope}

The proof has four analytic steps.  A Dobrushin estimate gives a
dimension-free exponential inverse on the stationary transverse space.  An
exact fold chart and a special-flow contraction construct an invariant
complete-history graph together with the finite state and parameter jets
needed for the response.  One-sided Gaussian Green operators solve the
trace, phase, and endpoint problems with constants independent of network
size.  Only after this range problem is solved do we pair the reduced field
with its adjoint.  Row neutrality cancels the direct projected delay term;
the delay moment, transverse resolvent, and curvature pairing then give
\cref{eq:intro-coefficient}.  Injectivity of the history graph upgrades
equality of the reduced phase-section states to equality of the retained RFDE
histories.  \Cref{fig:root-mechanism} displays this geometry and response
chain.

The theorem does not assert the first network canard, a preparation-
independent maximal canard, or a physical pulse threshold.  It treats finite
networks with a common mixing gap and fixed delay support; moving delay atoms,
a closing gap, infinite networks, and arbitrary preparation comparison require
additional arguments.  \Cref{sec:model-results} defines the network class and
states the blindness, root, genericity, universality, robustness, and
asynchrony results.  \Cref{sec:abstract-response} isolates the reusable
range-to-root implication.  \Cref{sec:network-mechanism} proves the
model-specific range and transverse-return mechanism.  The complete
special-flow contraction and finite-section gap bookkeeping are supplied in
\cref{subsec:invariant-history-details,subsec:finite-gap-bridge}, while
\cref{sec:conclusion} records the resulting mathematical principle and its
boundary.
