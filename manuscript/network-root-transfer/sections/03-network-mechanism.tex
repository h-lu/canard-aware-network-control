\section{The hidden transverse-response mechanism}\label{sec:network-mechanism}

\subsection{Invariant histories and the transverse-return mechanism}
\label{subsec:shared-proof}

We prove the analytic steps used in \cref{thm:shared-resource}.  The graph
and trace problems below are part of the theorem, not additional hypotheses.
They construct the fixed-preparation selected planar histories for the heterogeneous
shared-resource network.  The two endpoint
levels are exactly \(\mathscr H_\alpha=0\), as fixed in
\cref{def:canonical-preparation}; arbitrary outer levels, outer RFDE slow
manifolds, or alternative graph preparations are not included.

\subsubsection{Exact chart and dimension-free model fit}

For a complete chart history \(\phi\), write
\[
 \cL_{N,\zeta}[\phi](s)
 =\sum_{k=0}^m(B_{k,N}+\zeta R_{k,N})
       \{\phi(s)-\phi(s-\theta_k)\}.
\]
Set
\[
 g_N=A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}c_N,
 \qquad Z_N(X,h)=g_NX^2+h.
\]
Substitution of the fold chart preceding \cref{eq:shared-gap} into
\cref{eq:shared-rfde-v,eq:shared-rfde-w} gives the following identities,
with no Taylor remainder:
\begin{align}
 X'={}&Y-\alpha X^2\notag\\
 &+\delta\left[-2X\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)Z_N
          -\frac\beta3X^3
          +K\pi_N^T\cL_{N,\zeta}[\mathbf1X]\right]\notag\\
 &+\delta^2\left[-\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)Z_N^{\circ2}
          +K\pi_N^T\cL_{N,\zeta}[Z_N]\right]
 -\delta^3\beta X\pi_N^TZ_N^{\circ2}
 -\frac{\delta^4\beta}{3}\pi_N^TZ_N^{\circ3},
                                                               \label{eq:shared-exact-X}\\
 Y'={}&-X+\delta\nu,                                  \label{eq:shared-exact-Y}\\
 \delta h'={}&A_Nh\notag\\
 &+\delta\left\{P_{\perp,N}\left[-2X\diag(c_N)Z_N
               +K\cL_{N,\zeta}[\mathbf1X]\right]
               -2g_NX X'\right\}\notag\\
 &+\delta^2P_{\perp,N}\left[-\diag(c_N)Z_N^{\circ2}
             -\beta X^2Z_N+K\cL_{N,\zeta}[Z_N]\right]
 -\delta^3\beta X P_{\perp,N}Z_N^{\circ2}
 -\frac{\delta^4\beta}{3}P_{\perp,N}Z_N^{\circ3}.
                                                               \label{eq:shared-exact-h}
\end{align}
The term \(-2g_NXX'\) is the derivative of the translation \(g_NX^2\).
The choice of \(g_N\) is forced by
\(A_Ng_NX^2=P_{\perp,N}c_NX^2\); hence the singular stable graph in these
coordinates is \(h=0\), and the reduced singular field is
\[
 q_0(X,Y)=(Y-\alpha X^2,-X)^T.
\]

We record why the constants in this exact chart do not hide a dependence on
the number of nodes.  The Poisson expansion and Dobrushin contraction give
\begin{equation}
 \norm{e^{A_Nt}}_{E_N\to E_N}\le e^{-D\gamma t},
 \qquad \norm{A_N^{-1}}_{E_N\to E_N}\le(D\gamma)^{-1}.
                                                               \label{eq:poisson-bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover, in the norm \cref{eq:shared-norm},
\begin{equation}
 \norm{P_{c,N}},\norm{P_{\perp,N}}\le1,\qquad
 \norm{x}_\infty\le\norm{x}_N,\qquad
 \norm{x_1\circ\cdots\circ x_j}_N
 \le3\prod_{\ell=1}^j\norm{x_\ell}_N.               \label{eq:shared-products}
\end{equation}
Indeed, subtracting \((\pi_N^Tx)\mathbf1\) does not change oscillation,
and a coordinate differs from the \(\pi_N\)-average by at most
\(\osc x\).  The last inequality follows by first estimating each
coordinate and then using \(\norm{y}_N\le3\norm{y}_\infty\).
Consequently \cref{eq:shared-curvature,eq:shared-layer-bounds},
\cref{eq:poisson-bound}, and \cref{eq:shared-products} bound every
multilinear derivative in \cref{eq:shared-exact-X,eq:shared-exact-h} by one
constant independent of \(N\) on a common chart box.

For later use, put
\[
 \cX_N=\R^2\times E_N,
 \qquad
 \cY_N=\cX_N^{m+2}
\]
with maximum product norms.  Current evaluation and the \(m+1\) fixed
delay evaluations are represented by the fixed atomic measure
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf M_N=J_{-1}\delta_0+\sum_{k=0}^mJ_k\delta_{-\theta_k},
 \qquad \norm{\mathbf M_N}_{\rm TV}\le m+2,           \label{eq:shared-evaluation-TV}
\end{equation}
where every insertion \(J_k\) has norm one.  The matrices depending on
\(\zeta\) remain in the polynomial nonlinearities, so the evaluation
measure is parameter independent.  This avoids differentiating moving
Dirac masses and makes its affine-history anchor derivatives identically
zero.

Along the singular orbit, let
\[
 L_0(U,V)=(U'-sU-V,V'+U).
\]
We specify the spaces used for the receding one-sided trace problems.  Put
\(R=R_\delta\), \(J_a=[-R,0]\), and \(J_r=[0,R]\).  Fix a Green loss
\(d_0\le4\), and choose \(c>c_{\rm loss}\) and
\(M>m_{\rm loss}+d_0+2\), where \(c_{\rm loss},m_{\rm loss}\) dominate the
finite tame losses of the prepared field.  For
\(\sigma\in\{a,r\}\), define
\begin{align}
 \norm{\xi}_{\mathcal X_\sigma}
 &=\sup_{s\in J_\sigma}
   e^{-c|s|}\langle s\rangle^{-M}|\xi(s)|,\notag\\
 \norm f_{\mathcal Y_\sigma}
 &=\sup_{s\in J_\sigma}
   e^{-c|s|}\langle s\rangle^{-(M-d_0)}|f(s)|,       \label{eq:trace-strong-spaces}\\
 |\beta|_{\partial,\sigma}
 &=e^{R^2/2-cR}\langle R\rangle^{-(M-d_0)}|\beta|.  \label{eq:trace-boundary-norm}
\end{align}
Let
\[
 \mathcal D_\sigma
 =\{\xi\in C^1(J_\sigma;\R^2):
       \xi\in\mathcal X_\sigma,\ L_0\xi\in\mathcal Y_\sigma\}
\]
carry its graph norm, and let \(\R_{\partial,\sigma}\) denote the scalar
endpoint space with norm \cref{eq:trace-boundary-norm}.  The weaker Gaussian
pairing norm
\begin{equation}
 \norm g_{G;\widehat c,\widehat m,J}
 =\int_J e^{-s^2/2+\widehat c|s|}
          \langle s\rangle^{\widehat m}|g(s)|\,\dd s   \label{eq:trace-gaussian-space}
\end{equation}
will be used only for the scalar gap.  The strong source space embeds
continuously into this pairing space with a constant independent of \(R\);
the nonlinear trace fixed point is not taken in the Gaussian \(L^1\) norm.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform graph and trace range]
\label[lemma]{lem:shared-graph-trace}
Fix the common bounds in \cref{thm:shared-resource} and one datum
\(\mathbf p_{\rm can}\) from \cref{def:canonical-preparation}.  There are
\(\delta_*,\zeta_*,C,c,C_{\rm tr}>0\) and an integer \(M\),
depending on those data but independent of \(N\), with the following
properties.

For \(0<\delta<\delta_*\), \(\nu\in I_\nu\), and
\(|\zeta|<\zeta_*\), let
\(S_\delta=\sqrt{2p\log(1/\delta)}\).  On a buffered tube about
\(\gamma_0([-S_\delta,S_\delta])\), the exact system
\cref{eq:shared-exact-X,eq:shared-exact-h} has an injective invariant
complete-history graph \(h=H_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}(X,Y)\).  On every fixed
compact subset of the fold chart,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{N,\nu,\zeta}\|H_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}\|_N\le C_K\delta.
                                                               \label{eq:shared-graph-local-size}
\end{equation}
Its reduced field has the expansion
\begin{equation}
 Q_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}
 =q_0+\delta q_{1,N}+\delta^2q_{2,N}
       +\delta^3\mathcal R_{3,N},                     \label{eq:shared-graph-expansion}
\end{equation}
and, for the finite derivative list \(a\le3\), \(i\le2\), \(j\le2\),
\begin{equation}
 \abs{D_u^a\partial_\nu^i\partial_\zeta^j
       \mathcal R_{3,N}(\gamma_0(s)+\xi)}
 \le C\langle s\rangle^M e^{c|s|}                   \label{eq:shared-graph-remainder}
\end{equation}
on the nested retained tube.  The same growing-tube envelope holds for
\(q_{1,N}\) and \(q_{2,N}\), with the state and mixed parameter derivatives
in this list, as stated in \cref{eq:invdet-coefficient-envelope}; moreover,
\(\partial_\zeta q_{1,N}=0\).  The graph equation and its displayed mixed
derivatives form a finite block-lower-triangular prolonged system.  Every
diagonal block has inverse norm at most two.  In the explicit scaled block
norm \cref{eq:invdet-prolonged-norm}, the full finite prolonged inverse has
norm at most four, uniformly in \(N\) and \(\delta\).

Writing \(z^\sigma=z^{\sigma,\rm can}_N\) for the traces generated by the
fixed datum, the prepared tail conditions
\(\mathscr H_\alpha(z^a(-R))=\mathscr H_\alpha(z^r(R))=0\), where
\(R=S_\delta+B\) and \(B>2\Theta_*+2\) is fixed, together with the phase
\(X^a(0)=X^r(0)=0\), select unique one-sided traces.  Their retained central
pieces are exact histories on the unprepared graph, and their trace--phase--
normalized-endpoint linearizations are bounded isomorphisms with inverse norm
at most \(C_{\rm tr}\).  These inverses act on graph-tangent planar and
history sources; no inverse on the ambient RFDE history space is asserted.
In the tame weighted norms used in the proof,
\begin{align}
 \norm{z^\sigma-\gamma_0}_{\mathcal X_\sigma}&\le C\delta,&
 \norm{\partial_\nu z^\sigma}_{\mathcal X_\sigma}&\le C\delta,\notag\\
 \norm{\partial_\zeta z^\sigma}_{\mathcal X_\sigma}
  +\norm{\partial_{\zeta\zeta}z^\sigma}_{\mathcal X_\sigma}&\le C\delta^2,
 &\sigma&\in\{a,r\}.                                  \label{eq:shared-trace-jets}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\(\|\partial_{\nu\nu}z^\sigma\|_{\mathcal X_\sigma}\le C\delta^2\).
The same \(O(\delta^2)\) estimate holds for the mixed derivatives obtained
by applying one additional \(\nu\)-derivative to a \(\zeta\)-derivative.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We include the construction because it is the range bridge between the
finite-dimensional coefficient calculation and the complete-history root.
Use the coordinates \((\chi,d)\) and inverse map \(\Gamma\) from
\cref{def:canonical-preparation}; all graph-transform Lipschitz norms in
this part are taken in those coordinates.  The operator definitions,
flow-jet induction, and coordinate pullback are given without suppression in
\cref{subsec:invariant-history-details}.  We use the datum \(\mathbf p_{\rm can}\)
to prepare \(q_0\) by the fixed
target-frozen cutoff and every current and delayed stable slot by the fixed
componentwise saturation followed by \(P_{\perp,N}\).  The agreement region
is the fixed-buffer neighborhood of \(\mathfrak H^{[2]}_\delta\) from
\cref{def:canonical-preparation}.  Estimate \cref{eq:shared-products} shows
that, for
\[
 \mathcal I_k=\{(a,b,i,j)\in\mathbb N_0^4:
 b\le3,\ i\le2,\ j\le2,\ a+b+i+j\le k\},
 \qquad k\le12,
\]
all \(D_u^a\partial_\rho^b\partial_\nu^i\partial_\zeta^j\) derivatives of
the prepared graph data are bounded, without a factor of \(N\), by the
declared majorant
\(C_{\rm prep}\langle R_\delta\rangle^{M_{\rm prep}}
e^{c_{\rm prep}R_\delta}\).  The preparation equals the transformed
physical RFDE on the common stable ball containing the retained flow hull.

For a bounded planar field \(Q\) and a graph \(H:\R^2\to E_N\), let
\[
 \mathcal I_{Q,H}(u)(\vartheta)
 =\bigl(\Phi_Q^\vartheta u,H(\Phi_Q^\vartheta u)\bigr),
 \qquad -\Theta_*\le\vartheta\le0.
\]
For each target \(\delta\), freeze this preparation at
\(S=S_\delta\), introduce a dummy amplitude
\(\rho\in[-\delta,\delta]\), and recover the physical chart at
\(\rho=\delta\).  Put
\[
 \Lambda_\delta=[-\delta,\delta]_\rho\times I_\nu
                  \times[-\zeta_*,\zeta_*],
 \qquad
 \mathfrak Z_N^0=C_b(\R^2;\R^2)\times C_b(\R^2;E_N),
\]
with the maximum norm.  If \(q_{0,S}\) denotes the globally bounded,
globally Lipschitz prepared singular field, let \(Z_0=(q_{0,S},0)\).  We
iterate on the closed subset of
\(C(\Lambda_\delta;\mathfrak Z_N^0)\)
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb B_{N,\delta}
 =\left\{Z:
 \sup_{\Lambda_\delta}\norm{Z-Z_0}_0\le R_0(\delta,S),\quad
 \sup_{\Lambda_\delta}\operatorname{Lip}(Z-Z_0)\le R_1(\delta,S)
 \right\}.                                            \label{eq:shared-graph-ball}
\end{equation}
The Lipschitz constraint is closed under uniform convergence, so this is a
complete metric space; every planar field in it has a complete flow on
\(\R^2\).  Writing the prepared version of
\cref{eq:shared-exact-X,eq:shared-exact-h} as
\(u'=q_{0,S}(u)+\rho F_N\),
\(\rho h'=A_Nh+\rho G_N\), its special-flow transform is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T_{Q,N}(Q,H)(u)
 &=q_{0,S}(u)+\rho F_N
   \bigl(u,H(u),\mathbf M_N\mathcal I_{Q,H}(u);\rho,\nu,\zeta\bigr),
                                                               \label{eq:shared-TQ}\\
 \mathcal T_{H,N}(Q,H)(u)
 &=\rho\int_0^\infty e^{A_Nr}G_N
   \bigl(\mathcal E_{Q,H}(\Phi_Q^{-\rho r}u);
               \rho,\nu,\zeta\bigr)\,\dd r.          \label{eq:shared-TH}
\end{align}
Here \(\mathcal E_{Q,H}\) denotes the current state and the evaluation
tuple in \cref{eq:shared-evaluation-TV}.  A reduced-flow variation over a
time \(\Theta_*+\delta r\), followed by
\cref{eq:poisson-bound,eq:shared-evaluation-TV}, gives the common
majorant
\begin{equation}
 C\delta P(S_\delta)e^{cS_\delta}
 \int_0^\infty(1+r^M)
 e^{-\{D\gamma-c\delta(1+S_\delta)\}r}\,\dd r=o(1)   \label{eq:shared-graph-contraction}
\end{equation}
for the Lipschitz constant of \((\mathcal T_{Q,N},\mathcal T_{H,N})\).
More precisely, with
\[
 A_g(S)=P_g(S)e^{C_gS},\qquad
 \varepsilon_\delta=C_*\delta A_{10}(S+B),
\]
decrease \(\delta_*\) so that \(\varepsilon_\delta\le1/2\) and
\(C_*\delta(1+S+B)\le D\gamma/2\).  Taking
\begin{equation}
 R_g(\delta,S)=\frac{\delta A_g(S)}{1-\varepsilon_\delta}
               \le2\delta A_g(S),\qquad g=0,1,       \label{eq:shared-graph-radii}
\end{equation}
makes \cref{eq:shared-graph-ball} invariant and the transform a strict
contraction, uniformly in \(N\).
To control jets only where they are later used, choose
\(1>\kappa_0>\cdots>\kappa_D>0\) and buffered constants \(B_k\), and set
\[
 \mathcal U_k(\delta)
 =\{\Gamma(\chi,d):|\chi|\le S+B_k,\ |d|\le\delta^{\kappa_k}\},
 \qquad
 \norm V_{k;c,m}
 =\sup_{\Gamma(\chi,d)\in\mathcal U_k(\delta)}
   \frac{\norm{V(\Gamma(\chi,d))}}
        {\langle\chi\rangle^m e^{c|\chi|}}.
\]
These local fibers are paired with the global \(C^0\) contraction above;
the nested buffers absorb the finite flow and differentiation losses.
The same estimate holds for the highest current block after any derivative
in the finite list of the lemma; all other terms contain already controlled
lower blocks.  More explicitly, order
\[
 J_{a,b,i,j}=D_u^a\partial_\rho^b\partial_\nu^i
                 \partial_\zeta^j,
 \qquad (a,b,i,j)\in\mathcal I_{10},
\]
first by total order and then by parameter order.  Fa\`a di Bruno's formula
and the flow variational equations put each current block in the form
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf J_\ell\mathcal T_N(Z)
 =B_\ell(\mathbf J_{<\ell}Z)
 +\rho\,\mathscr L_\ell(\mathbf J_{<\ell}Z)
          [\mathbf J_\ell Z].                        \label{eq:shared-jet-triangle}
\end{equation}
If a derivative hits a flow argument, the vector-field jet gains one state
slot and loses one parameter derivative, so it precedes the current block;
a product of two positive-order jets has two lower total orders.  Derivatives
of \(\mathbf M_N\) vanish, and
\cref{eq:shared-evaluation-TV} bounds every remaining history evaluation.
The coefficient of the last term in
\cref{eq:shared-jet-triangle} has the same \(o(1)\) bound as
\cref{eq:shared-graph-contraction}; in particular,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho\mathscr L_\ell}\le\varepsilon_\delta\le\frac12,
 \qquad
 \norm{(I-\rho\mathscr L_\ell)^{-1}}\le2.            \label{eq:shared-jet-block-inverse}
\end{equation}
Induction over this finite block list
gives invariant jet balls and, for consecutive Picard iterates, recurrences
of the form
\[
 a_{n+1}\le\kappa a_n+Cq^n,\qquad \max\{\kappa,q\}<1,
\]
with the same constants for every \(N\).  Uniform convergence of a jet and
its first derivative, followed by the fundamental theorem of calculus on
state and parameter line segments, identifies the limits as genuine mixed
derivatives.  This proves convergence of the mixed Picard jets, uniformly
for \(|\rho|\le\delta\).  The full finite prolonged system is lower
triangular with the diagonal inverses in
\cref{eq:shared-jet-block-inverse}.  Solving in block order with the scaled
norm \cref{eq:invdet-prolonged-norm} gives the uniform bound four in
\cref{eq:invdet-prolonged-inverse}.  This is a
finite jet statement, not a same-regularity Fr\'echet inverse on a single
\(C_b^3\) graph space.  Taylor's formula in \(\rho\), evaluated at
\(\rho=\delta\), gives
\cref{eq:shared-graph-expansion}; the corresponding graph-pair remainder is
the Banach-valued integral
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal R}_{3,N}(\rho)
 =\frac12\int_0^1(1-t)^2
       \partial_\rho^3(Q_{N,t\rho},H_{N,t\rho})\,\dd t.
\]
The \(Q\)-component of \(\widehat{\mathcal R}_{3,N}\) is
\(\mathcal R_{3,N}\) in \cref{eq:shared-graph-expansion}.

For the pointwise estimate, split the stable convolution at
\(L\log(1/\delta)\).  Decrease \(\delta_*\) so that
\(c\delta(1+S_\delta)<D\gamma/2\), and then choose \(L\), independently of
\(N\), so that
\begin{equation}
 CP(S_\delta)e^{cS_\delta}
 \{1+L\log(1/\delta)\}^M
 e^{-D\gamma L\log(1/\delta)/2}=O(\delta^6).          \label{eq:shared-convolution-tail}
\end{equation}
On the finite part, the additional reduced-flow backtrack is at most
\(\delta L\log(1/\delta)=o(1)\).  The same triangular induction in nested
normal tubes now gives \cref{eq:shared-graph-remainder}.  At
\(\rho=0\), one derivative uses one delay-support backtrack; two derivatives
use at most two backtracks and the finite semigroup moments
\(\int_0^\infty r^ke^{A_Nr}\dd r\), \(k=0,1\), which are bounded by
\(k!(D\gamma)^{-k-1}\).  Thus the first two coefficients use only the
declared depth-two uncut hull.  Finally,
the fixed-point identity in \cref{eq:shared-TH}, split at any time, is the
mild stable equation.  Present-time evaluation is a left inverse, so the
resulting complete-history map is injective and its retained histories solve
the unprepared RFDE exactly.  On a fixed compact chart set the prepared
coefficients in \(G_N\) are uniformly bounded, and splitting the stable
convolution as above gives
\[
 \|H(u)\|_N
 \le C\delta\int_0^\infty e^{-D\gamma r/2}\,\dd r;
\]
this is \cref{eq:shared-graph-local-size}.

For the trace solve, repeat the graph construction with the frozen target
\(S_\delta+B\).  Extend its reduced field in the normal coordinate by one
fixed finite-order extension: it agrees with the graph field on the
shrinking retained tube and equals \(q_0\) near the two endpoints
\(\gamma_0(\pm R)\).  The extension uses the unscaled normal distance from
the boundary of the shrinking tube, so it introduces no inverse power of
\(\delta\) into the state or parameter estimates.  Only the central pieces
which bootstrap back into the exact graph are called RFDE histories; the
outer planar tails are selection devices.

It remains to solve for the two selected planar traces.  Put \(E(s)=e^{s^2/2}\),
\(I(s)=\int_0^se^{t^2/2}\,\dd t\), and
\[
 \tau(s)=(-1,s)^T,
 \qquad n(s)=(I(s),E(s)-sI(s))^T.
\]
These are respectively the tangent and Gaussian normal solutions, with
\(\det[\tau,n]=-E\).  If \(\xi=a\tau+bn\) and \(L_0\xi=f\), then
\begin{align}
 a'&=E^{-1}\{-(E-sI)f_1+If_2\}=:A_f,&
 b'&=E^{-1}(sf_1+f_2)=:B_f.                           \label{eq:shared-ab}
\end{align}
The phase \(U(0)=0\) is exactly \(a(0)=0\).  With the no-incoming normal
coordinate prescribed at the outer endpoint, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr A_0^\sigma\xi
 =\bigl(L_0\xi,U(0),b(s_\sigma)\bigr):
 \mathcal D_\sigma\longrightarrow
 \mathcal Y_\sigma\times\R\times\R_{\partial,\sigma},
 \qquad s_a=-R,\quad s_r=R.                          \label{eq:shared-trace-operator}
\end{equation}
Given source \(f\), phase value \(\varphi\), and normalized endpoint value
\(\beta\), the two inverses are
\begin{align}
 a_a(s)&=-\varphi-\int_s^0A_f(t)\,\dd t,&
 b_a(s)&=\beta+\int_{-R}^sB_f(t)\,\dd t,\notag\\
 a_r(s)&=-\varphi+\int_0^sA_f(t)\,\dd t,&
 b_r(s)&=\beta-\int_s^RB_f(t)\,\dd t.                \label{eq:shared-one-sided-green}
\end{align}
The elementary estimates
\[
 |I(s)|\le CE(s)\langle s\rangle^{-1},
 \qquad |E(s)-sI(s)|\le CE(s)
\]
and the one-sided Gaussian tail bounds show that
\begin{equation}
 \norm{(\mathscr A_0^\sigma)^{-1}(f,\varphi,\beta)}_{\mathcal D_\sigma}
 \le C_G\left(\norm f_{\mathcal Y_\sigma}
                  +|\varphi|+|\beta|_{\partial,\sigma}\right),
                                                               \label{eq:shared-green-bound}
\end{equation}
where \(C_G\) is independent of \(R\).  Thus
\(\mathscr A_0^\sigma\) is a bounded isomorphism, and the Gaussian growth is
paired with its one-sided integral before a norm is taken.

The tail equation \(\mathscr H_\alpha=0\) is not an assumed stable
manifold.  At an endpoint it is exactly
\begin{equation}
 E(s)b=\alpha\{-a+I(s)b\}^2,                           \label{eq:shared-tail-level}
\end{equation}
so the implicit-function theorem solves \(b=\mathcal B_\sigma(a)\) with
zero value and derivative at the origin, uniformly in the normalized
boundary norm of \cref{eq:shared-green-bound}.  For
\(z=\gamma_0+\xi\), the nonlinear trace equation has the form
\(L_0\xi=N_{\delta,\nu,\zeta}(s,\xi)\), with
\[
 |N(s,0)|\le C\delta e^{c|s|}\langle s\rangle^M,
 \qquad
 |D_\xi N(s,\xi)|\le
 C\{\delta e^{c|s|}\langle s\rangle^M+|\xi|\}.
\]
Use the two tame weights in \cref{eq:trace-strong-spaces}, separated by the
finite loss \(d_0\), and let \(\mathcal G_R^\sigma\) denote
\((\mathscr A_0^\sigma)^{-1}\) with zero phase value.  The nonlinear trace
map is
\[
 \mathcal K_\sigma(\xi)
 =\mathcal G_R^\sigma
   \bigl(N_{\delta,\nu,\zeta}(\cdot,\xi),
         \mathcal B_\sigma(a(s_\sigma))\bigr).
\]
On \(\norm\xi_{\mathcal X_\sigma}\le C_0\delta\), the preceding pointwise
bounds and \cref{eq:shared-green-bound} give
\begin{align}
 \norm{N(\cdot,\xi)}_{\mathcal Y_\sigma}
 &\le C_1\delta+C_2\delta^2\Lambda_R,\notag\\
 \norm{N(\cdot,\xi)-N(\cdot,\widetilde\xi)}_{\mathcal Y_\sigma}
 &\le C_3\delta\Lambda_R
       \norm{\xi-\widetilde\xi}_{\mathcal X_\sigma},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_R=e^{c_*R}\langle R\rangle^{M_*}.           \label{eq:shared-trace-source}
\end{align}
Choose \(C_0\) and then \(\delta_*\) so that
\begin{equation}
 C_G(C_1\delta+C_2\delta^2\Lambda_R)\le C_0\delta,
 \qquad
 \kappa_{\rm tr}:=C_GC_3\delta\Lambda_R\le\frac12.  \label{eq:shared-trace-contraction}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the Lipschitz factor is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 C\delta e^{cR}\langle R\rangle^M=o(1),
 \qquad R=S_\delta+B.                                 \label{eq:shared-trace-smallness}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathcal K_\sigma\) has a unique fixed point and
\(\norm{(I-D\mathcal K_\sigma)^{-1}}\le2\).  The corresponding
trace--phase--normalized-endpoint residual has inverse norm at most
\(2C_G\).  The \(\nu\)-source is \(O(\delta)\); atomwise row neutrality
makes the first \(\zeta\)-source \(O(\delta^2)\).
The field is affine in \(\nu\), so a second \(\nu\)-derivative contains two
first-derivative factors and is \(O(\delta^2)\).  Differentiating the same
fixed-point equation proves
\cref{eq:shared-trace-jets} and the stated mixed bounds.

The graph and trace constructions are therefore solved in sequence; no
joint right inverse for arbitrary graph--trace sources is needed.  Finally,
in the normal coordinate one has the exact identity
\[
 d(\gamma_0(s)+(U,V))=V+sU-\alpha U^2.
\]
The first estimate in \cref{eq:shared-trace-jets} makes this
\(O(\delta e^{cS_\delta}\langle S_\delta\rangle^{M+1})\), which is
\(o(\delta^\kappa)\) for every fixed \(\kappa<1\).  Hence the central trace
and all of its retained delay backtracks lie inside the uncut shrinking
graph, completing the exact-history assertion.
\end{proof}

The two operator statements in \cref{lem:shared-graph-trace} are deliberately
separate: a finite prolonged graph system and, after its graph has been
fixed, two one-sided trace isomorphisms.  The history lift
\(\mathcal I_{Q,H}\) is injective because present evaluation returns \(u\),
and it maps only graph-tangent sources.  No inverse for arbitrary forcing off
the graph in the ambient RFDE history space is claimed or used below.

\subsubsection{The mixed curvature return and the normalized gap}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:shared-resource}]
We calculate the coefficient after, rather than before, the range problem
has been solved.  Let \(H_{1,N}\) be the first stable graph coefficient in
the expansion furnished by \cref{lem:shared-graph-trace}.  On the singular
orbit,
\begin{equation}
 X_0(s)-X_0(s-\theta_k)=-\frac{\theta_k}{2\alpha}.
                                                               \label{eq:shared-affine-delay}
\end{equation}
Differentiating the order-\(\delta\) stable graph equation and using
\cref{eq:shared-affine-delay} gives
\[
 A_ND_\zeta H_{1,N}(\gamma_0(s))
 -\frac{K}{2\alpha}P_{\perp,N}\dot M_{1,N}\mathbf1=0.
\]
Thus
\begin{equation}
 D_\zeta H_{1,N}(\gamma_0(s))=h_{*,N},\qquad
 h_{*,N}=\frac{K}{2\alpha}A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}
             \dot M_{1,N}\mathbf1.                  \label{eq:hstar-rn-a}
\end{equation}
This vector is independent of \(s\).  Put
\begin{equation}
 r_N=\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)h_{*,N}.                         \label{eq:hstar-rn}
\end{equation}
Because \(\pi_N^TR_{k,N}=0\) as a row identity, the structural derivative
of \(\pi_N^T\cL_{N,\zeta}[\phi]\) vanishes for every vector history
\(\phi\).  It therefore removes the direct critical delay term, the
translated history \(g_NX^2\), and the flow-history corrections before any
moment reduction is made.  The translation is independent of \(\zeta\).
The only order-\(\delta^2\) structural term left in
\cref{eq:shared-exact-X} is
\(-2X\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)h_{*,N}\).  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 D_\zeta q_{1,N}=0,\qquad
 D_\zeta q_{2,N}(\gamma_0(s),\nu,0)
   =\left(\frac{r_N}{\alpha}s,0\right)^T.              \label{eq:q2-return}
\end{equation}
This proves both the cancellation of the lower-order response and the
mixed-curvature return; it is an identity for the actual graph jets of
\cref{eq:shared-graph-expansion}, not a formal current-state expansion.

We next include the trace and endpoint contributions.  The normalized
adjoint and the first-integral differential along \(\gamma_0\) are
\begin{equation}
 \psi(s)=e^{-s^2/2}(s,1)^T,\qquad
 \nabla\mathscr H_\alpha(\gamma_0(s))
       =\frac{\alpha e}{2}\psi(s).                    \label{eq:shared-adjoint}
\end{equation}
The first reduced coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 q_{1,N,X}=-\kappa_NX^3
          +K\pi_N^T\cL_{N,0}[\mathbf1X],\qquad
 q_{1,N,Y}=\nu.                                       \label{eq:shared-q1}
\end{equation}
Along \(\gamma_0\), the delay term in \cref{eq:shared-q1} is constant in
\(s\), by \cref{eq:shared-affine-delay}, and hence pairs to zero with the
odd function \(se^{-s^2/2}\).  Using
\(\int e^{-s^2/2}\dd s=\sqrt{2\pi}\),
\(\int s^2e^{-s^2/2}\dd s=\sqrt{2\pi}\), and
\(\int s^4e^{-s^2/2}\dd s=3\sqrt{2\pi}\), we obtain
\begin{align}
 \int_\R\psi^Tq_{1,N}(\gamma_0(s),\nu)\,\dd s
 &=\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\nu+\frac{3\kappa_N}{8\alpha^3}\right)
 =\sqrt{2\pi}(\nu-\nu_{0,N}),                        \label{eq:shared-leading-gap}\\
 \int_\R\psi^TD_\zeta q_{2,N}(\gamma_0(s),\nu,0)\,\dd s
 &=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{r_N}{\alpha}.                    \label{eq:shared-melnikov}
\end{align}

For clarity, these whole-line integrals do not replace a finite-section
boundary-value problem.  The two endpoint values of
\(\mathscr H_\alpha\) are zero by construction, and integrating
\(\dd\mathscr H_\alpha/\dd s\) along the two one-sided traces includes the
phase, both endpoints, and the moving complete histories.  The Green bounds
in \cref{lem:shared-graph-trace}, the coefficient envelope
\cref{eq:invdet-coefficient-envelope}, and the remainder estimate
\cref{eq:shared-graph-remainder} verify the hypotheses of
\cref{prop:finite-gap-bridge}.  That proposition starts from the exact two-trace
finite-section identity \cref{eq:gap-finite-section-identity} and separates
endpoint, singular-tail, moving-trace, parameter-Taylor, graph-jet, and
preparation errors before differentiating them.  In particular, the omitted
Gaussian tails of the fixed preparation are bounded, with the same parameter
derivatives, by
\begin{equation}
 C e^{-S_\delta^2/2+cS_\delta}
       \langle S_\delta\rangle^M
 =C\delta^{p-o(1)}.                                   \label{eq:shared-gaussian-tail}
\end{equation}
Because \(p>4\), the subalgebraic factor in
\cref{eq:shared-gaussian-tail} gives
\(\delta^{p-o(1)}=o(\delta^4)\); hence the tail is smaller than the
algebraic remainders below.  Applying
\cref{prop:finite-gap-bridge} to the pairings in
\cref{eq:shared-leading-gap,eq:shared-melnikov} yields, uniformly
in \(N\) and on the common parameter box,
\begin{align}
 \frac{\mathscr D^{\rm can}_N}\delta
 &=\sqrt{2\pi}(\nu-\nu_{0,N})+O(\delta),
                                                               \label{eq:shared-gap-expansion}\\
 \partial_\nu\mathscr D^{\rm can}_N
 &=\delta\sqrt{2\pi}+O(\delta^2),                    \label{eq:shared-gap-nu}\\
 \partial_\zeta\mathscr D^{\rm can}_N
 &=\delta^2\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{r_N}{\alpha}
       +O(\delta^3+\delta^2|\zeta|),                 \label{eq:shared-gap-zeta}\\
 \partial_{\zeta\zeta}\mathscr D^{\rm can}_N
 &=O(\delta^2),                                       \label{eq:shared-gap-zz}\\
 |\partial_{\nu\nu}\mathscr D^{\rm can}_N|
  +|\partial_{\nu\zeta}\mathscr D^{\rm can}_N|
 &\le C\delta^2.                                      \label{eq:shared-gap-mixed}
\end{align}
Thus the graph, trace, endpoint, and remainder estimates needed for the
root are all part of the same uniform construction.  They are the
range-to-gap hypotheses of \cref{thm:abstract-root-response}, with
\(a_N=\sqrt{2\pi}\) and
\(b_N=\sqrt{2\pi}r_N/\alpha\).  The following paragraph records its
specialization to the present notation.

Choose a common \(c_0>0\) smaller than the boundary distance of the
\(\nu_{0,N}\) from \(\partial I_\nu\).
\Cref{eq:shared-gap-expansion,eq:shared-gap-nu} give opposite endpoint
signs and strict monotonicity on
\(|\nu-\nu_{0,N}|<c_0\) for small \(\delta,\zeta\), proving the unique root
in \cref{eq:shared-root}.  Along that root,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\zeta\nu^{\rm can}_{c,N}
 =-\delta\frac{r_N}{\alpha}
       +O(\delta^2+\delta|\zeta|).                    \label{eq:shared-root-derivative}
\end{equation}
Integrating \cref{eq:shared-root-derivative} from \(0\) to \(\zeta\) and
multiplying by \(\delta^2\) gives
\cref{eq:shared-response,eq:shared-coeff}, with a constant independent of
\(N\) (and allowed to depend on \(\mathbf p_{\rm can}\)).  Evaluation of
\cref{eq:shared-root-derivative} at \(\zeta=0\) gives
\cref{eq:shared-response-derivative}.  The twice differentiated trace and
gap estimates in \cref{lem:shared-graph-trace}, followed by the implicit-
function theorem, give the asserted \(C^2\) root dependence.  On the section
\(X=0\), the map
\(Y\mapsto\mathscr H_\alpha(0,Y)\) has derivative \(\alpha e/2\ne0\) at
the singular crossing.  Hence a zero of the gap is equality of the two
reduced current states.  Injectivity of the history graph in
\cref{lem:shared-graph-trace} then makes it equality of the two retained
complete RFDE histories.
This completes the proof of \cref{thm:shared-resource}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:projection-nonidentifiability}]
The error in \cref{eq:shared-response} is at most
\(C\delta^3|\zeta|(\delta+|\zeta|)\).  Choose the common parameter box so
that \(C(\delta+|\zeta|)\le c_*/2\); then
\cref{eq:root-separation} follows.  The projected fields remain identical by
\cref{eq:projection-blindness}, which proves the non-identifiability claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:response-directions}]
Since \(\|\mathbf1\|_N=1\), \(\|P_{\perp,N}\|\le1\), and
\(\|A_N^{-1}\|\le(D\gamma)^{-1}\),
\[
 \left\|A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}
       \left(\sum_k\theta_kR_k\right)\mathbf1\right\|_N
 \le\frac{\Theta_*}{D\gamma}\sum_k\|R_k\|.
\]
For \(x\in E_N\),
\(|\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)x|\le c_+\|x\|_N\), proving
\cref{eq:response-functional-bound}.

Let \(z_N=c_N-\alpha\mathbf1\in E_N\).  If \(z_N=0\), then
\(\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)x=\alpha\pi_N^Tx=0\) for every \(x\in E_N\), so
\(\Lambda_N\) vanishes on both direction spaces.  Conversely,
\begin{equation}
 \pi_N^T\diag(c_N)z_N
 =\pi_N^T(c_N^{\circ2})-\alpha^2>0                    \label{eq:positive-curvature-variance}
\end{equation}
whenever \(z_N\ne0\), because \(\pi_N\) is strictly positive.  If
\(\theta_j>0\), the row-neutral choice
\(R_j=(A_Nz_N)\pi_N^T/\theta_j\), with all other layers zero, makes the
argument of \(A_N^{-1}\) in \cref{eq:response-functional} equal to
\(A_Nz_N\).  Thus the restriction to \(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm sr}\) is
nonzero.  With two distinct delays, the choice in
\cref{eq:variance-witness} is row neutral, has zero layer sum, and satisfies
\[
 \left(\sum_k\theta_kR_k\right)\mathbf1=A_Nz_N.
\]
This proves \cref{eq:variance-response} and nontriviality on
\(\mathfrak T_N^{\rm red}\).  The kernel of a nonzero linear functional on
the finite-dimensional matrix direction space is a closed codimension-one
hyperplane; its complement is open and dense.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:preparation-universality}]
Apply \cref{thm:shared-resource} to each of the two fixed data and intersect
their parameter boxes.  Both expansions have the same coefficient
\(\mathscr C_N=\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N)\), because this coefficient contains
only the physical low-order jets.  Subtracting the two instances of
\cref{eq:shared-response} proves \cref{eq:preparation-comparison}; dividing
each instance by \(\delta^3\zeta\) gives the normalized estimate.  The
argument deliberately subtracts each preparation's own \(\zeta=0\) root and
therefore makes no statement about the two baselines.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A robust nonzero family and its asynchronous root orbit}

We verify the robust and asynchronous claims for the explicit profile stated
after \cref{cor:preparation-universality}.  Direct summation gives
\begin{equation}
 \pi_N^Ts_N=0,\qquad \pi_N^Ts_N^{\circ2}=1,\qquad
 \norm{s_N}_\infty
 =\sqrt{\frac{3(N-1)}{N+1}}<\sqrt3,                  \label{eq:shared-profile-audit}
\end{equation}
and its entries are pairwise distinct.  Thus
\(c_N=\mathbf1+\sigma s_N\) is positive for
\(0<\sigma<1/\sqrt3\).  Moreover,
\[
 \norm{s_N\pi_N^Tx}_N
 \le2\sqrt3\,\norm{x}_N,
\]
so the layer bound is uniform.  Since
\(B_{k,N}\pm\zeta s_N\pi_N^T\) has entries
\((b\pm\zeta s_{i,N})/N\), both perturbed layers are positive throughout
the common interval \(|\zeta|<b/\sqrt3\).

Here \(A_N=-D\Id\) on \(E_N\) and
\(\dot M_{1,N}\mathbf1=(\theta_0-\theta_1)s_N\).
Together, \cref{eq:hstar-rn-a,eq:hstar-rn,eq:shared-profile-audit} give
\begin{equation}
 r_N=-\frac{K\sigma(\theta_0-\theta_1)}{2D},
 \qquad
 \mathscr C_N=\frac{K\sigma(\theta_0-\theta_1)}{2D}\ne0,
                                                               \label{eq:constant-witness}
\end{equation}
the same number for every \(N\ge2\).

Finally, suppose a synchrony polydiagonal had a cell containing distinct
nodes \(i\ne j\).  Test invariance on a time-constant history with
\(v_i=v_j\ne1\).  The balanced delayed term vanishes, the two rows of
\(P_{c,N}\) give identical instantaneous coupling, and the shared resource
and cubic terms agree at the two nodes.  Their quadratic terms differ by
\((c_{i,N}-c_{j,N})(v_i-1)^2\ne0\).  The vector field is therefore not
tangent to that polydiagonal.  Since all curvatures are distinct, only the
singleton partition survives.  Thus \cref{eq:constant-witness} is a genuine
transverse heterogeneous-network response and not a lifted two-node root.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:robust-asynchronous}]
For this family \(\alpha=\gamma=1\), \(\Theta_*=\theta_1\), and the common
curvature bound may be taken as \(c_+=1+\sigma\sqrt3\).  Hence
\cref{eq:response-functional-bound} gives
\[
 |\Lambda_N(\widetilde{\mathbf R}_N)
       -\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N^*)|
 \le \frac{|K|(1+\sigma\sqrt3)\theta_1}{2D}
       \|\widetilde{\mathbf R}_N-\mathbf R_N^*\|_{\mathfrak T_N}.
\]
Together with
\(|\Lambda_N(\mathbf R_N^*)|=|K|\sigma\Delta\theta/(2D)\), the radius in
\cref{eq:robust-radius} proves \cref{eq:robust-response}.

Decrease \(\delta_0\) first so that \(S_\delta>1\).  At the fixed attracting
slow time \(s=-1\), the point \(z^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1)\) lies in one fixed
compact subset of the retained chart.  Define
\(h^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1)
=H_{N,\delta,\nu,\zeta}(z^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1))\).
The trace estimate in \cref{eq:shared-trace-jets} and the local graph estimate
\cref{eq:shared-graph-local-size} give
\[
 X^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1)=\frac12+O(\delta),
 \qquad \|h^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1)\|_N\le C\delta,
\]
uniformly in \(N\), \(\nu\), and \(\zeta\).  Since
\(g_N=-(\sigma/D)s_N\), and \(s=\delta t\), the exact chart relation yields
\begin{align*}
 \delta^{-2}P_{\perp,N}v^{\rm can}_{c,N}(-\delta^{-1})
 &=g_N\{X^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1)\}^2
      +h^{a,\rm can}_{c,N}(-1)\\
 &=-\frac{\sigma}{4D}s_N+\eta_N(\delta),
 \qquad \|\eta_N(\delta)\|_N\le C_a\delta,
\end{align*}
which is \cref{eq:asynchronous-profile}.  Finally,
\[
 \osc(s_N)=2\sqrt{\frac{3(N-1)}{N+1}}\ge2.
\]
Both \(s_N\) and \(P_{\perp,N}v^{\rm can}_{c,N}\) belong to \(E_N\), on
which \(\|\cdot\|_N=\osc(\cdot)\).  The reverse triangle inequality and a
further common decrease of \(\delta_0\), chosen so that
\(C_a\delta\le\sigma/(4D)\), give \cref{eq:asynchronous-lower}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Scope of the network theorem}

The shared-resource structural-root theorem allows heterogeneous
curvature under a common Dobrushin gap, fixed delay support, an atomwise
row-neutral structural direction, and the fixed admissible preparation datum
in \cref{def:canonical-preparation}.  The graph and selected-trace
constructions are proved in \cref{lem:shared-graph-trace}; they are not extra
hypotheses.  The exact finite-\(\delta\) root remains selection dependent.
The response increment, but not the baseline root, has the preparation-
universal leading coefficient in \cref{cor:preparation-universality}.  The
theory does not cover order-one changes of the base generator, moving delay
atoms, a closing mixing gap, infinite networks, comparison of arbitrary
preparations, a preparation-independent root, or identification with an
independently selected physical outer canard.  These are mathematical
boundaries, not numerical omissions.
