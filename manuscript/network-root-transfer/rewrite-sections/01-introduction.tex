\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Consider a finite network with row-stochastic instantaneous coupling and
additional linear feedback acting at several delays.  Let
\(P_N\) have stationary probability \(\pi_N\), and
write
\[
 \Pi_N(v,w)=(\pi_N^Tv,w)
\]
for the stationary network average together with a shared scalar variable.  A
representative system considered below is
\begin{align}
 \dot v(t)={}&f_N(v(t),w(t))+D(P_N-\Id)v(t) \notag\\
 &+\delta^2K\sum_{k=0}^m B_{k,N}(\eta)
   \left\{v(t)-v\left(t-\frac{\theta_k}{\delta}\right)\right\},
                                                        \label{eq:intro-network-form}\\
 \dot w(t)={}&\delta^2\{\pi_N^Tv(t)-a\},              \label{eq:intro-recovery-form}
\end{align}
where \(0\leq\theta_0<\cdots<\theta_m\), the node nonlinearities may be
heterogeneous, and \(0<\delta\ll1\).  We perturb the delayed-coupling
matrices by writing \(B_{k,N}(\eta)=B_{k,N}+\Delta B_{k,N}\) and imposing
\begin{equation}
 \pi_N^T\Delta B_{k,N}=0\quad(0\leq k\leq m),
 \qquad
 \sum_{k=0}^m\Delta B_{k,N}=0.                       \label{eq:intro-neutrality}
\end{equation}
Thus each perturbation vanishes after left multiplication by \(\pi_N^T\),
and the sum of the delayed-coupling matrices is unchanged.

Equation \eqref{eq:intro-network-form} is schematic; the polynomial voltage
field and the retarded functional differential equation (RFDE) phase space
used in the theorems are defined in
\cref{sec:model-results}.

Let \(\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}\) be the RFDE vector field on its phase
space.  Conditions~\eqref{eq:intro-neutrality} imply the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(\Phi)
 =\Pi_N\mathcal F_{N,\delta,\nu,0}(\Phi)              \label{eq:intro-projected-identity}
\end{equation}
for every admissible history \(\Phi\).  This identity does not make the
projected variables a closed subsystem: the common projected functional can
still depend on transverse histories in
\[
 E_N=\ker\pi_N^T.
\]
Our first question is whether a constrained perturbation satisfying
\eqref{eq:intro-projected-identity} can change a collective Fredholm
solvability condition near a fold, and whether that change can be estimated
uniformly as \(N\) grows.  Transfer of the resulting coefficient to a
heteroclinic-connection parameter is a separate, conditional step.

The answer is governed first by the delay locations.  The first-moment term
entering the network-mean-zero equation near the fold is
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S_N(\eta)
 =\frac{K}{2\alpha}P_{\perp,N}
   \left(\sum_{k=0}^m\theta_k\Delta B_{k,N}\right)\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 P_{\perp,N}=\Id-\mathbf1\pi_N^T.                    \label{eq:intro-delay-moment}
\end{equation}
On the unrestricted matrix tangent space used for the baseline theorem, if
two distinct delay locations are present, every vector in \(E_N\) is obtained
from \(\mathsf S_N\) by perturbing the matrices at two distinct delays with
opposite signs.
If terms with the same delay are combined and only one delay value remains,
then \(\mathsf S_N=0\).  Under the uniform Dobrushin condition
\begin{equation}
 \tau(P_N)\leq1-\gamma,
 \qquad \gamma>0,                                    \label{eq:intro-dobrushin-gap}
\end{equation}
the transverse generator
\[
 A_N=D(P_N-\Id)|_{E_N}
\]
has an inverse bounded independently of \(N\).  If \(\mathfrak r_N\in E_N^*\)
is the bounded linear functional obtained from the scalar Fredholm
solvability condition, the local Fredholm sensitivity functional is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \eta\ \longmapsto\ \mathsf S_N(\eta)\ \longmapsto\
 A_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N(\eta)\ \longmapsto\
 \Lambda_N(\eta),\qquad
 \Lambda_N=\mathfrak r_NA_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N .\quad}  \label{eq:intro-response-covector}
\end{equation}
In the upper-triangular Fredholm problem used below, once every remaining
direct perturbation term belongs to the range of the collective
linearization, the last arrow has the standard form
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak r_N=-\frac{\ell_N\mathcal B_N}{\ell_Nf_{\nu,N}},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_N
 =-\frac{\ell_N\mathcal B_NA_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N}
         {\ell_Nf_{\nu,N}}.                         \label{eq:intro-full-factorization}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal B_N\) is the off-diagonal block through which a transverse
correction enters the collective equation, \(\ell_N\) annihilates the range
of the collective linearization, and \(f_{\nu,N}\) is the parameter
direction, with \(\ell_Nf_{\nu,N}\ne0\).  Thus \(\mathsf S_N\) produces a
transverse forcing, \(A_N^{-1}\) solves for the transverse correction, and
\(\mathcal B_N\) and \(\ell_N\) measure its contribution to the collective
solvability condition.  Cancellation of the direct perturbation under the
stationary projection therefore does not force this first variation to
vanish.

The reusable operator argument requires an exact projected-vector-field
identity, an invertible transverse block, a collective linearization with
one-dimensional cokernel, and all remaining direct perturbation terms to lie
in its range.  The two-delay formula for \(\mathsf S_N\), the Dobrushin
criterion, the Gaussian adjoint, and the curvature pairing verify those
ingredients for the network studied here; another RFDE must establish its own
versions of these model-dependent objects.

The explicit right inverse used for this full-range statement is generally a
dense rank-one matrix and is therefore not a fixed-topology perturbation.  For
a prescribed linear matrix pattern \(\mathcal U_N\), the attainable
directions are instead exactly
\(\{R\mathbf1:R\in\mathcal U_N\}\).  This exact range formula
shows that layerwise row-sum preservation forces the present
first-moment response to vanish, and includes fixed-support generator
directions; see \cref{prop:fredholm-structured-delay-range}.

The unrestricted two-delay right inverse also makes the map
\(\mathfrak r_N\mapsto\Lambda_N\) injective, with bounds independent of \(N\).
At this point \(\Lambda_N\) is a local
sensitivity functional for the solvability condition; for a fixed
perturbation direction, its value is the corresponding first-order
coefficient.  Identifying this functional with the derivative of a
heteroclinic-connection parameter requires the additional invariant-manifold
hypotheses stated below.

This calculation is already nontrivial for three nodes.  Set
\[
 \pi=\tfrac13(1,1,1)^T,
 \qquad P=\mathbf1\pi^T,
 \qquad c=(1-\sigma,1,1+\sigma)^T,
 \qquad s=(-1,0,1)^T,
\]
where \(0<\sigma<1\).  For the two positive delays
\(\theta_0=1\), \(\theta_1=2\), take
\[
 R_0=s\pi^T,
 \qquad R_1=-s\pi^T.
\]
Then \(\pi^TR_k=0\) and \(R_0+R_1=0\), while
\[
 \theta_0R_0\mathbf1+\theta_1R_1\mathbf1=-s\neq0.
\]
For \(D=1\), \(K=2\), and \(\alpha=\pi^Tc=1\), the restriction of the
transverse generator is \(A=-\Id\), and the Fredholm calculation below gives
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(R_0,R_1)=-\pi^T\diag(c)s=-\frac{2\sigma}{3}. \label{eq:intro-three-node-response}
\end{equation}
If \(B_0=B_1=P/2\), the matrices \(B_k+\zeta R_k\) remain nonnegative for
\(|\zeta|<1/2\), retain their common full support, and preserve
\(B_0+B_1=P\).  Moreover, the unequal components of \(c\) exclude every
coordinate-equality subspace associated with a nontrivial partition of the
nodes.  The response in
\eqref{eq:intro-three-node-response} is therefore not produced by copying a
lower-dimensional synchronous solution across the network.

The uniformity in network size is realized by an explicit growing family, not
only by the three-node example.  Let
\[
 \pi_N=N^{-1}\mathbf1,\qquad
 P_N=(1-\rho)\Id+\rho\mathbf1\pi_N^T,\qquad 0<\rho\le1,
\]
and choose the distinct, centered coefficients
\[
 q_{i,N}=\sqrt{\frac{12}{N^2-1}}
       \left(i-\frac{N+1}{2}\right),
 \qquad c_N=\mathbf1+\sigma q_N,\qquad 0<\sigma<\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\]
Then \(\pi_N^Tq_N=0\), \(\pi_N^Tq_N^{\circ2}=1\),
\(\tau(P_N)=1-\rho\), and the two positive-delay direction
\(R_0=q_N\pi_N^T\), \(R_1=-q_N\pi_N^T\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_N(R_0,R_1)=-\frac{K\sigma}{2D\rho}
 \qquad(N\ge2).                                      \label{eq:intro-growing-family}
\end{equation}
Thus the effect remains nonzero as \(N\) grows, while the distinct entries of
\(c_N\) prevent its interpretation as replication of one lower-dimensional
synchronous cell.  By contrast, a lazy nearest-neighbor cycle has no uniform
Dobrushin gap and its transverse inverse grows at least linearly with \(N\);
both claims are proved in \cref{prop:fredholm-growing-families}.

The finite-section computations in \cref{sec:fold-passage} illustrate the
sign and scale of the three-node coefficient and its persistence along the
growing family.  They are diagnostics, not the matching function used in the
proof or computations of heteroclinic connections.

\subsection{Relation to existing theory}

Canards in finite-dimensional fast--slow systems are organized by the
interaction of attracting and repelling slow manifolds near loss of normal
hyperbolicity \citep{krupa2001relaxation,krupa2001extending}.  Canard phenomena
have also been established in delay equations, and local fast--slow theory is
available for broad classes of infinite-dimensional evolution equations
\citep{campbell2009delay,krupa2016complex,krupa2016explosion,%
avitabile2020local,hummel2022slow}.  Related transitions have been studied in
stochastic, adaptive, consensus, and mean-field networks
\citep{touboul2015noise,ciszak2021collective,jardon2020consensus,%
qin2023tipping,balzer2024cascading}.  This literature supplies the fast--slow
setting, but it does not determine the first variation of a collective
solvability condition when a perturbation of the delayed-coupling matrices
vanishes identically under a fixed network projection.

Invariant manifolds, exponential dichotomies, Fredholm operators, and Lin-type
constructions provide a general framework for heteroclinic connections in
RFDEs \citep{hale1986heteroclinic,lin1986exponential,%
magalhaes1987persistence,bates1998invariant,malletparet1999fredholm,%
hupkes2009lin,henot2023homoclinic}.  These results do not by themselves identify
how the constrained perturbations in \eqref{eq:intro-neutrality} enter the
connection equation, nor do they supply estimates uniform over a sequence of
growing network phase spaces.
The closest functional-differential-equation baseline is the Lin reduction of
\citet{hupkes2009lin}, built on Fredholm theory such as
\citet{malletparet1999fredholm}: it reduces a prescribed orbit-matching problem
for a given equation to finite-dimensional bifurcation equations, but it does
not produce the constrained first-moment map or estimates uniform in network
size.  The Dobrushin theory of \citet{gaubert2015dobrushin} controls contraction
of Markov operators on the mean-zero space; it contains neither the
delay-moment map nor its Fredholm pairing with a collective cokernel.
The abstract Lyapunov--Schmidt step used here is standard.  The additional
result is the explicit passage from perturbations annihilated by the
stationary projection to the first-moment map \(\mathsf S_N\), its two-delay
surjectivity and norm-optimal right inverse in the specified norms, the exact
range formula for prescribed matrix patterns, and the dimension-uniform
factorization through \(A_N^{-1}\).  The rank-one right inverse is elementary
linear algebra; its role is to make the unrestricted map to the Fredholm
solvability condition quantitative and independent of \(N\).

The issue is also different from exact lumpability, where the projection
defines a closed quotient \citep{horstmeyer2016lumpability}, and from structural
unidentifiability, where a parameter transformation preserves an entire
input--output behavior
\citep{bearup2013inputoutput,villaverde2017dynamical}.  Mori--Zwanzig
representations express the return of unresolved variables to resolved ones
\citep{chorin2002optimal,herrera2018memory,herrera2020tractable}, while adjoint
sensitivity formulas express scalar responses by covector pairings
\citep{cacuci1981sensitivity,rihan2003sensitivity}.  The problem addressed here
is to identify the first variation that remains after the projected
perturbation has vanished, and to determine how it enters a codimension-one
solvability condition.  The resulting factorization has constants that do not
deteriorate with \(N\).  Its use for an RFDE heteroclinic connection requires a
separately verified invariant-manifold connection condition; this global
hypothesis is kept distinct from the local fold calculation.

\subsection{Results and proof outline}

The unconditional results are the projection identity, the delay-moment range
and structured-range theorems, the dimension-uniform Fredholm functional, the
local invariant manifold, and the derivative estimates for
\(D_N^{\rm fin}\).  The heteroclinic conclusion is only the implication from
an independently verified global defining function and comparison estimate.
This hierarchy is reflected in the theorem names and section order below.

The first main theorem makes the linear factorization
\eqref{eq:intro-response-covector} precise.  With two separated delay
locations, \(\mathsf S_N\) maps the admissible perturbation tuples onto
\(E_N\) and has an explicit norm-optimal right inverse in the stated norms;
with one merged location it
vanishes.  Dobrushin contraction supplies the dimension-independent inverse of
\(A_N\), and elimination of the transverse variable in the upper-triangular
Fredholm problem gives \(\Lambda_N\).  Conversely, its values on admissible
perturbations determine the bounded linear functional \(\mathfrak r_N\).  The known network
matrix, stationary distribution, delay locations, and scalar coefficients are
inputs to this reconstruction; the theorem does not identify an unknown
network from data.

For the conditional connection application, a global connection must first be
well defined.  The
unmodified recovery equation \eqref{eq:intro-recovery-form} does not provide
the two equilibria outside the fold neighborhood used in the conditional
application.  We therefore fix an
autonomous modified recovery equation that agrees identically with
\eqref{eq:intro-recovery-form} throughout the phase-space neighborhood used in
the fold analysis and has two constant equilibria outside that neighborhood.
This modification is part of the equation, not a boundary condition.  We
state explicit uniform hyperbolicity and invariant-manifold hypotheses under
which the relevant unstable and stable manifold sections define a scalar
function for their intersection.  If that function is \(C^1\)-close to the finite-interval
matching function constructed near the fold, the implicit-function theorem
gives a unique local parameter value
\[
 \mu_{c,N}^{g}(\delta,\eta)=\delta^2\nu_{c,N}^{g}(\delta,\eta).
\]
Under these hypotheses, the resulting connection-parameter map is
continuously Fr\'echet differentiable in the delayed-coupling perturbations
and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\|D_\eta\mu_{c,N}^{g}(\delta,\eta)-\delta^3\Lambda_N\bigr\|
 \leq C\{\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|\},                \label{eq:intro-root-derivative}
\end{equation}
with \(C\) independent of \(N\).  In particular,
\eqref{eq:intro-three-node-response} yields
\[
 \partial_\zeta\mu_{c,3}^{g}(\delta,0)
 =-\frac{2\sigma}{3}\delta^3+O(\delta^4).
\]

The proof first combines the two-delay right inverse with the Dobrushin
estimate and a triangular-block Lyapunov--Schmidt reduction.  An invariant graph
then converts the transverse displacement into a forcing of the scalar fold
equation, whose one-dimensional cokernel is represented by an explicit
Gaussian adjoint solution.  A finite-interval problem supplies a matching
function and its parameter derivatives.  The separate global hypotheses ask
for a defining function for the invariant-manifold intersection on a common
phase section and a uniform \(C^1\) estimate comparing it with this
finite-interval function.
Under those hypotheses, the general sensitivity theorem transfers the
Fredholm functional to the heteroclinic defining function and proves
\eqref{eq:intro-root-derivative}.  We do not claim here that the chosen
modified recovery equation satisfies the required uniform invariant-manifold
estimates.

The finite-\(\delta\) parameter value belongs to the specified modified RFDE
and may depend on the recovery equation outside the fold neighborhood.  The
assumptions, uniformity class, and limitations are collected in
\cref{sec:scope-transfer}.
