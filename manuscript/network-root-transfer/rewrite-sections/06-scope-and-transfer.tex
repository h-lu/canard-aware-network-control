\section{Hypotheses for application to other retarded equations}
\label{sec:scope-transfer}

The calculation in this paper separates into an operator argument and a
model-specific construction.  We make that distinction explicit because it
identifies which hypotheses and conclusions are independent of the
model-specific calculations.

\subsection{Required hypotheses for another RFDE}

The parameter-sensitivity argument in
\cref{thm:fredholm-heteroclinic-sensitivity} requires four independently
verified ingredients: an exact projected-vector-field identity, a
collective/transverse Fredholm decomposition, a \(C^1\) defining
function for an invariant-manifold connection, and the derivative expansion
of that same defining function.  Equality only on an invariant subspace is
insufficient, and a finite-interval matching zero cannot replace membership
in the stable-manifold section.

More precisely, one must specify Banach spaces and bounded maps
\[
 \mathsf S:\mathcal T\to\mathcal Y_\perp,\quad
 L_\perp:\mathcal D_\perp\to\mathcal Y_\perp,\quad
 \mathcal B:\mathcal D_\perp\to\mathcal Y_\parallel,\quad
 L_\parallel:\mathcal D_\parallel\to\mathcal Y_\parallel,
\]
where \(L_\perp\) is invertible and
\(\operatorname{Ran}L_\parallel=\ker\ell\) has codimension one for some
\(\ell\in\mathcal Y_\parallel^*\).  The parameter direction
\(f_\nu\in\mathcal Y_\parallel\) must satisfy
\(\ell f_\nu\ne0\), and every additional direct perturbation term must lie in
\(\operatorname{Ran}L_\parallel\).  The bounded linear functional governing
the parameter derivative is then
\[
 -\frac{\ell\mathcal B L_\perp^{-1}\mathsf S}{\ell f_\nu}.
\]
Finally, the same defining function \(G\) must satisfy the uniform \(C^1\)
estimates in
\cref{eq:fredholm-exact-gap-level,eq:fredholm-exact-gap-slope,%
eq:fredholm-exact-gap-structural}.
The theorem does not construct these ingredients for an arbitrary RFDE;
hyperbolicity of two equilibria alone does not supply the connection or the
uniform comparison estimate.

The Fredholm quotient and its norm estimate are the general operator
statements.  The two-delay formula for \(\mathsf S_N\) and the Dobrushin bound
for \(A_N^{-1}\) are specific to the present class of retarded networks.  The
Gaussian cokernel and the quadratic-coefficient pairing are specific to the
polynomial fold model.  Construction of \(G\) and verification of its
uniform \(C^1\) estimates are specific to each global RFDE.

\subsection{Features specific to the present model}

Several calculations used to verify the preceding conditions do not follow
from the abstract theorem.  They use the affine singular fold orbit, the
Gaussian cokernel of the resulting whole-line operator, the heterogeneous
quadratic coefficients, and the explicit pairing
\[
 -\alpha^{-1}\pi_N^T\diag(c_N)h.
\]
The proposed global application also depends on the particular modification
of the recovery equation and its two equilibria.  To turn that application
into an unconditional model theorem one must establish the uniform
invariant-manifold sections and a \(C^1\) estimate comparing the heteroclinic
defining function with the finite-interval matching function in the history
space with delays of length \(\theta_k/\delta\).  Those estimates are
not consequences of the local calculation and are not proved in this paper.

The two-delay statement has the same restricted scope.  It is an exact range
result for the first delay moment on the constraint space in
\cref{eq:rw-structural-parameter-space}.  It does not exclude responses generated by
higher delay moments, by perturbations outside that space, or by other
coupling mechanisms when only one delay is present.

\subsection{Interpretation and remaining boundary}

Canard-mediated transitions occur in experimentally studied electronic,
optoelectronic, photonic, machining, and thermo-fluid systems
\citep{campbell2009delay,tallambe2015mixed,romeira2016regenerative,%
itoh1990experimental,marino2011mixed,bhavi2024canard}.
Other work provides rigorous canard connections for delay equations and
mathematically explicit canards in networks
\citep{krupa2016explosion,jardon2020consensus,awal2024strong}.
The present calculation belongs to the latter, mathematical category.  The
globally modified recovery equation is not calibrated to an experiment, and
we do not identify a conditional connection parameter with a measured
threshold.

Nor do we prove a maximal canard for the unmodified recovery law.  That law
has no equilibria on the two prescribed components outside the fold
neighborhood.  Different fixed modified recovery laws may therefore have
different finite-\(\delta\)
connection parameters.  Under the hypotheses of
\cref{cor:modified-conormal}, what is common after centering each parameter
locus at its own baseline is the leading conormal.  This is a conditional
statement about parameter sensitivity, not equality of global connections.

Within this scope, the unconditional result establishes a precise limitation
of inference from stationary projection alone: equality of the projected
vector field at every history eliminates the direct derivative, but it does
not eliminate the first variation transmitted through transverse histories.
Distinct delay locations make that variation range over the full transverse
space.  Whenever a defining function for a codimension-one heteroclinic
condition satisfies the
stated hypotheses, the Fredholm reduction identifies its contribution to the
connection parameter.
