\begin{abstract}
A stationary projection can erase a perturbation of a delayed network at
every full history without erasing its effect on a complete-history matching
problem.  We prove this phenomenon for a shared-resource RFDE on finite
directed Markov networks.  At a fold, attracting and repelling one-sided
traces are selected on one invariant history graph; a complete-history root
is the parameter at which the two histories, rather than only their present
states, agree on a phase section.

For atomwise stationary-row-neutral changes of the delayed layers, the
stationary-projected vector-field functional is exactly independent of the
structural parameter.  Nevertheless, we construct a locally unique selected
root and prove
\[
 \mu^{\rm can}_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)
 -\mu^{\rm can}_{c,N}(\delta,0)
 =\mathscr C_N\delta^3\zeta
  +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),
\]
with constants independent of network size.  The perturbation creates a
transverse displacement through the Markov resolvent, and heterogeneous
curvature returns it to the scalar matching gap.

For each fixed network, nonzero leading-response directions are the complement
of a codimension-one hyperplane precisely when node curvatures are heterogeneous;
a pure delay-redistribution witness reduces the coefficient to a stationary
curvature variance.  An explicit family has a response bounded away from zero
on a dimension-uniform neighborhood, no nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal,
and a quantitatively asynchronous root orbit.  The leading response increment
is the same for any two fixed canonical preparations, although their baseline
roots need not agree.  Thus the result is a projection non-identifiability
theorem for a selected complete-history object, not a claim of preparation-
independent canard selection.
\end{abstract}
