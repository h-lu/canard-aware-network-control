\begin{abstract}
We exhibit a delay redistribution that leaves the stationary projection of
an RFDE vector field unchanged at every fixed full history yet moves a
complete-history canard in a fixed globally anchored RFDE.  The setting is a
finite retarded Markov network with a common Dobrushin gap.
Redistributing coupling between
delay layers in stationary-row-neutral directions leaves the stationary
projection of the RFDE vector field exactly unchanged at every full history;
this is stronger than agreement on synchronized states but does not make the
projection dynamically closed.  With two distinct delay locations, the same
redistributions generate every transverse source direction.  We give an
explicit sharp right inverse and a dual reconstruction bound, both independent
of network size.  With only one merged delay location, the leading transverse
source vanishes identically on the pure-redistribution class.

To turn this hidden source into an intrinsic nonlinear observable, we construct
a globally anchored shared-resource RFDE which agrees exactly with the original
equation on the entire retained fold-history tube.  Its two hyperbolic anchor
equilibria carry a one-dimensional incoming unstable branch and a
codimension-one outgoing stable history sheet.  A forward RFDE construction,
a half-line Lyapunov--Perron problem, and quantitative tail forgetting produce
a unique local parameter root at which these objects form a complete
heteroclinic orbit.  The orbit follows attracting and repelling slow-history
graphs for times of order \(\delta^{-2}\), so the connection is a canard in a
quantitative dynamical sense.  If \(\eta\) denotes a blind redistribution and
\(\mu_c(\delta,\eta)\) the fixed-model root, then
\[
 D_\eta\mu_c(\delta,\eta)
   =\delta^3\Lambda_N+O(\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|),
\]
in the full dual norm, uniformly in network size.  The covector \(\Lambda_N\)
is precisely the dimension-uniform transverse return covector obtained from
the projection-blind source.  Hence a same-history projection-neutral
parameter moves an exact complete-history heteroclinic canard at order
\(\delta^3\).  Exact roots
may differ between admissible global anchors, but after modelwise baseline
centering their weighted conormals converge uniformly to
\((1,-\Lambda_N)\).  No maximal-canard claim is made for the unanchored
recovery law, which is proved not to supply the required outer equilibria.
\end{abstract}
