\begin{abstract}
We study phase-fixed roots defined by matching complete histories in finite
retarded networks.  The principal result treats a shared-resource family on
directed Markov networks with a common Dobrushin gap, fixed delay support,
and heterogeneous node curvatures.  A dimension-uniform invariant-history
graph and two selected one-sided traces reduce the matching problem to a
scalar normalized gap.  For every finite network size and every preparation
in a bounded common class, this gap has a unique local root.  Its response to
an atomwise row-neutral perturbation has an explicit nonzero leading
coefficient and a remainder uniform in network size and in the preparation.
An explicit family with pairwise distinct curvatures shows that the response
does not arise from a nontrivial synchrony quotient.

The proof separates the collective and transverse mechanisms.  Dobrushin
contraction supplies a dimension-free stable inverse, while a graph--trace
range construction turns the formal fold expansion into equality of two
retained RFDE histories.  For a companion leaky FitzHugh--Nagumo network, we
also prove network-size-independent transverse decay and a causal
complete-line Green bound.  An exact upper-triangular row reduction then
transfers any supplied scalar simple Lin root through delayed layers that
need not share a left stationary measure.  The shared-resource root is
preparation indexed: the theorem does not identify it with a
preparation-independent physical canard, and the leaky scalar root is not
constructed here.
\end{abstract}
