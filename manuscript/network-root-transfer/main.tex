\begin{abstract}
We study finite networks of retarded functional differential equations with
row-stochastic instantaneous coupling and linear feedback distributed over
several delays.  The admissible perturbations preserve the sum of the delayed
coupling matrices and are annihilated by the stationary row vector at each
delay.  The stationary projection of the vector field is therefore unchanged
at every history, although the projected variables need not form a closed
system.  We prove that, when two distinct delays are present, the first moment
of the perturbation matrices maps onto the full transverse space.  The map has
an explicit right inverse, and its norm and the relevant transverse inverse
bound are independent of network size.  With only one delay, the first-moment
map vanishes.

We next give a Lyapunov--Schmidt formula for the derivative of any
codimension-one heteroclinic connection condition satisfying stated uniform
regularity and transversality hypotheses.  For a polynomial fast--slow
network, a two-dimensional locally invariant manifold in the RFDE phase
space, represented as a graph over the fold variables, and a finite-interval
matching problem determine the coefficient explicitly:
\[
 \Lambda_N=-\frac1\alpha\pi_N^T\operatorname{diag}(c_N)
 A_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N.
\]
If a specified smooth modification of the recovery equation outside the fold
neighborhood has uniform stable
and unstable manifold sections and its heteroclinic defining function is
\(C^1\)-close to the finite-interval matching function, the implicit-function theorem gives
\[
 D_\eta\mu_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)
 =\delta^3\Lambda_N+O(\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|)
\]
uniformly in \(N\).  These global invariant-manifold and comparison
properties are explicit hypotheses, not conclusions of the local fold
calculation.  Thus the unconditional results are the first-moment range
theorem, the dimension-uniform Fredholm solvability formula, and the local
coefficient; the existence of a parameter value yielding a heteroclinic
connection must be established separately for each global equation.
\end{abstract}
