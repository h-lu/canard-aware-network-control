\begin{abstract}
We study the sensitivity of local fold matching in finite networks of
retarded functional differential equations.  Perturbations of the delayed
coupling preserve the sum of the coupling layers and are annihilated by the
stationary row at each delay.  The stationary projection of the vector field
is therefore unchanged at every history, although the projected variables
need not form a closed system.  For a polynomial fast--slow network, we
construct a two-dimensional locally invariant manifold of compatible
histories and a finite-interval matching function with parameter-derivative
bounds uniform in network size.  Its unique local zero satisfies
\(D_\eta\mu_N^{\rm fin}=\delta^3\Lambda_N+
O(\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|)\).  The functional
\(\Lambda_N=\mathfrak r_NA_N^{-1}\mathsf S_N\) combines the first delay
moment, a transverse inverse, and the feedback from heterogeneous quadratic
coefficients.  Two distinct delays give a norm-optimal right inverse for the
unrestricted first-moment map; prescribed matrix patterns have an exact range
criterion.  Dobrushin contraction makes these bounds independent of network
size, and an explicit growing family has a nonzero uniform response.  A
separate conditional result transfers the coefficient to a heteroclinic
parameter when global invariant-manifold sections and the required comparison
with local matching are available.
\end{abstract}
