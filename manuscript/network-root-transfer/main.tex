\begin{abstract}
We study how a constrained change in the distribution of delayed coupling
affects a codimension-one connection condition in finite networks of retarded
functional differential equations.  The perturbations preserve the sum of
the delayed-coupling matrices and are annihilated by the stationary row
vector.  Consequently, the stationary projection of the vector field is
unchanged at every history, although the projected variables need not form a
closed system.  With two distinct delay values, we prove that the first delay
moment of these perturbations maps onto the network-transverse space and has
an explicit sharp right inverse.  Combining this map with a Dobrushin
estimate gives a transverse inverse and a Fredholm sensitivity functional
whose bounds are independent of network size.  With one delay value, the
first-moment map vanishes on the same perturbation space.

We then give a Lyapunov--Schmidt formula for the derivative of a
codimension-one heteroclinic defining function under uniform regularity and
transversality hypotheses.  For a polynomial fast--slow network, we construct
a two-dimensional locally invariant manifold in the RFDE phase space and use
a finite-interval boundary-value problem to compute the local sensitivity
functional.  If a fixed smooth modification outside the fold neighborhood
has the required stable and unstable manifold sections and its heteroclinic
defining function is \(C^1\)-close to the finite-interval matching function,
then the connection parameter satisfies
\[
 D_\eta\mu_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)
 =\delta^3\Lambda_N+O(\delta^4+\delta^3\|\eta\|)
\]
uniformly in \(N\).  The global invariant-manifold and comparison properties
are hypotheses; the paper does not prove a heteroclinic connection for the
unmodified recovery equation.
\end{abstract}
