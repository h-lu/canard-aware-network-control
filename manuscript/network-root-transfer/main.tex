\begin{abstract}
Near a fold of a retarded equation, equality of present states does not imply
equality of the histories seen by the delayed vector field.  We therefore
select attracting and repelling one-sided traces on a common invariant
history graph and call a parameter value a complete-history root when those
traces agree on a fixed phase section.

For a shared-resource family on finite directed Markov networks, we prove
existence and local uniqueness of this root with constants independent of
network size.  The hypotheses are a common Dobrushin gap, fixed delay
support, uniformly bounded heterogeneous curvatures, and a canonical trace
preparation fixed across the network family.  Under an atomwise
stationary-row-neutral redistribution of the delayed layers, the root has an
explicit nonzero leading response and a quadratic remainder uniform in
network size.  The direct collective response cancels: the perturbation first
creates a transverse displacement through the Markov resolvent, and
heterogeneous curvature then returns that displacement to the scalar matching
gap.  An explicit family with pairwise distinct curvatures shows that this
mechanism does not arise from a nontrivial synchrony quotient.

A companion result gives network-size-independent transverse decay and a
causal complete-line Green bound for a leaky delayed FitzHugh--Nagumo
network.  A bounded upper-triangular row reduction then transfers any
supplied scalar simple Lin root through delayed layers that need not share a
left stationary measure.  The constructed shared-resource root is selected
by the fixed canonical preparation; it is not identified here with a
preparation-independent physical canard, and the companion scalar root is
not constructed.
\end{abstract}
