\small\textit{Running title: Long-Delay Canard Connections}
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}
Slow--fast oscillators can switch from small responses to large pulses across
an exponentially sensitive canard threshold.  We ask whether this threshold
is determined by the delayed feedback visible in the critical mode, or
whether the organization of delay pathways transverse to that mode also
matters.  For a two-module FitzHugh--Nagumo retarded functional differential
equation with weak feedback and physical delays of order
\(\eps^{-1/2}\), we construct a family in which the total delayed gain and
the complete delay measure seen by the critical projection are independent
of a redistribution parameter \(\eta\).  Nevertheless, the transverse delay
channel changes.  We construct an invariant complete-history graph through
the fold, extend its mixed parameter estimates to a logarithmically growing
inner region, and use phase-normal one-sided Green operators to define a
canonical local history connection.  For every fixed admissible preparation
\(\cP\), its unique root satisfies
\[
 \mu_{c,\cP}(\del,\eta)-\mu_{c,\cP}(\del,0)
 =\frac{K(\theta_0-\theta_1)}{\sqrt6}\del^3\eta
  +O(\del^4\abs{\eta}+\del^3\eta^2),
 \qquad \del=\sqrt\eps.
\]
At the root, two retained complete histories coincide; the result is not an
observable-defined event.  The coefficient is generated by a transverse
delay response that returns nonlinearly to the critical direction, showing
that critical projections alone need not determine canard thresholds.  A
method-of-steps computation provides an independent sign and scale check.
The theorem is local and preparation-indexed; its transfer to an arbitrary
outer Fenichel selection requires an additional parameter-coherent boundary
estimate.
\end{abstract}
