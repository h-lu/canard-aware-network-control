\section{A logarithmic complete-history graph}
\label{sec:history-graph}

The rescaling in \cref{eq:blowup-scaling} makes the delay interval fixed,
but the two transverse equations remain singular.  This section removes
those equations by a graph that remembers complete histories.  The graph is
constructed by a special-flow Lyapunov--Perron equation: delayed states are
obtained from the backward flow of an unknown planar vector field, not by
inverting the RFDE semiflow.

\subsection{Special-flow form}

Shift the singular algebraic graph by setting
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stable-shift}
 \widehat Z=Z+\frac{\alpha}{2}X^2,
 \qquad
 \widehat W=W+\frac{\alpha}{2d_w}X^2,
 \qquad u=(X,Y)^T,\quad h=(\widehat Z,\widehat W)^T.
\end{equation}
The exact equations derived in \cref{sec:fold-jet} take the abstract form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:special-flow-system}
\begin{aligned}
 u'(s)&=q_0(u(s))+\del F\bigl(u(s),h(s),
             (u(s-\theta_j),h(s-\theta_j))_{j=0,1};
             \del,\nu,\eta\bigr),\\
 \del h'(s)&=Ah(s)+\del G\bigl(u(s),h(s),
             (u(s-\theta_j),h(s-\theta_j))_{j=0,1};
             \del,\nu,\eta\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:q0-A}
 q_0(X,Y)=\vect{Y-\alpha X^2\\-X},
 \qquad
 A=\mat{-2&0\\1&-d_w}.
\end{equation}
The matrix \(A\) is Hurwitz for every fixed \(d_w>0\), including the
nonsemisimple case \(d_w=2\).  Every term not shown in
\cref{eq:special-flow-system} is exactly divisible by \(\del\); no formal
division by the singular parameter is being made.

For a complete vector field \(Q\), let \(\Phi_Q^s\) be its flow and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graph-evaluation}
 \cE_{Q,H}(u)=\bigl(u,H(u),
   (\Phi_Q^{-\theta_j}u,H(\Phi_Q^{-\theta_j}u))_{j=0,1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
An invariant graph \(h=H(u)\), with internal vector field \(Q\), satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graph-invariance}
\begin{aligned}
 Q(u)&=q_0(u)+\del F(\cE_{Q,H}(u);\del,\nu,\eta),\\
 \del DH(u)Q(u)&=AH(u)+\del G(\cE_{Q,H}(u);\del,\nu,\eta).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Rather than solve the derivative-losing second equation directly, we use
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graph-transform}
\begin{aligned}
 \cT_Q(Q,H)(u)&=q_0(u)+\del F(\cE_{Q,H}(u);\del,\nu,\eta),\\
 \cT_H(Q,H)(u)&=\del\int_0^\infty \e^{Ar}
 G\bigl(\cE_{Q,H}(\Phi_Q^{-\del r}u);\del,\nu,\eta\bigr)\,\dd r.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The exponential kernel supplies a factor \(O(\del)\); the backward time in
the unknown planar flow is only \(\del r\), while the fixed delay
translations occur inside \(\cE_{Q,H}\).

\subsection{Admissible preparations}

Write the singular canard coordinates as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canard-coordinates}
 \chi=-2\alpha X,
 \qquad d=Y-\alpha X^2+\frac1{2\alpha}.
\end{equation}
The inverse coordinate map used below is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gamma-coordinate-map}
 \Gamma(\chi,d)
 =\left(-\frac{\chi}{2\alpha},
 d+\frac{\chi^2}{4\alpha}-\frac1{2\alpha}\right).
\end{equation}
Then \(\gamma_0(s)\) is the line \((\chi,d)=(s,0)\), and the singular
flow is exactly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canard-coordinate-flow}
 \chi'=1-2\alpha d,
 \qquad d'=\chi d.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Admissible preparation skeleton]
\label{def:admissible-preparation}
Fix finite derivative orders \(0\le a\le3\),
\(0\le i\le1\), \(0\le j\le2\), a buffer
\(B>2\theta_1+2\), a fixed hit buffer
\(0<B_{\rm hit}<B\), and exponents
\(1>\kappa_0>\cdots>\kappa_D>0\), where \(D\) is the finite number required
by the nested ordered-jet induction in \cref{app:growing-tube}.  Set
\(\widehat S_\del=S_\del+B_{\rm hit}\) and
\(R_\del=S_\del+B\).  An admissible
preparation skeleton \(\widehat{\cP}\) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item fixed smooth graph-preparation cutoffs and stable-state cutoff
      profiles, together with a fixed rule producing a complete singular
      preparation \(q_{0,\del}^{\rm pr}\) that equals \(q_0\) on the entire
      declared uncut flow hull and on fixed neighborhoods of
      \(\Gamma(\pm R_\del,0)\);
\item the nested flow-hull buffers
\(
 |\chi|\le \widehat S_\del+B_j, |d|\le\del^{\kappa_j}
\), with \(B_j=j(\theta_1+1)\), enlarged once so that every continuous
depth-two delay backtrack is contained in the uncut set;
\item a fixed degree-three extension operator in the normal coordinate,
      used to pass from the shrinking exact tube to a fixed-width planar
      tube, and separate planar trace-join profiles
      \(\vartheta_{\rm nor}\), \(\vartheta_{{\rm long},\del}\).  The latter
      equals one for \(\abs{\chi}\le\widehat S_\del\) and vanishes on fixed
      neighborhoods of \(\chi=\pm R_\del\); the normal profile equals one
      on \(\abs d\le\del^{\kappa_0}\);
\item the phase condition \(X(0)=0\) and the two endpoint level conditions
\(\mathscr H=0\) introduced in \cref{sec:connection}.
\end{enumerate}
The base profiles are independent of \((\del,\nu,\eta)\); their translated
and longitudinally rescaled supports are prescribed by the displayed tube
formula.  The graph-preparation cutoffs equal one on the declared flow hull,
make the extended vector fields complete, and belong to a common bounded
\(C^{12}\) class after the polynomial-in-\(S_\del\) weights used below are
included.  A bounded admissible class means that
all of these finite bounds and buffers are common.  Once the estimates have
produced \(p_*(\widehat{\cP})\), choose \(p>p_*\) and call
\(\cP=(p,\widehat{\cP})\) the resulting admissible preparation.
\end{definition}

The preparation datum determines the prepared planar field, rather than
merely a class of fields.  Let
\(\mathcal E_{\perp,\del}^{(3)}\) be its frozen left--right
Taylor--Seeley extension through degree three in the unscaled normal
coordinate, let \(\vartheta_{\rm nor}\) be its fixed-width normal cutoff,
and let \(\vartheta_{{\rm long},\del}\) be its prescribed translated
longitudinal join.  Let
\(Q^{[\widehat S]}_{\del,\nu,\eta}\) be the exact graph field obtained by
freezing the graph transform at that target.  Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prepared-planar-field}
 Q^{\rm pr}_{\del,\nu,\eta}(u)
 =q_{0,\del}^{\rm pr}(u)
 +\vartheta_{{\rm long},\del}(\chi(u))
  \vartheta_{\rm nor}(d(u))
  \mathcal E_{\perp,\del}^{(3)}
   [Q^{[\widehat S]}_{\del,\nu,\eta}-q_0](u).
\end{equation}
The fixed rules that extract the boundary Taylor jets, the two extension
operators, and every blending profile in
\cref{eq:prepared-planar-field} are components of \(\widehat\cP\); the jet
values themselves are supplied by the exact graph.  Thus \(Q^{\rm pr}\),
and hence the exact finite-\(\del\)
root, is uniquely determined once \(\cP\) is fixed.  The extension has the
rectangular \(C_u^3C_\nu^1C_\eta^2\) bounds of the graph, and
\(Q^{\rm pr}=Q\) only where the cutoffs equal one and the extension samples
the exact shrinking tube.  Outside the longitudinal join it is the fixed
complete singular preparation.

Such data exist: take a fixed one-dimensional bump that equals one on
\([-1,1]\), use its prescribed translated/rescaled copy in the longitudinal
variable with scale
\(\widehat S_\del+B_D+2(\theta_1+1)+2\), keep a fixed normal width, and use a fixed
Taylor--Seeley extension across the boundary of the shrinking normal tube.
The derivative bounds needed below are polynomial in \(S_\del\); no cutoff
on the scale \(\e^{-S_\del^2/2}\) is used.  This explicit construction also
shows why a flow hull, rather than a finite list of delayed endpoints, is
part of the definition.

\subsection{The graph theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Logarithmic special-flow history graph]
\label{thm:history-graph}
Fix the model data, a compact \(\nu\)-interval, a sufficiently small compact
\(\eta\)-interval contained in \cref{eq:eta-positive}, and a bounded
class of admissible preparation skeletons.  There is a finite \(p_*>4\)
depending only on their common bounds.  After fixing one skeleton and
\(p>p_*\), there exist \(\del_0,C,c,m>0\) with the following
properties for every \(0<\del<\del_0\).

For each fixed target \(\del\), freeze the cutoff at
\(S=\widehat S_\del=S_\del+B_{\rm hit}\) and use a dummy amplitude
\(\rho\in[-\del,\del]\) in
\cref{eq:graph-transform}.  The transform has a unique fixed point
\((Q_{\rho,S},H_{\rho,S})\) in its declared Lipschitz neighborhood.  At
\(\rho=\del\), on the core tube
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:core-tube}
 |\chi|\le \widehat S_\del,\qquad |d|\le\del^{\kappa_0},
\end{equation}
it agrees with the uncut RFDE and has the expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:growing-graph-expansion}
 (Q,H)=(q_0,0)+\del(Q_1,H_1)+\del^2(Q_2,H_2)
       +\del^3(R_Q,R_H).
\end{equation}
For \(0\le a\le3\), \(0\le i\le1\), and \(0\le j\le2\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:growing-graph-remainder}
 \bigl|D_u^a\partial_\nu^i\partial_\eta^j(R_Q,R_H)
       (\Gamma(\chi,d))\bigr|
 \le C\langle\chi\rangle^m\e^{c|\chi|}.
\end{equation}
The coefficients \(Q_k,H_k\), \(k\le2\), depend only on the uncut
depth-two flow hull and hence are common to all admissible preparations
that agree there.

Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:history-embedding}
 \iota_{\del,\nu,\eta}(u)(\vartheta)
 =\bigl(\Phi_Q^\vartheta u,
        H(\Phi_Q^\vartheta u)\bigr),
 \qquad -\theta_1\le\vartheta\le0,
\end{equation}
is an injective \(C^3\) embedding into the history space \(\cC\).  On every
special-flow segment whose current points and continuous delay backtracks
remain in the uncut hull, the RFDE semiflow \(\mathcal S_t\) obeys the exact
semiconjugacy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact-semiconjugacy}
 \mathcal S_t\iota_{\del,\nu,\eta}(u)
 =\iota_{\del,\nu,\eta}(\Phi_Q^tu).
\end{equation}
All constants are uniform over the declared parameter box and any bounded
preparation class with the same fixed \(p\).
\end{theorem}

In the remainder of the paper, \(Q,H\), and \(\iota\) always denote this
single fixed point at the target \(\widehat S_\del\) frozen in \(\cP\).
No later trace or history lift switches to a graph constructed with a
different longitudinal target.

\begin{proof}
On a fixed tube, \cref{eq:graph-transform} maps a ball with
\(\norm{Q-q_0}_{C^{0,1}}+\norm{H}_{C^{0,1}}=O(\del)\) into itself.
Gronwall's inequality bounds the change of \(\Phi_Q^{-t}\) under a change of
\(Q\), and the Hurwitz estimate
\(\norm{\e^{Ar}}\le M\e^{-\beta r}\) makes both components a contraction
with Lipschitz constant \(C\del\).  The fixed-point equation for \(H\) is
equivalent, along the complete \(Q\)-flow, to the stable differential
equation in \cref{eq:special-flow-system}.  This proves the exact orbit and
semiconjugacy assertions without differentiating the graph.

Finite regularity is recovered by an ordered fiber contraction.  Each
highest mixed jet satisfies an affine recurrence with linear part
\(O(\del)\); the exceptional first spatial jet is a nonlinear Lipschitz map
with the same external factor.  Strong convergence of parameter difference
quotients identifies the limiting jets and Taylor's integral formula gives
the third-order remainder.  The complete fixed-tube argument, including the
common jet fibers, is given in \cref{app:compact-graph,app:mixed-jets}.

For the growing tube, freeze the target-dependent cutoff and Taylor expand
only in the dummy amplitude \(\rho\).  Fixed-time flow jets in
\cref{eq:canard-coordinate-flow} cost at most
\(P(S)\e^{CS}\).  Since \(S=O(\bigl(\log(1/\del)\bigr)^{1/2})\),
\(\del P(S)\e^{CS}=o(1)\).  Nested local fibers ensure that every lower
jet is evaluated on the next larger tube, while the unique global
highest-block term retains the factor \(\rho\).  Finite atom depth makes
the first two coefficients independent of the outer cutoff.  This proves
\cref{eq:growing-graph-expansion,eq:growing-graph-remainder}; the complete
induction is in \cref{app:growing-tube}.

Finally, present-time evaluation followed by projection to \(u\) is the
identity, so \(\iota\) is injective.  Substitution of the fixed-point
equations into the curve
\((\Phi_Q^tu,H(\Phi_Q^tu))\) gives
\cref{eq:exact-semiconjugacy}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is deliberately model-specific at the singular limit.  A
standard parameter-dependent RFDE center-manifold theorem cannot simply be
applied before removing the transverse block, because its rescaled
generator contains \(A/\del\).  The special-flow transform is what makes
the estimates regular at \(\del=0\).
