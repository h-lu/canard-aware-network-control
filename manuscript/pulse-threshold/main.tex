\begin{abstract}
A finite pulse applied to a delay equation produces a curve in an
infinite-dimensional history space.  A physical threshold is therefore not a
current-state voltage event: it is an intersection of that pulse curve with a
codimension-one stable sheet, and it becomes a biological onset only when the
two sides are routed to different attractors.  We develop and validate the
return-map ingredients for this construction in a two-delay leaky
FitzHugh--Nagumo equation.  A selected complete-history event is proved to be
\(C^2\) on an explicit ball of arbitrary continuous histories of radius
\(1.2\times10^{-8}\).  Its center derivative is the square of the one-return
linear map, with two-step stable and unstable-inverse rates at most \(0.01\)
and \(0.302184\).  A continuous RFDE adjoint, an exact stable-row quotient,
and a signed-bimeasure residual formulate the six Hessian estimates needed by
a quantitative graph transform.  For pulse amplitudes
\(J\in[0.30105,0.30120]\), we validate a common event branch, its
event-aligned derivative, opposite endpoint functional signs, and containment
of all stable coordinates in an explicit ball.  These results give a strict
crossing implication conditional on the still-missing preferred-scale stable
graph.  A microscopic nonlinear outer return proves local attraction but does
not yet capture the post-pulse history.  Thus this paper establishes a
checkable route to a stable-sheet pulse threshold while explicitly leaving
the graph, crossing, and two-sided routing as open gates.
\end{abstract}
