\section{Claim ledger, reproducibility, and outlook}\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Four threshold and coordinate notions}

The terminology of the paper is fixed by the following distinctions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item A \emph{selected canard root} is a zero of a normalized
  complete-history Lin or graph--trace gap.  It depends on the declared
  phase and preparation until a zero-fiber theorem identifies the selected
  histories geometrically.
\item A \emph{selected pulse/stable-sheet threshold} is a zero of
  \cref{eq:stable-gap}.  It is defined by an actual physical pulse history,
  the declared event branch and section, and the local stable manifold of the
  inner periodic orbit.
\item A \emph{biological onset} is a selected pulse/stable-sheet threshold
  together with two-sided routing to the quiet and outer attracting objects.
\item The signed offset \(S=J-J_c(\xi)\) is always a
  \emph{selected-threshold target coordinate} once \(J_c\) has been proved.
  It is a \emph{biological safety coordinate} only on a neighborhood where
  the preceding onset and routing are uniform.
\end{enumerate}
There is no implication between (a) and (b) without a separate comparison
theorem; (c) and the safety interpretation in (d) require the additional
routing conclusion stated explicitly above.  In particular, a projected
voltage event is neither a complete-history canard root nor a basin separator.
