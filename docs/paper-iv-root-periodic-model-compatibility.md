# The lifted selected-root model is not the dual-scaffold periodic FHN model

Status: **exact incompatibility audit.**  The arbitrary-size lifted
selected-root theorem, the balanced bounded/autonomous handoff results, and the
validated frequency--amplitude branch cannot presently be combined into a
single three-input--three-output theorem.  The first result is proved for a
different RFDE.  Moreover, its delay-redistribution direction does not
preserve the completely synchronous periodic branch used by the other two
results.

In particular, the two declared plants are not the same RFDE.

This conclusion is narrower than a universal no-go theorem.  It rules out the
literal composition of the existing results and the proposed use of their
current \(\eta\)-direction.  It does not rule out a new dual-scaffold canard
root theorem, a nonsynchronous periodic branch, or a higher-order structural
channel.

generated by
[fhn_root_periodic_compatibility.py](../experiments/fhn_root_periodic_compatibility.py),
and tested in

## 1. The RFDE of the lifted selected-root theorem

For clarity it suffices to compare the exact two-module restrictions.  The
arbitrary-size theorem is an exact lift of this restriction, so a mismatch
already at \(N=2\) cannot be repaired by changing the module sizes.

The selected-root parent uses the fast field

\[
 F(z,y)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 z_1-z_1^3/3-y_1+(z_2-z_1)/2\\
 z_2-z_2^3/3-y_2+2(z_1-z_2)
 \end{pmatrix}
\tag{1.1}
\]

and the slow field

\[
 G(z;\mu)=
 \begin{pmatrix}z_1-\sigma-\mu\\ z_2-2\mu\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \sigma=\sqrt{3/2}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Its fold state and critical pair are

\[
 z_*=(\sigma,0)^T,
 \qquad y_*=(0,2\sigma)^T,
 \qquad r_*=(1,2)^T,
 \qquad \ell_*=(1/2,1/4)^T.
\tag{1.3}
\]

At the fold, the fast voltage Jacobian is

\[
 J_*^{\rm root}=
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&1/2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad J_*^{\rm root}r_*=0.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The recovery scaffold in the two-module lift, at the reference recovery rate
one, is

\[
 L_w^{\rm root}
 =-(I-r_*\ell_*^T)
 =\begin{pmatrix}-1/2&1/4\\1&-1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The two linear delay layers are

\[
 C_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}1/6&1/12\\1/6&1/4\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C_1=
 \begin{pmatrix}1/3&1/6\\1/2&5/12\end{pmatrix},
\tag{1.6}
\]

and the root-response direction is

\[
 C_0^\eta=C_0+\eta T,
 \qquad C_1^\eta=C_1-\eta T,
 \qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

For compatible prepared-tail selection, the parent theorem proves, for
sufficiently small \(\delta\) and \(\eta\),

\[
 \mu_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)-\mu_{c,N}(\delta,0)
 =\frac{K(\theta_0-\theta_1)}{\sqrt6}\,\delta^3\eta
 +O(\delta^4|\eta|+\delta^3\eta^2).
\tag{1.8}
\]

This is a local preparation-indexed complete-history root.  It is not a
periodic-orbit, detector, or autonomous-onset theorem.

## 2. The dual-scaffold periodic and handoff model

The frequency--amplitude validation and the balanced/handoff results use

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&f(v)-w+3(P-I)v\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_1
 \{B_0v(t-\tau_0)+B_1v(t-\tau_1)-v\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_3
 \{B_0h(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1h(v(t-\tau_1))-h(v)\}+u^v,\\
 \dot w={}&\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+2(P-I)w+u^w,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

where

\[
 f(s)=s-s^3/3,
 \qquad h(s)=(s-1)^3,
 \qquad (\varepsilon,a)=(1/5,3/5).
\tag{2.2}
\]

For the fixed rank-one two-module instance,

\[
 P^{(2)}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B_0^{(2)}=\frac12 I,
 \qquad
 B_1^{(2)}=\frac12
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The synchronous right fold has voltage \(\mathbf1=(1,1)^T\), critical pair

\[
 r_{\rm sync}=\mathbf1,
 \qquad \ell_{\rm sync}=\frac12\mathbf1,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and fold voltage and recovery scaffolds

\[
 J_*^{\rm per}=3(P^{(2)}-I)
 =\begin{pmatrix}-3/2&3/2\\3/2&-3/2\end{pmatrix},
\tag{2.5}
\]

\[
 L_w^{\rm per}=2(P^{(2)}-I)
 =\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

On a completely synchronous history, (2.1) reduces to the validated scalar
two-delay RFDE.  The periodic parameter-box theorem varies only
\((\kappa_1,\kappa_3)\) near \((0.2,0.25)\); it contains no \(\eta\)-column.
The autonomous handoff theorem uses the same baseline (2.1) and proves a
controlled onset followed by a finite autonomous excursion on the exactly
prepared synchronous leaf.

## 3. Exact model mismatches

The common nodewise \((v_i,w_i)\) state type and the local term
\(v_i-v_i^3/3-w_i\) do not make the RFDEs equal.  The decisive differences
are exact:

| object | lifted selected-root model | periodic/control model |
|---|---|---|
| fold voltage | \((\sqrt{3/2},0)\) | \((1,1)\) |
| critical voltage direction | \((1,2)\) | \((1,1)\) |
| fast fold Jacobian | (1.4) | (2.5) |
| recovery scaffold | (1.5) | (2.6) |
| unfolding column | \((-1,-2)^T\) for \(\mu\) | \((-1,-1)^T\) for \(a\) |
| delay layers | (1.6) | (2.3) |
| linear current compensator | \(C_0+C_1=\begin{psmallmatrix}1/2&1/4\\2/3&2/3\end{psmallmatrix}\) | \(I\) |
| delayed nonlinearity | one linear channel with fixed \(K\) | linear and shifted-cubic channels \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3)\) |

The lifted layers also fail the balanced two-half-delay identities required by
the control theorem.  With \(e=(1,1)^T\),

\[
 C_0e-\frac12e=
 \begin{pmatrix}-1/4\\-1/12\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C_1e-\frac12e=
 \begin{pmatrix}0\\5/12\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For \(\pi^T=(1/2,1/2)\), their left-balance residuals are

\[
 \pi^TC_0-\frac12\pi^T=(-1/12,-1/12),
 \qquad
 \pi^TC_1-\frac12\pi^T=(1/6,1/24).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the selected-root layers cannot be substituted into the balanced sign
cone or autonomous handoff theorem.  Conversely, the symmetric layers (2.3)
cannot be substituted into the selected-root theorem while retaining its
proved coefficient, because that theorem pins the full model and the
direction (1.7).

There is a further scale gap.  The root theorem holds for an unspecified
\(0<\delta<\delta_0\), whereas the periodic and handoff validations are at
\(\delta=1/\sqrt5\).  No current estimate proves that this fixed value is in
the root theorem's small-\(\delta\) interval.  This is secondary to the
literal equation mismatch, but it would remain a gate even after redesigning
the model.  The centers also cannot simply be identified: the root satisfies
\(\mu_c=\delta^2\nu_c=O(\delta^2)\), with \(\nu_c\) near
\(-11/(24\alpha)\), whereas the periodic box fixes \(a=3/5\) and varies
\((\kappa_1,\kappa_3)\) near \((1/5,1/4)\).  Their unequal unfolding columns
show that relabelling \(\mu\) as \(a\) is not a same-model identification.

## 4. Why the existing \(\eta\)-direction is not periodic-branch invisible

The selected-root coefficient uses two simultaneous identities:

\[
 Tr_*=(1,-2)^T\ne0,
 \qquad
 \ell_*^TTr_*=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The perturbation is therefore invisible to the root critical projection but
forces the root stable direction.  This is the mechanism behind (1.8).

The validated periodic branch has a different critical direction.  For that
direction,

\[
 Tr_{\rm sync}=(1,-2)^T\ne0,
 \qquad
 \ell_{\rm sync}^TTr_{\rm sync}=-\frac12\ne0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently the lifted \(\eta\)-direction does not preserve the validated
completely synchronous branch and is not even invisible to its collective
projection.  Total-layer cancellation
\((C_0+\eta T)+(C_1-\eta T)=C_0+C_1\) is insufficient: the two layers act at
different delays.

This is not only a generic-history objection.  The validated orbit is
nonconstant, has exactly one maximum and one minimum per validated period
near \(16.5404\), and its two delays differ by \(\sqrt5\), strictly between
zero and that period.  Hence
\(V(t-\tau_0)-V(t-\tau_1)\) cannot vanish identically: otherwise
\(\sqrt5\) would be an additional smaller period, contradicting the unique
extrema.  Thus transplanting (1.7) into the periodic linear delay channel
produces a nonzero transverse and collective forcing somewhere on the actual
validated orbit.

The following elementary lemma makes the obstruction precise.

> **Lemma 4.1 (two-atom synchronization obstruction).**
> Let \(B_0^\eta=B_0+\eta T\) and
> \(B_1^\eta=B_1-\eta T\) be a linear redistribution in the delayed-voltage
> channel at two distinct fixed delays.  On a scalar synchronous history,
> the \(\eta\)-dependent contribution is
> \[
>   \eta T\mathbf1\{s(t-\tau_0)-s(t-\tau_1)\}.
> \]
> It vanishes for every scalar history if and only if
> \(T\mathbf1=0\).  Under that condition the same direction supplies no
> direct forcing of the synchronous critical history.

**Proof.** Sufficiency is immediate.  For necessity, prescribe a continuous
scalar history for which the two displayed delayed values differ.
The coefficient of that difference must vanish, so \(T\mathbf1=0\).  The
synchronous critical vector is \(\mathbf1\), proving the last statement.
\(\square\)

Lemma 4.1 is restricted to two fixed delay atoms, linear layer
redistribution, and exact invisibility on every synchronous history.  It is
not a universal statement that every sync-invisible parameter has zero canard
effect.  It says that the *existing direct lifted mechanism*, which needs
nonzero action on the critical history, cannot simultaneously be exactly
invisible on this synchronous branch.  Higher-order effects, off-synchrony
objects, moving delays, or an additional actuator are not excluded; each
would require a new theorem.

## 5. What can and cannot currently be composed

Two positive same-model links remain valid:

1. the balanced bounded control chain and the autonomous handoff theorem use
   the same baseline (2.1); and
2. the balanced model's synchronous restriction is the scalar RFDE used by
   the validated frequency--amplitude branch.

Hence the existing results already give a **controlled onset followed by a
finite autonomous excursion**, together with separate synchronous-branch
frequency and amplitude outputs.  They do not give autonomous onset, landing
on the periodic branch, or a biological basin.

The selected root cannot be appended as a third output.  In particular, no
current result proves a parameter-linked map

\[
 (\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)
 \longmapsto(F,A,\mu_c),
\tag{5.1}
\]

or replaces \(\mu_c\) by an exact detector latency or autonomous safety
margin.  The periodic theorem has no \(\eta\)-neighborhood, the selected-root
theorem has no \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3)\) dual-scaffold family, and the handoff
certificate gives rigorous deadlines and corridors rather than a validated
differentiable third-output response column.

Even a future parameter-linked theorem would not by itself be
trajectory-linked.  A trajectory-linked theorem would additionally have to
show that the history selected by the canard root enters the controlled or
autonomous handoff channel.  The present handoff starts from a feedback-created
constant history \(\Phi_{\pm1/2}\), not from either selected canard history.

## 6. Minimum route to a genuine three-input--three-output theorem

A valid positive theorem needs all of the following in one declared RFDE.

1. Prove a complete-history selected-root theorem for the dual-scaffold model
   (2.1), including the shifted-cubic delay channel and the fixed scaffold
   values \(D=3,E=2\).
2. Choose a third structural parameter.  Either preserve synchrony and find a
   mechanism beyond the direct forcing excluded by Lemma 4.1, or allow the
   periodic orbit to leave synchrony and validate the resulting full-network
   periodic branch uniformly in module size.
3. Validate the periodic branch and its \((F,A)\) sensitivities on a genuine
   \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)\) box.
4. Define an exact third output and prove its differentiability.  A conservative
   deadline is not automatically an exact detector-latency observable, and the
   reset coordinate \(-r\) is only operational safety.
5. Prove nonsingularity of the resulting \(3\times3\) response matrix with an
   honest uniform remainder.
6. Extend the bounded preparation and autonomous handoff barriers over the
   same three-parameter box.
7. For a trajectory-linked or biological claim, prove a same-model comparison
   from the selected root histories to an intrinsic pulse/quiet separator and
   then to the autonomous excursion channel.

Items 1--6 would yield a parameter-linked control theorem.  Item 7 is the
additional physical onset and basin gate.
