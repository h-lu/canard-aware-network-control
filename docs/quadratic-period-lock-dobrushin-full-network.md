# A Dobrushin full-network lift of the quadratic period-locked canard root

Status: **proved model fit and full-network canonical-graph root theorem for
one arbitrary-finite uniformly mixing balanced class; fixed operating
parameter and biological onset remain open.**  This note promotes the
synchronous theorem in
[the quadratic period-lock paper](quadratic-period-locked-selected-root.md)
to a genuine finite-network statement.  The topology need not be rank one,
and the network size is arbitrary.  What is unique is the selected connection
on the
canonical retained graph in the declared local tube.  This is not uniqueness
among arbitrary RFDE histories outside that graph tube, and it is not a
global pulse separator.

The key point is that the dual scaffold, after its anisotropic fold blow-up,
has a uniformly stable triangular network-transverse generator.  Under a
common Dobrushin mixing gap the constants do not deteriorate with network
size.  Synchrony gives the exact invariant transverse graph \(h=0\); the
dimension-uniform graph theorem makes that graph locally unique.  Therefore
the scalar complete-history root is also the full-network canonical root.

in

generated by

## 1. The admitted finite-network class

For every finite \(N\), let \(Q_N\ge0\) be row stochastic with a strictly
positive stationary probability row \(\pi_N^T\):

\[
 Q_N\mathbf1=\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi_N^TQ_N=\pi_N^T,
 \qquad \pi_N^T\mathbf1=1.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Its Dobrushin coefficient is

\[
 \tau(Q_N)=\frac12\max_{i,k}
 \sum_j |(Q_N)_{ij}-(Q_N)_{kj}|.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Fix one \(\gamma>0\), independent of \(N\), and assume

\[
 \boxed{\tau(Q_N)\le1-\gamma.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The two delay layers are nonnegative and balanced half-mass operators:

\[
 B_{j,N}\mathbf1=\frac12\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 \pi_N^TB_{j,N}=\frac12\pi_N^T,
 \qquad j=0,1.
\tag{1.4}
\]

No identity \(B_{0,N}+B_{1,N}=Q_N\) or
\(B_{0,N}+B_{1,N}=\Pi_N\) is imposed, where

\[
 \Pi_N=\mathbf1\pi_N^T.
\tag{1.5}
\]

On this class consider the extended dual-scaffold RFDE

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&v-\frac13v^{\circ3}-w+3(Q_N-I)v\\
 &+\delta^2\kappa_1
 \{B_{0,N}v(t-4/\delta)+B_{1,N}v(t-5/\delta)-v\}\\
 &+\delta^2\kappa_3
 \{B_{0,N}H(v(t-4/\delta))+B_{1,N}H(v(t-5/\delta))-H(v)\}\\
 &+\delta^2\eta\Pi_N\!\left[
 (v(t)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(v(t-\Theta_*/\delta)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}\right],\\
 \dot w={}&\delta^2(v-a\mathbf1)+2(Q_N-I)w,
 \qquad H(v)=(v-\mathbf1)^{\circ3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.6}
\]

The gain pair stays in the compact neighborhood used by the scalar
small-\(\delta\) theorem.  The delay support
\(\{0,4,5,\Theta_*\}\) is fixed in fold time, and the phase space and
canonical preparation use the enlarged horizon
\(\max\{4,5,\Theta_*\}\) before differentiating in \(\eta\).

Condition (1.3) is substantive.  The class of all balanced matrices contains
\(Q_N=I\), reducible matrices, and matrices with a closing mixing gap.  Such
families may have extra neutral modes and are not covered by the theorem
below.

## 2. Uniform critical and transverse coordinates

Put

\[
 E_N=\ker\pi_N^T,
 \qquad
 P_{c,N}=\Pi_N,
 \qquad
 P_{\perp,N}=I-\Pi_N.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Use the dimension-controlled norm

\[
 \|x\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}}
 =|\pi_N^Tx|+\operatorname{osc}(x),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{osc}(x)=\max_i x_i-\min_i x_i.
\tag{2.2}
\]

On \(E_N\) this is just \(\operatorname{osc}(x)\).  Moreover,

\[
 \|P_{c,N}\|\le1,
 \qquad
 \|P_{\perp,N}\|\le1,
 \qquad
 \|x\|_\infty\le\|x\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}},
\tag{2.3}
\]

without a lower bound on \(\min_i\pi_{N,i}\).  Thus neither the critical
projection nor the transverse projection becomes ill conditioned as
\(N\) grows.

The balance identities imply \(Q_NE_N\subset E_N\) and
\(B_{j,N}E_N\subset E_N\).  Since \(2B_{j,N}\) is row stochastic,

\[
 \|B_{j,N}z\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}}
 \le\frac12\|z\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}},
 \qquad z\in E_N.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Pointwise products have dimension-independent bounds; for example,

\[
 \|x\circ y\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}}
 \le3\|x\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}}
       \|y\|_{\pi,\mathrm{osc}}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently every derivative of the componentwise cubic needed by the
special-flow theorem has a common operator bound on a common coordinate
tube.

## 3. Exact anisotropic blow-up

With fold time \(s=\delta t\), write

\[
\begin{aligned}
 v&=\mathbf1+\delta X\mathbf1+\delta^2z,
 &\pi_N^Tz&=0,\\
 w&=\frac23\mathbf1-\delta^2Y\mathbf1+\delta^4W,
 &\pi_N^TW&=0,\\
 a&=1+\delta^2\nu.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The collective equations have leading part

\[
 X'=Y-X^2,
 \qquad Y'=-X,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and their restriction to \(z=W=0\) is exactly the scalar RFDE in the
quadratic period-lock theorem.  The transverse equations have the form

\[
 \delta\binom{z}{W}'
 =A_N\binom{z}{W}
 +\delta G_N(X,Y,z,W,\mathcal D z,\mathcal D W;
             \delta,\nu,\eta),
\tag{3.3}
\]

with leading block

\[
 A_N=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  3L_N&0\\ I&2L_N
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad L_N=(Q_N-I)|_{E_N}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Indeed, the transverse voltage scaling gives
\(\delta z'=3L_Nz+O(\delta)\), while the recovery equation gives the exact
leading relation

\[
 \delta W'=z+2L_NW.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The weak linear delay layer first appears in \(G_N\) with an additional
factor \(\delta\), and the cubic delay layer is still higher order.  The
quadratic period-lock term has collective range and hence contributes no
transverse output at any history.

There is also no hidden order-\(\delta\) return from a transverse state into
the quadratic carrier.  Since \(\pi_N^Tz=0\),

\[
 \pi_N^T(\delta X\mathbf1+\delta^2z)^{\circ2}
 =\delta^2X^2+\delta^4\pi_N^Tz^{\circ2}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus the mixed term \(2\delta^3X\pi_N^Tz\) vanishes exactly.  The first
transverse-square return is two orders beyond the scalar quadratic forcing
and is part of the uniform nonlinear remainder.  On the invariant graph
\(z=W=0\) it vanishes identically.

## 4. The transverse semigroup theorem

> **Proposition 4.1 (uniform triangular scaffold decay).**  Under (1.3),
> the block generator (3.4), acting on
> \(E_N\times E_N\) with the maximum oscillation norm, satisfies
> \[
>  \boxed{
>  \|e^{A_Nt}\|
>  \le(1+\gamma^{-1})e^{-2\gamma t},
>  \qquad t\ge0,}
> \tag{4.1}
> \]
> for every admitted \(N\) and topology.

**Proof.**  The Poisson expansion and Dobrushin inequality give

\[
 \|e^{cL_Nt}\|_{E_N\to E_N}
 \le e^{-c\gamma t},
 \qquad c>0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because all diagonal blocks are functions of the same \(L_N\),

\[
 e^{A_Nt}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  e^{3L_Nt}&0\\
  \displaystyle\int_0^t
  e^{2L_N(t-s)}e^{3L_Ns}\,ds&e^{2L_Nt}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The lower-left block is bounded by

\[
 \int_0^t e^{-2\gamma(t-s)}e^{-3\gamma s}\,ds
 =e^{-2\gamma t}\frac{1-e^{-\gamma t}}{\gamma}
 \le\gamma^{-1}e^{-2\gamma t}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Taking the maximum row sum proves (4.1). \(\square\)

This is a semigroup estimate, not a bare eigenvalue statement.  It remains
valid for directed and nonnormal \(Q_N\) because the norm and the Dobrushin
coefficient control the full semigroup.

## 5. Dimension-uniform history-graph model fit

> **Proposition 5.1 (dual-scaffold graph fit).**  Fix a common
> \(\gamma>0\), bounded gain box, fixed fold-time support
> \(\{0,4,5,\Theta_*\}\), and the enlarged-horizon canonical preparation.
> The transformed RFDE (3.2)--(3.3) satisfies the hypotheses of the
> [dimension-uniform special-flow history-graph theorem](dimension-uniform-special-flow-history-graph.md)
> with constants independent of \(N,Q_N,B_{0,N},B_{1,N}\) in the class
> (1.1)--(1.4).

**Proof.**  Proposition 4.1 supplies the required stable semigroup with
common constants.  Equations (2.3)--(2.5) give uniformly conditioned
projectors, delay-operator bounds, and polynomial multilinear bounds.  The
delays are fixed atoms in fold time, so there is no moving-support derivative
loss.  Balance makes every differentiated delay measure annihilate the
affine collective anchor required by the graph theorem; in the present
structural \(\eta\)-direction the support and layers are fixed, so the
measure derivatives actually vanish.  The vector field is polynomial in the
state and \(C^\infty\) in all declared parameters.

For completeness, the preparation does not use the nonsmooth oscillation
norm as a radial cutoff.  Use the scalar theorem's compact
\(C^\infty\) cutoff in the planar \((X,Y)\) fold coordinates.  In every
current and delayed transverse \(z_i,W_i\) slot use one common scalar
\(C^\infty\) saturation, equal to the identity on the retained coordinate
interval, and apply \(P_{\perp,N}\) to every prepared transverse output.
The coordinate bound in (2.3), the product bound (2.5), and the fact that the
same scalar saturation is used in every slot give one set of \(C_b^{12}\)
operator bounds without an \(N\)-factor.  Thus no smooth bump function on the
oscillation-norm space is being assumed.

The same continuous depth-two flow hull used by the scalar theorem contains
every backtrack through \(0,4,5,\Theta_*\), and these slotwise preparations
supply the common logarithmic tube.  These are precisely the model-fit
hypotheses; none depends on the number of coordinates. \(\square\)

Proposition 5.1 does not claim a separately constructed full-network
Banach-scale trace inverse.  None is needed here: graph uniqueness identifies
the full retained graph with \(h=0\), and the one-sided traces and their
simple gap are then literally the already-proved scalar trace problem.  A
full \(2N\)-state Lin--Fredholm or off-graph trace theorem would be a stronger
and different statement.

The exact synchronous subspace makes this fit stronger than a perturbative
graph calculation.  At \(z=W=0\), every transverse residual in (3.3)
vanishes.  Hence

\[
 H_{N,\delta,\nu,\eta}(X,Y)\equiv(0,0)
\tag{5.1}
\]

is an invariant complete-history graph for every admitted topology and
parameter in the local graph cylinder.  The contraction theorem gives a
unique graph in its admissible ball, so (5.1) is the canonical retained
graph, not merely one invariant graph among several nearby candidates.

## 6. Full-network canonical selected-root theorem

> **Theorem 6.1 (uniformly mixing full-network root lift).**  Fix
> \(\Theta_*>0\), a common Dobrushin margin \(\gamma>0\), the compact gain
> neighborhood of the scalar theorem, and one enlarged-horizon canonical
> preparation.  There are \(\delta_0,\eta_0,c_0,C>0\), independent of
> \(N\) and of every topology satisfying (1.1)--(1.4), such that for
> \[
> 0<\delta<\delta_0,
> \qquad |\eta|<\eta_0,
> \tag{6.1}
> \]
> the full network RFDE (1.6) has exactly one canonical selected
> complete-history connection on its retained graph with
> \(|\nu+1/8|<c_0\).  It is synchronous, and in the physical parameter
> \(a=1+\delta^2\nu\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> a_{c,N}(\delta,\eta)-a_{c,N}(\delta,0)
> =-\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\delta^3\eta
> +O(\delta^4|\eta|+\delta^3\eta^2).}
> \tag{6.2}
> \]
> The remainder constant can be chosen uniformly over the admitted finite
> network class.  In particular, the root and its leading coefficient are
> independent of \(N\) and topology.

**Proof.**  Proposition 5.1 constructs the unique retained complete-history
graph.  Exact synchrony gives the invariant graph (5.1), so uniqueness makes
the retained graph identically zero in transverse coordinates.  The reduced
flow, one-sided phase-normal traces, complete-history matcher, and preparation
on that graph are therefore literally the scalar objects in
[Theorem 4.1 of the quadratic period-lock paper](quadratic-period-locked-selected-root.md).
That theorem gives the unique scalar root and (6.2).  The injective
full-history embedding of the graph converts zero scalar gap into equality of
the two complete \(2N\)-state retained histories.  The common graph bounds
and the topology-independent scalar trace/simple-root bounds make the
constants uniform over the class. \(\square\)

The phrase *full-network canonical root* has a precise local meaning here:
the attracting and repelling histories are selected by the proved retained
graph and the fixed preparation, and their entire RFDE histories agree at the
root.  The theorem does not assert that every arbitrary initial history lies
on this graph, that no other global RFDE connection exists outside the tube,
or that the root is a basin boundary.

### Fixed-\(N\) and closing-gap variants

For any fixed finite balanced topology with
\(\gamma_N=1-\tau(Q_N)>0\), the same argument applies with constants depending
on \(\gamma_N\).  For a sequence with \(\gamma_N\downarrow0\), (4.1) loses both
rate and prefactor, so no size-uniform theorem follows.  Reducible or identity
topologies require a vector-gap or multiple-center analysis rather than a
silent application of Theorem 6.1.

## 7. What this closes and what it does not

Theorem 6.1 closes a genuine part of the earlier general-network gap:

- it quantifies over every finite topology in an explicit directed,
  nonnormal, non-rank-one mixing class;
- it proves a dimension-uniform transverse semigroup rather than assuming a
  current-state spectral gap;
- it verifies the dual-scaffold model fit to the full-history graph theorem;
- it proves local full-network canonical root uniqueness, not only existence
  of one lifted synchronous solution;
- it retains the complete delay horizon and preparation dependence.

It does not close the fixed-\(\varepsilon\) gate.  With
\(\delta_*=1/\sqrt5\) and \(\Theta_*=\delta_*T_*\), formula (6.2) predicts
\(-T_*/50\), but the theorem does not prove that
\(\delta_*<\delta_0\).  A fixed-\(\varepsilon=1/5\) complete-history BVP,
augmented inverse, and dynamic-adjoint enclosure are still required.

It also does not identify the selected root with input-independent physical
onset.  The controlled clamped reset remains fixed because the quadratic
carrier vanishes on constant histories.  A physical outer selection,
root/event factorization, two-channel capture, and no-return or basin theorem
remain separate obligations.
