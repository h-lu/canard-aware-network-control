## 1. The sliding-window equivalence theorem

Consider the RFDE

\[
 \dot z(t)=F(z_t;\lambda),
 \qquad z_t(\theta)=z(t+\theta),
 \qquad -h\le \theta\le0.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(z^r(t;\lambda)\) be a classical orbit family defined on every history
window under consideration.  To obtain a \(C^1\), \(W^{1,p}\)-valued chart,
assume at least \(W^{2,p}\) time regularity on those windows; \(C^2\) is a
stronger sufficient hypothesis, not an equivalent one.  The second-jet
formulas below require the explicitly stated higher joint regularity.  The
superscript \(r\) is only a label here; repelling selection is an additional
hypothesis below.  Fix a bounded parameter-independent affine history phase
\(\chi(\phi)=\ell(\phi)+c\), with bounded linear part \(\ell\), and put

\[
 p(t,\lambda)=\chi(z^r_t(\lambda)).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Assume on a compact interval that

\[
 |p_t(t,\lambda)|\ge \kappa>0.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The inverse-function theorem gives a local inverse
\(t=\tau(\xi,\lambda)\).  Define

\[
 \Gamma^r(\xi;\lambda)(\theta)
 =z^r(\tau(\xi,\lambda)+\theta;\lambda),
 \qquad
 q^r(\xi,\lambda)=p_t(\tau(\xi,\lambda),\lambda).
 \tag{1.4}
\]

> **Theorem 1.1 (monotone orbit \(\Longleftrightarrow\) nonstationary
> invariant history chart).**  The chart in (1.4) satisfies
>
> \[
> \chi(\Gamma^r)=\xi,
> \qquad \ell(\Gamma^r_\xi)=1,
> \tag{1.5}
> \]
>
> \[
> q^r\Gamma^r_\xi(\theta)=\partial_\theta\Gamma^r(\theta),
> \qquad
> q^r\Gamma^r_\xi(0)=F(\Gamma^r;\lambda).
> \tag{1.6}
> \]
>
> The first identity in (1.6) is an equality in \(L^p\), hence almost
> everywhere in \(\theta\).  Since its left-hand side belongs to
> \(W^{1,p}\), it bootstraps each chart history to \(W^{2,p}\), so the
> endpoint derivative trace agrees with \(q^r\Gamma^r_\xi(0)\).
>
> Its image lies in the classical solution manifold and
> \(\operatorname{rank}\Gamma^r_\xi=1\).  Conversely, every \(C^1\) pair
> \(\Gamma\in C^1(I_\xi;W^{1,p})\) and \(q\) satisfying (1.5)--(1.6) with
> \(|q|\ge\kappa\), and whose scalar characteristics remain in the chart
> domain throughout every required interval of length \(h\), is locally the
> sliding-window chart of one nonstationary RFDE trajectory segment, unique
> up to time translation.  A complete orbit follows only if the scalar flow
> has the corresponding completeness.

**Proof.**  Differentiating (1.4) gives

\[
 \Gamma^r_\xi(\theta)
 =\frac{\dot z^r(\tau+\theta)}{p_t(\tau)},
 \tag{1.7}
\]

which proves (1.5)–(1.6).  At \(\theta=0\), (1.1) gives the boundary
identity and therefore solution-manifold compatibility.

For the converse, solve \(\dot\xi=q(\xi)\).  Using the absolutely continuous
representatives of the Sobolev histories, the weak transport equation in
(1.6) gives along each retained characteristic

\[
 \Gamma(\xi(t))(\theta)
 =\Gamma(\xi(t+\theta))(0).
 \tag{1.8}
\]

Thus \(z(t)=\Gamma(\xi(t))(0)\) has
\(z_t=\Gamma(\xi(t))\), and the boundary identity in (1.6) gives (1.1).
No inverse of the ambient RFDE semiflow is used. \(\square\)

If \(q>0\) on \([\xi_-,\xi_+]\), the exact available backward time from
\(\xi_0\) is

\[
 b_-(\xi_0,\lambda)
 =\int_{\xi_-}^{\xi_0}\frac{d\zeta}{q(\zeta,\lambda)}.
 \tag{1.9}
\]

This is the certified backward time before leaving the validated phase-chart
patch.  It moves along the already constructed trajectory; when that
trajectory belongs to a selected complete orbit, no ill-posed backward RFDE
initial-value problem is being solved.

### 1.1 What the theorem does not select

Equations (1.5)–(1.6) express invariance and parameterization.  They do not
distinguish the canard-threshold orbit from another invariant orbit.  The
orbit \(z^r\) must first be selected by a tail/outer equation and a transverse
anchor that contain no Lin connection variables \(x^-,x^+\).

It is permissible to solve the selection block and the Lin block in one
large system.  At fixed shared parameter, its derivative must have the block
structure

\[
 \begin{bmatrix}
 D_z\mathcal T&0\\
 *&D_{x^-,x^+}\mathcal L
 \end{bmatrix},
 \tag{1.10}
\]

where \(\mathcal T\) is the independent tail/outer problem, and the anchored
operator \(D_z\mathcal T\) must be an isomorphism (equivalently, the selected
orbit must be locally unique before the Lin equations are used).  If a
shared parameter is varied, its \(\mathcal T_\lambda,\mathcal L_\lambda\)
columns must also be retained.

It is not permissible to use a chart defined from the same unknown right Lin
branch as an **independent endpoint constraint**.  Such a sliding chart can
be mathematically valid, but it remains connection dependent.  If
\(\xi_{L_+}=p_{x^+}(L_+)\), then

\[
 \operatorname{End}_{L_+}x^+-\Gamma_{x^+}(\xi_{L_+})\equiv0,
 \tag{1.11}
\]

and the total derivative of this composed “endpoint condition” also
vanishes.  It supplies no endpoint codimension and is a strict circularity
as a selection argument.  More precisely, put
\(E=\operatorname{End}_{L_+}x^+\) and
\(R(x^+,\xi)=E-\Gamma_{x^+}(\xi)\).  At \(\xi=\chi(E)\), differentiating the
inverse phase gives

\[
 d\tau=\frac{d\xi-\ell(dE)}q,
 \qquad
 \boxed{dR=\Gamma_\xi\{\ell(dE)-d\xi\}}.
 \tag{1.12}
\]

Thus, even if \(\xi\) is held independent, the range of \(DR\) is contained
in \(\operatorname{span}\{\Gamma_\xi\}\): it can impose at most one phase
condition, not an independent \(H\)-valued endpoint condition.  Composing
\(\xi=\chi(E)\) makes \(dR=0\) identically.

## 2. Parameter jets are one-dimensional orbit jets

Let

\[
 u_\lambda(t)=\partial_\lambda z^r(t;\lambda)
 \tag{2.1}
\]

at fixed orbit time.  It satisfies

\[
 \dot u_\lambda
 =D_\phi F(z^r_t;\lambda)(u_\lambda)_t
  +F_\lambda(z^r_t;\lambda),
 \tag{2.2}
\]

together with the derivatives of the independent selection and anchor
equations.  For the affine phase above, set

\[
 p_\lambda(t)=\ell((u_\lambda)_t).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

At fixed chart coordinate \(\xi\), ordinary inverse-function calculus gives

\[
 \tau_\lambda=-\frac{p_\lambda}{q},
 \tag{2.4}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma^r_\lambda
 =(u_\lambda)_\tau-p_\lambda\Gamma^r_\xi,
 \qquad
 q^r_\lambda
 =p_{t\lambda}-\frac{p_\lambda}{q}p_{tt}.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

In particular, \(\ell(\Gamma^r_\lambda)=0\).  Formula (2.5) is exactly the
fixed-phase correction that was previously assigned to a two-dimensional
chart sensitivity solve.  The second inverse-phase jet is

\[
 \tau_{\lambda\mu}
 =-\frac{
 p_{\lambda\mu}+p_{t\lambda}\tau_\mu
 +p_{t\mu}\tau_\lambda+p_{tt}\tau_\lambda\tau_\mu}{q}.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Thus a \(C^1_aC^2_\eta\) family of independently selected one-dimensional
orbits supplies the parameter differentiability needed by the root response,
provided it also has the required time regularity.  A sufficient Sobolev
contract for \(\Gamma_{\lambda\mu}\in W^{1,p}\) is
\(z\in W^{3,p}\), \(u_\lambda,u_\mu\in W^{2,p}\), and
\(u_{\lambda\mu}\in W^{1,p}\) on every retained window.  Explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_{\lambda\mu}={}&
 (u_{\lambda\mu})_\tau
 +(\dot u_\lambda)_\tau\tau_\mu
 +(\dot u_\mu)_\tau\tau_\lambda\\
 &+\ddot z_\tau\tau_\lambda\tau_\mu
 +\dot z_\tau\tau_{\lambda\mu},
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6a}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_{\lambda\mu}={}&p_{t\lambda\mu}
 +p_{tt\lambda}\tau_\mu+p_{tt\mu}\tau_\lambda\\
 &+p_{ttt}\tau_\lambda\tau_\mu+p_{tt}\tau_{\lambda\mu}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6b}
\]

All \(p\)-derivatives in (2.6)--(2.6b) are first taken at fixed orbit time
and then evaluated at \(t=\tau(\xi,\lambda)\).  For a general
\(\ell\in(W^{1,p})^*\), a simple sufficient condition for the
\(p_{ttt}\)-term is \(z\in W^{4,p}\), together with the matching mixed-jet
regularity.  If \(\ell\) is only current-state evaluation, weaker pointwise
\(C^3\) hypotheses can suffice.

### 2.1 A natural terminal-local phase in the quadratic model

In fold variables,

\[
 V=1+\delta X,
 \quad W=\frac23-\delta^2Y,
 \quad a=1+\delta^2\nu,
 \quad s=\delta t,
 \quad \delta=\frac1{\sqrt5}.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

Choose

\[
 \xi=Y(0)
 =\frac{2/3-W(0)}{\delta^2}.
 \tag{2.8}
\]

The slow equation gives the exact identities

\[
 q_{\rm fold}=\frac{d\xi}{ds}=-X+\delta\nu,
 \qquad
 q_{\rm phys}=\frac{d\xi}{dt}=a-V,
 \qquad
 q_{\rm fold}=\frac{q_{\rm phys}}\delta.
 \tag{2.9}
\]

Hence one directed bound

\[
 -X+\delta\nu\ge\kappa_{\rm fold}>0
 \tag{2.10}
\]

simultaneously proves phase transversality, injectivity, rank one, and the
available backward scalar flow.  Its first jets are

\[
 q^f_\nu=\delta-(\Gamma^f_\nu)_X(0),
 \qquad q^f_\eta=-(\Gamma^f_\eta)_X(0),
 \tag{2.11}
\]

\[
 q^p_a=1-(\Gamma^p_a)_V(0),
 \qquad q^p_\eta=-(\Gamma^p_\eta)_V(0).
 \tag{2.12}
\]

At fixed \(\delta,T_*,\xi\), first define
\[
 \Gamma^f(\xi;\nu,\eta)
 =\mathcal S_\delta
 \Gamma^p(\xi;1+\delta^2\nu,\eta).
\tag{2.12a}
\]
For a physical history \(\phi=(\phi_V,\phi_W)\), the history rescaling and

\[
 (\mathcal S_\delta\phi)(\sigma)
 =\left(
 \frac{\phi_V(\sigma/\delta)-1}{\delta},
 \frac{2/3-\phi_W(\sigma/\delta)}{\delta^2}
 \right),
 \qquad

 \tag{2.12b}
\]
The physical/fold conversions are then

\[
 \Gamma^f_\nu=\delta^2D\mathcal S_\delta\Gamma^p_a,
 \quad

 \quad
 q^f_\nu=\delta q^p_a,
 \quad

 \tag{2.13}

This phase is **not** asserted to be global on the canonical repelling trace.

\tag{2.14}

## 3. The \(W^{1,p}\) function-space correction

Fix \(1<p<\infty\).  Let \(E_N\) be the two-component continuous
piecewise-polynomial space of degree at most six on 16 history cells.

> **Lemma 3.1 (\(C^0\) piecewise polynomials are conforming in
> \(W^{1,p}\)).**  Every element of \(E_N\) belongs to
> \(W^{1,p}([-h,0],\mathbb R^2)\).  Internal first-derivative jumps require
> no additional equations.

**Proof.**  The distributional derivative of a piecewise smooth function is
its piecewise derivative plus Dirac masses weighted by value jumps.  The
values agree at every cell join, so every Dirac coefficient is zero.  The
remaining piecewise-polynomial derivative belongs to \(L^p\). \(\square\)

Consequently,

\[
 \dim E_N=2(16\cdot6+1)=194
 \tag{3.1}
\]

is already the conforming \(W^{1,p}\) history count.  Requiring global
\(C^1\), equivalently \(W^{2,p}\) inside this finite-element space, is a
strictly stronger choice.  It adds

\[
 2(16-1)=30
 \tag{3.2}
\]

derivative seams, giving 164 coefficients; two classical endpoint
compatibility equations then give 162.  Those \(164/162\) numbers remain
correct, but they are not the natural first-order \(W^{1,p}\) count.

For a full 24-cell branch the corresponding counts are

\[
 2(24\cdot6+1)=290
 \quad(W^{1,p}),
 \qquad
 290-2(24-1)=244
 \quad(C^1/W^{2,p}).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

### 3.1 Compatibility is not a \(W^{1,p}\) boundary row

The classical solution-manifold condition is

\[
 C(\phi)=\phi'(0)-F(\phi)=0.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The derivative trace \(\phi\mapsto\phi'(0)\) is not continuous on
\(W^{1,p}\), so (3.4) does not define a closed codimension-two submanifold in
that topology.  The obstruction is quantitative.  For
\(0<\ell<\min\{4,5,\Theta_*\}\), define

\[
 b_\ell(\theta)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\theta\le-\ell,\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle\frac{\theta(\theta+\ell)^2}{\ell^2},
     &-\ell\le\theta\le0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
 b_\ell(-\ell)=b_\ell'(-\ell)=b_\ell(0)=0,
 \qquad b_\ell'(0)=1,
 \tag{3.6}
\]

while

\[
 \|b_\ell\|_{L^2}^2=\frac{\ell^3}{105},
 \qquad
 \|b_\ell'\|_{L^2}^2=\frac{2\ell}{15}.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

Thus \(\|b_\ell\|_{W^{1,2}}\to0\), although its endpoint derivative is one.
The analogous \(W^{1,p}\) norm is \(O(\ell^{1/p})\).  Its support misses all
active positive delays and its current value is zero.  Applied separately in
the two components, it corrects any smooth history's compatibility defect at
arbitrarily small \(W^{1,p}\) cost.  After first approximating an arbitrary
\(W^{1,p}\) history by a smooth one and choosing \(\ell\) sufficiently small,
one obtains:

> **Proposition 3.2.**  For the present finite fixed-delay polynomial field,
> classical compatible histories are dense in \(W^{1,p}\).  Hence the
> fixed-mesh algebraic reduction \(194\to192\) is not a conforming
> discretization of a continuous codimension-two \(W^{1,p}\) solution
> manifold.

The 194-to-192 rank theorem remains a correct statement about endpoint
derivatives in one fixed finite polynomial space.  It cannot be used as the
continuous Fredholm history count, and the norm of that discrete endpoint
derivative row, measured against the \(W^{1,p}\) norm along a family whose
terminal-cell width \(\ell\to0\), satisfies
\(\|DC_N\|\gtrsim\ell^{-1/p}\).

The proper construction is intrinsic: a repelling endpoint may be a sliding
window of a selected complete orbit, while an attracting endpoint bundle may
come from an exact forward-flow/stable-fibre embedding.  Both constructions
build compatibility into their images.  No derivative-trace row is added to
the \(W^{1,p}\) operator.

### 3.2 Why \(W^{2,p}\to L^p\) is the wrong principal scale

On a bounded interval,

\[
 W^{2,p}\xrightarrow{d/dt}W^{1,p}
 \hookrightarrow L^p
 \tag{3.8}
\]

has a compact final embedding.  Fixed-delay terms have the same or stronger
compactness, and fixed history restriction satisfies

\[
 W^{2,p}(I^h)\longrightarrow W^{1,p}([-h,0])
 \tag{3.9}
\]

compactly.  Therefore every branch-only column of the old
\(W^{2,p}\to L^p\times H\) linearization is compact.  If the full operator
were Fredholm, its standard estimate modulo a finite-dimensional kernel
would make this compact restriction bounded below on an infinite-dimensional
complement, which is impossible.

This is an operator-level no-go, not just an inconvenient count.  A strong
alternative would have to use \(W^{2,p}\to W^{1,p}\) and rebuild all traces,
compatibility conditions, and discretization counts.  It is unnecessary for
fixed \(a,\eta,T_*,L_\pm\).  Moving a delay or a flight endpoint, or taking
higher derivatives of translations, does require a stronger/Banach-scale
contract.

## 4. The corrected fixed-parameter BVP contract

Let

\[
 H=W^{1,p}([-h,0],\mathbb R^2),
 \tag{4.1}
\]

and take both branch orbits in \(W^{1,p}\) on their history-extended
intervals.  The two flow residuals belong to \(L^p\), while entry, seam, and
exit history residuals belong to \(H\).  On a bounded interval, for finitely
many fixed retarded delays
\(0=\tau_0<\tau_1<\cdots\le h\) and
\(A_j\in L^\infty\), method of steps and Grönwall estimates give the bounded
isomorphism

\[
 u\longmapsto
 \left(u'-\sum_jA_j(\cdot)u(\cdot-\tau_j),
 u|_{[-h,0]}\right)
 \tag{4.2}
\]

from \(W^{1,p}([-h,L])\) to
\(L^p([0,L])\times H\).  Fixed translations and the present polynomial
Nemytskii map are smooth on this scale.

An exact zero automatically regularizes on a flight: for the fixed-shift
polynomial field, \(u'=F(u_t)\in W^{1,p}\), hence
\(u\in W^{2,p}\subset C^1\) there.  Further bootstrap depends on the
older-history regularity and elapsed flight time.  If two complete histories
agree at the seam, both one-sided derivatives equal the same \(F(u_0)\).
Classical seam regularity is a posteriori, not an additional trial-space
condition.

## 5. The exact abstract index-\(-1\) reduction

After eliminating the two IVP isomorphism blocks in (4.2), let \(E_a,E_r\)
be the closed tangent trace ranges at the seam.  Define

\[
 D:E_a\times E_r\longrightarrow H,
 \qquad D(v,w)=v-w.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
 \ker D\cong E_a\cap E_r,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{coker}D\cong H/(E_a+E_r).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

> **Theorem 5.1 (one-gap trace-pair reduction).**  Suppose
> the endpoint-chart tangent maps are split topological embeddings,
> \(E_a,E_r\) are closed, and \(E_a+E_r\) is closed,
>
> \[
> \dim(E_a\cap E_r)=1,
> \qquad \operatorname{codim}(E_a+E_r)=1,
> \tag{5.3}
> \]
>
> and the intersection is generated by the common translation tangent.
> Then \(D\) is Fredholm of index zero.  Appending one phase row transverse
> to that tangent gives an injective operator \(L\) with one-dimensional
> cokernel and
>
> \[
> \operatorname{ind}L=-1.
> \tag{5.4}
> \]
>
> If a jump-slot column \(e\) pairs nontrivially with the cokernel, then
>
> \[
> \widehat L(z,d)=Lz-de
> \tag{5.5}
> \]
>
> is an isomorphism.

This theorem is exact.  The missing result is (5.3) for the actual selected
fixed-\(\varepsilon\) traces.  Neither \(775-774=1\) nor a square artificial
candidate Jacobian proves it.

The preferred route is a canonical special-flow graph continuation, but it
must keep two cutoffs and two fields separate.  Freeze the retained half
window \(S=S_{\delta_*}\), set and freeze the wider graph target
\(\widehat S=S+B\), fix the canonical preparation datum \(\mathcal P\), and
fix the full history horizon \(H_{\rm hor}\) required by the model (including
\(\Theta_*\)); then introduce a dummy amplitude
\(\varrho:0\to\delta_*=1/\sqrt5\).  First solve the frozen graph transform
for
\[
 Z^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}
 =(Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S},
   H^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}),
 \qquad
 Z^{\rm gr}_{0,\widehat S}=(q_{0,\widehat S},0),
 \qquad H^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}\in E_{\rm net}=\{0\}
 \quad\text{in the synchronous quotient}.
\tag{7.0}
\]
The symbols \(F_S,G_S\) in that transform are cutoff perturbation data; they
are not a globally prepared planar trace field.  On the retained uncut flow
hull, where every cutoff is inactive,
\(Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}\) satisfies

\[
 Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S,Y}(X,Y)=-X+\varrho\nu,
 \tag{7.1}
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S,X}(u)={}&Y-X^2
 +\varrho\left[-\frac{X^3}{3}
 +\frac15\left(\frac{x_4+x_5}{2}-X\right)\right]\\
 &+\varrho^2\eta(X^2-x_{\Theta_*}^2)
 +\frac{\varrho^3}{4}
 \left(\frac{x_4^3+x_5^3}{2}-X^3\right),
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7.2}
\]

where
\(x_\tau=\pi_X\Phi_{Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}}^{-\tau}u\).  From this graph
field construct a **separate** complete planar trace field
\(Q^{\rm pr}_{\varrho,S,\mathcal P}\): it equals
\(Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}\) on the retained flow hull, equals \(q_0\) near
the two canonical tails, and uses the fixed joining and normal extension in
\(\mathcal P\) between them.  Outside the retained hull neither the bare
polynomial formula (7.1)–(7.2) nor the graph-transform datum \(F_S\) may be
substituted for this prepared trace field.

Solve the attracting and repelling traces as ordinary planar ODEs

\[
 \dot z^a=Q^{\rm pr}_{\varrho,S,\mathcal P}(z^a),
 \qquad
 \dot z^r=Q^{\rm pr}_{\varrho,S,\mathcal P}(z^r),
 \tag{7.3}
\]

with independent canonical tail anchors and impose
\[
 X^a(0)=X^r(0)=0.
\tag{7.3a}
\]
Let the fixed-preparation scalar section gap be
\[
 D_{S,\mathcal P}(\varrho,\nu,\eta)=Y^a(0)-Y^r(0).
\tag{7.3b}
\]
At \(\varrho=0\), the graph and zero-order prepared traces are insensitive
to \(\nu,\eta\), so \(D_{S,\mathcal P}(0,\nu,\eta)=0\).  The regularized gap
has the fixed-window extension
\[
 \widetilde D_{S,\mathcal P}=\frac{D_{S,\mathcal P}}{\varrho},
 \qquad
 \widetilde D_{S,\mathcal P}(0,\nu,0)
 =\partial_\varrho D_{S,\mathcal P}(0,\nu,0)
 =A_{S,\mathcal P}\nu+B_{S,\mathcal P}.
\tag{7.3c}
\]
The coefficients in (7.3c) must be obtained from the actual finite,
prepared, linearized one-sided BVP.  The starting certificate requires an
interval enclosure \(0\notin A_{S,\mathcal P}\) and the seed
\[
 \nu_{0,S,\mathcal P}=-B_{S,\mathcal P}/A_{S,\mathcal P}.
\tag{7.3d}
\]
The small-\(\delta\) theorem proves only the moving-window limits
\[
 A_{S_\delta,\mathcal P}\to\sqrt{2\pi},
 \qquad
 B_{S_\delta,\mathcal P}\to\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}8,
 \qquad
 \nu_{0,S_\delta,\mathcal P}\to-\frac18
 \quad(\delta\downarrow0);
\tag{7.3e}
\]
it does **not** make \(\sqrt{2\pi}(\nu+1/8)\) the exact row at a frozen
finite \(S\), especially not at \(\delta_*=1/\sqrt5\).  An alternative is
to validate a root at one small positive \(\varrho_0\) for this same frozen
\((S,\mathcal P)\) and continue from there.  For nonzero \(\eta\), the
corresponding prepared limiting row must likewise be derived rather than
imported from the infinite-window pairing.

On retained central trace pieces, complete histories are generated by the
special-flow graph embedding, not by the prepared planar tails.  Namely,
define

\[
 \iota^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}(u)(\theta)
 =\Phi_{Q^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}}^\theta u,
 \qquad -H_{\rm hor}\le\theta\le0,
 \tag{7.4}
\]
and set
\[
 \Gamma^\sigma(s)=\iota^{\rm gr}_{\varrho,\widehat S}(z^\sigma(s)),
 \qquad \sigma\in\{a,r\},
\tag{7.4a}
\]
Thus the exact synchronous two-dimensional quotient solves only the
nontrivial \(Q^{\rm gr}\) component.  In an unreduced network graph one must
append a nonzero \(H^{\rm gr}\)-component and enlarge the ambient history
space; that is a separate full-network lift, not part of the present
\(W^{1,p}([-h,0],\mathbb R^2)\) trace contract.

At a zero of the regularized gap, (7.3a)–(7.3b), together with verified
transverse and locally unique \(X=0\) crossings of both anchored traces, give
equality of the current graph states.
The same injective complete-history embedding in (7.4) then gives equality
of the entire histories and selects the old interval \([-H_{\rm hor},-5)\)
coherently.  Current-state equality alone would not give this conclusion for
arbitrary RFDE histories; the common injective graph is essential.

The first numerical gate must separately validate: the graph-transform
residual and inverse; the prepared trace-BVP inverse and unique transverse
\(X=0\) hits; (7.3c)–(7.3d), or a same-preparation positive root seed; the
regularized-gap border; every depth-two delayed backtrack and the full
\([-H_{\rm hor},0]\) uncut physical hull; and equality under the same graph
embedding.
Continuation should check
\(\nu(\varrho)-\nu_{0,S,\mathcal P}=O(\varrho)\), not
\(\nu+1/8=O(\varrho)\) unless the finite-window truncation error is enclosed
separately.  The small-parameter graph theorem alone does not cover
\(\delta_*=1/\sqrt5\), so the graph and trace inverses require validation
throughout the continuation.  Only after reaching \(\delta_*\) should the
\(a,\eta\) jets be assembled.  Before the Lin–Fredholm operator, one must
also construct the attracting stable-fibre endpoint bundle (or an equivalent
defining submersion) and its closed tangent trace range.  The one-dimensional
attracting base orbit alone cannot serve as \(E_a\): paired with a
one-dimensional repelling trace in the infinite-dimensional history space,
it would leave infinite codimension rather than the hypothesis in (5.3).
The candidate's positive \(Y\)-phase diagnostic applies only to the terminal
right segment; global continuation requires phase patches, time, or another
transverse functional.

If this continuation fails, an independent outer Lyapunov–Perron BVP is the
fallback.  It must still impose a complete tail/outer selection; a scalar
current-state equation such as \(G=0\) cannot replace it.
