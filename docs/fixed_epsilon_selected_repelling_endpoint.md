## 1. The exact RFDE and the object that is missing

At \(\varepsilon=1/5\), put \(\delta=1/\sqrt5\), use fold time \(s=\delta t\),
and write

\[
 a=1+\delta^2\nu,
 \qquad \Theta_*=\delta T_*.
\]

On the synchronous leaf the exact quadratic RFDE is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X'={}&Y-X^2
 +\delta\left[-\frac{X^3}{3}
 +\frac15\left(\frac{X_4+X_5}{2}-X\right)\right]\\
 &+\delta^2\eta(X^2-X_{\Theta_*}^2)
 +\frac{\delta^3}{4}
 \left(\frac{X_4^3+X_5^3}{2}-X^3\right),\\
 Y'={}&-X+\delta\nu .
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The physical phase space and its fold-time pullback are, respectively,

\[
 \mathcal X_{\rm phys}=C([-T_*,0],\mathbb R^2),
 \qquad
 \mathcal X_{\rm fold}=C([-\Theta_*,0],\mathbb R^2).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The corresponding strong compatible histories are

\[
 \mathcal M^{1,{\rm phys}}_{a,\eta}
 =\{\phi\in C^1([-T_*,0],\mathbb R^2):
   \phi'(0)=F_{\rm phys}(\phi;a,\eta)\},
\tag{1.3a}
\]

and, under \(s=\delta t\),

\[
 \mathcal M^{1,{\rm fold}}_{\nu,\eta}
 =\{\Phi\in C^1([-\Theta_*,0],\mathbb R^2):
   \Phi'(0)=F_{\rm fold}(\Phi;\nu,\eta)\}.
\tag{1.3b}
\]

They are related by the affine state-history pullback

\[
 (\mathcal S_\delta\phi)(\sigma)
 =\left(
 \frac{\phi_V(\sigma/\delta)-1}{\delta},
 \frac{2/3-\phi_W(\sigma/\delta)}{\delta^2}
 \right),
 \qquad -\Theta_*\le\sigma\le0.
\tag{1.3c}
\]

Thus these manifolds represent the same physical histories, but not with
identical state components or history arguments.  Equation (1.1) and the
counterexamples below use the fold representation.

The two-sided candidate imposes at its right endpoint only

\[
 G(X,Y)=\frac{X^2}{2}-\frac Y2-\frac14=0.
\tag{1.4}
\]

This is one equation on current state.  What the Lin BVP instead needs is a
map

\[
 \Gamma^r:I_\xi\times P\longrightarrow
 \mathcal M^{1,{\rm phys}}_{a,\eta},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}\partial_\xi\Gamma^r=1,
\tag{1.5}
\]

whose image consists of histories on one fixed backward-extendible selected
repelling family.  Equation (1.4) is not a definition of (1.5).

## 2. Exact compatible-history counterexamples

The failure of (1.4) is not merely dimensional.  It can be tested directly
in the exact RFDE.

Work on the scaled horizon \([-\Theta_*,0]\), where \(\Theta_*>5\), and define

\[
 B(s)=\frac{s(s+4)(s+5)(s+\Theta_*)}{20\Theta_*}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus \(B\) vanishes at \(0,-4,-5,-\Theta_*\) and \(B'(0)=1\).  Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X_0(s)&=1+\left(-\frac12-\frac\delta3\right)B(s),\\
 Y_0(s)&=\frac12+(-1+\delta\nu)B(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

All active delayed voltage samples and the current voltage equal \(1\), while
the current recovery equals \(1/2\).  Direct substitution into (1.1) gives

\[
 (X_0',Y_0')(0)=F((X_0,Y_0);\nu,\eta).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Hence \((X_0,Y_0)\in\mathcal M^{1,{\rm fold}}_{\nu,\eta}\) for every
\((\nu,\eta)\), equivalently for every \((a,\eta)\) at fixed
\(\varepsilon\), and

\[
 G(X_0(0),Y_0(0))=G(1,1/2)=0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

### 2.1 The \(T_*\)-atom witness for \(\eta\ne0\)

Let

\[
 L_T(s)=
 \frac{s(s+4)(s+5)}{(-\Theta_*)(4-\Theta_*)(5-\Theta_*)}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

It equals \(1\) at \(-\Theta_*\) and vanishes at \(0,-4,-5\).  For a real
amplitude \(r\), set

\[
 D_T=-\delta^2\eta\{(1+r)^2-1\},
\tag{2.6}
\]

and use the Hermite-corrected perturbation

\[
 q_T(s)=rL_T(s)+\{D_T-rL_T'(0)\}B(s).
\tag{2.7}
\]

Then

\[
 q_T(0)=q_T(-4)=q_T(-5)=0,
 \quad q_T(-\Theta_*)=r,
 \quad q_T'(0)=D_T.
\tag{2.8}
\]

The two histories \((X_0,Y_0)\) and \((X_0+q_T,Y_0)\) therefore have the same
current state, the same value \(G=0\), and the same delay-\(4\) and delay-\(5\)
samples.  Their fast vector fields differ by exactly

\[
 \Delta_TF_X
 =-\delta^2\eta\{(1+r)^2-1\}=q_T'(0).
\tag{2.9}
\]

The last equality proves that the perturbed history also belongs to the
solution manifold.  With \(r=1\),

\[
 \Delta_TF_X=-\frac{3\eta}{5}\ne0
 \qquad(\eta\ne0).
\tag{2.10}
\]

More generally, (2.9) equals
\(-\eta r(r+2)/5\), so nonuniqueness occurs for arbitrarily small
\(r\ne0\), not only for the displayed unit witness.

### 2.2 A baseline-delay witness at \(\eta=0\)

The failure is not caused only by the added period-lock channel.  Define

\[
 L_4(s)=\frac{s(s+5)(s+\Theta_*)}{(-4)(\Theta_*-4)},
\tag{2.11}
\]

so that \(L_4(-4)=1\) and it vanishes at \(0,-5,-\Theta_*\).  Put

\[
 D_4=\frac{\delta r}{10}
 +\frac{\delta^3}{8}\{(1+r)^3-1\},
\tag{2.12}
\]

and

\[
 q_4(s)=rL_4(s)+\{D_4-rL_4'(0)\}B(s).
\tag{2.13}
\]

Again both histories are compatible.  They have the same current state,
the same scalar exit value, and the same delay-\(5\) and period-delay samples,
but their fast derivatives differ by \(D_4\).  For \(r=1\),

\[
 \Delta_4F_X=\frac{11\sqrt5}{200}>0,
\tag{2.14}
\]

including at \(\eta=0\).
In fact \(D_4=\sqrt5\,r(r^2+3r+7)/200\); the quadratic factor is strictly
positive, so this witness is also arbitrarily small in history norm.

> **Proposition 2.1 (scalar exit is not an RFDE endpoint chart).**  In the
> exact quadratic RFDE there are pairs of \(C^1\)-compatible histories with
> the same current state and the same value \(G=0\), but with different
> forward derivatives.  This holds through the \(T_*\)-atom for every
> \(\eta\ne0\), and through a baseline delay atom at \(\eta=0\).  Consequently
> current-state equality and \(G=0\) determine neither the complete history
> nor the ensuing right flight.

The proposition does **not** assert that either explicit polynomial history
is on the selected repelling family.  Its role is logical: it disproves the
implication from the presently imposed endpoint data to a selected RFDE
history.

## 3. The minimum one-dimensional backward-extendible chart

From this section onward, \(\Gamma^r\), \(F\), and the history coordinate
\(\theta\in[-T_*,0]\) are written in physical time.  Applying
\(\mathcal S_\delta\) gives the equivalent fold-time chart on
\([-\Theta_*,0]\).  If \(\dot\xi=q_{\rm phys}\) uses physical time, its fold
internal field is

\[
 q_{\rm fold}=\frac{q_{\rm phys}}{\delta},
\tag{3.0}
\]

so the invariance equations retain their form only after this rescaling.

A clean way to construct a genuinely backward-extendible curve without
solving an ill-posed ambient backward RFDE initial-value problem is an
invariance equation.  Seek the complete-history chart (1.5) together with a
scalar internal field

\[
 \dot\xi=q^r(\xi;a,\eta).
\tag{3.1}
\]

They must satisfy, for \(-T_*<\theta<0\),

\[
 q^r\partial_\xi\Gamma^r(\theta)
 =\partial_\theta\Gamma^r(\theta),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and at current time

\[
 q^r\partial_\xi\Gamma^r(0)
 =F(\Gamma^r;a,\eta).
\tag{3.3}
\]

The equations must be supplemented by one fixed nonlinear
repelling-tail/outer-history selection condition.  Without it, (3.2)--(3.3)
merely describe an invariant curve and do not choose the canard threshold.

Choose a parameter-independent bounded history functional \(\chi\), transverse
to the curve, and impose

\[
 \chi(\Gamma^r(\xi;a,\eta))=\xi,
 \qquad
 \chi(\partial_\xi\Gamma^r)=1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Together with one selected anchor, (3.4) removes curve reparameterization
and autonomous translation.  It is a chart gauge.  The two-sided Lin BVP's
matching phase, such as \(V^-(0)=1\) (equivalently fold \(X^-(0)=0\)),
remains a separate equation and must not be counted a second time.

> **Lemma 3.1 (invariant chart gives an exact backward extension).**  Suppose
> \((\Gamma^r,q^r)\) satisfies (3.2)--(3.3), its image lies in
> \(\mathcal M^{1,{\rm phys}}_{a,\eta}\), and the scalar equation (3.1) has a
> backward flow
> that remains in \(I_\xi\).  Then
> \(z_t=\Gamma^r(\xi(t);a,\eta)\) is an exact backward-extendible solution
> history of the RFDE.

**Proof.**  Along (3.1), (3.2) gives

\[
 \partial_t\Gamma^r(\xi(t))(\theta)
 =\partial_\theta\Gamma^r(\xi(t))(\theta).
\]

The characteristic identity is therefore

\[
 \Gamma^r(\xi(t))(\theta)
 =\Gamma^r(\xi(t+\theta))(0).
\]

With \(z(t)=\Gamma^r(\xi(t))(0)\), equation (3.3) becomes
\(z'(t)=F(z_t;a,\eta)\).  Backward extension is supplied by the scalar flow,
not by inversion of the ambient RFDE semiflow. \(\square\)

Lemma 3.1 is an exact implication.  The repository does not yet contain a
fixed-\(\varepsilon\) solution of (3.2)--(3.4).

## 4. The \(a\)- and \(\eta\)-columns and the phase gauge

In physical variables the two direct vector-field columns are

\[
 F_a=\binom{0}{-1/5},
 \qquad
 F_\eta=\binom{
 \frac15\{(V(0)-1)^2-(V(-T_*)-1)^2\}}{0}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

In fold variables,

\[
 F_\nu=\binom0\delta,
 \qquad
 F_\eta=\binom{\delta^2\{X(0)^2-X(-\Theta_*)^2\}}0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Here \(T_*\) is the frozen delay of the plant when differentiating in
\(a\) or \(\eta\).  If \(T_*\) were varied as a third parameter, (4.2) would
acquire a translated-history derivative and the BVP would need a separate
strong-space delay column.  No such delay motion is hidden in the formulas
below.

Since \(a=1+\delta^2\nu=1+\nu/5\), derivatives within one fixed coordinate
representation satisfy

\[
 \Gamma_a^r=5\Gamma_\nu^r
\tag{4.3}
\]

at fixed \(\varepsilon\).  This conversion applies only after the same chart
gauge and selected anchor are differentiated.  When physical and fold
history components are compared, the derivative of
\(\mathcal S_\delta\) (or its inverse) must also be applied; the component
vectors themselves are not identified.

For \(\lambda\in\{a,\eta\}\), differentiating (3.2)--(3.3) gives the complete
history sensitivity equations

\[
 q^r\Gamma^r_{\xi\lambda}
 +q^r_\lambda\Gamma^r_\xi
 =\partial_\theta\Gamma^r_\lambda,
\tag{4.4}
\]

\[
 q^r\Gamma^r_{\xi\lambda}(0)
 +q^r_\lambda\Gamma^r_\xi(0)
 =D_\phi F(\Gamma^r)\Gamma^r_\lambda
 +F_\lambda(\Gamma^r).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The fixed-\(\xi\) gauge is

\[
 \chi(\Gamma^r_\lambda)=0,
\tag{4.6}
\]

together with the differentiated selected-anchor equation.  In particular,
\(\Gamma^r_\eta\) is a function on the entire \(T_*\) horizon.  Its source in
(4.1) reads the older value at \(-T_*\), even when the state linearization of
that channel vanishes at \(\eta=0\).  A current-state tangent or a derivative
of the scalar \(G\) cannot replace (4.4)--(4.6).

## 5. Composition with the right flight

For a right orbit piece \(x^+\), define complete-history endpoint evaluation

\[
 (\operatorname{End}_{L_+}x^+)(\theta)
 =x^+(L_++\theta),
 \qquad -T_*\le\theta\le0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The faithful terminal residual is

\[
 \mathcal R_+(x^+,L_+,\xi_+,a,\eta)
 =\operatorname{End}_{L_+}x^+
 -\Gamma^r(\xi_+;a,\eta)
 \in\mathcal X_{\rm phys}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For fixed \(L_+\), its first variation is

\[
 D\mathcal R_+
 =\operatorname{End}_{L_+}u
 -\Gamma^r_\xi\dot\xi_+
 -\Gamma^r_a\dot a
 -\Gamma^r_\eta\dot\eta.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If \(L_+\) moves, strong history regularity gives, pointwise in \(\theta\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D\mathcal R_+(\theta)={}&u(L_++\theta)
 +\dot x^+(L_++\theta)\dot L_+\\
 &-\Gamma^r_\xi(\theta)\dot\xi_+
 -\Gamma^r_a(\theta)\dot a
 -\Gamma^r_\eta(\theta)\dot\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

A scalar section can determine \(L_+\) only after its crossing derivative is
enclosed away from zero.  Even then it determines a hitting time; it does not
replace the full-history equality (5.2).

## 6. Compatibility bookkeeping in the \(194\)-coefficient ledger

The revised candidate records \(194\) value-continuous two-state history
coefficients.  The two endpoint equations
\(\phi'(0)=F(\phi)\) have exact rank \(2\), so its discrete
endpoint-compatible level has dimension

\[
 194-2=192.
\tag{6.1}
\]

That is still not the strong solution-manifold dimension.  The multicell
space has \(30\) internal derivative-continuity seams.  Imposing them gives

\[
 194-30=164
 \quad\hbox{globally \(C^1\) coefficients},
 \qquad
 164-2=162
 \quad\hbox{globally \(C^1\), endpoint-compatible coefficients}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The required repelling curve has dimension \(1\).  Its codimensions are
therefore \(193\) in the raw value-continuous space, \(191\) on the discrete
endpoint-compatible level, and \(161\) in the global-\(C^1\) compatible
level.  Equivalently,

\[
 193=30+2+161.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The endpoint bump \(B\) changes the current derivative without changing the
current value, so the compatibility derivative is surjective independently
of current evaluation.  At the explicit witness,
\(DG=(1,-1/2)\ne0\).  The equation \(G=0\) leaves a
\(191\)-dimensional fiber on the discrete endpoint-compatible level and a
\(161\)-dimensional fiber on the strong compatible level.  Those exceed the
selected curve dimension by \(190\) and \(160\), respectively.  Thus “one
scalar exit equation” and “one-dimensional exit chart” remain opposite
dimension statements after either compatibility convention.

The candidate's repaired ambient arithmetic retains \(193\) incoming
coordinates, imposes two compatibility rows on their image, replaces each
raw history equality by a \(192\)-row projected equality, and adds six
endpoint compatibility rows.  That arithmetic again yields a
\(775\)-by-\(774\) phase-fixed ledger.  It is not yet an assembled strong
endpoint operator: each represented 16-cell history block has \(30\)
internal derivative-continuity seams, while a raw 24-cell branch also has
the remaining flight-cell and history/flight-interface derivative joins.
All of them, or a corresponding \(W^{2,p}\) realization, require a fresh
strong-operator count.  An intrinsic strong-coordinate ledger has a
different matrix size but must have the same Fredholm index.

Accordingly, this note accepts the repaired arithmetic identity but does not
promote \(193+1\), \(775\times774\), or the bordered
\(775\times775\) matrix to a validated compatible Fredholm operator.

## 7. Minimum fixed-\(\varepsilon\) continuation problem

The smallest defensible new computation is a validated collocation of
(3.2)--(3.4), not another scalar shooting gap.  Its unknowns must include:

1. piecewise-Chebyshev coefficients of
   \(\Gamma^r(\xi,\theta)\) on
   \(I_\xi\times[-T_*,0]\);
2. the scalar internal field \(q^r(\xi)\), one selected repelling anchor, and
   the chart gauge;
3. the complete-history jets \(\Gamma^r_a,\Gamma^r_\eta\) and the associated
   \(q^r_a,q^r_\eta\);
4. an interval enclosure of \(T_*\) and a mesh closed under the three delays
   \(4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5,T_*\);
5. the right-flight trace and either fixed \(L_+\) or one validated
   transversal hitting-time variable.

A proof must then establish:

- a radii-polynomial or interval-Newton inverse with coefficient tails for
  the nonlinear chart, phase, and selected-anchor block;
- chart rank, injectivity, compatibility, and retention of the backward
  scalar flow in the validated chart;
- a parameter-coherent selected anchor, rather than an arbitrary invariant
  curve;
- interval bounds for the full \(a\)- and \(\eta\)-history columns;
- the complete-history composition (5.2)--(5.4);
- corrected compatible endpoint/Fredholm bookkeeping; and only after these
  gates,
- the two-sided one-cokernel BVP, its dynamic adjoint, the selected root, and
  the response \(\partial_\eta a_c\).

Direct validation at \(\delta=1/\sqrt5\) is sufficient.  A second admissible
route is a validated continuation of one already selected small-\(\delta\)
history chart up to \(\delta=1/\sqrt5\), but a singular scalar exit trace by
itself is not an initial chart for that continuation.
