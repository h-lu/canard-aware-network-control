## 2. Dimension-uniform selected-root response

Let \(P_N\) be a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix with strictly positive
stationary probability vector \(\pi_N\), and suppose

\[
 \tau(P_N)\le 1-\gamma
\tag{2.1}
\]

for one \(\gamma>0\) independent of \(N\). On
\(E_N=\ker\pi_N^T\), put

\[
 A_N=D(P_N-I)|_{E_N}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The oscillation norm gives the uniform estimates

\[
 \|e^{A_Nt}\|\le e^{-D\gamma t},
 \qquad
 \|A_N^{-1}\|\le(D\gamma)^{-1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For the shared-resource RFDE and the fixed-support, projection-neutral delay
direction stated in
[the heterogeneous-curvature theorem](paper-ii-heterogeneous-curvature-selected-root.md),
the invariant complete-history graph and its phase-normal one-sided traces
have a unique canonical root. Uniformly over the admitted network family,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mu_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)-\mu_{c,N}(\delta,0)
 ={}&\mathscr C_N\delta^3\zeta
 +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),\\
 \mathscr C_N={}&-
 \frac{K}{2\alpha^2}
 \pi_N^T\operatorname{diag}(c_N)
 A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}\dot M_{1,N}\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

One normalized family without a nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal satisfies

\[
 \inf_{N\ge2}|\mathscr C_N|>0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The coefficient in (2.4) has a clear mechanism. A structural delay direction
is invisible to the critical projection, forces the stable network fiber,
passes through the network Poisson inverse \(A_N^{-1}\), and returns to the
critical equation through heterogeneous fold curvature. The root can
therefore move even when the projected delay measure is unchanged.

This result is preparation indexed. It is not a theorem for arbitrary sparse
graphs, a closing Dobrushin gap, moving delay atoms, or an unspecified
physical outer Fenichel selection. The more abstract transfer statement and
its conditional full-Lin alternative are recorded in
[the one-gap transfer theorem](general-network-one-gap-root-transfer.md).

### 2.1 A one-RFDE policy transduction

The selected-root response can be turned into an exact controlled latency
response without changing the shared-resource phase space.  Choose a reset
depth

\[
 \rho_N(\delta,\zeta)
 =\rho_*+\lambda\{
 \mu_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)-\mu_{c,N}(\delta,0)\},
 \qquad \lambda\ne0,
\tag{2.6}
\]

prepare its constant complete history by bounded exact-model feedback, and
use the same scalar recovery coordinate during the event stage.  The exact
first-hit latency then satisfies

\[
 T_{\rm det,N}(\delta,\zeta)-T_{\rm det,N}(\delta,0)
 =-\frac{\lambda\mathscr C_N}
 {\rho_*^2(c_{\max,N}-\beta\rho_*/3)}\,\delta^3\zeta
 +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),
\tag{2.7}
\]

with a common remainder and a coefficient bounded away from zero in the
explicit all-size family.  This is the proved
[controller-mediated transduction](same-model-shared-resource-root-detector-bridge.md).
It presupposes offline knowledge of the exact roots and model.  Adding an
arbitrary policy offset \(\eta\delta^3\zeta\) to (2.6) changes the leading
latency coefficient without changing the uncontrolled root.  Thus (2.7)
cannot identify an input-independent physical onset.

## 3. Bounded complete-history preparation on balanced networks

Consider the delayed FHN network

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&f(v)-w+D(P-I)v
 +\varepsilon\left\{
 B_0G(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1G(v(t-\tau_1))-G(v)
 \right\},\\
 \dot w={}&\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+E(P-I)w,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
 f(s)=s-\frac{s^3}{3},
 \qquad
 G(s)=\kappa_1s+\kappa_3(s-1)^3,
\tag{3.2}
\]


\[
 \varepsilon=\frac15,
 \quad a=\frac35,
 \quad D=3,
 \quad E=2,
 \quad
 (\tau_0,\tau_1)=(4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Assume

\[
\begin{gathered}
 P\ge0,
 \qquad P\mathbf1=\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^TP=\pi^T,
 \qquad \pi_i>0,
 \qquad \pi^T\mathbf1=1,\\
 B_j\ge0,
 \qquad B_j\mathbf1=\frac12\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^TB_j=\frac12\pi^T,
 \qquad j=0,1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3.4}
\]

No irreducibility, Dobrushin contraction, rank-one representation, or
symmetry is imposed in this section.

Let the initial data lie in the bounded cylinder

\[
 \|\phi_v\|_\infty\le V_0,
 \qquad \|w(0)\|_\infty\le W_0,
 \qquad |r|\le R.
\tag{3.5}
\]

This cylinder is not claimed to be compact in the RFDE phase space. During
preparation apply

\[
\begin{aligned}
 u^v&=-F_v(v_t,w)-K_v\sigma_{1/2}(v-r\mathbf1),\\
 u^w&=-F_w(v,w)-K_w\sigma_{1/2}(w),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{1/2}(z)=\operatorname{sgn}(z)\sqrt{|z|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Here \(F_v,F_w\) are exactly the two uncontrolled right-hand sides in
(3.1). Consequently, with \(e=v-r\mathbf1\),

\[
 \dot e_i=-K_v\sigma_{1/2}(e_i),
 \qquad
 \dot w_i=-K_w\sigma_{1/2}(w_i).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Although the square-root field is not locally Lipschitz at zero, its
monotonicity gives a one-sided Lipschitz inequality and hence forward
uniqueness. Every coordinate reaches zero in finite time and remains there.
Keeping (3.6) active for one maximum-delay window after the predetermined
settling deadline creates the exact complete history

\[
 \Phi_r(\theta)=(r\mathbf1,0),
 \qquad -5\sqrt5\le\theta\le0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The general balanced topology changes none of the authority estimates,
because

\[
 \|P-I\|_\infty\le2,
 \qquad
 \|B_0\|_\infty+\|B_1\|_\infty=1.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For \(V_0=W_0=2\), \(R=3/4\), and \(K_v=K_w=1\), the directed bounds are

\[
 \|u^v\|_\infty<23.19,
 \qquad
 \|u^w\|_\infty<9.94,
 \qquad
 T_{\rm prep}<14.50.
\tag{3.10}
\]

They are per-node sup-norm bounds and are independent of \(N\). They require
exact knowledge of the current state, both delayed voltage layers, the model,
and the network matrices.

## 4. Bounded decision control and nonsynchronous first hits

At the release time, turn off \(u^v\) and apply only

\[
 u^w=-\{\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+E(P-I)w\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since preparation ends at \(w=0\), equation (4.1) makes \(w=0\) exactly
invariant. Until the voltage reaches a tube face \(\lVert v\rVert_\infty=H\),

\[
 \|u^w\|_\infty\le\varepsilon(H+a).
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, the decision input is at most \(0.42\) on
\(\lvert v_i\rvert\le3/2\). This realizes the nodewise recovery clamp used in
[the balanced-network sign-cone theorem](paper-iv-general-network-sign-cone.md)
by a bounded additive input for the exact model.

Let \(x=\pi^Tv\). Nonnegativity and the row-mass identities preserve both
complete-history orthants. Balance removes the topology from the mean
equation. Before a node reaches \(H\),

\[
 \dot x\ge c_+(H)x
 \quad\hbox{in the positive orthant},
 \qquad
 \dot x\le c_-(H)x
 \quad\hbox{in the negative orthant},
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
 c_+(H)=1-\frac{H^2}{3}
 -\varepsilon(\kappa_1+3\kappa_3),
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
 c_-(H)=1-\frac{H^2}{3}
 -\varepsilon\{\kappa_1+
 \kappa_3(H^2+3H+3)\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus, provided the target face has not already been met, whenever
\(x_0\ne0\), the initial history has its sign, and \(c_\pm(H)>0\),

\[
 T_\pm(H)\le
 \frac{1}{c_\pm(H)}\log\frac{H}{|x_0|}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The constant histories in the two reset charts have

\[
 r\in[0.499999999999,0.500000000001]
\tag{4.7}
\]

or

\[
 r\in[-0.500000000001,-0.499999999999].
\tag{4.8}
\]

They therefore satisfy the uniform mean condition used in the directed
sign-cone record. After preparation, some node reaches \(+1\) or \(-1\) and,
under the same bounded recovery control, some node reaches \(+3/2\) or
\(-6/5\) in finite time. The detector may be externally latched. The theorem
does not say that the detector node and the first excursion node coincide,
that crossing \(\pm1\) is irreversible, or that the trajectory returns as a
biological action potential.

Using the deliberately conservative parent lower bound
\(\lvert x_0\rvert\ge0.06\), the directed deadlines measured from the start
of preparation are

| chart | detector deadline | excursion deadline |
|---|---:|---:|
| positive | \(20.39922492281538\) | \(68.14489509303967\) |
| negative | \(24.66591907584100\) | \(52.90378869866757\) |

The actual reset charts have \(\lvert x_0\rvert\) close to \(1/2\), so these
published bounds are not intended to be sharp.

## 7. Mathematical value

The selected-root theorem contributes a genuinely network-dependent
mechanism: topology re-enters a projection-neutral canard calculation through
a stable resolvent and curvature heterogeneity. Its constants remain uniform
as \(N\) grows inside a nontrivial directed mixing class.

The controlled-output theorem contributes the complementary mechanism:
under positivity and balance, topology disappears exactly from the weighted
mean needed for a finite first-hit proof, while the additive preparation and
decision inputs have per-node bounds independent of \(N\). It also converts
the earlier ideal history overwrite and ideal recovery clamp into bounded
exact-model inputs on a declared bounded initial-data cylinder and voltage
tube.

Together, these results move beyond a single carefully chosen two-module,
two-delay example in two distinct senses. The canard-root response no longer
uses a synchrony quotient, and the controlled detector/excursion theorem no
longer uses a rank-one or two-module topology. The price is explicit:
Dobrushin mixing is retained for the selected root, and exact-model nodewise
recovery control is retained for the bounded excursion.

The autonomous-handoff theorem removes exact cancellation from the final
part of both synchronized excursion channels and, through the negative
unit-face reversal, identifies a mathematically necessary handoff depth. It
does not remove feedback from onset or supply a basin theorem. The
one-RFDE policy composition is valuable mainly as a sharp boundary result:
it proves that a root response can be transduced with uniform constants, and
its policy-offset falsifier proves why this alone has no intrinsic biological
meaning.

The robust-handoff tube supplies the first open-set result in the full delayed
history phase space for this final segment.  Its mathematical gain is not a
larger endpoint: it replaces exact synchrony and exact shutdown by quantified
asynchrony, parameter error and arbitrary-sign residual forcing, uniformly in
network size and admitted balanced topology.  Its sharp boundary is equally
important: robust entrance reachability and a biological destination remain
separate theorems.

The compatibility audit supplies a second boundary result: it proves that the
earlier selected-root and periodic/control mechanisms do not inhabit one
plant, and isolates the precise two-delay synchronization obstruction.  The
quadratic period lock then supplies the positive mathematical response: one
common plant can leave the center periodic outputs and fold linearization
unchanged while moving a canonical complete-history root.  The uniform
Dobrushin graph lift closes the corresponding arbitrary-finite network
question on its declared anisotropic canonical tube.  This closes the
mechanism-design and one genuine full-network question at small \(\delta\),
not the fixed-parameter, arbitrary-history, or biological-onset questions.

The explicit eta-Floquet box closes the corresponding stability seam on one
nonzero actuator interval.  Its value is not the small numerical width by
itself, but the proof architecture: every base right-half leaf is replayed,
the active period-length delay is handled on the enlarged history space, and
the same projection identity that preserves the orbit removes the carrier
from the entire transverse block.  Thus one nonlinear channel can move the
small-\(\delta\) canonical root while preserving full-network local periodic
attraction on a rigorously nonempty interval, without claiming that the
fixed-\(\varepsilon\) root has already been validated.

The reference-slice basin audit supplies the complementary negative result.
It identifies two structurally wrong shortcuts before a costly global proof
attempt: the only validated rest state is unstable, and detector-face
no-return cannot coexist with capture by an oscillation that recrosses those
faces.  This converts a vague missing-basin caveat into a precise model-design
decision between autonomous bistability, semantic latching, and controlled
switching.
