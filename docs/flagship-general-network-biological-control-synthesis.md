# Finite-network canard-root transfer and routes to autonomous pulse-onset control

## 1. What is proved, and what is not one theorem yet

The successor program now contains eight theorem or obstruction blocks
spanning genuine finite-network classes, a fixed-amplitude causal-chart
route, and an autonomous bistable replacement for the failed biological
slice.

1. A **selected complete-history canard-root theorem** applies to a
   uniformly Dobrushin family with one shared recovery resource. It proves a
   dimension-uniform, preparation-indexed root response without using a
   synchrony quotient.
2. A **bounded controlled-excursion theorem** applies to nonnegative balanced
   delayed FHN networks. Exact-model additive feedback prepares a uniform
   complete history in finite time, a bounded recovery input maintains the
   zero-recovery decision leaf, and every same-sign prepared history with
   nonzero \(\pi\)-mean reaches a voltage detector and a larger excursion
   face whenever the corresponding growth constant is positive. The
   synchronous periodic branch supplies a topology-independent
   frequency--amplitude--operational-safety map. On the exactly prepared
   synchronous leaf, a further theorem closes all inputs at a signed handoff
   face and proves a finite autonomous continuation to the excursion face. A
   robust extension starts instead from explicit asynchronous open cylinders
   in the full RFDE history space and retains finite terminal capture under
   small state/history/parameter errors and arbitrary-sign shutdown residuals.
3. A **controller-mediated policy-composition theorem** stays inside the
   shared-resource RFDE of item 1. A reset law using the exact selected roots
   as offline data transfers their \(\delta^3\zeta\) displacement into an
   exact detector-latency displacement. A policy-offset falsifier shows why
   this constructed response is not an intrinsic onset law.
4. A **quadratic period-locked common-RFDE theorem** applies to the balanced
   dual-scaffold plant.  Its nonlinear collective delay difference vanishes
   on the distinguished periodic orbit and to first order at the fold, yet
   moves the enlarged-horizon canonical synchronous canard root by
   \(-\Theta_*\delta^3\eta/2+O(\delta^4|\eta|+\delta^3\eta^2)\) for
   sufficiently small \(\delta\).  For every finite topology in a declared
   balanced class with one common Dobrushin gap, the zero transverse history
   graph is the unique canonical retained graph and the same root law lifts
   with constants uniform in network size and topology.  At the exact center
   gain pair, a separate 32,046-leaf replay proves that the same periodic
   orbit remains locally orbitally attracting for every fixed finite
   \(\tau(Q)\le1/4\) topology on \(|\eta|\le3\times10^{-6}\).  The
   corresponding fixed-\(\varepsilon\) root derivative and physical event
   comparison remain open.  The proofs and the fixed-parameter validation
   contract are recorded
   in the [scalar theorem](quadratic-period-locked-selected-root.md), the
   [Dobrushin lift](quadratic-period-lock-dobrushin-full-network.md), and the
   [fixed-\(\varepsilon\) BVP blueprint](fixed-epsilon-quadratic-root-bvp.md).
   The nonzero-eta stability theorem is in the
   [Floquet-box note](quadratic-period-lock-eta-floquet-stability.md).
   The [two-sided discrete candidate](fixed-epsilon-two-sided-candidate.md)
   is numerical progress toward that blueprint, not its validation.  The
   [sliding-window/\(W^{1,p}\) bridge](fixed-epsilon-sliding-window-w1p-bridge.md)
   proves that a nonstationary history chart reduces locally to one orbit,
   rejects the old \(W^{2,p}\to L^p\) principal scale by compactness, and
   isolates independent trace selection as the remaining fixed-parameter
   obstruction.
5. A **same-plant controlled terminal-transfer theorem** first proves that
   the existing root, preparation/handoff and periodic-attraction modules fail
   four exact composition gates.  It then extends the controlled hold to the
   full \(T_*\) history, cancels the quadratic carrier before release, closes
   every additive input at handoff, and absorbs the autonomous carrier into
   the robust residual budget for every admitted finite balanced topology
   when \(|\eta|<250000/12972264861\).  It is policy-dependent finite
   terminal transfer, not physical onset, basin capture or permanent
   no-return.
6. A **reference-slice dual-basin no-go theorem** proves that the unique
   synchronous equilibrium has two distinct open-right-half-plane
   characteristic roots throughout the microscopic gain box for
   \(|\eta|\le10^{-3}\).  Thus the natural rest state is not a quiet
   attractor.  On \(\eta=0\), the validated attracting periodic orbit crosses
   \(+1,+3/2,-1\) every period and stays strictly above \(-6/5\), so capture
   by that orbit is incompatible with permanent residence on the
   corresponding one-sided detector regions.  The theorem does not exclude
   another quiet attractor or prove global uniqueness.
7. A **fixed-amplitude clock obstruction and prepared-chart theorem**
   replaces the failed attempt to continue the singular \(\sigma\)-clock to
   \(\rho_*=1/\sqrt5\).  At the frozen candidate value of \(\nu\), an
   explicit zero tuple of the shifted independent raw-slot transform lies
   inside the previously proposed radius-\(10^{-3}\) product tube;
   consequently no planar current-state \(C^1\) phase can have a strictly
   positive Lie derivative on that specified geometry.  This is not an RFDE
   equilibrium and does not exclude a history-dependent phase or another
   causal tube.
   Conversely, any sufficiently smooth embedded family of exact prepared
   RFDE solutions \(\Psi(t,\lambda)\) induces a local planar field
   \(Q\circ\Psi=\partial_t\Psi\) whose backward flow shifts \(t\), satisfies
   a local physical-history identity on a smaller current image after a
   compatible complete extension, and has exact
   intrinsic clock and first-integral identities
   \(\mathcal L_Qt=1\), \(\mathcal L_Q\lambda=0\).  The implication is exact;
   the present target-amplitude chart is only a binary64 candidate for its
   hypotheses.
8. An **autonomous leaky-recovery bistable candidate** keeps the two delayed
   voltage channels and changes the recovery law to
   \(w'=\varepsilon(v-a-w)\).  At the declared center, the unique quiet
   equilibrium is proved locally exponentially stable for every pair of
   positive delays by an exact small-gain/argument-principle estimate.  An
   attracting outer periodic orbit, an index-one inner periodic-cycle
   candidate,
   a finite-duration physical-pulse onset bracket, and a nonsingular
   \((a,\kappa_3)\mapsto(F,A)\) response are reproducible numerical
   candidates.  They yield a precise conditional theorem for the local
   control map \((a,\kappa_3,J)\mapsto(F,A,J-J_c)\), but the two periodic
   BVPs, Floquet indices, history-space separator and pulse transversality
   remain to be validated.

The two original principal results answer different questions in different recovery
architectures. The policy composition removes that mismatch only for a
designed controlled transduction; it does **not** prove that the selected
canard root is the detector boundary of the balanced FHN theorem. In
particular, there is not yet a same-model, input-independent theorem
identifying a preparation-indexed local canard connection with an unforced
biological pulse boundary.  The quadratic period lock removes the model-level
obstruction for a small-\(\delta\) canonical full-network root on the retained
Dobrushin graph and the center periodic orbit, but it does not supply the
fixed-\(\varepsilon\) or event-level identification.  The new prepared-chart
route and leaky-recovery slice now give concrete objects on both sides of that
gap: a plausible target-amplitude causal history graph and a plausible
autonomous pulse separator.  Neither numerical object has yet been promoted
to a theorem, and equality of their codimension-one roots is a separate
comparison problem.

The distinction is structural rather than terminological:

| feature | selected-root theorem | bounded control theorem |
|---|---|---|
| fast variables | \(N\) network voltages near one fold | \(N\) FHN voltages on the full decision tube |
| slow variables | one shared recovery resource | one recovery coordinate per node |
| topology hypothesis | common Dobrushin gap | nonnegative balance with one positive stationary weight |
| history object | canonical one-sided selected histories | causally prepared constant history \(\Phi_r\) |
| conclusion | local complete-history root displacement | controlled detector hit, finite excursion, synchronized autonomous handoff, and staged output map |
| biological interpretation | none without outer selection | voltage-threshold operational channel, not a biological basin |

Thus the correct current flagship claim is a set of scalable root and control
theorems, together with a same-plant small-\(\delta\) root mechanism and a
separate controlled terminal-transfer bridge.  The fixed-parameter
root/event/basin interface remains open, so this is not yet a completed
canard-to-action-potential identity.

The separation is now an exact result rather than a caution based on notation.
The [root/periodic compatibility audit](paper-iv-root-periodic-model-compatibility.md)
compares the two-module restrictions and proves that the fold state, critical
direction, fast and recovery scaffolds, slow unfolding, delay layers and
linear current compensator differ. It also proves that the selected-root
redistribution matrix acts nontrivially on the periodic synchronous direction.
Consequently the earlier root coefficient cannot be appended to the
periodic/control output map by relabelling parameters or equations.  The
quadratic period-locked theorem is a genuinely new common-plant mechanism,
not a reinterpretation of that incompatible coefficient.

## 2. Dimension-uniform selected-root response

Let \(P_N\) be a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix with strictly positive
stationary probability vector \(\pi_N\), and suppose

\[
 \tau(P_N)\le 1-\gamma
\tag{2.1}
\]

for one \(\gamma>0\) independent of \(N\). On
\(E_N=\ker\pi_N^T\), put

\[
 A_N=D(P_N-I)|_{E_N}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The oscillation norm gives the uniform estimates

\[
 \|e^{A_Nt}\|\le e^{-D\gamma t},
 \qquad
 \|A_N^{-1}\|\le(D\gamma)^{-1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For the shared-resource RFDE and the fixed-support, projection-neutral delay
direction stated in
[the heterogeneous-curvature theorem](paper-ii-heterogeneous-curvature-selected-root.md),
the invariant complete-history graph and its phase-normal one-sided traces
have a unique canonical root. Uniformly over the admitted network family,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mu_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)-\mu_{c,N}(\delta,0)
 ={}&\mathscr C_N\delta^3\zeta
 +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),\\
 \mathscr C_N={}&-
 \frac{K}{2\alpha^2}
 \pi_N^T\operatorname{diag}(c_N)
 A_N^{-1}P_{\perp,N}\dot M_{1,N}\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

One normalized family without a nontrivial synchrony polydiagonal satisfies

\[
 \inf_{N\ge2}|\mathscr C_N|>0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The coefficient in (2.4) has a clear mechanism. A structural delay direction
is invisible to the critical projection, forces the stable network fiber,
passes through the network Poisson inverse \(A_N^{-1}\), and returns to the
critical equation through heterogeneous fold curvature. The root can
therefore move even when the projected delay measure is unchanged.

This result is preparation indexed. It is not a theorem for arbitrary sparse
graphs, a closing Dobrushin gap, moving delay atoms, or an unspecified
physical outer Fenichel selection. The more abstract transfer statement and
its conditional full-Lin alternative are recorded in
[the one-gap transfer theorem](general-network-one-gap-root-transfer.md).

### 2.1 A one-RFDE policy transduction

The selected-root response can be turned into an exact controlled latency
response without changing the shared-resource phase space.  Choose a reset
depth

\[
 \rho_N(\delta,\zeta)
 =\rho_*+\lambda\{
 \mu_{c,N}(\delta,\zeta)-\mu_{c,N}(\delta,0)\},
 \qquad \lambda\ne0,
\tag{2.6}
\]

prepare its constant complete history by bounded exact-model feedback, and
use the same scalar recovery coordinate during the event stage.  The exact
first-hit latency then satisfies

\[
 T_{\rm det,N}(\delta,\zeta)-T_{\rm det,N}(\delta,0)
 =-\frac{\lambda\mathscr C_N}
 {\rho_*^2(c_{\max,N}-\beta\rho_*/3)}\,\delta^3\zeta
 +O(\delta^4|\zeta|+\delta^3\zeta^2),
\tag{2.7}
\]

with a common remainder and a coefficient bounded away from zero in the
explicit all-size family.  This is the proved
[controller-mediated transduction](same-model-shared-resource-root-detector-bridge.md).
It presupposes offline knowledge of the exact roots and model.  Adding an
arbitrary policy offset \(\eta\delta^3\zeta\) to (2.6) changes the leading
latency coefficient without changing the uncontrolled root.  Thus (2.7)
cannot identify an input-independent physical onset.

## 3. Bounded complete-history preparation on balanced networks

Consider the delayed FHN network

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&f(v)-w+D(P-I)v
 +\varepsilon\left\{
 B_0G(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1G(v(t-\tau_1))-G(v)
 \right\},\\
 \dot w={}&\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+E(P-I)w,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
 f(s)=s-\frac{s^3}{3},
 \qquad
 G(s)=\kappa_1s+\kappa_3(s-1)^3,
\tag{3.2}
\]


\[
 \varepsilon=\frac15,
 \quad a=\frac35,
 \quad D=3,
 \quad E=2,
 \quad
 (\tau_0,\tau_1)=(4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Assume

\[
\begin{gathered}
 P\ge0,
 \qquad P\mathbf1=\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^TP=\pi^T,
 \qquad \pi_i>0,
 \qquad \pi^T\mathbf1=1,\\
 B_j\ge0,
 \qquad B_j\mathbf1=\frac12\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^TB_j=\frac12\pi^T,
 \qquad j=0,1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3.4}
\]

No irreducibility, Dobrushin contraction, rank-one representation, or
symmetry is imposed in this section.

Let the initial data lie in the bounded cylinder

\[
 \|\phi_v\|_\infty\le V_0,
 \qquad \|w(0)\|_\infty\le W_0,
 \qquad |r|\le R.
\tag{3.5}
\]

This cylinder is not claimed to be compact in the RFDE phase space. During
preparation apply

\[
\begin{aligned}
 u^v&=-F_v(v_t,w)-K_v\sigma_{1/2}(v-r\mathbf1),\\
 u^w&=-F_w(v,w)-K_w\sigma_{1/2}(w),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{1/2}(z)=\operatorname{sgn}(z)\sqrt{|z|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Here \(F_v,F_w\) are exactly the two uncontrolled right-hand sides in
(3.1). Consequently, with \(e=v-r\mathbf1\),

\[
 \dot e_i=-K_v\sigma_{1/2}(e_i),
 \qquad
 \dot w_i=-K_w\sigma_{1/2}(w_i).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Although the square-root field is not locally Lipschitz at zero, its
monotonicity gives a one-sided Lipschitz inequality and hence forward
uniqueness. Every coordinate reaches zero in finite time and remains there.
Keeping (3.6) active for one maximum-delay window after the predetermined
settling deadline creates the exact complete history

\[
 \Phi_r(\theta)=(r\mathbf1,0),
 \qquad -5\sqrt5\le\theta\le0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The general balanced topology changes none of the authority estimates,
because

\[
 \|P-I\|_\infty\le2,
 \qquad
 \|B_0\|_\infty+\|B_1\|_\infty=1.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For \(V_0=W_0=2\), \(R=3/4\), and \(K_v=K_w=1\), the directed bounds are

\[
 \|u^v\|_\infty<23.19,
 \qquad
 \|u^w\|_\infty<9.94,
 \qquad
 T_{\rm prep}<14.50.
\tag{3.10}
\]

They are per-node sup-norm bounds and are independent of \(N\). They require
exact knowledge of the current state, both delayed voltage layers, the model,
and the network matrices.

## 4. Bounded decision control and nonsynchronous first hits

At the release time, turn off \(u^v\) and apply only

\[
 u^w=-\{\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+E(P-I)w\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since preparation ends at \(w=0\), equation (4.1) makes \(w=0\) exactly
invariant. Until the voltage reaches a tube face \(\lVert v\rVert_\infty=H\),

\[
 \|u^w\|_\infty\le\varepsilon(H+a).
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, the decision input is at most \(0.42\) on
\(\lvert v_i\rvert\le3/2\). This realizes the nodewise recovery clamp used in
[the balanced-network sign-cone theorem](paper-iv-general-network-sign-cone.md)
by a bounded additive input for the exact model.

Let \(x=\pi^Tv\). Nonnegativity and the row-mass identities preserve both
complete-history orthants. Balance removes the topology from the mean
equation. Before a node reaches \(H\),

\[
 \dot x\ge c_+(H)x
 \quad\hbox{in the positive orthant},
 \qquad
 \dot x\le c_-(H)x
 \quad\hbox{in the negative orthant},
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
 c_+(H)=1-\frac{H^2}{3}
 -\varepsilon(\kappa_1+3\kappa_3),
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
 c_-(H)=1-\frac{H^2}{3}
 -\varepsilon\{\kappa_1+
 \kappa_3(H^2+3H+3)\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus, provided the target face has not already been met, whenever
\(x_0\ne0\), the initial history has its sign, and \(c_\pm(H)>0\),

\[
 T_\pm(H)\le
 \frac{1}{c_\pm(H)}\log\frac{H}{|x_0|}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The constant histories in the two reset charts have

\[
 r\in[0.499999999999,0.500000000001]
\tag{4.7}
\]

or

\[
 r\in[-0.500000000001,-0.499999999999].
\tag{4.8}
\]

They therefore satisfy the uniform mean condition used in the directed
sign-cone record. After preparation, some node reaches \(+1\) or \(-1\) and,
under the same bounded recovery control, some node reaches \(+3/2\) or
\(-6/5\) in finite time. The detector may be externally latched. The theorem
does not say that the detector node and the first excursion node coincide,
that crossing \(\pm1\) is irreversible, or that the trajectory returns as a
biological action potential.

Using the deliberately conservative parent lower bound
\(\lvert x_0\rvert\ge0.06\), the directed deadlines measured from the start
of preparation are

| chart | detector deadline | excursion deadline |
|---|---:|---:|
| positive | \(20.39922492281538\) | \(68.14489509303967\) |
| negative | \(24.66591907584100\) | \(52.90378869866757\) |

The actual reset charts have \(\lvert x_0\rvert\) close to \(1/2\), so these
published bounds are not intended to be sharp.

### 4.1 Closing every input after a synchronized handoff

At the exact chart centers \(r=\pm1/2\), the prepared history is
synchronous.  The
[autonomous-handoff theorem](paper-iv-autonomous-handoff-excursion.md)
uses recovery cancellation only up to a declared handoff face and then sets

\[
 u^v=u^w=0.
\tag{4.9}
\]

In the positive chart, handoff occurs at \(v=\mathbf1\) and the baseline
network reaches \(v=(3/2)\mathbf1\) autonomously.  In the negative chart,
the detector \(v=-\mathbf1\) is first latched, cancellation continues to
\(v=-(28/25)\mathbf1\), and the baseline network then reaches
\(v=-(6/5)\mathbf1\) autonomously.  Directed times from decision release to
the terminal faces satisfy

\[
 T_+\le2.73249432704410,
 \qquad
 T_-\le5.44906620694852,
 \qquad
 T_\pm<4\sqrt5.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Hence both trajectories finish before either delayed layer can see anything
other than the prepared value.  Exact rational phase barriers on that first
method-of-steps interval give strictly signed voltage velocities.  Closing
the negative control already at \(-1\) is rigorously invalid: the voltage
reverses before reaching \(-1.17\).

This exact theorem is restricted to the exactly prepared synchronous leaf and
the two exact center resets.  Its robust extension is stated next; neither
result is an autonomous onset theorem, a permanent no-return statement, or a
neighborhood-wide corollary for every reset in the target balls below.

### 4.2 An open asynchronous shutdown tube

The
[robust-handoff theorem](paper-iv-robust-handoff-tube.md)
works in the full phase space
\(C([ -\tau_1,0],\mathbb R^{2N})\) for every finite admitted balanced
topology.  It allows current-state and relevant remote voltage-history errors
below \(10^{-4}\), arbitrary-sign voltage and recovery shutdown residuals
below \(10^{-5}\), and the declared nonzero perturbations of
\(\varepsilon\) and \(a\).  A common tracking tube of radius
\(6\times10^{-4}\) gives

\[
 \dot v_i\ge0.131\quad\hbox{on the positive branch},
 \qquad
 \dot v_i\le-0.068\quad\hbox{on the negative branch}
\tag{4.11}
\]

until every component first enters its explicit terminal voltage--recovery
block in finite time.  The largest tracking error is
\(0.0005228041\), leaving strict slack
\(7.71959\times10^{-5}\).

The entrance cylinder is a hypothesis.  The theorem does not yet prove that
the preceding feedback robustly reaches it, that delay errors are allowed,
that the terminal block is invariant, or that it is an action-potential basin.

### 4.3 Why the reference slice is not yet a pulse/quiet dual basin

The
[reference-slice no-go theorem](quadratic-reference-slice-dual-basin-no-go.md)
proves that the unique synchronous equilibrium

\[
 (v_e,w_e)=\left({3\over5},{66\over125}\right)
\tag{4.12}
\]

has one characteristic root in each of two disjoint Rouché disks contained
in the open right half-plane.  The result is uniform on the microscopic gain
box and \(|\eta|\le10^{-3}\), with strict boundary margin greater than
\(0.02323\).  Hence this equilibrium is not a local quiet attractor and its
lower-dimensional stable set cannot be called a quiet basin.

On \(\eta=0\), the validated periodic orbit has
\(V_{\max}>1.934063\) and
\(-1.013315<V_{\min}<-1.013312\).  Every trajectory converging to that orbit
with asymptotic phase therefore revisits both sides of \(+1,+3/2,-1\)
infinitely often and cannot remain below \(-6/5\).  Permanent physical
detector-face no-return is consequently inconsistent with the proposed
periodic capture target.  A latched event label remains possible, but it is
event memory rather than an invariant voltage region or a biological basin.
The only repair preserving literal autonomous dual-basin biology is to find
and validate a different parameter slice containing both a stable quiet
attractor and the pulse attractor, together with trapping neighborhoods and
terminal-history basin inclusions.

### 4.4 A concrete autonomous replacement and its theorem gate

The
[leaky-recovery proposal](autonomous-leaky-recovery-bistable-rfde-proposal.md)
implements the autonomous repair without a detector latch or post-event
parameter switch.  It keeps the delayed voltage equation and replaces the
recovery channel by

\[
 \dot w=\varepsilon(v-a-w),
 \qquad
 (\varepsilon,a,\kappa_1,\kappa_3)
 =\left({1\over5},{1\over4},{1\over250},{1\over200}\right).
\tag{4.13}
\]

Writing \(\alpha=(3/4)^{1/3}\), the only constant solution is

\[
 E_q=(\alpha,\alpha-1/4).
\tag{4.14}
\]

An exact rational enclosure of \(\alpha\), followed by a scalar
small-gain estimate for the characteristic determinant, proves that
\(E_q\) has no characteristic root in
\(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ge0\) for **every** pair of positive delays.
Thus this slice passes the quiet-attractor local spectral gate that the
reference slice failed.

Binary64 continuation at the same point finds an outer periodic candidate of
period \(26.6044168026\) and voltage amplitude \(3.2860250879\), together
with an inner periodic candidate of period \(18.1862099491\).  Finite
monodromy discretizations consistently give zero and one nontranslation
multipliers outside the unit disk, respectively.  Starting from the quiet
history and applying a voltage current \(J\) only on \(0\le t\le1\) gives a
numerical rest/pulse bracket \(J\in[0.30,0.32]\); the RFDE is autonomous for
all \(t>1\).

Let \(K_\xi(J)\) be the terminal history at \(t=1\), with
\(\xi=(a,\kappa_3)\) and \(\varepsilon,\kappa_1\) and both physical delays
fixed.  If directed periodic BVPs and Floquet indices produce a stable outer
cycle \(\Gamma_p\) and an index-one inner cycle \(\Gamma_u\), if a
   history-space isolating neighborhood routes the two sides of
   \(W^s(\Gamma_u)\) to \(E_q\) and \(\Gamma_p\), and if the \(C^1\) map
   \(K_\xi\) has a positively oriented transverse zero of a defining
   function \(h(\xi,\phi)\) that is jointly \(C^1\) in parameter and history,
   then a unique threshold \(J_c(\xi)\)
   exists on a sufficiently small \(J\)-interval.  If in addition

\[
 \det D_{(a,\kappa_3)}(F,A_p)\ne0,
\tag{4.15}
\]

then

\[
 (a,\kappa_3,J)\longmapsto
 \bigl(F,A_p,J-J_c(a,\kappa_3)\bigr)
\tag{4.16}
\]

is a local \(C^1\) diffeomorphism.  The centered-difference candidate for
(4.15) has determinant \(1.11424778\) and smallest singular value
\(0.0815861\), but these are not directed bounds.  Equations (4.15)--(4.16)
are therefore a precise conditional biological-control theorem, not a
validated onset result.

## 5. Frequency--amplitude--operational-safety controllability

The row-mass identities in (3.4) make the synchronous history space
invariant. Its restriction is exactly the scalar two-delay FHN RFDE used by
the validated periodic branch. Therefore every admitted balanced topology
has the same synchronous branch frequency \(F(b)\) and unsquared voltage
amplitude \(A(b)\), where \(b=(\kappa_1,\kappa_3)\).

For the staged reset coordinate \(r\), define

\[
 Q_A(b,r)=(F(b),A(b),-r).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For each center \(r_0=\pm1/2\), the closed Euclidean input ball

\[
 \overline B_{10^{-12}}((0.2,0.25,r_0))
\tag{5.2}
\]

maps onto a set containing the closed output ball of radius

\[
 \rho_{F,A,S}
 =2.75138166016477172021072951467987182906462947064987861
 \times10^{-15}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Every target in that output ball has a unique preimage in (5.2). The
positive and negative input balls remain strictly inside their respective
signed decision channels. Combining Sections 3--4 with this inverse theorem
has the following meaning: for every target in either ball, its unique
parameter/reset triple determines a bounded exact-model preparation and a
finite controlled voltage-threshold excursion on every topology satisfying
(3.4).

This is a parameter-linked staged composition: the same parameter/reset
triple supports the controlled excursion and the synchronous periodic-branch
outputs. It is not a theorem that one controlled trajectory passes from the
excursion to that periodic branch.

The frequency and amplitude in this statement are properties of the
synchronous invariant periodic branch.  A complete directed right-half cover
now proves local synchronous orbital attraction on the microscopic gain box.
After composition with the separate transverse Halanay theorem, local
full-network attraction follows for each fixed finite size in the fixed
rank-one topology.  The later
[Dobrushin attraction theorem](paper-iv-dobrushin-periodic-attraction.md)
extends this conclusion on the \(\eta=0\) slice to every finite nonnegative
balanced topology with \(\tau(Q)\le1/4\): its transverse rate \(0.007\) is
uniform, while its nonlinear basin may depend on the fixed network.  At the
exact center gain pair, the

## 7. Mathematical value

The selected-root theorem contributes a genuinely network-dependent
mechanism: topology re-enters a projection-neutral canard calculation through
a stable resolvent and curvature heterogeneity. Its constants remain uniform
as \(N\) grows inside a nontrivial directed mixing class.

The controlled-output theorem contributes the complementary mechanism:
under positivity and balance, topology disappears exactly from the weighted
mean needed for a finite first-hit proof, while the additive preparation and
decision inputs have per-node bounds independent of \(N\). It also converts
the earlier ideal history overwrite and ideal recovery clamp into bounded
exact-model inputs on a declared bounded initial-data cylinder and voltage
tube.

Together, these results move beyond a single carefully chosen two-module,
two-delay example in two distinct senses. The canard-root response no longer
uses a synchrony quotient, and the controlled detector/excursion theorem no
longer uses a rank-one or two-module topology. The price is explicit:
Dobrushin mixing is retained for the selected root, and exact-model nodewise
recovery control is retained for the bounded excursion.

The autonomous-handoff theorem removes exact cancellation from the final
part of both synchronized excursion channels and, through the negative
unit-face reversal, identifies a mathematically necessary handoff depth. It
does not remove feedback from onset or supply a basin theorem. The
one-RFDE policy composition is valuable mainly as a sharp boundary result:
it proves that a root response can be transduced with uniform constants, and
its policy-offset falsifier proves why this alone has no intrinsic biological
meaning.

The robust-handoff tube supplies the first open-set result in the full delayed
history phase space for this final segment.  Its mathematical gain is not a
larger endpoint: it replaces exact synchrony and exact shutdown by quantified
asynchrony, parameter error and arbitrary-sign residual forcing, uniformly in
network size and admitted balanced topology.  Its sharp boundary is equally
important: robust entrance reachability and a biological destination remain
separate theorems.

The compatibility audit supplies a second boundary result: it proves that the
earlier selected-root and periodic/control mechanisms do not inhabit one
plant, and isolates the precise two-delay synchronization obstruction.  The
quadratic period lock then supplies the positive mathematical response: one
common plant can leave the center periodic outputs and fold linearization
unchanged while moving a canonical complete-history root.  The uniform
Dobrushin graph lift closes the corresponding arbitrary-finite network
question on its declared anisotropic canonical tube.  This closes the
mechanism-design and one genuine full-network question at small \(\delta\),
not the fixed-parameter, arbitrary-history, or biological-onset questions.

The explicit eta-Floquet box closes the corresponding stability seam on one
nonzero actuator interval.  Its value is not the small numerical width by
itself, but the proof architecture: every base right-half leaf is replayed,
the active period-length delay is handled on the enlarged history space, and
the same projection identity that preserves the orbit removes the carrier
from the entire transverse block.  Thus one nonlinear channel can move the
small-\(\delta\) canonical root while preserving full-network local periodic
attraction on a rigorously nonempty interval, without claiming that the
fixed-\(\varepsilon\) root has already been validated.

The reference-slice basin audit supplies the complementary negative result.
It identifies two structurally wrong shortcuts before a costly global proof
attempt: the only validated rest state is unstable, and detector-face
no-return cannot coexist with capture by an oscillation that recrosses those
faces.  This converts a vague missing-basin caveat into a precise model-design
decision between autonomous bistability, semantic latching, and controlled
switching.

## 8. Remaining theorem gates

Five gates remain before the word *biological* can be used without an
operational qualifier.

1. **Fixed-amplitude causal graph and selected root.**  The quadratic
   period-locked common plant has a proved small-\(\delta\) full-network
   canonical-graph coefficient for the uniformly Dobrushin class.  At
   \(\varepsilon=1/5\), however, the old singular \(\sigma\)-clock cannot be
   continued on the specified independent product geometry: an explicit
   zero-vector raw-slot tuple lies inside its radius-\(10^{-3}\) tube, so
   every planar current-state \(C^1\) phase has zero Lie derivative there.
   This exact obstruction forces abandonment of that product tube; an
   embedded prepared solution family and its intrinsic time provide one
   viable replacement.  The present binary64 chart has a sampled nonvanishing
   Jacobian, but interval global injectivity, a target-centered collar and
   cutoff, and the regularity required for a \(C_b^3\) field remain open.
   In particular, since \(Q\circ\Psi=\partial_t\Psi\), the intended
   \(C^3\) field normally requires a \(C^4\) prepared chart; the seam-jet
   count must include this one-derivative loss.

   Once that graph exists, the remaining selected-root gate is to validate
   the complete-history augmented BVP and dynamic-adjoint ratio.  The current exact blueprint
   includes the full-history jump and advanced adjoint.  An actual two-branch
   discrete solve now closes both flights, phase, and every represented jump
   node and gives \(\rho_N=-0.3463310348\) on its finest mesh.  Its entry
   history satisfies the RFDE solution-manifold compatibility equation, but
   the prescribed template is not the selected attracting trace.  The raw
   194-coefficient endpoint ledger has two independent compatibility rows,
   so its fixed-mesh compatible level is 192-dimensional and cannot contain
   a rank-193 fixed-parameter chart.  That exact finite-dimensional result is
   not the continuous \(W^{1,p}\) Fredholm count: endpoint derivative
   compatibility is not continuous in \(W^{1,p}\), while the existing
   continuous piecewise polynomials are already conforming there without
   derivative seams.  The corrected first-order contract uses
   \(W^{1,p}\) orbit/history spaces and \(L^p\) flow residuals; its natural
   full 194-row history ledger is arithmetically \(775\)-by-\(774\).
   The earlier \(W^{2,p}\to L^p\) branch scale is compact and cannot be the
   desired Fredholm principal operator.  Exact compatible counterexamples
   also show that
   the scalar exit observable \(G=0\) does not determine a complete history
   or the right flight, so it is not a backward-extendible repelling chart.
   A new equivalence theorem reduces the missing one-dimensional invariant
   chart to one independently selected complete orbit plus a monotone phase;
   using the connection-dependent right branch would make the terminal match
   a circular identity.  Hence the actual selected trace pair and Fredholm
   endpoint operator remain unconstructed, and
   neither this value nor the earlier shooting interval is a selected-root
   or sign enclosure.  After the scalar certificate, the fixed-
   \(\varepsilon\) Dobrushin/Fredholm lift is tracked separately in
   [issue 23](https://github.com/h-lu/canard-aware-network-control/issues/23);
   the small-\(\delta\) network theorem does not close that target gate.
2. **Autonomous periodic separator and physical-pulse onset.**  The failed
   reference slice is now replaced by the leaky-recovery candidate in
   Section 4.4.  Its quiet equilibrium is rigorously stable for arbitrary
   positive delays, but the outer and inner periodic orbits are still
   binary64 candidates.  Validate both periodic BVPs, their complete
   Floquet indices and simple extrema on one \((a,\kappa_3)\)-box.  Then
   construct a history-space isolating neighborhood (not an unexplained
   planar annulus), prove routing of its two sides into the quiet and pulse
   basins, and enclose the terminal-history map of the finite physical pulse.
   The acceptance condition is a unique transverse crossing
   \(K_\xi(J_c)\in W^s(\Gamma_u)\) and a directed nonzero bound for
   \(\det D_{(a,\kappa_3)}(F,A_p)\), not two long-time integrations or a
   pointwise determinant.
3. **Same-model canard/onset comparison.**  Even after gates 1--2, the
   selected complete-history canard root and the physical pulse threshold
   \(J_c\) are two different codimension-one objects.  Relate them in one
   parameter-coherent leaky-recovery plant by an input-independent outer or
   event map, including preparation, moving-delay, terminal-history and
   adjoint terms.  A correlation or nearby numerical values do not prove
   equality.  The existing root-linked reset policy also does not close this
   gate because it injects the exact root as offline control data.
4. **Trajectory landing and broader-topology attraction.** The
   [complete right-half cover](paper-iv-synchronous-floquet-right-half-cover.md)
   closes the synchronous stable-index gate.  The Dobrushin Halanay theorem
   proves local full-network attraction at \(\eta=0\) for every fixed finite
   topology with \(\tau(Q)\le1/4\), with a uniform transverse rate but no
   uniform nonlinear basin.  The quadratic eta-Floquet theorem now supplies
   a nonzero box \(|\eta|\le3\times10^{-6}\) at the exact center gain pair.
   Attraction for a joint gain--eta box, weaker/closing mixing gaps, and a
   proof that the controlled pulse enters the certified periodic basin remain
   open.
5. **Robust reachability and hardware containment.** The open shutdown tube
   covers bounded state/history/parameter errors and arbitrary-sign residual
   inputs after handoff.  It does not prove robust entrance reachability,
   delay uncertainty, measurement/model error, bandwidth, slew, energy or
   saturation margins.  A finite mathematical sup-norm bound alone is not a
   hardware result.

The very small radius (5.3) is mathematically nonzero and proves local
surjectivity and uniqueness. It is not evidence of useful laboratory-scale
authority.

## 10. Flagship-paper division

A mathematically honest flagship submission can now be organized around one
causal-history spine and one autonomous-onset application:

1. introduce complete-history roots and balanced controlled events as two
   different RFDE objects;
2. prove the abstract one-gap transfer and then the concrete Dobrushin
   Poisson/curvature response;
3. give the one-RFDE policy transduction and its policy-offset falsifier;
4. prove the exact cross-model incompatibility and restricted linear
   obstruction, followed by the quadratic common-RFDE carrier, scalar
   small-\(\delta\) root theorem, and uniform Dobrushin zero-graph lift;
5. prove the target raw-slot equilibrium no-go, then replace the impossible
   product-tube clock by the intrinsic-time prepared-chart theorem;
6. interval-validate the target chart, its \(C^4\) preparation and curved
   collar, then attach the \(W^{1,p}\) trace pair, bordered Fredholm inverse
   and fixed-\(\varepsilon\) selected root;
7. prove the reference-slice rest-instability no-go and the exact
   delay-independent quiet-state stability of the leaky-recovery replacement;
8. validate the replacement's outer/inner periodic BVPs and Floquet indices,
   followed by the history-space separator and physical-pulse threshold;
9. prove the block-triangular autonomous inverse
   \((a,\kappa_3,J)\mapsto(F,A,J-J_c)\) on a directed target ball and lift
   the transverse indices to the admitted Dobrushin network class;
10. compare the selected canard root with the physical onset manifold through
    an explicit outer/event factorization, keeping nearby but unequal roots
    distinct; and
11. place the earlier bounded preparation, controlled handoff and operational
    target-ball theorems in a boundary-results section rather than using them
    as substitutes for autonomous basin capture.

Items 1--5 and the exact quiet-state part of item 7 already support a strong
network-dynamics development.  A title or abstract claiming that a
general-network maximal canard organizes autonomous biological pulse onset
requires the interval and basin gates in items 6, 8 and 10; those claims are
not yet available.
