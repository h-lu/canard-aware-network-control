# Quadratic physical-onset and capture stop/go theorem

Status: **exact non-composition certificate and a controlled small-
\(\eta\) terminal-transfer theorem; physical onset, capture, and basin
claims remain open.**  The currently proved selected-root, bounded
preparation/handoff, and periodic-attraction modules do not compose into an
input-independent biological-onset theorem.  Four literal seams block the
composition: the \(\delta/a\) theorem slice, the preparation history, the
plant and history horizon, and the missing quantitative basin inclusion.
Equivalently, the existing module package **does not compose** on its pinned
theorem domains.
The Paper III physical outer construction is a fifth, separate model and
cannot repair any of these seams by relabelling it “FHN-type.”

There is one positive same-plant statement.  On the fixed reference slice,
extend preparation to the \(T_*\)-history cylinder and cancel the quadratic
carrier exactly throughout preparation and the controlled decision.  After
handoff all additive controls are zero, while the released quadratic plant
term is treated as a perturbation of the \(\eta=0\) comparison field.  For a
strict, explicitly computed common bound on \(|\eta|\), the existing robust
handoff proof then still reaches its two finite terminal blocks.  This is a
**controlled terminal transfer**.  It is not an input-independent onset theorem
and not a basin theorem.

## 1. The one RFDE to which “same model” refers

Let \(Q,B_0,B_1\ge0\) be balanced with stationary probability row
\(\pi^T\), put \(\Pi=\mathbf1\pi^T\), and write
\(H(v)=(v-\mathbf1)^{\circ3}\).  The quadratic period-locked plant is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&v-\frac13v^{\circ3}-w+3(Q-I)v\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_1
 \{B_0v(t-\tau_0)+B_1v(t-\tau_1)-v\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_3
 \{B_0H(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1H(v(t-\tau_1))-H(v)\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\eta\Pi\!\left[
 (v(t)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(v(t-T_*)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}\right]+u^v(t),\\
 \dot w={}&\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+2(Q-I)w+u^w(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The selected-root theorem uses \(\varepsilon=\delta^2\), fixed fold-time
supports \((4,5,\Theta_*)\), hence physical delays
\((4/\delta,5/\delta,\Theta_*/\delta)\), and a parameter-coherent canonical
history on the enlarged horizon.  Only at the reference value below is
\(\Theta_*=T_*/\sqrt5\) chosen so that the third physical delay equals the
validated period \(T_*\).  Period locking along the whole small-\(\delta\)
root family would require an additional theorem relating
\(T_*(\delta)=\Theta_*/\delta\); none is currently proved.  The control and
periodic parents use

\[
 \delta_*={1\over\sqrt5},\qquad \varepsilon_*={1\over5},
 \qquad a_{\rm per}={3\over5},\qquad
 (\tau_0,\tau_1)=(4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The raw bounded-preparation parent is certified only for its fixed rank-one
two-module instance.  The separately pinned balanced-control-chain theorem,
however, redoes the row-mass estimates and proves the same exact-model
preparation, finite complete-history creation, and topology- and
node-count-independent authority for every finite balanced two-half-delay-
layer network.  Therefore the positive terminal-transfer theorem below uses
that general parent and is not restricted to rank one.  To intersect it with
the available periodic-attraction theorem one must additionally impose
\(\tau(Q)\le1/4\) and \(\eta=0\); that intersection still gives no terminal-
block basin inclusion.

## 2. Exact stop theorem: the existing modules do not compose

> **Theorem 2.1 (four-gate non-composition).**  The pinned selected-root,
> fixed-\(\varepsilon\) shooting diagnostic, bounded preparation, robust and
> autonomous handoff, periodic branch, and Dobrushin periodic-attraction
> certificates do not imply an input-independent physical-onset/event
> comparison, pulse/quiet capture, or permanent no-return theorem for
> (1.1).

The obstruction is not a missing sentence.  Each row below is a failed
hypothesis needed for literal theorem composition.

| Gate | Root-side object | Control/periodic-side object | Exact status |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(\delta/a\) slice | sufficiently small \(\delta\), with \(a_c(\delta,0)=1-\delta^2/8+O(\delta^3)\) and fixed scaled support \(\Theta_*\) | \(\delta=1/\sqrt5\), \(a=3/5\), with physical third delay \(T_*\) | no common proved \(\delta\)-domain; the limiting \(a\)-gap is \(1-3/5=2/5\), and period locking away from the reference point is unproved |
| preparation | parameter-coherent enlarged-horizon canonical flow-hull history | policy-produced constants \(\Phi_{+1/2}\), \(\Phi_{-1/2}\) | no history equality or physical-selection theorem |
| plant/horizon | quadratic \(T_*\)-delay channel present | preparation and handoff parents omit that channel and hold only through \(\max\{\tau_0,\tau_1\}\) | literal identity only at \(\eta=0\), unless the cancellation and longer hold in Section 3 are added |
| basin | selected root is a complete-history matching statement | at \(\eta=0\), local orbital attraction for each fixed finite network with \(\tau(Q)\le1/4\) | no uniform basin radius, no \(\mathcal K_\pm\) containment, no quiet basin, and no \(\eta\ne0\) attraction |

For the first row, the asymptotic root theorem gives a neighborhood of
\(a=1\) as \(\delta\to0\); the fixed periodic plant is at \(a=3/5\).  More
fundamentally, “sufficiently small” has not been shown to include
\(1/\sqrt5\).  The finite-section calculation at \(\varepsilon=1/5\)
reports a nonzero numerical response candidate, but explicitly refuses a
complete-history BVP, an interval inverse, a selected root, and a rigorous
\(\rho_*\ne0\).  A diagnostic cannot fill this quantifier gap.

For the second row, a controller can manufacture \(\Phi_r\), but that does
not identify \(\Phi_r\) with the canonical flow-hull history used to define
the selected root.  Calling the manufactured reset “the onset history”
would make the answer depend on policy.

For the third row, the \(\eta\)-derivative samples \(-T_*\).  At \(\eta=0\)
an older history extension is dynamically inert, yet two such extensions
can give different \(\eta\)-derivatives.  Thus the full
\(\max\{\tau_0,\tau_1,T_*\}\) history must be prepared coherently; an
arbitrary inert extension is insufficient.

For the fourth row, local orbital attraction supplies an unspecified open
neighborhood for each fixed admitted network.  It supplies neither a
directed radius nor inclusion of the handoff terminal blocks.  Moreover the
attraction theorem is on \(\eta=0\), while the new root carrier is meant to
probe \(\eta\ne0\).

Finally, the Paper III physical outer/fold chain uses a heterogeneous-
curvature two-module RFDE, not (1.1).  Its U-SF/U-EX/U-CAP architecture is
conditional and biological U-CAP remains open.  Results from that plant
cannot be pasted onto the quadratic dual scaffold.

## 3. Exact go theorem: controlled small-\(\eta\) terminal transfer

### 3.1 Why the carrier disappears before release

For a constant complete voltage history \(v\equiv r\mathbf1\),

\[
 \Pi\big[(v(t)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(v(t-T_*)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}\big]=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

During a nonconstant controlled segment this identity need not hold.  Add
the measured cancellation input

\[
 u^v_{\rm q}(t)=
 -\varepsilon\eta\Pi\!\left[
 (v(t)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(v(t-T_*)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}\right].
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then the carrier plus (3.2) is pointwise zero throughout reaching, the
extended hold, and the controlled decision.  Consequently the controlled
trajectory is exactly the \(\eta=0\) parent trajectory.  This requires
full current and \(T_*\)-delayed voltage measurement and exact-model
cancellation; no noise, uncertainty, bandwidth, or hardware theorem is
being imported.

The hold must prepare the enlarged \(T_*\)-history cylinder, not merely the
two baseline delay layers.  Equivalently, at release the whole remote
window sampled by \(t-T_*\) during the handoff horizon must retain the
corresponding constant value \(r_\pm=\pm1/2\).  This is the explicit longer
history hypothesis missing from the parent preparation theorem.

On its declared cylinder \(\|v\|_\infty\le2\), each shifted square lies in
\([0,9]\), so

\[
 \|u^v_{\rm q}\|_\infty\le {9\over5}|\eta|.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The old voltage-authority upper bound is
\(23.1849790618559665\ldots\), below the ceiling \(23.19\) by
\(0.0050209381440334088\ldots\).  At the common \(\eta\)-bound derived
below, (3.3) adds at most
\(0.00003468939347306162\ldots\), leaving
\(0.0049862487505603472\ldots>0\).  Thus the exact cancellation has both an
algebraic identity and enough declared preparation authority.  It is an
extra control channel; the old theorem did not contain it implicitly.

### 3.2 Released carrier as the handoff residual

At handoff set \(u^v=u^w=0\), including (3.2).  The quadratic term remains
part of the autonomous plant.  Relative to the \(\eta=0\) nominal field it
can be entered in the robust theorem's voltage-residual slot as

\[
 d^v_\eta(t)=\varepsilon\eta\Pi\!\left[
 (v(t)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(v(t-T_*)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}\right].
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is proof bookkeeping, not a claim that a physical actuator remains on.
All other post-handoff residuals are set to zero.

The inherited positive current/delayed values lie in
\([0.4994,1.5006]\), hence their shifted squares lie in

\[
 \left[0,{6265009\over25000000}\right].
\tag{3.5}
\]

The negative values lie in \([-1.2006,-0.4994]\), hence their shifted
squares lie in

\[
 \left[{56205009\over25000000},
 {121066009\over25000000}\right].
\tag{3.6}
\]

Because \(\|\Pi\|_{\infty\to\infty}=1\) and the robust parameter box has
\(\varepsilon<0.200001\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|d^v_{\eta,+}\|_\infty
 &\le {1253008065009\over25000000000000}|\eta|,\\
 \|d^v_{\eta,-}\|_\infty
 &\le {12972264861\over25000000000}|\eta|.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
\]

The parent residual budget is \(10^{-5}\).  The two strict channel bounds
are therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\eta|&<\eta_+
 ={250000000\over1253008065009}
 =1.995198650203455\ldots\times10^{-4},\\
 |\eta|&<\eta_-
 ={250000\over12972264861}
 =1.927188526281201\ldots\times10^{-5}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus the strict common bound is

\[
 |\eta|<\eta_{\rm com}:=\min\{\eta_+,\eta_-\}
 ={250000\over12972264861}=\eta_-.
\tag{3.9}
\]

> **Theorem 3.1 (controlled terminal transfer).**  Work on any finite
> balanced two-half-delay-layer network admitted by the balanced control and
> robust-handoff parents, at \(\varepsilon=1/5\), \(a=3/5\), and the
> inherited microscopic gain box.  Assume the bounded preparation and
> controlled decision satisfy their parent hypotheses, extend the hold to
> the full \(T_*\)-remote history required above, apply (3.2) until the
> declared handoff, and assume the resulting release history is in the
> appropriate robust entrance cylinder.  Set every additive input to zero
> after release.  If \(|\eta|<\eta_{\rm com}\), then the released quadratic
> carrier fits strictly inside the parent \(10^{-5}\) voltage-residual
> budget on both channels.  The positive and negative trajectories retain
> the parent's finite-horizon componentwise no-reversal estimates and enter
> its terminal blocks \(\mathcal K_+\) and \(\mathcal K_-\), respectively.

The entrance-cylinder hypothesis is retained because the robust parent does
not prove its reachability from perturbed preparation data.  “No reversal”
in Theorem 3.1 ends at terminal entry.  It is not permanent face no-return,
capture by a biological basin, or selected-root onset.  In particular, the
controller selects the sign and manufactures the preparation, so this
theorem cannot be described as input-independent.

## 4. What an intrinsic root/event comparison would require

Let \(G(a,\eta)\) be the complete-history matching gap whose simple zero
defines the selected root.  A genuine physical comparison needs, on the
same RFDE and phase space:

1. a controller-independent physical history map
   \(a\mapsto\mathcal H_{\rm phys}(a,\eta)\);
2. a predeclared physiological event functional \(E\) independent of the
   selected-root calculation and of the control policy;
3. equality of \(\mathcal H_{\rm phys}\) with the canonical root history, or
   a proved comparison map between them;
4. a local factorization
   \[
     E(\mathcal H_{\rm phys}(a,\eta))
       =\chi(a,\eta)G(a,\eta),
       \qquad \inf|\chi|>0,
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
   together with the needed transversality; and
5. a fixed-\(\varepsilon=1/5\) selected-root and nonzero-response theorem if
   the periodic reference slice is to be used.

None of items 1--5 is currently proved.  Defining
\(E:=G\), or defining the event as “the controller chose the positive
branch,” would make (4.1) tautological.  An event functional can be
input-independent as a formula while its evaluated history remains
policy-dependent; that also does not constitute physical onset.

## 5. Why terminal entry is not pulse/quiet capture

The \(\eta=0\) Dobrushin attraction theorem gives local nonlinear orbital
attraction with asymptotic phase for each fixed finite admitted network with
\(\tau(Q)\le1/4\).  It gives no network-uniform nonlinear basin radius.  No
directed inclusion

\[
 \mathcal K_+\subset\mathcal B_{\rm pulse},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_-\subset\mathcal B_{\rm quiet}
\tag{5.1}
\]

has been proved, and no quiet attractor or quiet basin has been validated.
The attraction result also refuses \(\eta\ne0\) full-network attraction.
Consequently Theorem 3.1 cannot be concatenated with it.

Permanent face no-return is generally the wrong conclusion for an
oscillatory attractor.  Conditionally, if a continuous detector changes sign
along an attracting periodic orbit, convergence with asymptotic phase forces
nearby captured trajectories to revisit both signs.  Basin membership may
be permanent while membership in one detector half-space is not.  A latched
first-hit label is immutable by definition, but that is policy memory, not a
physical invariant region.  The present certificates prove neither a
trapping half-space nor permanent face no-return.

## 6. Minimal remaining go gates

A full theorem would require all of the following new results, not merely a
change of terminology:

1. validate the selected complete-history root and \(\rho_*\ne0\) at
   \(\varepsilon=1/5\), with the enlarged \(T_*\)-history preparation;
2. construct the controller-independent physical history map and prove the
   non-tautological factorization (4.1) for a predeclared event;
3. continue and validate full-network periodic stability for an explicit
   \(\eta\)-interval containing the root comparison;
4. compute directed pulse and quiet basin subsets and prove terminal-block
   containment; and
5. formulate no-return as basin trapping or a clearly labelled latch, not as
   an unsupported forever-one-sided detector claim.

If a directed basin radius is obtained first, the least invasive control
repair is to retarget the terminal controller into that certified subset.
If an \(\eta\)-stability interval is obtained first, Theorem 3.1 already
provides a compatible finite transfer bound.  Neither repair by itself
creates input-independent physical onset.
