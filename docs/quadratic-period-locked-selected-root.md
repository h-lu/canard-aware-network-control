# A quadratic period lock with a nonzero canonical canard-root response

Status: **exact carrier theorem and proved small-δ canonical synchronous
selected-root response; fixed-ε response and physical onset remain open.**
The quadratic collective delay difference introduced below preserves the
distinguished periodic orbit and its frequency--amplitude outputs, but its
first nontrivial fold-chart forcing has a nonzero Gaussian canard pairing.
For a fixed delay support in fold time it gives

\[
 a_c(\delta,\eta)-a_c(\delta,0)
 =-\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\delta^3\eta
  +O(\delta^4|\eta|+\delta^3\eta^2).
\tag{0.1}
\]

This repairs the leading-order parity cancellation of the linear period-lock
channel.  It does **not** prove that the small-parameter interval contains
\(\delta=1/\sqrt5\), and therefore does not yet prove
\(\rho_*=\partial_\eta a_c\ne0\) at the periodic operating value
\(\varepsilon=1/5\).  The fixed-ε gate is an interval-validated
complete-history BVP and dynamic-adjoint pairing, not another moment
calculation.

The exact identities are implemented in
[quadratic_period_locked_root_carrier.py](../src/canard_control/quadratic_period_locked_root_carrier.py),
generated by
[quadratic_period_locked_root_carrier.py](../experiments/quadratic_period_locked_root_carrier.py),
and tested in
[test_quadratic_period_locked_root_carrier.py](../tests/test_quadratic_period_locked_root_carrier.py).
The fixed-ε adjoint gate and the linear-channel cancellation are recorded in
[dual-scaffold-period-locked-root-adjoint-gate.md](dual-scaffold-period-locked-root-adjoint-gate.md).

## 1. One extended RFDE and one reference slice

Let \(N\ge1\), \(\pi^T\mathbf1=1\), and put
\(\Pi=\mathbf1\pi^T\).  Assume

\[
 Q\mathbf1=\mathbf1,\qquad \pi^TQ=\pi^T,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
 B_j\mathbf1=\frac12\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^TB_j=\frac12\pi^T,
 \qquad j=0,1.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Nonnegativity of \(Q,B_0,B_1\) may be added for the balanced control
theorems; the identities below use only (1.1)--(1.2).  With componentwise
\(f(v)=v-v^{\circ3}/3\) and \(H(v)=(v-\mathbf1)^{\circ3}\), consider

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&f(v)-w+3(Q-I)v\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_1
 \{B_0v(t-\tau_0)+B_1v(t-\tau_1)-v\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_3
 \{B_0H(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1H(v(t-\tau_1))-H(v)\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\eta\Pi\!\left[
 (v(t)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(v(t-\tau_*)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 \right],\\
 \dot w={}&\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+2(Q-I)w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.3}
\]

For the singular family put

\[
 \varepsilon=\delta^2,\qquad
 \tau_0=\frac4\delta,\qquad
 \tau_1=\frac5\delta,\qquad
 \tau_*=\frac{\Theta_*}{\delta},
 \qquad \Theta_*>0.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Let \(\delta_*=1/\sqrt5\), and let \(T_*\) be the exact period of the
already validated synchronous center orbit at

\[
 (\delta,a,\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)
 =\left(\delta_*,\frac35,\frac15,\frac14,0\right).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Choose once and for all

\[
 \boxed{\Theta_*=\delta_*T_*.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

Then the physical delay in (1.3) satisfies \(\tau_*=T_*\) on the reference
slice (1.5), while (1.4) supplies the fixed fold-time support required by the
canonical small-δ theorem.  This is one \(\delta\)-dependent RFDE family;
at \(\delta=\delta_*\) its drift is literally the desired fixed
\(\varepsilon=1/5\) plant.

The phase space is fixed **before** differentiating in \(\eta\): in fold
time its horizon is

\[
 \Theta_{\max}=\max\{4,5,\Theta_*\},
\tag{1.7}
\]

and in physical time it is
\(C([-\Theta_{\max}/\delta,0],\mathbb R^{2N})\).  At \(\eta=0\) the drift
does not read the extra \(-\Theta_*\) slot, but the canonical selected
histories are nevertheless constructed on this enlarged horizon using the
same backward flow-hull embedding and the same frozen preparation for every
\(\eta\).  An arbitrary constant or ``inert'' extension of a shorter parent
history is not admitted: its \(\eta\)-derivative can change the endpoint and
history pairing and can cancel the coefficient in (0.1).  The root below is
therefore explicitly indexed by this enlarged-horizon canonical
preparation.

## 2. Exact carrier theorem

Define the nonlinear history operator

\[
 \mathcal A_2(\phi)=\Pi\left[
 (\phi_v(0)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 -(\phi_v(-\tau_*)-\mathbf1)^{\circ2}
 \right].
\tag{2.1}
\]

> **Theorem 2.1 (quadratic period-lock carrier).**  For every finite
> balanced topology satisfying (1.1)--(1.2):
>
> 1. \(\mathcal A_2\) preserves the synchronous history space and vanishes
>    on every constant history;
> 2. it vanishes pointwise on the distinguished synchronous
>    \(T_*\)-periodic orbit on the reference slice, for every \(\eta\);
> 3. it vanishes at the right fold \(v=\mathbf1\), and its full Fréchet
>    derivative there is zero, so the fold state, voltage Jacobian, and
>    critical pair are unchanged;
> 4. along a synchronous base history its first variation on every pure
>    transverse history in \(\ker\pi^T\) is zero;
> 5. the distinguished periodic solution, its period, its frequency, and its
>    voltage range are exactly independent of \(\eta\) at the center.

**Proof.**  On a synchronous history \(\phi_v(\theta)=s(\theta)\mathbf1\),
\(\Pi\mathbf1=\mathbf1\) gives

\[
 \mathcal A_2(\phi)
 =\{(s(0)-1)^2-(s(-\tau_*)-1)^2\}\mathbf1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This proves synchrony preservation and constant-history cancellation.
Periodicity and \(\tau_*=T_*\) prove item 2.  At \(\phi_v=\mathbf1\),
both the value and the derivative of the square vanish, proving item 3.
At a synchronous base history, the derivative in direction \(z\) is

\[
 2\Pi\left[
 (s(0)-1)z(0)-(s(-\tau_*)-1)z(-\tau_*)
 \right].
\tag{2.3}
\]

If \(\pi^Tz(0)=\pi^Tz(-\tau_*)=0\), (2.3) is zero.  This proves item 4.
The exact same periodic solution exists for every \(\eta\); using the
phase-fixed local solution selected by the periodic parent makes all its
listed outputs constant in \(\eta\), proving item 5. \(\square\)

The theorem is nonlinear.  It is not described by a nonzero linear delay
moment at the fold: its fold linearization is exactly zero.  Its canard
effect first appears through the quadratic fold profile.

The carrier also supplies a qualitative three-parameter periodic theorem,
not merely an exact solution at one parameter.

> **Corollary 2.2 (qualitative periodic branch and zero response column).**
> On the synchronous restriction at \(a=3/5\) and
> \(\delta=\delta_*\), there is a neighborhood of
> \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)=(1/5,1/4,0)\) on which the phase-fixed
> periodic BVP for (1.3) has a unique \(C^1\) solution branch.  At the
> center,
> \[
>  \partial_\eta(V,W)=0,
>  \qquad \partial_\eta T=0,
>  \qquad \partial_\eta F=\partial_\eta A=0,
> \tag{2.4}
> \]
> where \(F=T^{-1}\) and \(A\) is either the validated squared range or
> its separately validated positive square root.

**Proof.**  At \(\eta=0\), the new term contributes neither to the state
linearization nor to the period column, because it is multiplied by
\(\eta\).  Hence the phase-bordered derivative at the center is exactly the
already validated invertible bordered operator of the periodic parent.  On
the fixed physical delay support \(T_*\), the new BVP term is a polynomial
of present and shifted periodic evaluations and is \(C^1\) on the same
strong periodic space.  The Banach implicit-function theorem therefore
gives the local three-parameter branch.

The inhomogeneous \(\eta\)-column of the differentiated BVP is
\(\mathcal A_2(V_*)\), which vanishes pointwise because the center orbit is
\(T_*\)-periodic.  The \(\eta\)-sensitivity consequently solves the
homogeneous bordered equation; invertibility makes the orbit and period
sensitivities zero.  Frequency follows from \(F=T^{-1}\).  The range
derivative is zero because the orbit derivative is zero (equivalently, use
the already validated simple extrema and the envelope formula). \(\square\)

Corollary 2.2 is qualitative.  It proves the central zero column needed for
the block determinant, but it does not provide a directed radius in
\(\eta\), a boxwise bordered inverse, persistence of unique extrema on that
larger box, or a three-parameter target-ball radius.

## 3. Exact fold chart

On the synchronous leaf set

\[
 v=1+\delta X,\qquad
 w=\frac23-\delta^2Y,\qquad
 a=1+\delta^2\nu,\qquad s=\delta t.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Writing \(X_j=X(s-j)\) and \(X_\Theta=X(s-\Theta_*)\), direct substitution
into (1.3)--(1.4) gives the exact scalar fold-chart RFDE

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X'={}&Y-X^2\\
 &+\delta\left[-\frac{X^3}{3}
  +\kappa_1\left\{\frac{X_4+X_5}{2}-X\right\}\right]\\
 &+\delta^2\eta\{X^2-X_\Theta^2\}\\
 &+\delta^3\kappa_3
  \left\{\frac{X_4^3+X_5^3}{2}-X^3\right\},\\
 Y'={}&-X+\delta\nu.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

There is no Taylor remainder in (3.2).  The singular canard and its decaying
adjoint are

\[
 \gamma_0(s)=\left(-\frac{s}{2},\frac{s^2-2}{4}\right),
 \qquad
 \psi(s)=e^{-s^2/2}(s,1)^T.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The order-δ baseline coefficient along (3.3) is

\[
 q_{1,X}(\gamma_0(s))
 =\frac{s^3}{24}+\frac94\kappa_1,
 \qquad q_{1,Y}=\nu.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The delayed linear term in (3.4) is constant because the singular canard is
affine in \(X\).  Hence

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}\psi^Tq_1\,ds
 =\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\nu+\frac18\right),
\tag{3.5}
\]

so the leading canonical root is \(\nu_0=-1/8\).

For the quadratic structural column,

\[
 X_0(s)^2-X_0(s-\Theta_*)^2
 =\frac{\Theta_*}{2}s-\frac{\Theta_*^2}{4}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Its complete Gaussian pairing is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_\eta
 &=\int_{\mathbb R}s e^{-s^2/2}
 \left(\frac{\Theta_*}{2}s-\frac{\Theta_*^2}{4}\right)ds\\
 &=\boxed{\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\sqrt{2\pi}}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
\]

The constant term is odd after multiplication by the fast adjoint; the
linear term is even and survives.  This is the mechanism absent from the
linear period lock.

## 4. Canonical selected-root theorem

Fix \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3)\) in a compact neighborhood of
\((1/5,1/4)\), fix \(\Theta_*>0\), and fix one admissible canonical
preparation datum in the sense of the
[canonical long-delay theorem](canonical-long-delay-theorem.md), now on the
enlarged horizon (1.7).  In particular, the preparation includes the
depth-two backtrack through \(-\Theta_*\) even at \(\eta=0\); it is not
obtained by appending arbitrary older values to the shorter two-delay
selected traces.

> **Theorem 4.1 (quadratic period-lock selected-root response).**  There are
> \(\delta_0,\eta_0,c_0,C>0\) such that, for
> \(0<\delta<\delta_0\) and \(|\eta|<\eta_0\), the synchronous restriction
> of (1.3) has a unique canonical selected complete-history root
> \(\nu_c(\delta,\eta)\) in
> \[
>  |\nu+1/8|<c_0.
> \tag{4.1}
> \]
> The selected attracting and repelling retained histories meet exactly at
> that root.  In the physical parameter \(a=1+\delta^2\nu\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> a_c(\delta,\eta)-a_c(\delta,0)
> =-\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\delta^3\eta
> +O(\delta^4|\eta|+\delta^3\eta^2).}
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> The coefficient is independent of \(N,Q,B_0,B_1\).  The synchronous
> history connection therefore lifts as an exact solution of every balanced
> network (1.3), although full-network local uniqueness is not asserted.

**Proof.**  Equation (3.2) has the required special-flow form

\[
 Q=q_0+\delta q_1+\delta^2q_2+\delta^3R_3.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The model fit to the existing abstract results is as follows.

1. The history-output map consists of the finite evaluation tuple at the
   fixed fold-time supports \(0,4,5,\Theta_*\).  Its operator-valued atomic
   measure is independent of \((\nu,\eta)\), has finite total variation, and
   has vanishing parameter derivatives.  Hence the balanced-anchor and
   moving-support seams of the
   [Banach-scale history-graph theorem](banach-scale-history-schur-link.md)
   are absent.
2. The network-transverse stable fiber disappears after exact restriction to
   synchrony: \(E_{\mathrm{net}}=\{0\}\).  This makes the network-stable
   semigroup and inverse hypotheses vacuous.  It does **not** make the scalar
   RFDE history graph trivial.  The present and delayed planar evaluations
   are still related by the nontrivial special-flow graph constructed in the
   [growing-tube theorem](growing-tube-graph-proof.md).
3. Equation (3.2) is an exact polynomial
   \(q_0+\delta q_1+\delta^2q_2+\delta^3R_3\).  After the same frozen planar
   cutoff and scalar saturation used by that theorem, its state derivatives
   through the required order, one \(\nu\)-derivative, and two
   \(\eta\)-derivatives have common polynomial bounds on the logarithmic
   tube.  The structural parameter first occurs in \(q_2\), so the required
   trace orders are
   \(\partial_\eta z=O(\delta^2)\) and
   \(\partial_{\eta\eta}z=O(\delta^2)\).
4. The uncut continuous depth-two flow hull is enlarged before the cutoff is
   frozen so that it contains every backtrack through
   \(0,4,5,\Theta_*\), including the iterated backtracks used by the first two
   graph jets.  This is the enlarged-horizon preparation fixed in (1.7), not
   an inert extension of a shorter history.
5. The reduced leading field is exactly
   \(q_0=(Y-X^2,-X)\), with the same tangent phase, opposite one-sided normal
   projections, first integral, and Gaussian adjoint as the
   [phase-normal Green theorem](green-phase-selected-traces.md).  The new
   \(q_2\) column in (3.6) has only linear growth in \(s\); it and its
   derivatives satisfy the Gaussian-weighted integrability hypotheses of the
   [trace-to-gap theorem](long-delay-selected-trace-proof.md).
6. The attracting and repelling endpoints use that theorem's fixed canonical
   tail levels and parameter-independent preparation.  Its moving-hit and
   Gaussian endpoint estimates therefore include the older-history
   derivative; no endpoint term is silently discarded.

These six checks specialize the proved growing-graph and one-sided Green
theorems to (3.2).  They do not identify the canonical traces with an
unspecified physical outer selection.

Equations (3.5) and (3.7) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{D}{\delta}
 &=\sqrt{2\pi}(\nu+1/8)+O(\delta),\\
 \partial_\nu D
 &=\delta\sqrt{2\pi}+O(\delta^2),\\
 \partial_\eta D
 &=\delta^2\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\sqrt{2\pi}
   +O(\delta^3+\delta^2|\eta|),\\
 \partial_{\eta\eta}D&=O(\delta^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The trace theorem includes the moving hits, opposite one-sided normal
projections, and prepared Gaussian tails; (3.7) is therefore not used as an
unjustified finite-window integral.  The simple-root implicit-function
argument gives

\[
 \partial_\eta\nu_c
 =-\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\delta
 +O(\delta^2+\delta|\eta|).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Integrating in \(\eta\) and multiplying by \(\delta^2\) proves (4.2).
Balanced synchrony makes the resulting scalar complete history an exact
history of (1.3) for every topology. \(\square\)

This theorem is preparation indexed in exactly the same sense as the parent
canonical theorem.  It is not yet an outer physical maximal-canard theorem.
Changing the older-history selection at \(\eta=0\) changes the threshold
object and is not a coordinate change; equation (4.2) is not claimed for
such arbitrary extensions.
It is also not a full-network one-critical-mode theorem: the class
(1.1)--(1.2) contains \(Q=I\) and reducible matrices with extra neutral
directions.  A full-network version must restrict the topology class and
prove the transverse complete-history trace inverse.

## 5. Why the linear carrier did not suffice

For the linear channel \(X(s)-X(s-\Theta_*)\), the singular canard gives

\[
 X_0(s)-X_0(s-\Theta_*)=-\frac{\Theta_*}{2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}s e^{-s^2/2}
 \{X_0(s)-X_0(s-\Theta_*)\}\,ds=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is an exact parity cancellation.  It does not prove that the fixed-ε
linear response is zero; it proves that its advertised nonzero first moment
does not give the required leading canonical root coefficient.  Flow-history
corrections, endpoints, and the dynamic adjoint would determine the next
term.

## 6. Fixed fold-time support versus fixed physical support

Theorem 4.1 keeps \(\Theta_*\) fixed and hence uses
\(\tau_*=\Theta_*/\delta\).  If instead the physical delay \(T\) is held
fixed while \(\delta\to0\), then the fold-time support is
\(\Theta(\delta)=\delta T\).  Formal substitution into (4.2) gives

\[
 \partial_\eta a_c\sim-\frac{T}{2}\delta^4.
\tag{6.1}
\]

But this is **not** a corollary of (4.2): its
\(O(\delta^4|\eta|)\) remainder is now the same order as the proposed
leading term.  In addition, a moving point evaluation requires a strong
history-space composition theorem.  A fixed-physical-delay result needs the
next gap coefficient, an \(O(\delta^5|\eta|)\) remainder, and the
moving/coalescing-support derivative-loss estimate.

At the single reference value \(\delta_*\), the two descriptions agree
exactly because of (1.6).  Substituting only in the leading coefficient gives

\[
 -\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\delta_*^3
 =-\frac{T_*}{50}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Using the diagnostic period near \(16.5404\), (6.2) is near \(-0.3308\).
This is a **leading asymptotic candidate**, not a directed enclosure of
\(\rho_*\) at \(\varepsilon=1/5\).  The unknown remainder may contain zero
or reverse the sign at that value.

## 7. The shortest fixed-ε bridge

The small-δ theorem supplies a nonzero mechanism and the correct sign, but
the flagship three-output determinant requires the actual derivative at
\(\varepsilon=1/5\).  Let \(\mathfrak F(z,a,\eta)\) be a phase-fixed
complete-history Lin/trace residual for the synchronous equation, with one
jump column \(e\).  At a selected root require the augmented derivative

\[
 (\dot z,\dot d)\longmapsto
 D_z\mathfrak F\,\dot z-\dot d\,e
\tag{7.1}
\]

to be an isomorphism.  If \(\Psi D_z\mathfrak F=0\) and
\(\Psi e=1\), then the exact response is

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_*=\partial_\eta a_c
 =-\frac{\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_\eta\rangle}
          {\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_a\rangle}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For residual convention \(\dot x-F\), the interior columns at \(\eta=0\)
are

\[
 \mathfrak F_\eta^{\rm int}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 -\varepsilon\{(V-1)^2-(V_{T_*}-1)^2\}\\
 0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak F_a^{\rm int}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The pairing in (7.2) must also include entry, exit, phase, and full-history
jump derivatives.  The minimum executable validation is:

1. construct one canonical synchronous selected-history BVP at
   \(\varepsilon=1/5\), including the full horizon
   \(\max\{4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5,T_*\}\);
2. use piecewise Chebyshev coefficients with breaks at every shifted support,
   and validate the nonlinear BVP plus (7.1) by interval Newton or a
   radii-polynomial argument;
3. validate the advanced dynamic cokernel covector and its endpoint/jump
   multipliers with \(\Psi e=1\);
4. evaluate directed intervals \(I_a\ni\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_a\rangle\)
   and \(I_\eta\ni\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_\eta\rangle\);
5. prove \(0\notin I_a\), \(0\notin I_\eta\), and report
   \(-I_\eta/I_a\) as the enclosure of \(\rho_*\);
6. obtain a \(C^2\) residual bound to turn the point derivative into a
   nonempty \(\eta\)-neighborhood root theorem.

Only after a transverse trace inverse is added may this synchronous theorem
be promoted to a full-network selected-root theorem.  Algebraic balance by
itself cannot provide that inverse for arbitrary balanced \(Q\).

## 8. The existing operational threshold is not this root

The recovery-clamped separator uses the constant reset history
\(v=0,w=0\).  For that history,

\[
 (v(t)-1)^2-(v(t-T_*)-1)^2=1-1=0.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus the zero reset remains an exact clamped equilibrium for every
\(\eta\).  Wherever the local saddle and reset transversality persist, its
local operational reset threshold remains \(r_c=0\), so its \(\eta\)-column
is exactly zero.  The quadratic carrier can move the canonical canard root;
it cannot turn the existing reset coordinate into an intrinsic safety root.
An input-independent event/root comparison and autonomous pulse/quiet
capture remain separate theorems.
