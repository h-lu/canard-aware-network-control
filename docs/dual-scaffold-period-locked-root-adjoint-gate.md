## 1. Exact augmented-BVP formula

Let \(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Y\) be the declared strong domain and residual
spaces of a phase-fixed complete-history Lin or selected-trace BVP.  Let

\[
 \mathfrak F:\mathcal Z\times\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\mathcal Y,
 \qquad (z,a,\eta)\longmapsto\mathfrak F(z,a,\eta)
\tag{1.1}
\]

be \(C^1\), and suppose
\(\mathfrak F(z_*,a_*,0)=0\).  The residual contains the differential
equations, entry and exit conditions, phase, and the **complete** history
jump.  Let \(e\in\mathcal Y\) be a jump-supported complement.

> **Theorem 1.1 (normalization-invariant root response).**  Suppose
> \(L=D_z\mathfrak F(z_*,a_*,0)\) has one-dimensional cokernel and
> \[
>  \widehat L(\dot z,\dot d)=L\dot z-\dot d\,e
> \tag{1.2}
> \]
> is an isomorphism.  Let \(\Psi\in\mathcal Y^*\) satisfy
> \(\Psi L=0\) and \(\Psi e=1\).  If
> \[
>  m_a:=\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_a\rangle\ne0,
> \tag{1.3}
> \]
> there is a unique local selected root \(a_c(\eta)\), and
> \[
>  \boxed{
>  \partial_\eta a_c(0)
>  =-\frac{m_\eta}{m_a},
>  \qquad
>  m_\eta:=\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_\eta\rangle.}
> \tag{1.4}
> \]
> In particular, \(\rho_*\ne0\) if and only if
> \(m_a m_\eta\ne0\).

**Proof.**  Solve locally
\(\mathfrak F(z(a,\eta),a,\eta)=d(a,\eta)e\) using (1.2).  Differentiation
in a parameter \(p\in\{a,\eta\}\) gives

\[
 Lz_p+\mathfrak F_p=d_p e.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Applying \(\Psi\) yields \(d_p=\langle\Psi,\mathfrak F_p\rangle\).
The scalar implicit-function theorem applied to \(d(a,\eta)=0\) gives
(1.4). \(\square\)

Every endpoint and selection derivative is already part of
\(\mathfrak F_p\) in (1.4).  Omitting it both from the residual and from the
pairing changes the derivative.  Rescaling \(e\) or \(\Psi\) multiplies
numerator and denominator by the same nonzero factor, leaving their ratio
unchanged.

## 2. The linear period-lock columns

On the synchronous restriction of the linear extension,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot V={}&F_0(V_t,W_t;a)
 +\varepsilon\eta\{V(t)-V(t-T_*)\},\\
 \dot W={}&\varepsilon(V-a),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

use residual convention \(\dot x-F\).  The interior parameter columns at
\(\eta=0\) are exactly

\[
 \mathfrak F_\eta^{\rm int}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 -\varepsilon\{V(t)-V(t-T_*)\}\\
 0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak F_a^{\rm int}
 =\begin{pmatrix}0\\\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus, if the endpoint equations are parameter independent, their dynamic
parts are

\[
 m_\eta^{\rm int}
 =-\varepsilon\int p_V(t)
 \{V(t)-V(t-T_*)\}\,dt,
 \qquad
 m_a^{\rm int}=\varepsilon\int p_W(t)\,dt.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For invariant entry/exit selections, the corresponding endpoint terms must
be added to (2.3).  The covector \((p_V,p_W)\) is the advanced dynamic
adjoint of the full BVP, not the static collective left vector \(\pi\).

## 3. Exact failure of the first-moment shortcut

Write \(\varepsilon=\delta^2\), use fold time \(s=\delta t\), and set

\[
 V=1+\delta X,\qquad
 W=\frac23-\delta^2Y,\qquad
 a=1+\delta^2\nu.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a fixed physical delay \(T_*\), the fold-time shift is
\(h=\delta T_*\).  The linear period lock enters the \(X\)-equation as

\[
 \delta\eta\{X(s)-X(s-h)\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Along the singular canard

\[
 X_0(s)=-\frac{s}{2\alpha},
 \qquad
 \psi_V(s)=s e^{-s^2/2},
\tag{3.3}
\]

one has

\[
 X_0(s)-X_0(s-h)=-\frac{h}{2\alpha}
 =-\frac{\delta T_*}{2\alpha}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently its first interior Melnikov candidate is

\[
 -\frac{\delta^2T_*}{2\alpha}
 \int_{\mathbb R}s e^{-s^2/2}\,ds=0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The same parity cancellation holds for a fixed fold-time shift.  By
contrast, the actual linear delay measure has nonzero collective first
moment \(\varepsilon T_*=T_*/5\) at the reference value.  Equations
(3.5) and \(T_*/5>0\) constitute an exact counterexample to the implication

\[
 \text{nonzero delay moment}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \partial_\eta a_c\ne0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The fixed-ε linear response may still be nonzero through higher flow-history,
endpoint, and dynamic-adjoint terms.  Equation (3.5) proves only that its
claimed leading mechanism is absent.

## 4. A second exact no-go: the clamped reset threshold

The existing recovery-clamped operational separator has the constant reset
\(V=0,W=0\).  Both linear and quadratic period locks vanish on every
constant history.  Hence this reset remains an exact clamped equilibrium for
every \(\eta\).  Under persistence of the already proved local saddle and
reset transversality, the local reset threshold remains \(r_c=0\), so

\[
 \partial_\eta r_c=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore the existing operational safety coordinate cannot be substituted
for the desired moving canard/onset root.  A new selected root and a separate
input-independent event comparison are necessary.

## 5. Minimum fixed-ε certificate

The shortest rigorous route to \(\rho_*\) at \(\varepsilon=1/5\) is:

1. define one parameter-coherent attracting/repelling selection or retain
   the canonical prepared selection explicitly;
2. validate the selected complete-history BVP and the augmented inverse
   (1.2) on the horizon \(\max\{4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5,T_*\}\);
3. validate the dynamic adjoint together with its entry, exit, phase, and
   jump multipliers;
4. enclose \(m_a\) and \(m_\eta\) by directed intervals and exclude zero
   from each;
5. report \(-m_\eta/m_a\), not a delay moment, as the enclosure of
   \(\rho_*\);
6. add a second-derivative bound to obtain a nonempty parameter box.

For the quadratic carrier, the small-δ theorem supplies a nonzero analytic
predictor and the sign of the asymptotic response.  It is therefore the
preferred column for this fixed-ε validation.
