# A period-locked collective-delay escape on balanced FHN network RFDEs

Status: **exact general-balanced-topology carrier theorem, qualitative local
three-parameter periodic theorem, exact leading linear parity cancellation,
conditional selected-root corollary, and open physical comparison.**  On an
arbitrary finite balanced topology for the baseline, a collective third
delayed-voltage channel locked to the exact period of the validated center
orbit preserves that orbit, the synchronous subspace, constant histories,
and the pure transverse action, while retaining a nonzero synchronous
collective first moment.  This removes the finite-dimensional algebraic
obstruction identified in the
[model-compatibility audit](paper-iv-root-periodic-model-compatibility.md).

It does not yet prove a selected complete-history canard root for any member
of the linear extended dual-scaffold RFDE class.  More sharply, the
[dynamic-adjoint gate](dual-scaffold-period-locked-root-adjoint-gate.md)
shows that its first singular-canard interior pairing is exactly zero by
parity despite the nonzero moment.  Thus that moment is not a substitute for
a root theorem, a uniform remainder, or validation at the common value
\(\varepsilon=1/5\).  The proved positive leading mechanism belongs instead
to the distinct
[quadratic period-locked carrier](quadratic-period-locked-selected-root.md),
whose fixed-\(\varepsilon\) response is still open.  The fixed voltage events
introduced below are input-policy-independent functionals, but this note
does not identify their zero sets with the selected root or with autonomous
biological onset.

The exact identities are implemented in
[unified_rfde_period_locked_escape.py](../src/canard_control/unified_rfde_period_locked_escape.py),
generated by
[unified_rfde_period_locked_escape.py](../experiments/unified_rfde_period_locked_escape.py),
and tested in
[test_unified_rfde_period_locked_escape.py](../tests/test_unified_rfde_period_locked_escape.py).

## 1. The declared extension and its time convention

Let \(N\ge1\), let \(\mathbf1\in\mathbb R^N\), and choose a strictly positive
row vector \(\pi^T\) and matrices \(Q,B_0,B_1\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}\)
satisfying

\[
 \pi^T\mathbf1=1,
 \qquad Q\mathbf1=\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^TQ=\pi^T,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
 B_0\mathbf1=B_1\mathbf1=\frac12\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 \pi^TB_0=\pi^TB_1=\frac12\pi^T.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Define the collective projector

\[
 \Pi=\mathbf1\pi^T.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

To remain inside the pinned balanced control class one also takes
\(Q,B_0,B_1\ge0\); nonnegativity is not used in the carrier identities below.
No equality \(B_0+B_1=Q\) or \(B_0+B_1=\Pi\) is assumed.  The rank-one
two-module plant is one member of this larger class.  Put

\[
 f(s)=s-s^3/3,
 \qquad H(s)=(s-1)^3.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

The uncontrolled dual-scaffold RFDE is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot v={}&f(v)-w+3(Q-I)v\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_1
   \{B_0v(t-\tau_0)+B_1v(t-\tau_1)-v\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\kappa_3
   \{B_0H(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1H(v(t-\tau_1))-H(v)\}\\
 &+\varepsilon\eta \Pi\{v(t)-v(t-T_*)\},\\
 \dot w={}&\varepsilon(v-a\mathbf1)+2(Q-I)w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Equivalently, the third line of (1.5) contains the delayed nonlinearity

\[
 B_0H(v(t-\tau_0))+B_1H(v(t-\tau_1))-H(v).
 \tag{1.6}
\]

We display it separately to remove any ambiguity in the long equation.  The
periodic-output fiber and center values are

\[
 \varepsilon=\frac15,
 \qquad a_{\mathrm{per}}=\frac35,
 \qquad
 (\tau_0,\tau_1)=(4\sqrt5,5\sqrt5),
 \qquad
 \kappa_*=(1/5,1/4).
 \tag{1.7}
\]

The symbol \(a\) in (1.5) remains a parameter of the RFDE family.  The
periodic outputs below are evaluated on the slice
\(a=a_{\mathrm{per}}\), whereas \(a_c(\kappa,\eta)\) denotes a selected
canard-threshold value of that same family.  No equality
\(a_c(\kappa_*,0)=a_{\mathrm{per}}\) is assumed or claimed.

Let \((V_*,W_*)\) denote the exact phase-fixed synchronous periodic solution
validated at \((a,\kappa,\eta)=(a_{\mathrm{per}},\kappa_*,0)\), and let
\(T_*>0\) be its exact period.  The
binary64 value near \(16.5403877982\) is only a diagnostic approximation;
the definition of (1.5) uses the mathematical period \(T_*\), not that
floating-point number.

The balance identities give

\[
 (Q-I)(s\mathbf1)=0,
 \qquad B_j(s\mathbf1)=\frac{s}{2}\mathbf1.
 \tag{1.8}
\]

Hence every topology in the class has the same synchronous scalar
restriction.  In particular,
\((V_*\mathbf1,W_*\mathbf1)\) is the same exact center periodic solution for
every such \(Q,B_0,B_1\).  This is a synchronous-branch statement, not a
full-network attraction statement.

The equation is nondimensionalized in the same convention as the periodic
parent.  The gain \(\eta\) therefore has the same gain dimension as
\(\kappa_1\), and the common factor \(\varepsilon\) in (1.5) is part of the
declared channel rather than an asymptotic omission.

All delays in (1.5) are measured in the **common physical time** \(t\).
Thus \(T_*\), \(\tau_0\), and \(\tau_1\) have the same units.  In a fold-time
coordinate \(s=\delta t\), the third support point is
\(\Theta_*=\delta T_*\).  A small-\(\delta\) root theorem with a fixed
fold-time delay support would have to control \(\delta T_*(\delta)\)
uniformly.  No such scaling theorem is inherited from the fixed
\(\varepsilon=1/5\) periodic validation.

The declared phase space uses
\(\tau_{\max}=\max\{\tau_1,T_*\}\).  For a parent history \(\phi\), define one
explicit constant inert-history embedding by

\[
 (\iota\phi)(\theta)=
 \begin{cases}
   \phi(\theta),&-\tau_1\le\theta\le0,\\
   \phi(-\tau_1),&-\tau_{\max}\le\theta< -\tau_1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1.9a}
\]

At \(\eta=0\), the vector field ignores the older segment, so the trajectory
from \(\iota\phi\) projects to the parent trajectory.  Other continuous older
extensions give the same projected trajectory; the lift is not unique.  This
exact embedding transfers trajectory statements at \(\eta=0\), but it does
not supply the longer complete-history hold needed when \(\eta\ne0\).

This trajectory identity must not be differentiated in \(\eta\) without an
additional choice.  Although the older segment is inert at \(\eta=0\), the
\(\eta\)-column samples \(v(-T_*)\).  A selected-root theorem must therefore
work on the **enlarged horizon**
\(\max\{\tau_0,\tau_1,T_*\}\) and fix one parameter-coherent preparation of
that entire history for all nearby \(\eta\).  Arbitrary older extensions that
are equivalent for the \(\eta=0\) trajectory can change endpoint or history
pairings in the derivative, and may change or cancel a proposed response.

There is a typographical reason for keeping (1.5) explicit: the new term is
current minus delayed,

\[
 \mathcal A_{T_*}\phi
 =\Pi\{\phi_v(0)-\phi_v(-T_*)\},
 \tag{1.9}
\]

whereas the baseline linear channel is delayed minus current.  The sign in
(1.9) fixes the first-moment convention used below.  The map \(\Pi\) uses the
global weighted mean \(\pi^Tv\); no sparse or local implementation theorem is
claimed.

## 2. Conditions a genuine unified theorem must satisfy

The following conditions concern one declared RFDE family, not three models
sharing only an FHN cubic.

### 2.1 Literal plant identity

The selected histories, periodic orbit, event surface, and any autonomous
excursion must use the same phase space, recovery dimension, instantaneous
scaffolds, delay supports and layers, nonlinear delayed channel, and values
of all parameters not explicitly varied.  A controlled preparation may add
inputs to this drift, but it cannot replace the drift and still count as an
uncontrolled connection theorem.

### 2.2 Balanced synchronous restriction

For every delayed operator \(L_j\), synchrony requires

\[
 L_j\mathbf1=\lambda_j\mathbf1.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The stronger left identity

\[
 \pi^TL_j=\lambda_j\pi^T
 \tag{2.2}
\]

decouples the collective and transverse linear blocks.  The nonlinear field
must also map every synchronous history to \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\).
For a full-network periodic conclusion, invariance alone is insufficient;
one additionally needs the required transverse variational and nonlinear
estimates.

### 2.3 Selected complete-history root

At the relevant fold, a root theorem needs a simple critical pair \((r,\ell)\),
nonzero fold curvature and unfolding pairings, invertibility of the stable
history equation, compatible attracting and repelling preparations, and a
simple selected gap

\[
 D(\nu,\kappa,\eta)=0,
 \qquad |\partial_\nu D|\ge c_0>0.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

The balance identities alone do not supply that simple pair.  The class
contains \(Q=I\) and reducible \(Q\), for which \(\ker(Q-I)\) can be
multidimensional.  A future general-network root theorem must therefore prove
simplicity of the relevant characteristic root and invertibility of the
stable history operator, possibly after imposing additional spectral or
mixing assumptions on \(Q,B_0,B_1\).  Neither property is part of the exact
carrier theorem.

To use \(\eta\) as the third response direction, it must prove in the same
model, with an honest remainder,

\[
 \partial_\eta a_c(\kappa_*,0)\ne0.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Here \(a_c\) is the root output: the future root expansion is based at
\((a,\kappa,\eta)=(a_c(\kappa_*,0),\kappa_*,0)\), not at
\((a_{\mathrm{per}},\kappa_*,0)\) unless a new theorem proves those values
equal.

The nonzero delay moment in Section 4 does not determine the sign or
nonvanishing in (2.4).  Section 5 gives an exact counterexample to the
corresponding **leading-order moment argument**: the linear channel has
nonzero moment but zero leading singular-canard interior pairing.  Endpoint
terms, stable returns,
preparation dependence, and higher-order terms still require a
complete-history root and dynamic-adjoint calculation.

### 2.4 Periodic response

Here

\[
 F_{\mathrm{per}}=T_{\mathrm{per}}^{-1},
 \qquad
 A_{\mathrm{per}}=R_h=(V_{\max}-V_{\min})^2
 \tag{2.5a}
\]

are the two outputs of the pinned periodic parameter-box theorem, evaluated
on \(a=a_{\mathrm{per}}\).  Thus \(A_{\mathrm{per}}\) denotes the validated
squared voltage range; an unsquared amplitude requires its separate transfer
theorem.

The phase-fixed periodic equation must have an invertible bordered
linearization, and

\[
 \det D_{(\kappa_1,\kappa_3)}
 (F_{\mathrm{per}},A_{\mathrm{per}})(\kappa_*,0;a_{\mathrm{per}})\ne0.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

For a quantitative three-parameter theorem, the orbit, its extrema, and its
response derivatives must be validated on a genuine
\((\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)\)-neighborhood.  An exact orbit at the center does
not supply such a box.

### 2.5 Input-independent event

Put \(\tau_{\max}=\max\{\tau_1,T_*\}\).  An event functional is a fixed map
on the uncontrolled history phase space,

\[
 \mathcal E:C([-\tau_{\max},0],\mathbb R^{2N})\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \tag{2.6}
\]

whose definition contains no reset depth, feedback policy, controlled root,
or offline root table.  A simple first hit also needs a nonzero Lie
derivative at crossing.  To identify \(\mathcal E=0\) with pulse onset, one
must additionally prove sign-to-channel capture, a pulse/quiet basin
statement, and the required no-return property.

To identify its parameter root with the selected canard root, a sufficient
comparison is

\[
 E(\nu,\kappa,\eta)
 =\chi(\nu,\kappa,\eta)D(\nu,\kappa,\eta),
 \qquad \inf|\chi|>0,
 \tag{2.7}
\]

on a common neighborhood.  Neither a common zero at one parameter nor a
controller-created detector latency implies (2.7).

### 2.6 One parameter regime

The canard theorem and periodic theorem must overlap at the same
\(\varepsilon\).  Here the periodic result is fixed at \(\varepsilon=1/5\).
An unspecified smallness condition \(0<\delta<\delta_0\), with
\(\varepsilon=\delta^2\), does not show that
\(\delta=1/\sqrt5\) is admitted.

## 3. Why an arbitrary third fixed atom does not solve the obstruction

The two-atom obstruction extends to every finite number of distinct fixed
support points.

> **Lemma 3.1 (finite-atom synchronous invisibility).**
> Let \(0\le\sigma_0<\cdots<\sigma_m\), and let
> \[
>  \mathcal R\phi=\sum_{j=0}^mR_j\phi(-\sigma_j)
>  \tag{3.1}
> \]
> be a linear structural direction.  Then \(\mathcal R(s\mathbf1)=0\) for
> every continuous scalar history \(s\) if and only if
> \[
>  R_j\mathbf1=0\qquad(0\le j\le m).
>  \tag{3.2}
> \]
> Consequently, adding a third distinct fixed atom does not restore direct
> forcing of the synchronous critical history while retaining invisibility
> on every synchronous history.

**Proof.** Sufficiency is immediate.  For necessity, evaluation at finitely
many distinct points maps the scalar continuous-history space onto
\(\mathbb R^{m+1}\): prescribe arbitrary values at the support points and
interpolate them piecewise linearly.  Hence

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^m s(-\sigma_j)R_j\mathbf1=0
 \tag{3.3}
\]

for every coefficient vector only when each column \(R_j\mathbf1\) is zero.
The synchronous critical vector is \(\mathbf1\), which proves the last
claim. \(\square\)

Lemma 3.1 is limited to a finite set of distinct fixed delays, linear layer
perturbations, and exact invisibility on all synchronous histories.  It does
not rule out orbit-specific annihilation, higher-order effects, moving
delays, or an off-synchronous critical direction.

There is also a second exact obstruction to importing the existing
heterogeneous-curvature root theorem.  On a synchronous displacement
\(v=(1+x)\mathbf1\), its quadratic term is \(-c\,x^2\).  Invariance for all
small \(x\) forces \(c=c_0\mathbf1\).  But its stable response
\(h_*\) lies in \(\ker\pi^T\), so the theorem's heterogeneous return becomes

\[
 \pi^T\operatorname{diag}(c)h_*
 =c_0\pi^Th_*=0.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the already-proved nonzero heterogeneous return and an exact balanced
synchronous branch cannot simply be imposed simultaneously.  A different
root mechanism is necessary.

## 4. The balanced-topology period-locked identity

The escape from Lemma 3.1 is to weaken universal invisibility to exact
annihilation of the distinguished periodic orbit.

> **Theorem 4.1 (period-locked algebraic carrier).**
> For every finite \(N\) and every \(Q,B_0,B_1,\pi\) satisfying
> (1.1)--(1.2), the RFDE (1.5) and the operator \(\mathcal A_{T_*}\) have the
> following exact properties.
>
> 1. The synchronous restriction of the baseline RFDE is independent of
>    \(Q,B_0,B_1\).  Thus the lifted center orbit
>    \((V_*\mathbf1,W_*\mathbf1)\) exists for every topology in the class.
> 2. The added operator maps every synchronous history into
>    \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\).
> 3. It vanishes on every constant history.
> 4. It vanishes pointwise on the lifted distinguished
>    \(T_*\)-periodic orbit.  That specified solution, its period, frequency,
>    and squared voltage range are therefore unchanged for every \(\eta\).
> 5. Its action on a pure \(\ker\pi^T\) transverse history is zero, and its
>    output has zero
>    transverse projection.
> 6. The normalized operator has atoms \(+\Pi,-\Pi\), while the actual
>    \(\eta\)-derivative of (1.5) has atoms
>    \(R_0^\eta=\varepsilon\Pi\) and
>    \(R_{T_*}^\eta=-\varepsilon\Pi\).  Both have zero total mass, and
>    \[
>    \begin{aligned}
>      M_1^{\mathrm{norm}}&=-T_*\Pi,
>      &-\pi^TM_1^{\mathrm{norm}}\mathbf1&=T_*,\\
>      M_1^\eta&=-\varepsilon T_*\Pi=-\frac{T_*}{5}\Pi,
>      &-\pi^TM_1^\eta\mathbf1&=\frac{T_*}{5}>0.
>    \end{aligned}
>      \tag{4.1}
>    \]
> 7. On the affine synchronous history
>    \(\phi_v(\theta)=(x+c\theta)\mathbf1\),
>    \[
>      \mathcal A_{T_*}\phi=cT_*\mathbf1.
>      \tag{4.2}
>    \]

**Proof.** The balance relations give, for arbitrary scalars
\(s,s_0,s_1\),

\[
 (Q-I)(s\mathbf1)=0,
 \qquad
 B_0(s_0\mathbf1)+B_1(s_1\mathbf1)
 =\frac{s_0+s_1}{2}\mathbf1.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

The same identities hold after applying the componentwise function \(H\),
so item 1 follows without any equality among \(Q\), \(B_0+B_1\), and
\(\Pi\).  From \(\Pi=\mathbf1\pi^T\),

\[
 \Pi\mathbf1=\mathbf1,
 \qquad \pi^T\Pi=\pi^T,
 \qquad \Pi(I-\Pi)=(I-\Pi)\Pi=0.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

These identities prove items 2 and 5.  Equal current and delayed vectors prove
item 3.  Periodicity gives
\(V_*(t-T_*)=V_*(t)\), proving item 4.  The normalized atoms of (1.9) are
\(+\Pi\) at zero and \(-\Pi\) at \(T_*\); differentiating the last term of
(1.5) with respect to \(\eta\) multiplies both by
\(\varepsilon=1/5\).  Their sums and positive-delay moments give (4.1),
proving item 6.  Finally,

\[
 \Pi\{x\mathbf1-(x-cT_*)\mathbf1\}=cT_*\mathbf1,
\]

which proves item 7. \(\square\)

Here the positive-delay convention is
\(M_1=\sum_{\sigma\ge0}\sigma R_\sigma\).  Both the normalized and actual
identities in (4.1), together with
\(\varepsilon=1/5\), are recorded in the executable certificate.  They are
**synchronous collective moments**, not canard-critical coefficients.  They
become projections on a critical pair only if a future root theorem proves
that the relevant simple pair is the collective pair and closes the stable
history inverse described in Section 2.3.  The convention also confirms that
\(T_*\) has the same time unit as \(\tau_0,\tau_1\).

Theorem 4.1 does **not** say that (1.9) is invisible on every synchronous
history.  Indeed, on \(s\mathbf1\) it is

\[
 \{s(0)-s(-T_*)\}\mathbf1,
 \tag{4.5}
\]

which is generally nonzero.  This deliberate loss of universal invisibility
is what permits the nonzero moment in (4.1).
In particular, the period-locked channel is not invisible on every
synchronous history.

The topology independence in item 1 concerns only the invariant synchronous
branch and its scalar outputs.  Although the added channel itself kills pure
transverse histories, neither Theorem 4.1 nor the pinned periodic result proves
transverse attraction, full-network periodic attraction, or persistence of
those properties for \(\eta\ne0\).

The result is also center-specific.  The delay remains the fixed number
\(T_*=T(\kappa_*)\).  For \(\kappa\ne\kappa_*\), generally

\[
 V_\kappa(t-T_*)\ne V_\kappa(t),
 \tag{4.6}
\]

because \(T(\kappa)\ne T_*\).  Therefore Theorem 4.1 gives exact
non-invasiveness at the center, not period locking on the full gain box.  A
parameter-dependent delay \(T(\kappa)\) would place the unknown orbit output
inside the plant definition and is not the fixed-delay RFDE considered here.

The center identity nevertheless combines with the already validated
phase-bordered inverse to give a local qualitative result.

> **Corollary 4.2 (qualitative periodic branch and zero \(\eta\)-column).**
> On the synchronous restriction at \(a=a_{\mathrm{per}}=3/5\) and
> \(\varepsilon=1/5\), there is a neighborhood of
> \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)=(1/5,1/4,0)\) on which the phase-fixed periodic
> BVP for (1.5) has a unique \(C^1\) solution branch.  At the center,
> \[
>  \partial_\eta(V,W)=0,
>  \qquad \partial_\eta T=0,
>  \qquad \partial_\eta F_{\mathrm{per}}
>  =\partial_\eta A_{\mathrm{per}}=0.
>  \tag{4.7}
> \]

**Proof.**  At \(\eta=0\), the new term contributes neither to the state
linearization nor to the period column, since every such derivative is
multiplied by \(\eta\).  Thus the phase-bordered derivative at the center is
exactly the invertible bordered operator validated in the periodic parent.
For the fixed physical support \(T_*\), shifted evaluation is bounded on the
same strong periodic space, and the added term is \(C^1\).  The Banach
implicit-function theorem gives the local three-parameter branch.

The inhomogeneous \(\eta\)-column is
\(\mathcal A_{T_*}(V_*)\), which vanishes pointwise by \(T_*\)-periodicity.
The \(\eta\)-sensitivity therefore solves the homogeneous bordered equation;
invertibility makes the orbit and period sensitivities zero.  Frequency
follows from \(F=T^{-1}\).  The squared-range derivative is zero because the
orbit derivative is zero; equivalently one may use the already validated
simple extrema and the envelope formula. \(\square\)

Corollary 4.2 is qualitative.  It supplies no directed radius in \(\eta\),
no three-parameter boxwise inverse, no proof that the unique extrema persist
on such a box, and no three-parameter target-ball radius.  These quantitative
claims remain open.  It also concerns the synchronous branch only and does
not imply full-network attraction.

Period mistuning is likewise not covered.  If \(V_*\) is \(C^1\), then a
delay error \(e\) gives only the elementary bound

\[
 \|V_*(\cdot)-V_*(\cdot-T_*-e)\|_\infty
 \le |e|\,\|\dot V_*\|_\infty.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

A robust periodic or control theorem would need to propagate this residual
through its own inverse and barrier margins.

## 5. Exact linear cancellation, the quadratic escape, and the conditional block

### 5.1 The linear first moment does not produce the leading root column

Write \(\varepsilon=\delta^2\), use fold time \(s=\delta t\), and let
\(\alpha>0\) denote the fold-curvature normalization.  A fixed physical
delay \(T_*\) becomes \(h=\delta T_*\).  Along the affine singular canard and
its leading fast adjoint component,

\[
 X_0(s)=-\frac{s}{2\alpha},
 \qquad \psi_v(s)=s e^{-s^2/2},
 \tag{5.1}
\]

the linear period lock gives

\[
 X_0(s)-X_0(s-h)=-\frac{\delta T_*}{2\alpha}.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

After division of the physical fast equation by \(\delta^2\), its
\(\eta\)-coefficient is \(\delta\{X(s)-X(s-h)\}\).  Therefore the first
singular interior pairing is exactly

\[
 -\frac{\delta^2T_*}{2\alpha}
 \int_{\mathbb R}s e^{-s^2/2}\,ds=\boxed{0}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

This is an exact parity cancellation, while (4.1) simultaneously gives the
nonzero actual moment \(T_*/5\) at \(\varepsilon=1/5\).  Together they refute
the proposed inference from a nonzero moment to a nonzero **leading
singular-canard pairing**.  They do not decide the full fixed-\(\varepsilon\)
response: higher-order flow-history, entry, exit, phase, jump, and
dynamic-adjoint terms may survive.  Thus \(\partial_\eta a_c\) for the linear
RFDE (1.5) remains open, and the linear moment alone is not a positive
mechanism for it.

Any such calculation must also use the enlarged horizon and one fixed
parameter-coherent older-history preparation described after (1.9a).
Differentiating an arbitrary \(\eta=0\) inert extension is not a defined root
response.

### 5.2 The proved positive leading mechanism is quadratic

The separate quadratic carrier replaces the linear added operator by

\[
 \mathcal A^{(2)}_{\tau_*}\phi
 =\Pi\bigl\{(\phi_v(0)-1)^2-(\phi_v(-\tau_*)-1)^2\bigr\}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

In the quadratic theorem's normalized fold chart \(X_0=-s/2\).  For fixed
fold-time support \(\Theta_*>0\), so that
\(\tau_*=\Theta_*/\delta\), its singular profile satisfies

\[
 X_0(s)^2-X_0(s-\Theta_*)^2
 =\frac{\Theta_*}{2}s-\frac{\Theta_*^2}{4},
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}\psi_v(s)
 \{X_0(s)^2-X_0(s-\Theta_*)^2\}\,ds
 =\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\sqrt{2\pi}>0.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

With its explicitly fixed canonical enlarged-horizon preparation, the
quadratic theorem proves

\[
 a_c(\delta,\eta)-a_c(\delta,0)
 =-\frac{\Theta_*}{2}\delta^3\eta
  +O(\delta^4|\eta|+\delta^3\eta^2)
 \tag{5.6}
\]

for sufficiently small \(\delta\) on that fixed-scaled-support family.  This
is the positive escape from (5.3).  It belongs to a **different extended
RFDE** from the linear carrier (1.5), and its theorem does not show that the
unspecified small-\(\delta\) interval contains
\(\delta=1/\sqrt5\).  In particular, the formal reference coefficient
\(-T_*/50\) is not a rigorous fixed-\(\varepsilon\) enclosure, and no physical
onset conclusion follows from (5.6).

### 5.3 Conditional fixed-\(\varepsilon\) block response

The exact periodic response on the slice \(a=a_{\mathrm{per}}\) has zero
\(\eta\)-column by Corollary 4.2.  Suppose a future theorem for the **same
linear extended RFDE family (1.5)** proves, on its possibly different canard
slice, a selected complete-history root

\[
 a_c=a_c(\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)
 \tag{5.7}
\]

at the common value \(\varepsilon=1/5\), with

\[
 \rho_*:=\partial_\eta a_c(\kappa_*,0)\ne0.
 \tag{5.8}
\]

It must obtain (5.8) from the full selected-history gap, not from (4.1).

> **Corollary 5.1 (conditional block response).**
> Under (5.7)--(5.8) and the validated nonsingularity (2.5), the
> parameter-linked response
> \[
>  \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{par}}(\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)
>  =\bigl(
>    F_{\mathrm{per}}(\kappa,\eta;a_{\mathrm{per}}),
>    A_{\mathrm{per}}(\kappa,\eta;a_{\mathrm{per}}),
>    a_c(\kappa,\eta)
>   \bigr)
>  \tag{5.9}
> \]
> has at \((\kappa,\eta)=(\kappa_*,0)\) the derivative
> \[
>  D\mathcal Q=
>  \begin{pmatrix}
>   F_1&F_3&0\\ A_1&A_3&0\\ p&q&\rho_*
>  \end{pmatrix},
>  \qquad
>  \det D\mathcal Q=(F_1A_3-F_3A_1)\rho_*\ne0.
>  \tag{5.10}
> \]

**Proof.** Corollary 4.2 gives the zero central periodic \(\eta\)-column.
Expanding the determinant along that column gives (5.10). \(\square\)

Corollary 5.1 is conditional because no current theorem proves (5.7)--(5.8)
for (1.5).  It is not legitimate to import either the incompatible lifted-root
coefficient or the quadratic coefficient (5.6).  Nor does the fixed-
\(\varepsilon\) periodic theorem place the quadratic small-parameter result at
\(\varepsilon=1/5\).

The subscript “par” is essential: (5.9) is a **parameter-linked** response
across two \(a\)-slices of one RFDE family.  It does not place the canard root
and periodic orbit in one parameter instance, connect them by one trajectory,
or imply a root-to-event or root-to-pulse mechanism.

## 6. Fixed autonomous event functionals

The positive and negative excursion faces used by the autonomous-handoff
analysis suggest the fixed history functionals

\[
 \mathcal E_+(\phi)=\pi^T\phi_v(0)-\frac32,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_-(\phi)=-\pi^T\phi_v(0)-\frac65.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

They are input-policy-independent: their definitions contain no reset
coordinate, selected root, feedback law, or controlled latency.  For the
uncontrolled semiflow \(\mathcal S_t\) of (1.5), one may define, whenever the
sets are reached,

\[
 \tau_\pm(\phi)
 =\inf\{t>0:\mathcal E_\pm(\mathcal S_t\phi)=0\}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

At \(\eta=0\), the constant inert-history embedding in Section 1 transfers the
pinned balanced control-chain theorem to (1.5): for every topology in its
declared class, bounded controlled preparation leads to positive and negative
**controlled voltage excursions**.  Those crossings depend on the applied
preparation/decision policy.  They are neither unforced onset nor a biological
action-potential theorem, and the parent result says so explicitly.

This makes (6.1) intrinsic as an event definition; it does not make it a
physical onset boundary.  For the pinned rank-one member at \(\eta=0\), the
handoff theorem proves finite autonomous crossings after specially controlled
complete-history preparation.  It does not prove this for every balanced
topology.  The preparation is not intrinsic onset, and its proof does not show
that selected canard histories reach (6.1).

For \(\eta\ne0\), the new collective term generally does not vanish during
the handoff excursion.  The old barrier theorem therefore cannot be inherited
unchanged.  A new proof must bound

\[
 \varepsilon|\eta|
 \|\Pi\{v(t)-v(t-T_*)\}\|_\infty
 \tag{6.3}
\]

against every inward barrier margin and must hold the complete history for
the enlarged maximum delay \(\tau_{\max}\).  No \(\eta\)-neighborhood
controlled-excursion or autonomous-handoff claim is made here.

To promote (6.1) to a canard-to-pulse theorem one still needs all of:

1. physical or parameter-coherent outer histories entering the selected
   local fold chart;
2. the zero-set comparison (2.7), or an equivalent uniform theorem;
3. simple autonomous crossings from those histories;
4. capture of opposite signs in distinct pulse/quiet channels; and
5. the appropriate basin and no-return conclusion.

## 7. Why the other minimal extensions are weaker

Here “minimal” means one added linear delay support and no added dynamic
state, within the alternatives listed below.  It is not a global minimality
theorem over all nonlinear or infinite-dimensional controller architectures.

### 7.1 One additional dynamic state

If an auxiliary state \(q\) has \(q=0\) invariant for every synchronous
history and enters the voltage field as \(\eta q\mathbf1\), then a canard
prepared with \(q=0\) receives no direct forcing.  Choosing \(q(0)\ne0\) from
a reset policy merely moves the earlier policy injection into an extra state.

A bistable gate can make \(q=0\) carry the periodic model and \(q=1\) carry
the old root model.  This is a smooth formula on one enlarged phase space,
but the two results live on disconnected invariant leaves.  Without an
autonomous transition and comparison theorem, it is a direct-product
packaging, not a trajectory-linked unification.

An internal-model state calibrated to annihilate the exact periodic orbit can
work algebraically, but it must encode at least its period and usually its
waveform or phase.  The period-locked delay (1.9) uses less auxiliary data and
no new state.

### 7.2 A generic third delay atom

Lemma 3.1 rules this out if exact invisibility on every synchronous history
is retained.  The successful third atom is not generic: its support is the
distinguished period and its cancellation is orbit-specific.

### 7.3 A nonlinear synchronization-vanishing actuator

Let \(\mathcal N\) be \(C^1\) and vanish on the synchronous history manifold.
If \(\phi\) is synchronous and \(\psi\) is tangent to that manifold, then
\(\phi+s\psi\) is synchronous and

\[
 D\mathcal N(\phi)\psi
 =\left.\frac d{ds}\right|_{s=0}\mathcal N(\phi+s\psi)=0.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Thus such an actuator cannot provide direct first-order forcing of a
synchronous canard critical direction.  It may contribute through mixed
higher-order terms or act on an off-synchronous fold, but either route needs a
new root expansion.

The algebraic off-synchronous possibility is nonempty.  With

\[
 r=(1,2)^T,
 \qquad \ell=(1/2,1/4)^T,
 \qquad
 T_\perp=
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\2&-2\end{pmatrix},
 \tag{7.2}
\]

one has

\[
 T_\perp\mathbf1=0,
 \qquad T_\perp r=(1,-2)^T\ne0,
 \qquad \ell^TT_\perp r=0.
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Hence universal sync invisibility and direct off-sync stable forcing can
coexist.  What is absent is a theorem constructing that off-synchronous fold,
its selected root, and the balanced periodic branch inside one
dual-scaffold RFDE.  Equation (7.3) is a witness, not that theorem.

## 8. Exact theorem, conditional theorem, and open theorem

The strongest honest conclusion is the following three-level statement.

> **Exact carrier theorem.**  For every finite balanced topology in
> (1.1)--(1.2), the RFDE (1.5) has the same invariant synchronous scalar
> restriction, contains the fixed input-policy-independent events (6.1), and
> retains the exact lifted center periodic orbit for every \(\eta\).  Its added
> collective channel has zero total mass, zero pure-transverse action, and
> nonzero normalized and actual synchronous collective first moments.  Its
> leading linear singular-canard interior pairing is nevertheless exactly
> zero by parity.

This theorem is algebraic and uses the existence of the pinned center orbit.
It does not assert a selected root for (1.5), full-network attraction, or a
sparse realization of the collective channel.
At \(\eta=0\), its constant inert-history embedding also carries the pinned balanced
controlled voltage excursions; that statement remains policy-dependent.

> **Qualitative periodic theorem.**  The validated center bordered inverse
> and the fixed-support \(C^1\) linear carrier give a unique local
> \((\kappa_1,\kappa_3,\eta)\) synchronous periodic branch, with zero central
> \(\eta\)-derivatives of the orbit, period, frequency, and squared range.

This does not supply a quantitative \(\eta\)-radius or full-network
attraction.  The distinct quadratic carrier supplies a positive leading
small-\(\delta\) root response for one canonical enlarged-horizon preparation;
it does not validate nonzero response at \(\varepsilon=1/5\).

> **Conditional root-response theorem.**  If a complete-history analysis of
> (1.5) at the common \(\varepsilon=1/5\) proves a simple selected root with
> \(\partial_\eta a_c\ne0\), then the parameter-linked center response map
> (5.9) is locally nonsingular by (5.10).

The hypothesis is a substantive missing theorem, not a genericity
assumption that may be silently accepted.

> **Open intrinsic onset theorem.**  Prove that the physically selected
> unforced histories have a signed event satisfying (2.7), that its two signs
> enter distinct global channels, and that the pulse side has the required
> basin and no-return properties.

No controlled reset, event cancellation, or period lock proves this last
statement.
